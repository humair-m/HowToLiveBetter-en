# How To Live Better · Project Rules

The following rules apply to all work in this repository and must be followed.

## Project positioning
- This is a life guide sorted by "cost-effectiveness." It borrows the quantitative style of geekan/HowToLiveLonger but covers a wider scope: it optimizes not just lifespan but four kinds of resources — lifespan, time and energy, money, and personal freedom. It covers not just staying alive, but also money, law, work, family, and parenting.
- Every piece of advice must answer two questions: what does it cost (money / time / energy / willpower), and what does it get back (change in all-cause mortality / reduction in specific causes of death / time and energy saved / money saved / protection and personal freedom).
- Sorting principle: cost-effectiveness first, not category first. Entries with "near-zero cost, large benefit" go to the front.

## Whose benefit is being counted (beneficiary metric)
- The whole book accounts by cost-effectiveness. Beneficiaries fall into four tiers, sorted by "expected likelihood that this benefit eventually comes back to you" from high to low: ① **Yourself**; ② **Spouse and immediate family** (parents, children, grandparents on both sides, grandchildren on both sides); ③ **Friends, colleagues, and other relatives** — reciprocal relationships, help given may come back; ④ **Strangers** — lowest tier, but not zero: low probability of return, you don't know their character, and there's a separate risk of being extorted, falsely accused, or retaliated against.
- Benefits across tiers are not combined. When writing about tier ④, you must spell out the risk surface together with the benefit (extortion, getting pulled into a case, retaliation) — neither listing only the benefits nor writing it as "never help strangers" is acceptable.
- For entries involving others, follow this order: ① First write what to do when this happens to you or your family — this is the main line and the bulk of the "Benefit" column; ② Then write the differences when it involves friends/colleagues vs. strangers, focusing on self-preservation actions and immunity clauses (don't enter the water, don't physically break up fights, don't move people casually, don't break the law even if you don't call the police); ③ In the Note, point out which tier this entry's beneficiary mainly falls into.
- The beneficiary tier does not change the benefit magnitude (the magnitude is still mechanically fit from the entry's own "Benefit" column); it affects whether this entry is worth that cost for you, and noting it in the Note is enough.

## When writing "the law supports you" entries, you must write the process cost
- For any entry that lands on "the law supports you," "you can demand," or "you will eventually win the case," writing only the outcome without the process amounts to treating the win rate as a benefit. You must also explain: does it require a lawsuit, roughly how long (first-instance ordinary procedure starts at 6 months and can be extended; summary procedure 3 months), and who pays the lawyer's fee (lawyer's fees are not in the litigation costs listed in Article 6 of the *Measures on the Payment of Litigation Costs*, and "borne by the losing party" in Article 29 does not cover them).
- Likewise, when citing an immunity or evidence rule to reassure the reader, be clear about which segment it actually covers: a rule about the judgment outcome does not equal a rule about "getting pulled into the case"; a rule about civil matters does not equal a rule about administrative or criminal matters. Before writing, ask: "Is the risk this law addresses the same risk the reader actually fears?"

## Evidence grading (every entry must carry one)
- Grade A: Has quantifiable evidence from a meta-analysis, large cohort, or RCT, with specific numbers (e.g., HR, RR, percentage decrease)
- Grade B: Has research support but hard to quantify, or evidence is from small-sample / single-study
- Grade C: Author's experience or general consensus, with no direct literature
- Controversial A/B-grade entries must be flagged "controversial" and list counter-evidence

## Citation rules
- Cite only primary literature: journal papers (with DOI or PubMed link), reports from official bodies such as WHO / CDC / National Bureau of Statistics
- Citing secondary paraphrases from Zhihu, WeChat Official Accounts, Sohu, NetEase, Baijiahao, etc., is forbidden
- Numbers must be findable in the source; if a number is uncertain, don't write it — write "to be verified"
- Don't fabricate DOIs or paper titles from memory; if you can't find the original, mark it TODO — don't fill in blindly

## Entry format
Each entry uses:
- **Advice** (one sentence, verb-first)
- Cost:
- Plain-language summary:
- Benefit:
- Evidence grade: A / B / C
- Source:
- Note: (controversies, applicable populations, cautions)
- Below the title line, immediately followed by an HTML comment `<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0|少|多 时间=少|中|多 毅力=否|些|是 收益=大|中|小 口径=死亡率|金钱|时间|自由 -->`. It's invisible when GitHub renders the markdown, and is used by index.html's online search to filter by cost dimensions; maintain it whenever entries are added or modified. Meanings: 钱 (money) 0 = no cost or saves money, 少 (low) = tens to hundreds of yuan or under tens of yuan per month, 多 (high) = thousands of yuan or significant long-term spending; 时间 (time) 少 = one-time within minutes or in passing, 中 = one-time several hours or weekly hour-level, 多 = daily continuous occupation; 毅力 (willpower) 否 = one-off action, 些 = change a habit or endure mild discomfort, 是 = long-term fight against inertia
- **Literature links always go in the Source column — don't pile them up in the Note** (2026-09-21: a user looking at a card on the search page asked "why are there a bunch of links in this Note"). Cause: the same entry was expanded across three rounds, each round casually adding a DOI to the Note, accumulating to 3 and inflating the Note to nearly a thousand characters. Supplementary evidence, counter-evidence, and "way-out" evidence **all go in the Source column too**; the Note only states conclusions and numbers, with no parenthetical citations. At most one link may remain in the Note, and only the one pointing to the long-form doc under docs/. Criterion: a `grep -c http` scan of all Notes in the book should return 0 to 1 for a normal entry; 2 warrants a look, 3 means it has accumulated. Notes over 700 characters are also a signal — move the detail into a long-form doc per "long explanations go to docs/" or merge into the Source column.
- The "Plain-language summary" translates the statistics in the "Benefit" column into one to two everyday sentences: HR/RR/OR are uniformly converted into "about X-tenths higher" / "about X percent lower"; no population, no study design — only the direction and magnitude the reader cares about. Words like HR, RR, OR, CI, cohort, meta-analysis may not appear, nor may numbers not in the "Benefit" column. **It's not just numbers that can't be added**: symptoms, factual claims, and mechanism interpretations not in the Benefit column are equally forbidden (2026-09-19: a user caught two — the blood-donation entry said "'looking pale, sensitive to cold' isn't made up," but "looking pale" had no source anywhere in the entry; the cold-shower entry interpreted "fewer sick days but no change in total days" as "still went to work sick," which the original study did not say. Both were fixed). **The plain-language summary must also be self-contained**: most readers only read this one line, not the source or the verification record, so don't quote a claim they haven't seen and then evaluate it — "'XX' isn't made up" / "'XX' is true" is forbidden; either state the claim with its source within this line, or rewrite as a positive statement (the blood-donation line was eventually changed to "Conversely, donating too often does make people tired and sensitive to cold — the mechanism is iron depletion"). Don't modify the "Benefit" column while writing the plain-language summary. Money- and freedom-metric entries follow the same rule — translate into "how much money can be recovered / what consequences you'll face." The search page renders this as the most prominent paragraph on the card; if missing, the card won't display, so it can be added section by section.
- **The entire book must be written in Chinese that an adult without professional training can read end-to-end on a single pass** (2026-09-20 user decided: "I kind of don't understand, make it plainer" / "Otherwise the bar is too high"). This covers entry titles, Cost, Plain-language summary, Benefit, Note, and long-form docs under docs/; **the Source column is excepted** — bibliographic records and clause numbers stay verbatim so they can be verified. Method: explain a technical term on the spot in everyday language while keeping the term itself ("辅料 (excipients) — that is, the fillers and coatings other than the active ingredient"); translate research jargon into actions ("observational cohort" → "only tracked, no control group"); convert legalese into "what consequences you'll face, what to do." **Numbers, confidence intervals, population and year descriptors in the Benefit column may not be deleted or modified**; HR/RR/OR get a "about 28% lower"-style conversion added in place, with the original value retained — this column is the foundation of the entire book's verifiability, only translate, don't touch the values. **Entry titles may only have words added for explanation — never delete or replace words**: cross-references in other sections rely on words in the title for anchor matching (tools/check-refs.mjs takes any two consecutive Chinese characters to compare), and deleting one word can downgrade a reference from "with anchor" to a bare entry number.
- **Sentence-level hard standard** (2026-09-21 user decided: "Some people say it reads like AI-generated garbage, can't understand it" / "Make it as plain and simple as possible, a single whole sentence should be understandable without looking at other places, without any extra thought," and named elderly and disabled readers who read slowly, word by word, as needing accommodation). That day, all 552 entries and four long-form docs were revised entry by entry under this standard; average sentence length dropped from 66 characters to 29, and sentences over 70 characters dropped from 1,385 to 64 (the remainder being unbreakable statute quotes and English-language bibliographic records). New and modified entries going forward follow this standard:
  - **One sentence says one thing only**, kept around 30 characters, max 50. What was previously joined with semicolons becomes one sentence per period; em dashes are avoided as much as possible.
  - **Every sentence has a clear subject** (you, doctor, police, company, court); use passive voice sparingly, and avoid 其, 该, 之, 予以, 系. Replace written-style with spoken-style: 及 → 和, 若 → 如果, 应当 → 要 (Source column unchanged), 自……之日起 → 从……那天算起.
  - **Nested parentheses are always broken apart**; supplements over ten characters inside parentheses get pulled out into their own sentence; keep term explanations short — write "95% CI 0.50–0.63 (confidence range)" rather than a full-sentence explanation, and explain the same term at most once per entry.
  - **Every sentence must be readable on its own**: the sentence before a cross-reference must complete its meaning; the reference is only "where to look if you want detail."
  - **Plain-language summary 2 to 4 sentences, within 120 characters**, keeping only direction and magnitude, the rest goes in the Benefit column. Don't write sample size, grouping, or study design ("257 cases," "that group," "randomized"), don't write metaphors like "另一头" / "产出" that require the reader to translate. `node tools/check-plain.mjs` mechanically checks these three things; in CI it's the "plain-language check" job, and sync-stats.mjs also runs it (2026-09-28 issue #42: readers complained about AI flavor; that day 173 non-conforming entries were found and all fixed). What it can't catch is telegraphic style: every sentence a dozen characters, every sentence a period, reading like an outline. Sentences with cause or contrast can be joined into one with 因为 (because), 所以 (so), 但 (but); don't append summary or elevating sentences at the end.
  - **Notes with controversies must still begin with "争议" (Controversial)**: sync-stats.mjs counts by "Note line begins with 争议"; moving the controversy to the middle of the Note makes the README's controversy count drop (2026-09-21: 9 entries dropped this way during the plain-language rewrite, all fixed back).
- **Don't write in AI-ese** (2026-09-28 issue #42: readers said the whole book had "too much AI flavor" / "reads like a pain"; that day all Cost, Benefit, Note, section-opening intros, and long-form docs were rewritten). The following are patterns that actually appeared in this book:
  - **Meta-narration**: "This entry only addresses one thing" / "This entry doesn't advise you to go or not go" / "This entry accounts for… not money."
  - **Paragraph-ending elevation sentences**: "From the moment of registration, this decision is irreversible" / "Visa-free doesn't mean responsibility-free."
  - **Repeated "也就是 (that is)"**: used several times in one paragraph. Before the rewrite, the book had 306 occurrences.
  - **Metaphors the reader has to translate**: "那一侧" / "另一头" / "产出" / "换了张皮" / "挡箭牌".
  - **Telegraphic style**: a list broken into a string of fragments, one period per sentence.
  - **Empty intensifiers**: "值得注意的是" / "本质上".
- **The rewrite prioritizes fidelity**; the rules are taken from the reader-recommended skill "shuorenhua" (github.com/MrGeDiao/shuorenhua, SKILL.md and references/editing-guide.md):
  - **Negation, scope, condition, stance, and primary/secondary judgments are content, not packaging.** Sentences like "This entry doesn't forbid drawing lines" / "doesn't advocate that everyone should leave night-shift jobs" — only drop the self-reference to "this entry" / "this section"; the qualification itself must stay. In the first round that day, these were deleted as meta-narration; afterward, 199 sentences were checked one by one, and 23 were restored.
  - **Paragraph-ending sentences that draw a conclusion, set priorities, or say what to do — keep the meaning, only delete the rhetoric.** Only delete when it's a complete repetition of what came before.
  - **The plain-language translation that follows a statute quote must be preserved** — readers rely on it to understand the statute.
  - **Don't replace AI-ese with bureaucratic-ese**: don't write "一是……二是……" / "即" / "意思是" / "具体而言". "也就是" that can be deleted is deleted outright; what can't be deleted is replaced with "比如" or a parenthetical note under ten characters.
  - **Never make the meaning more specific or more absolute than the original**; if uncertain, keep the original sentence.
  - **The sentence pattern itself is not wrong**; don't replace by hit count. "不是 X，而是 Y" is content when it rules out a misreading.
  - **Adjacent short sentences with cause or contrast get joined with 因为 / 所以 / 但**; a single sentence still doesn't exceed 50 characters.
- Benefit and Metric are two fields added later to answer "is it worth doing" (the evidence grade only answers "is the number trustworthy"; the two are orthogonal). The metric is determined by what this entry mainly gets back: mortality (including health endpoints, survival rate), money (including benefits), time (including energy), freedom (including legal liability, personal information). **Metrics are not compared against each other.**
- The benefit magnitude is, as much as possible, mechanically fit by threshold from the entry's own "Benefit" column, not by feel: mortality metric uses relative reduction (≥20% large / 10–20% medium / <10% or only surrogate endpoint small); money uses amount (ten-thousand-yuan level large / hundreds to thousands medium / tens of yuan small); freedom uses consequence (avoiding criminal liability large / avoiding detention or administrative penalty medium / avoiding civil dispute small); time uses amount saved (daily hour-level large / weekly hour-level medium / one-time small). Only when numbers are insufficient to determine does judgment get used, and the verification record notes the basis.
- When entry count or A-grade count changes, sync four places: README (the body numbers and the two badges), index.html (five description spots, numberOfPages, the header entry count), CLAUDE.md's table of contents summary, and **tools/og.html + re-screenshot og.png** (the command is in the og.html file header comment; the link count's metric is the total number of http(s) in all "- Source:" and "- Note:" lines under book/). Run `node tools/sync-stats.mjs` to do it in one shot (since 2026-09-29 everything uses node; the old sync-stats.ps1 has been deleted; runs the same on Linux and macOS): it recomputes entry count, section count, A/B/C counts, controversy count, TODO count, link count, and the three cost-effectiveness tiers, then rewrites README's nine spots (including "split into N files"), index.html's four categories of locations (including the footer "N files"), tools/og.html's three numbers, and re-issues og.png with a fresh user-data-dir (`--no-screenshot` to just change numbers; if Chrome isn't in a common location, set the `CHROME` env variable). **To add a new number that varies with statistics, add a row to the EDITS table in sync-stats.mjs** — don't hard-code another spot in the body. CI's "statistics check" job runs `node tools/sync-stats.mjs --check`; if numbers are stale, it goes red (added 2026-09-29; previously, PRs merged directly from the web completely bypassed this script); it only checks, doesn't modify — og.png still needs to be regenerated locally. **CLAUDE.md's TOC summary is not touched by the script; it still needs to be edited manually.** The cost-effectiveness tier rules are bound to two lines in index.html (COST_W and e.ratio); if those two lines change, the script will directly error and demand synchronization. **Run it once after every entry edit, and commit it alongside the body change** (2026-09-18 user decided; the earlier "wait until I say I'm done editing" of that morning is voided — the script compresses the cost of one sync to a few hundred tokens, no need to batch). After running, no need to open og.png: the script itself verifies the image was written this run and is between 120KB and 400KB; only worth a glance when you've changed the tools/og.html layout. Don't write "to be synced" in your report — just give the post-sync numbers.

- **After inserting or deleting an entry, you must scan the diff of `docs/引用对照.md`** (set 2026-09-19 when the user asked "how do you prevent reference misalignment"). The 257 "Entry X" references in the body only record the entry number, not the content; insert one entry and all subsequent entry numbers shift, but the references stay in place — and the shifted entry number is usually **still within range**, so out-of-range checks don't catch it (Section 7 once had 6 misaligned references: medical-assistance pointers pointed to dibao, relief-station pointers pointed to wrong entries, all from this). `sync-stats.mjs` automatically runs `tools/check-refs.mjs`, which writes the **entry title each reference actually points to** along with the context at the reference site into `docs/引用对照.md`; that file is committed. **Criterion: if the entry number didn't change but the "pointed-to entry" column did, that's a reference that got knocked off**; the table also includes the first dozen or so characters at the reference site, and comparing with the title tells you if it's pointing at the right thing. CI uses `node tools/check-refs.mjs --check` (exits 1 on problem); it's a standalone job in book.yml named "cross-reference check," **deliberately not chained into build**: an unfilled anchor shouldn't block the EPUB, PDF, and offline-single-file releases; the PR still goes red, and what should ship on main still ships. This job was added 2026-09-21 — before that, CLAUDE.md said "CI uses" but the workflow didn't actually have this step; it relied entirely on the local `sync-stats.ps1`, which only ran on Windows and silently skipped when it couldn't find node (the script just printed "skipping reference table" and continued), and external PRs couldn't run it at all.

- **Every reference must carry an anchor** (set the same round, 2026-09-19): the surrounding context of a reference must include a word that matches the target entry title — "医疗救助 (medical assistance) see Entry 11" has "医疗救助" as the anchor; if there's really no such word, write it explicitly as "see Entry 16 (IOU and guarantor)," with the parenthetical word taken from the target title. **Bare entry numbers are not allowed** ("the actual algorithm is in Entry 34," "choose between this and Entry 4"), because after a shift knocks it off, even the reference table diff won't show an anomaly — the entry number didn't change, the pointed-to title did, but that reference never said what it was pointing at in the first place. `--check` will flag bare entry numbers as failures and list the locations; `--suspect` shows the full list. **Relative references are also forbidden** (added 2026-09-20): "see next entry," "for penalty see previous entry," "follow the previous entry's channel" — these don't carry an entry number, shift along with insertions, and a knock-off won't show in the table diff; bare-number checks don't catch them either. A single scan that day caught three that had long been misaligned — the HPV vaccine entry's "see next entry" pointed to breast-cancer screening (should have been cervical), the "threatening" entry's "penalty see previous entry" pointed to its own impulse-to-harm entry, the unemployment-registration entry's "previous entry: don't sign voluntary resignation" pointed to the evidence-preservation entry; all 19 in the book were changed one by one to "Entry N (anchor word)," and `--check` now flags them as failures. That day, all 309 references in the book had anchors filled in, dropping from 75 bare numbers to 0; during the fill-in, two more misalignments were caught (Section 9 Entry 6 had "withdrawing cash for someone" pointing to "watching porn yourself is your own business," and the two mutually-referencing exercise entries in Section 2 each pointed at the wrong one). When writing the body, just include an anchor in passing — readers don't have to jump to know what's being referred to.

- **Statute clause numbers and entry references must be distinguishable; the criterion is "is there a citation marker immediately adjacent"** (set 2026-09-21 when the user asked for a scan of similar errors). `check-refs` uses this to judge whether a "Entry N" is a statute or an entry reference: if immediately preceded by `《…》`, `〔…〕`, "14 号," "该解释 / 该意见," or ending with a statute name ("Public Security Administration Punishment Law Article 26"), treat it as a statute and skip. This gives two requirements: ① **Statute citations must carry their own document name** — when listing one per line, write "Article 11 of that Interpretation is about evidence collection," not "the court order … Article 11 is about evidence collection" leaning on the previous sentence (this is also a "every sentence readable on its own" requirement); ② Entry references must not be immediately preceded by a statute name. **The criterion for this class of error is not whether `--check` is green, but whether the total reference count is right**: add N references, and the total in `docs/引用对照.md` should rise by N. If it rose by less, some references were skipped as statutes — they never entered the comparison table, and `--check` having nothing to check反而 shows "pass," which is silent failure. The full scan on 2026-09-21 caught 12 such references ("specifics in the next four entries: Entry 3," "legal aid see Entry 3," "illegally-terminated see Entry 6," "look-up method see Entry 12" — the old criterion looked for "法" in the first 16 characters, and "办法," "查法," "违法" all got caught up); after the criterion was fixed, 423 became 435.
  **The scan scope is the entry body in book/ + section-opening intros + long-form docs under docs/** (extended 2026-09-21; previously only entry bodies were scanned). Section intros and long docs' entry numbers get shifted by insertions too, but were never scanned before; `--check` showed pass — same silent failure as the statute filter above, just with a different cause (never scanned at all). After extension, 15 anchors were backfilled that day, **baseline rose from 454 to 533**, and the "add N references, total rises by N" criterion starts from 533. Three things were settled then: ① long docs have no "this section," so a bare "Entry N" is always treated as a statute and not scanned; long-doc references must be written as "Section X, Entry Y"; ② the "source" column of the comparison table — entries write "Entry N," section intros write "section intro," long docs write the nearest subheading; ③ **range references ("see Section 8, Entries 11 to 14," "Entries 5 to 10") are exempt from anchor verification** — they refer to a whole block of entries, can't have an anchor for each one, and rely only on the comparison-table diff; this pattern previously matched nothing, equivalent to not scanned; there are 5 in the book.
  **Anchors must be long enough and close enough** (tightened 2026-09-20): three consecutive Chinese characters in the whole sentence match the target title, or two characters match within the comma-delimited clause where the reference sits, to count; matching only two-character common words like "自己" / "公司" outside the clause fails `--check`, and the fix is the same as for bare numbers — add "(anchor word)." Cause: PR #18 inserted an entry in Section 31, and "the loan … see this section, Entry 15" in Entry 1's Note got shifted onto the new entry "working remotely for an overseas company from home … file your own individual income tax," with "你自己还" two commas away faking an anchor; `--check` reported pass at the time, and it was only caught by the comparison-table diff. After tightening, 8 explicit annotations were added across the book; the comments in `tools/check-refs.mjs` record the sensitivity test — shifting all in-section references by one catches about 70% on the spot, and the rest (adjacent entries on the same topic, titles sharing words) still rely on the comparison-table diff, so scanning that diff can't be skipped.
- The cost-effectiveness tier is not written into README; index.html synthesizes it in real time from "benefit magnitude + three cost items" (large and all costs zero = extremely high; large and cost score ≤ 2, or medium and all costs zero = high; the rest = average). Change the tier rules only in index.html.

## Directory structure

The body is split into `book/01-*.md` … `book/31-*.md` (split on 2026-09-08; originally a single 531 KB file, exceeding GitHub's 512 KB Markdown-rendering limit, and later sections couldn't display or jump to anchors). README keeps only the intro, glossary, and TOC; adding or modifying entries edits the corresponding book file; the search page index.html reads README to get the file list from the TOC, then reads those files in parallel. The first line of each section file is a link back to the master TOC, the second line is blank, and the third line is `# N. Section Name`.

The "Questions This Book Tries to Answer" table at the top of README **occupies exactly one row per section** (set 2026-09-19 by user): the same link pasted twice makes the reader stop twice at the same destination, with no logical difference between the two rows. If a section covers a lot, string several questions in one row with commas and end with one question mark (Section 19's "How to calculate overtime pay and annual leave, how much compensation when laid off, and how to certify and get paid for workplace injuries?" is the model) — don't split into two rows. That day, Sections 6, 8, and 24's original two-row entries were merged; it's now 32 rows for 32 sections.

The three ebook formats are all auto-generated by `.github/workflows/book.yml` on main after body changes, attached to the fixed Release `epub-latest` (download link unchanged; README references it); PRs only generate and validate, don't publish; outputs all go to `dist/` (gitignored). **The outputs themselves are not committed** — the body changes almost daily, and committing binaries only makes them stale and bloats history (decided in 2026-09-18 issue #12). README's structural parsing and file listing live in `tools/lib/book.mjs`, shared by all three builds; new sections or long docs require no script changes. **But new docs long-form docs must manually have four links added** (twice missed in a row on 2026-09-21, both caught by the user): ① the "Long Reads" cell in README's top nav table (HTML table; the cell must leave a blank line and use markdown `[x](docs/x.md)` — written as `<a href>` it's not recognized by the regex below; 2026-09-28 nav changed to a table and dropped the "(long read)" suffix); ② the "long reads also see" sentence at the end of README; ③ the corresponding section's TOC row in README (written as "Long read see [docs/x.md](docs/x.md)."); ④ the `class="doc-links"` row in index.html, with the URL percent-encoded. Verification: `grep -c "docs/<name>.md" README.md` should be 3, and `grep -c "<name>" index.html` should be 1. The first one is hard — `book.mjs` line 54 uses regex `\]\((docs/[^)#/]+\.md)\)` to scrape the long-doc list from README, and only links inside book/ entries get picked up; the EPUB, PDF, and offline-single-file builds all miss it otherwise. sync-stats.ps1 doesn't check these three spots.
- EPUB: `tools/epub/build.mjs` (only depends on marked, zips itself); CI runs epubcheck. Locally: `cd tools/epub && npm ci && npm run build`.
- Offline single-file HTML: `tools/offline/build.mjs` (zero dependencies) inlines the body **and long-form docs** into index.html's `window.__CORPUS__` (`parts` for book/, `docs` for long reads), double-click to view, works offline; index.html's `init()` recognizes this variable and doesn't make a request — modifying init's data-fetching logic must take this path into account. **The long reads on the search page open as in-place modal pop-ups, not GitHub jumps**: clicks uniformly intercept "GitHub links pointing to `docs/*.md`" (the long-read row in the header, the relative links converted by `hrefOf()` in entry Notes — they all share this shape); the modal has its own clickable chapter TOC with scrollspy, and markdown is handled by the small renderer in index.html that only recognizes headings / paragraphs / lists / tables, no library. If long docs start using new syntax like blockquotes or code blocks, the renderer must be updated in sync.
- PDF: `tools/pdf/build.mjs` + `tools/pdf/template.typ`; pandoc converts to typst, typst does layout (A4, each section starts on a new page, header carries section name, TOC has page numbers, bookmarks down to entry level). Locally requires pandoc ≥ 3.1 and typst ≥ 0.13; use env vars `PANDOC` and `TYPST` to point to the executables; in CI, the versions pinned at the top of the workflow env are downloaded. Layout changes go in template.typ only.

## Writing rules
- **Entries are written for the reader, not to answer the asker** (2026-09-21 the user caught: I wrote "I asked 'whether to memorize' wrong" as the title of Section 23 Entry 19, and he said "You're writing this for me, the reader didn't ask that"). The user's question only determines **what topic to write about**; the question itself doesn't go in the body — the reader doesn't have that question in hand, so "asked wrong" has no referent. After writing, read the title alone and pass three checks: ① Is it a verb-first piece of advice? ② Can it be understood without this conversation (appearance of "XX asked wrong" / "actually it's not like this" / "many people think" — these responsive openers are signals); ③ Is it giving an action, or evaluating a claim. The plain-language summary and Note's opening and closing sentences go through together — endings are the easiest to casually write as "so what you should really ask is…". That entry was eventually renamed "Test content to memorize by future-use questions, don't just recite it as is and call it done," and the two sentences in the plain-language summary's end and the Note that carried an evaluative tone were both deleted.
- **Negative-form entries must first confirm that the target is what the reader actually believes; "is there harm" must go in the Benefit column, not the Note** (2026-09-21 the user caught: Section 6 Entry 26's draft plain-language summary said "Eating breakfast helps control weight' is the reverse," and he said "For a normal person, eating breakfast and being heavier is normal, and whether long-term skipping has harm isn't written either"). Two separate problems: ① **Lost the target** — "breakfast eaters weigh more" isn't counterintuitive to the reader, an extra meal obviously means extra intake; what that meta-analysis actually refutes is "skipping breakfast makes you binge at lunch and dinner and total daily intake反而 goes up," and the key number is that the skipping-breakfast group's total intake was 259.79 kcal lower — i.e., not made up. Before writing a negative-form entry, first ask "which sentence does the reader actually believe," then check whether the number in hand refutes that sentence. ② **The question the reader most wants to ask must go in the Benefit column** — by the book's rule, the plain-language summary can only use things in the Benefit column; put "long-term harm of skipping breakfast" in the Note and that sentence can never reach the most prominent position. The Benefit column can be written multi-paragraph as "refute the popular claim first, then look at alternative approaches, then answer long-term harm" — Section 6 Entry 17 (blood donation) is precedent for both sides going in the Benefit column.
- **Before judging an entry as "only prohibition, no way out," first confirm that the person really has no choice — don't treat a high-cost choice as no choice** (2026-09-21 the user caught: I recommended not writing the night-shift entry, on the grounds that "the reader can't avoid night shifts, only risks without a way out," and he said "But I think the reader can choose whether to do night shifts or not — some might switch jobs for their health, which is better than nothing"). Switching jobs, applying for transfer, factoring the accounting into career choice — these are all real choices; high cost doesn't equal no choice; and the dose-response number (every additional 5 years of night shifts adds 7% to cardiovascular disease) is precisely the basis for "switch early if you're going to switch." This kind of topic is written as "only accounts, doesn't conclude" (Section 10 marriage and Section 18 child-rearing are the model): the Cost column lists both the expensive option and the zero-cost option, the Note explicitly says "doesn't advocate that everyone should leave," and points to existing modifiable items. This entry is a supplement to the 2026-09-18 "only prohibition no way out" audit criterion, not a reversal of it.

## Working method
- You may change across multiple sections in one pass — no need to stop after finishing one section; the scope of changes is written section by section in your report
- Don't proactively expand the directory structure; ask me first before adding a new section
- **Translations are never merged into this repository** (decided 2026-09-18 issue #4 / PR #5, #7): a language the author can't read is a blind merge — can't be reviewed, can't be maintained later. Translators maintain their own fork; this repository only adds a link in the "Other Languages" line at the top of README, noting that it's unofficial and the Chinese original prevails. Translation PRs are replied to and closed on this basis — don't review the translation itself.
- When modifying existing entries, preserve the original evidence grade and source unless a better primary source replaces them
