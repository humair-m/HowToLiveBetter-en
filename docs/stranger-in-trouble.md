# When a Stranger Is in Trouble on the Road: Walk Away or Stop

This is the long-form version of Section 13, Entry 2 (when an older person falls, when someone collapses — do not rush to help them up). The book sorts beneficiaries into four tiers, ranked by how likely the benefit is to come back to you. Strangers are the lowest tier. The chance that they will repay you is small, and you do not know what kind of person they are. So this piece does not urge you to do anything; it only lays out the full bill for each of the three paths, and which one you choose is for you to decide.

**This piece gives no probabilities.** Each layer of cost below is real and traceable to a clause. But "out of a hundred who rescue someone, how many actually run into trouble" — such figures have no public statistics. The book's rule is that when a number is uncertain it is better left unwritten. So do not read "seven layers are listed here" as "all seven will fall on your head."

## The three paths

| | Walk away | Stop but do not touch | Stop and help to the end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Legality | Legal. An ordinary person has no duty to rescue a stranger, and the criminal law has no crime of "seeing and not helping" | Legal | Legal |
| Time | 0 | A few minutes. Calling 120 leaves your number | Half a day at minimum, may require following to the hospital |
| Likelihood of being sought out afterward | Lowest | You are the person who left a number | Highest |
| Benefit to the other party | 0 | The ambulance arrives sooner | Greatest |

## The costs that may arise once you stop, from light to heavy

**One, follow-up phone calls and being questioned.** When you call 120 or 110, your number is left. The other party may call you back to ask exactly where you are. Afterward, you may also be called in for questioning. In a criminal case, if the person reporting does not wish to make their name public, the investigating authority must keep it confidential for them (Criminal Procedure Law, Article 111). Calling 120 has no such confidentiality provision.

**Two, being sought out by the injured party's family.** If surveillance cannot find the offending vehicle, the easiest person for the family to find is you — because you left a number and you were on the scene. They may call to press you, or they may show up at your door.

**Three, being sued in court.** Whoever claims you hit them must produce the evidence. If they cannot produce evidence, the consequences fall on them (Interpretation of the Civil Procedure Law, Article 90). **But this clause only governs how the case is ultimately decided, not whether you will be dragged in.** A case heard under ordinary procedure must conclude at first instance within 6 months, with special circumstances allowing an additional 6 months. Under summary procedure, 3 months (Civil Procedure Law, Articles 152, 164). Even if you win cleanly, the only three items you can make the other side pay are: case acceptance fee, application fee, and travel and lost-work costs for witnesses and expert witnesses appearing in court (Measures for the Payment of Litigation Costs, Articles 6, 29). **Your own lawyer's fees are not among these three; you pay them yourself.**

**Four, being harassed at your workplace.** If someone comes to your workplace to cause disruption, you can call the police. For disrupting the order of an enterprise or institution and preventing work from proceeding normally, the police issue a warning, or a fine of up to 500 yuan. If the circumstances are more serious, detention of 5 to 10 days, plus a fine of up to 1,000 yuan. For a group coming together to cause disruption, the ringleader is detained for 10 to 15 days (Public Security Administration Punishments Law, Article 26, item 1). Repeated harassment, entanglement, or stalking that prevents you from living a normal life is punished under Article 50, item 5. With approval from the responsible person at the public security authority, an order may also be issued barring them from contacting you for a specified period. If the company fires you over this incident, it generally counts as an unlawful termination of the labor contract, and the damages are calculated as 2N (Section 19, Entry 6). But whether you call the police or file for labor arbitration, you yourself must make the trips. And the period during which you have already lost your job will not be made up just because you receive 2N later.

**Five, being filmed and posted online.** **You may not be able to stop others from filming.** Without the consent of the individual, the portrait of another may not be made, used, or made public (Civil Code, Article 1019). But there is one exception: "making, using, or making public the portrait of a portrait-right holder, unavoidably in the course of implementing news reporting" counts as fair use and is not an infringement (Article 1020, item 2). A journalist filming for news reporting falls under this exception. What you can do is decline the interview, not appear on camera, and not speak in front of the lens. Whether you consent is your own decision; no one can agree for you.

**Six, the video being passed around, then being cursed at.** Once the video spreads, the comment section is beyond your control. The measures you can take yourself are written in the "Provisions on the Governance of Online Violence Information" (effective August 1, 2024). The platform must provide you with these protective settings: block strangers or specified individuals, and restrict who can see your information. You may also forbid others from reposting or commenting, accept private messages only from friends, or accept private messages from no one (Article 23). The platform must provide a one-click evidence-collection feature to fix the abusive content in place (Article 25). The platform must set up a dedicated, quick complaint-and-report entry point in a prominent position (Article 26). When the platform discovers suspected illegal or criminal activity, it must report it to the public security authority in a timely manner and provide leads (Article 15). How to preserve your own evidence and how to call the police, see Section 8, Entry 15.

**Seven, when you can no longer hold up.** The six layers above can each be addressed with a clause; the seventh cannot. How much being continually mobbed affects your mood — there is no reliable domestic data to cite. The book gives no number, only one outlet: the psychological aid hotline 12356, see Section 1, Entry 25.

## Two situations in which "walk away" is no longer free

- **You are the operator or manager of the venue, or the organizer of a mass event.** A venue means a place such as a hotel, shopping mall, bank, station, airport, sports stadium, or entertainment venue. You owe a safety-guarantee duty to the people inside. If you fail to meet this duty, you bear tort liability (you must pay). If the harm is caused by another person and you failed to meet this duty, you must compensate within the portion for which you are responsible — this is called supplementary liability (Civil Code, Article 1198). So an on-duty employee or the venue party who walks away bears a different responsibility than a passerby who walks away.
- **The matter concerns you to begin with.** You hit the person, it was your car, or the danger was caused by your prior actions. What to look at here is not whether to involve yourself with someone else, but how to handle a traffic accident and a hit-and-run, see Section 8, Entry 1.

## When you decide to stop, the least-trouble approach

1. **Do not lift or move them, do not try to help them up.** There is no second answer on this point. The reason is medical: forcing them up can worsen intracranial bleeding, and can also worsen spinal injuries. Legally, there is nothing to worry about: damages caused in the course of rescuing someone are exempted under Article 184 of the Civil Code.
2. Call out to them from a slight distance and see whether they are breathing. If they are not breathing, press hard on their chest, see Section 13, Entry 1. If they are breathing, stay in place and call 120, reporting the location clearly.
3. Get a passerby to join you and at the same time start recording video on your phone. Having a witness and having video works better than arguing with people afterward.
4. Do not lift them into a car alone, and do not pay out of your own pocket to take them to the hospital. This step costs the most and is the hardest to settle afterward.
5. If you were injured while rescuing them, or you put in money. To get that money back, see Section 13, Entry 39 (what to do when you were injured or put in money while rescuing someone).
