## Grading and Trade-offs

- **Set B, not A**: Both primary sources are observational studies that only record without intervention; reverse causation is heavy (sleep apnea, anemia, hypothyroidism, depression, and chronic disease all cause long daytime sleep). The umbrella review itself also pools observational meta-analyses; AMSTAR-2/GRADE in the abstract does not give per-outcome grading, so this cannot be counted as A-grade "quantifiable and design-hard."
- **Benefit set "Medium," not "Large" by threshold**: The mortality metric's threshold for "Large" is a relative reduction ≥20%; coronary heart disease 30%, mortality risk 13% per hour would nominally reach "Large." Pushed down to "Medium" because these are associations, not intervention effects, while the MR side supports only weak causation on blood pressure and waist circumference, with no causal effect found on coronary heart disease or diabetes — the actual discount readers can obtain by following this is far smaller than the nominal numbers. Falls under "use judgment when numbers are insufficient to decide," recorded here per the rule.
- **Marked "disputed"**: The two sides are genuinely opposed — observational evidence ties long naps to mortality and coronary heart disease, while genetic evidence finds no causal chain. Both are written into the Note.
- **Dashti's SD units not converted to mmHg**: The paper uses the UK Biobank sample standard deviations (SBP 11.25 mmHg, DBP 20.65 mmHg) for standardization; DBP's SD is clearly too large and SBP's too small — the converted mmHg figures are unreliable. The body text states only the direction, "blood pressure and waist circumference slightly higher," with no numbers.
- **The "check for causes" half is C grade**: That nighttime sleep apnea, anemia, hypothyroidism, and depression cause long daytime sleep is clinical common sense, but "how much mortality drops after checking" has no citable intervention evidence. The Note notes this is an author recommendation.
- **Not written**: Whether naps can make up for the previous night's lost sleep — lab RCTs only have short-term surrogate endpoints and overlap with Section 3, Entry 11; not collected. The Greek EPIC cohort (Naska 2007) said naps reduce coronary heart disease mortality, Häusler 2019 said 1–2 naps per week carry lower risk — opposite direction and small samples, both already covered by the umbrella review, not listed separately.
