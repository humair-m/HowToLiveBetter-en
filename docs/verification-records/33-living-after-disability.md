# Section 33: Living After Disability

2026-09-21. The trigger was a reader pointing out that the book lacks a disability section, and asking whether to cover physical, psychological, and daily-living aspects.

First checked existing coverage: only four places in the book touch on disability, all on the "documentation and money" side — Section 24, Entries 10 and 11 (timing of disability assessment, disability certificate), Section 7, Entry 9 (two subsidies), Section 19 (workplace-injury labor capacity appraisal), Section 17, Entries 7 and 8 (long-term care insurance, pressure ulcers from long-term bed rest, written about the elderly not the disabled). That is, the book answers "how to prove the disability and which money to claim", with no answer at all to "how to live after the certificate is in hand". What's missing is rehabilitation, complications, assistive devices, accessibility, employment, education, psychology, caregivers, and guardianship for mental and intellectual disabilities. After confirming with the user, a new Section 33 was opened, covering all five areas, for a total of 21 entries.

Scope: Only after a disability has occurred. How to prevent the disability-causing event is in Sections 1 and 13, not duplicated. Four metrics run in parallel (mortality, money, time, personal freedom), with no cross-metric conversion.

## Per-Entry Source Verification

The data-fetching paths follow the repository's established method: the State Council policy document library JSON interface (`searchfield=title`) for administrative regulations and departmental documents; the `flk.npc.gov.cn` search API + docx download for laws enacted by the National People's Congress, with the `sxx` field confirming currently in force; and Europe PMC REST for English-language bibliographic records and original abstracts. Europe PMC returned 500/503 multiple times this round, but retrying with exact titles worked.

## Grading Notes

- Entry 1 graded B: the three on-scene actions have only clinical consensus and physiological data (level 5); the review itself states that most interventions are supported only by uncontrolled studies. Benefit magnitude "large" — what's prevented is fatal outcomes such as cerebral hemorrhage from sustained hypertension; all costs zero, resulting in "extreme".
- Entry 4 graded A (prospective cohort with quantifiable numbers) but marked "controversial": the lower bound of the 95% CI for RR 1.63 is exactly 1.00.
- Entry 5 graded B: 232-person sample, primary endpoint P=.04 just past the line, secondary endpoint not significant, in an elderly nursing-home population. Same reasoning as Section 17, Entry 8 (pressure ulcers).
- Entry 10 graded B: the law only says "appropriate subsidies shall be given", with no national unified number; how much and eligibility are entirely set locally.
- Entries 17 and 18 graded A and marked "controversial": the evidence itself is large RCT and Cochrane review; the points of controversy are, respectively, "extrapolating stroke evidence to other disability causes" and "primary result is negative but the circulating figure is from a subgroup".
- Entry 12 benefit magnitude set to "small": the law gives no numbers; provincial reductions are mostly in the hundreds-to-thousands of yuan.
- All other Chinese-regulation entries are A: article text verifiable verbatim.

## Beneficiary Metric

The main line of this section is the reader themselves or their family becoming disabled, falling in tiers ①②. Three places are separately flagged: Entry 4 (caregivers) the beneficiary is the reader themselves; Entries 9 and 15 (rehabilitation assistance, school enrollment) the beneficiary is the child, in tier ②. No entries touch tiers ③④.

## What Was Not Written In

- **Urinary tract infections and bladder management**: common complications of spinal cord injury, but no verbatim verifiable original source with usable numbers was found; not written this round, and no general statements from reviews were used as filler.
- **Day-care and respite services**: The "Law on the Protection of Disabled Persons" only states "the state encourages and supports social forces to run disability-support and day-care institutions" and two sentences prohibiting institutions from insulting, abusing, or abandoning residents, with no operational application path or standards; writing it in would amount to a single sentence. The China Disabled Persons' Federation's "Sunshine Home" program is a departmental project not included in the policy library.
- **Regulatory basis for "curative" therapies such as stem cells**: The "Stem Cell Clinical Research Management Measures" could not be found in the gov.cn policy library; nhc.gov.cn is 412 across the entire site, so verbatim verifiable original text could not be obtained. Entry 6 instead takes the angle of Advertising Law Articles 16 and 17; the criterion useful to the reader ("whether they dare make absolute claims") is more direct.
- **China's total disabled population**: The often-cited 85 million comes from the estimate of the 2006 Second National Sample Survey on Disability, now too dated. A 2026-07-27 State Council Information Office press conference mentioned the "2025 Statistical Communiqué on the Development of the Cause of Disabled Persons", stating "in 2025, 448,000 new certificates were issued to disabled persons employed in urban and rural areas nationwide" and "80% of disabled persons in China live in rural areas", but the full communiqué could not be found in the policy library. The lead-in therefore uses only WHO's global figure and does not state China's total.
