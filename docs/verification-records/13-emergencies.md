## 2026-09-07 Supplementary Verification: Earthquake Entry (Section 13, Entry 13) Rewritten

Reason for rewrite: A reader questioned "drop, find cover, hold on; don't run outside" as not applicable to earthquakes that are sudden and violent and cause buildings to collapse, citing cases where people who ran out quickly had lower casualties.

Originals opened and verified:

1. Europe PMC record MED/1600585, <https://europepmc.org/article/MED/1600585>
   Armenian HK, Noji EK, Oganesian AP (1992). A case-control study of injuries arising from
   the earthquake in Armenia, 1988. Bulletin of the World Health Organization, 70(2), 251-257.
   Abstract (verbatim): "A total of 189 such individuals were identified through neighbourhood
   polyclinics in the city of Leninakan and 159 noninjured controls were selected from the same
   neighbourhoods." / "98% of persons who were hospitalized with injuries were inside a building
   at the time of the earthquake, compared with 83% of the controls (odds ratio = 12.20, 95%
   confidence interval (CI) = 3.62-63.79)." / "The odds ratio of injuries for individuals who were
   in a building that had five or more floors, compared with those in lower buildings, was 3.65
   (95% CI = 2.12-6.33)." / "Leaving buildings after the first shock of the earthquake was a

   Limitations (already noted in the body's Notes): controls were uninjured persons from the same community; the deceased were not included; the abstract does not state whether the analysis adjusted for building floor count.

The video does not separately address whether single-story houses or unfortified self-built houses should run or hide; the "old buildings — get out" portion of this entry still relies on the Armenia study and is marked as disputed.
