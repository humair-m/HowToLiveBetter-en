Task origin: A reader followed up on a previous question: "What about infants—two or three years old, four or five years old—does prolonged screen use really cause no harm?"

Graded B: The thesis comes from official tiered rules, not from experimental numbers; on the research side, the lower bound of the OR sits exactly at 1.00 with heterogeneity of 87%, and the original authors wrote only "might be associated". Benefit magnitude set to "medium" rather than "large": the metric is mortality / health endpoint, but causal evidence for screens and myopia is weak; what has randomized-trial support is outdoor time (Entry 4), so the entry does not take "large" by the strength of the official red line. Cost willpower recorded as "yes"—long-term resistance to inertia, plus parents themselves looking at screens less in front of the child.

Graded A: A meta-analysis with every stratum giving a citable OR and confidence interval. The note states two limitations clearly: the pooled studies are all observational; for high myopia on macular degeneration, the interval runs from 230 to 3104, so only the order of magnitude is meaningful. Benefit magnitude "large".
