## I. Comparison with geekan/HowToLiveLonger

Original text taken from <https://raw.githubusercontent.com/geekan/HowToLiveLonger/main/README.md> (33,511 bytes).
That project's evidence chains are mostly "Zhihu/Sohu/NetEase retellings + source links"; this project's citation rules accept only primary literature, so each entry was verified against the original text.

Merged in (14 entries; see the per-entry verification below): nuts, coffee, tea, replacing red meat with white meat, betel nut, chili, milk, eggs, brushing/oral health, racket sports, intermittent vigorous activity, sun exposure, sweetened beverages (including sugar-free), hot baths; plus pessimism (placed in Section 3).

Not merged and reasons:
- Multivitamins, glucosamine: Section 6 of this book has already classified these as "don't buy" based on RCTs and meta-analyses; the conclusion is opposite to the single observational studies cited by that project, so this book's original judgment is retained.
- Metformin, spermidine, NMN: No human trials demonstrating lifespan extension in healthy people were found; not written this round.
- Sodium: That project cites Messerli 2021 arguing that higher sodium intake correlates with lower mortality. The original was verified as an ecological study of 181 countries, with a lower evidence grade than the SSaSS randomized controlled trial cited in Section 2 of this book, so the conclusion is unchanged; it is only noted in the "controversy" portion of the Notes for that entry.
- Caloric restriction, fruit juice, COVID-19, wealth disparity: not processed this round.
