# Addendum: Audit of Immunity and "What's Punished Is Not…" Overreach · Verification Record (2026-09-09)

Task source: Section 13, Entry 2 was flagged for resting the entire "fear of being falsely accused" weight on Article 184 of the Civil Code — which only immunizes "harm caused to the assisted person during the rescue process," and does not cover "being turned around and accused of being the one who caused the injury." The author asked for a whole-book audit for the same type of problem.

Audit metric, two criteria: ① **beneficiary mismatch** — entry's default scenario is a stranger, yet cost-benefit is written as if "you should get involved"; ② **legal-clause overreach** — using an immunity or narrowing clause that only covers A to reassure against risk B.

## Audit Scope

- Beneficiary mismatch: scanned all 471 titles with "someone|others|another person|stranger|passerby|other side|colleague|friend|neighbor|Good Samaritan|rescue|people around you" (37 hits); already handled item-by-item in the same day's beneficiary-four-tier round, no new cases this round.
- Legal-clause overreach: scanned the whole book for "does not bear|no liability|does not need to pay|immunity|not illegal|does not constitute|not pursued|no need to bear|not counted as illegal|no criminal liability" as well as "as long as not|then not counted|will not file a case|no need to worry|what's punished is not," read each against the original to compare clause coverage. 2 real problems hit, 2 cleared after review.

## Change One: Section 9, Entry 3 (overseas content — only view, don't forward)

The original Note read "what the law punishes is 'inciting,' 'spreading,' 'providing' — not 'viewing.'" This holds for the Criminal Law Articles 103 and 105 cited in that entry, but was written as a universal conclusion, missing one explicit exception:

## Two Cases Cleared After Review

- Section 8, Entry 29 (dog-keeping strict liability): correctly distinguishes Civil Code Article 1245 (where the victim was at fault intentionally or by gross negligence, liability may be reduced or waived) from Article 1246 (where the manager violated safety-management rules and failed to take safety measures, only the victim's intentional conduct reduces liability). The Plain-language summary and Benefit field both clearly state "not leashing the dog closes off the gross-negligence path"; no overreach.
- Section 8, Entry 15's penalty numbers themselves (detention days, fine amount, sentence) match the Public Security Administration Punishment Law 2025 revision and Criminal Law Article 246; only the "will not proactively file a case" sentence needed changing.

## Second Round: Missing Process Cost (same-day addendum)

The author again pointed out that Section 13, Entry 2's citation of Interpretation of the Civil Procedure Law Article 90 to reassure "fear of being falsely accused" is still insufficient: when the surveillance footage fails to find the offending vehicle, the bystander who left a phone number becomes the easiest target for the family to find — phone calls, door-knocking, and being directly named as a defendant can all happen; Article 90 only guarantees the verdict outcome, not freedom from being dragged in, and you can win the case yet still lose time and attorney's fees. This is the third form of "legal-clause overreach" — **using an outcome rule to reassure against a process risk**.

New verification this round:

|---|---|---|

Where there are special circumstances requiring extension, with the approval of the president of the court, an extension of six months may be granted; where further extension is needed, report to the higher people's court for approval"; Article 164 "Cases tried by the people's court under summary procedure shall be concluded within three months from the date of filing. | <https://www.gov.cn/ziliao/flfg/2006-12/29/content_483682.htm> (Measures for the Payment of Litigation Costs, State Council Order No. 481) | Yes | Article 6 "Litigation costs that parties shall pay to the people's court include: (1) case-acceptance fee; (2) application fee; (3) transportation, accommodation, living, and lost-work subsidies for witnesses, expert witnesses, translators, and calculators who appear in court on dates designated by the people's court"; Article 29 "Litigation costs are borne by the losing party, except where the winning party voluntarily assumes them…" |

Attorney's fees are not within the three items listed in Article 6, so Article 29's "borne by the losing party" does not cover attorney's fees — the body text accordingly reads "even if you win, you pay your own attorney's fees," derived from the article's enumeration, not by finding another article.

Change: Section 13, Entry 2's Cost field adds "if the family really comes after you, responding to the suit is a months-long matter, and attorney's fees are outside the scope of 'borne by the losing party'"; the Note rewrites "basically drops to zero" as "surveillance, fellow bystanders, dashcams lower the risk of losing — they do not lower the cost of being dragged in; these two accounts must be calculated separately," and writes in the trial deadline and litigation-cost scope. CLAUDE.md adds a new section "When writing 'the law supports you' entries, process cost must be written," locking this in as a writing rule.

Also scanned the whole book for "may sue|may claim|can recover|may request prosecution|refund and treble damages|may apply for arbitration|win the case|may return|the right to demand|shall compensate|may apply|can get back|may claim compensation," reading each original to judge whether only outcome was written without process:

- Two places where process cost was added (only changing the Cost field, no new numbers): Section 5, Entry 9 (child top-up refund) adds "if no agreement, only a lawsuit, which is months-long at best, with attorney's fees at your own expense"; Section 14, Entry 8 (personal-information access and deletion rights) adds "if it really goes to litigation, it's months-long at best, with attorney's fees at your own expense — filing a complaint first is the better deal."
- Four already-correct cases that can serve as models: Section 8, Entry 21 (online-shopping fraud) Note gives the full calculation "after-tax hourly wage × estimated time + litigation fees, then discounted by whether the win is collectible"; Section 8, Entry 32 (false accusation) Note states "don't treat holding them accountable as a guaranteed outcome" and explains where the burden-of-proof difficulty differs; Section 5, Entry 22 (prepayment) Note states "this entry gives the ex-post legal basis, not ex-ante peace of mind — if the merchant really runs, the money is probably already off the books"; Section 8, Entry 34 (state compensation) Note states the compensation-obligation agency, the upward-application process, and that attorney's fees are outside the scope of state compensation.

## Third Round: Escalation to the Workplace and Job Loss (same-day addendum)

The author added another cost layer: the family may make trouble at the reader's workplace, the company may fire the person over it, and the job is gone outright. Verified and written into Section 13, Entry 2's Note, verified against the SPP-reposted full text of the Public Security Administration Punishment Law 2025 revision at <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml>:

- Article 26: "Anyone who commits any of the following acts shall be given a warning or fined up to 500 yuan; where the circumstances are relatively serious, detained for 5 to 10 days and may also be fined up to 1,000 yuan: (1) disrupting the order of an organ, group, enterprise, or public institution, so that work, production, business, medical care, teaching, or scientific research cannot proceed normally, without yet causing serious losses…" "Those who gather others to commit the acts in the preceding paragraph, ringleaders shall be detained for 10 to 15 days and may also be fined up to 2,000 yuan."
- Article 50, item 5: "Those who repeatedly send obscene, insulting, or threatening messages, or who harass, pester, or stalk and thereby interfere with another person's normal life," detained for up to 5 days or fined up to 1,000 yuan, with the more serious case detained 5 to 10 days and possibly also fined up to 1,000 yuan; "those who commit the harassment, pestering, or stalking described in item 5 of the preceding paragraph… with the approval of the person in charge of the public security organ, may be ordered to refrain from contacting the victim for a certain period."

Article-number cross-check: the same fetch's Article 50 items (item 2 public insult and defamation, item 3 fabricating facts to falsely accuse) are consistent with the references previously verified for Section 8, Entries 15 and 31, so this fetch's Article 26 article number is also accepted.

Wording follows the rule set this round: not stopping at "the company cannot fire you this way." The Note states that the company firing over this is generally an illegal termination, with severance calculated as 2N (pointing to Section 19, Entry 6), but also states that calling the police and arbitration both require the reader to run themselves, and the lost-income stretch from losing the job is not made whole by eventually receiving 2N — so the conclusion of this layer remains "walk away, it's legal and least hassle," not "there's a legal safety net."

## Fourth Round: Reporters, Secondary Spread, and Cyberbullying, plus Splitting Out a Long Read (same-day addendum)

The author added yet another layer: the family may bring reporters to interview, the video may go online, drawing cyberbullying — "the cost is a bottomless pit." At this point, Section 13, Entry 2's Note had stacked seven or eight accounts, exceeding the project rule of "no single entry more than 10 lines, long explanations go to docs/," so this round split the whole account into a long read [docs/遇到陌生人出事该不该停.md](../遇到陌生人出事该不该停.md), with the entry's Note shrunk to a conclusion plus a pointer, finishing at 8 lines of body text (originally 9).

The portrait-right article is this round's most counterintuitive finding, and the long read's only "you may not be able to stop it" line: the fair use for news reporting means on-site filming may not require your consent; what you can control is only whether to accept the interview, whether to appear on camera, whether to speak.

No numbers on cyberbullying and suicide were written in this round: the available meta-analyses are mostly on adolescent samples and do not match this entry's reader population and scenario; per the book's rule of "when uncertain numbers, prefer not to write," only an outlet (12356, Section 1, Entry 25) was left, with no probabilities given. The long read's opening also states that no incidence rate is given, to avoid reading "seven layers listed" as "all seven layers will occur."
