# issue #31: Emotional Regulation, Partner Conflict, Domestic Violence, and Workplace Bullying (2026-09-25)

Task source: GitHub issue #31 (kkxly2-dot), asking whether content on emotional control and channeling could be added, with examples of interpersonal relationships, workplace relationships, and family relationships.

## How the Scope Was Set

Existing emotion entries in the book: Section 3, Entries 14, 15, 16, 18, 19, 22, 23; Section 22, Entries 7 to 11; Section 29 the entire section. Section 3, Entry 18 already includes the meta-analysis of emotion-regulation strategies (cognitive reappraisal d = 0.36; suppression ineffective) and the backfire of revenge. So no new section was opened; only gaps were filled, 5 entries in total, all appended at the end of each section, not inserted in the middle, to avoid renumbering:

- Section 3, Entry 24: Venting does not dissipate anger; methods that lower arousal work.
- Section 3, Entry 25: Expressive writing (disputed).
- Section 8, Entry 43: Domestic violence and personal-safety protection orders. Previously the book had no entry on domestic violence.
- Section 10, Entry 18: Hostility and health in partner conflict.
- Section 19, Entry 17: Workplace bullying.

"How to get along with a mother-in-law," "how to communicate with your boss," and similar technique-style content was not written — the quantifiable original evidence is too thin; written, it would only be C-grade experience-talk.

## Grade and Metric Decisions

- 3-24 set A: meta-analysis provided a specific effect size. Metric recorded as Time, benefit Medium, following the precedent of Section 3, Entry 18 — judgment call.
- 3-25 set B and marked disputed: the supporting side's effect is very small, and the two other pooled analyses of only randomized controlled trials were non-significant. Benefit recorded as Small.
- 8-43 set A: legal clauses are explicit, and the homicide numbers come from a systematic review. Metric recorded as Freedom, benefit Large, judgment call: what this entry recovers is personal safety; there is no number for "do this and mortality drops X%," so the mortality threshold was not applied.
- 10-18 set B: observational meta-analysis plus a 42-couple experiment; r = .11, only association, no relative reduction; benefit recorded as Small.
- 19-17 set B: the health numbers are observational cohort, and bullying is self-reported. HR 1.59 would meet the threshold for Large under the metric, but there is no number for "how much risk drops after exiting the bullying," so judgment was used to set it Medium.
