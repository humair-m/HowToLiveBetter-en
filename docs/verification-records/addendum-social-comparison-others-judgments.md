## How benefit magnitudes were set (where numbers were insufficient to apply the threshold mechanically, the basis is stated here per the rules)

- Section 3, Entry 21 = large: time metric by amount saved; the RCT directly gives 60 minutes per day, "hour-level per day".
- Section 3, Entry 22 = small: time metric, no measurement of time saved; what changes is a one-off estimation method, recorded as small per "one-off".
- Section 3, Entry 23 = small: the mortality-metric threshold is set by relative reduction; this entry has no reduction, only correlations with suicidal ideation and suicide attempts (both surrogate endpoints); recorded as small per "surrogate endpoints only".
- Section 6, Entry 23 = medium: the money metric by amount would normally be large (changing a house or car is 10,000-yuan-class), but the money saved by this entry has no "saves this much for that much" numerical support; the outcome variable is self-reported happiness, not money, so it follows Section 22's "medium" (which also uses happiness evidence to persuade the reader not to spend).

## Selection choices

- **Not written as slogans like "don't compare yourself to others" or "don't mind how others see you"**: every entry in this book must answer what you pay and what you get back; a slogan is empty, with neither cost nor benefit. All four entries land on verifiable numbers and one concrete action: set limits / turn off; divide the estimate by two; treat a class of thoughts as a symptom; before adding budget, ask whether you're buying the thing or the position.
- **No new section opened**: changing the directory structure requires asking the user first; these four fit within the existing frameworks of Sections 3 and 6.
- **No cross-metric aggregation**: Entries 21 and 22 use time (including energy), Entry 23 uses mortality, and the Section 6 entry uses money; they are not compared to each other.
- **Correlation and causation are kept separate**: the upward social comparison meta-analysis is correlational evidence (the authors themselves state longitudinal is not stronger than cross-sectional and more longitudinal studies are needed); deactivation and limiting are RCTs; the body states them separately, and the grade-A basis is the latter.
- **The cost is written into the body**: deactivating Facebook for four weeks also reduced current-affairs knowledge by 0.19 SD; this is the cost side of this entry and must not be reported only as benefit.
- **External-validity boundaries are written into the notes**: the populations of the three trials and the two experiments are all US undergraduates and Facebook users; Chinese platforms and populations have no citable comparable data; the "spotlight effect" setting is wearing a T-shirt and talking to strangers, with no direct evidence for extending to workplace evaluation.
