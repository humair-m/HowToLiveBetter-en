Landing point: Section 9, new Entry 22. Placed in this section because it shares a shape with Section 9, Entry 5 (selling cards for cash), Entry 16 (lending out an ID card), and Entry 20 (taking money to give a child away) — trading away something that shouldn't be traded for a small sum, with all the consequence on the other side. Metric recorded as "Freedom" (fines and criminal liability); the health side is written into Plain-language summary and Note, not pooled with the mortality metric. Benefit magnitude "Large" by mechanical threshold under the freedom metric (avoiding imprisonment of five years or more); all three cost components zero, landing in the very-high cost-effectiveness tier.

Article 5: "No organization or individual may, in any form, buy or sell human organs, nor engage in activities related to the buying and selling of human organs." Article 10: "No organization or individual may obtain the living organs of citizens under 18 years of age for transplantation." Article 11: "Recipients of living organs are limited to the spouse, direct blood relatives, or collateral blood relatives within three generations of the living-organ donor." Article 37: "Anyone who, in violation of these Regulations, buys or sells human organs or engages in activities related to the buying or selling of human organs shall have their illegal gains confiscated by the health department of the local people's government at or above the county level, and shall be fined between 10 and 20 times the transaction amount… medical personnel who participate in the above activities shall also have their practicing certificates revoked by the original registration department, and shall be permanently prohibited from engaging in medical and health services; where a crime is constituted, criminal liability shall be pursued in accordance with law." |
| <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff808181796a636a0179822a19640c92> | Yes (backend API docx download, verified verbatim, sxx=3 currently effective — the consolidated text as amended by Criminal Law Amendment XI) | Criminal Law Article 234-1: "Anyone who organizes others to sell human organs shall be sentenced to imprisonment of up to 5 years and fined; where the circumstances are serious, imprisonment of 5 years or more, and fined or with property confiscated." "Where organs are removed without the person's consent, or organs are removed from a person under 18 years of age, or organs are donated under coercion or deception, the matter shall be convicted and punished under Articles 234 and 232 of this Law." |

Two deliberate trade-offs: ① **The fine sentence uses the new number after the old regulation was replaced.** The old "Regulations on Human Organ Transplantation" set "between 8 and 10 times the transaction amount"; online circulated versions still mostly use this old figure — Order No. 767 has changed it to 10 to 20 times, and the body text uses the new figure. ② **"Those who sell their kidneys will be criminally punished" was not written into the body** — the seller does not themselves constitute the crime of organizing the sale of human organs; the Regulation provides for confiscation of illegal gains plus fines. Those who constitute a crime are the organizers, intermediaries, and the medical personnel who perform the surgery. Without drawing this line, it would be scare-mongering the reader.

## Grading and Trade-offs

- Set A: All four are large cohorts or multicenter surveys; numbers verifiable verbatim from the original.
- Marked "disputed": Ibrahim uses the general population as controls and concludes that kidney donors' survival and ESRD risk are comparable to the general population; Muzaale and Mjøen, switching to "equally healthy, equally eligible-to-donate" controls, find significantly elevated risk. The disagreement is over who the control group is; the Note states this, without hiding the dissent.
- Benefit magnitude "Large" by the mortality metric's mechanical threshold (ESRD drops from 30.8 per 10,000 to 3.9 per 10,000, and all-cause-mortality HR 1.30 corresponds to a reduction exceeding the 20% line); the Note also notes that absolute risk is small, to avoid using relative values to scare. All three cost components zero, landing in the very-high cost-effectiveness tier.
- **Not written**: The "bedridden" in the user's description. The endpoints the four original studies give are ESRD, dialysis, albuminuria, hypertension, and self-reported worsened health — no numbers like bed-bound proportion. The Note explicitly states "this is not made up here."
- **Not cited**: Studies on complications and follow-up of black-market donors found no large-sample original text verifiable verbatim this round, so the layer "black market has no screening, no follow-up" is written only as a statement about the four studies' sample characteristics (the first three studies' subjects were all screened, followed-up regular donors), without any black-market complication-rate numbers.
