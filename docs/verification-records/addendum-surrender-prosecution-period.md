# Addendum: Surrender, Honest Confession, and Prosecution Time Limits · Verification Record (2026-09-21)

Task origin: A user asked "should we add content about not harboring illusions of luck? In many cases defendants get caught because they harbored illusions; in traffic accidents, stop the car first, save the person, call the police, do not run — like this." Handled as "the example is not the topic": the traffic accident entry already exists as Section 8, Entry 1; the topic is whether "illusions of luck" as a pattern should be its own entry.

**Did not add a general entry on "do not harbor illusions of luck."** The reason is that it is not quantifiable and not actionable; written out, it would only be C-grade empty talk, conflicting with the book's rule that "every entry must answer what is spent and what is bought back." Instead, illusions of luck were broken into two judgment questions with clearly numbered answers, combined into one entry:

1. "Lay low first, see if anyone investigates" — can you escape? Criminal Law Article 88: After case filing for investigation or court acceptance, evading investigation or trial is not subject to the prosecution time limit.
2. "Will voluntarily confessing make things worse?" — Surrender (自首), honest confession (坦白), and guilty plea with punishment acceptance each have clearly numbered reduction percentages in the Supreme Court and Supreme Procuratorate sentencing guidance.

Landing point: Section 8 gets Entry 6, inserted after "when accused or summoned, hire a lawyer first" (cost zero, benefit large, ordered by the section's "the actions with the biggest one-time loss and the easiest to do come first" metric). Original Entries 6 to 39 are renumbered to 7 to 40.

Graded A: All three documents are verifiable word for word and currently effective. Benefit tier "large" mechanically applies the threshold for the freedom metric (what is bought back is a reduction in sentence length up to and including waiver of punishment). Cost field money 0, time few; willpower "some" — the action itself is one-off (a phone call or a trip to the police station); the difficult part is suppressing the "wait a bit longer" thought at that moment, not long-term inertia.

## Two deliberate omissions

1. **Did not write "how likely you are to be caught."** This is the most direct counter to illusions of luck, but China's case-by-case-charge clearance rates and traffic hit-and-run detection rates have no verifiable official statistics (mps.gov.cn long-standing 521); padding with media-reported figures violates the citation rules. So this entry takes the "legal consequences" side, entirely landing on verifiable provisions.
2. **The traffic accident section was not written as "staying on scene reduces your sentence."** Fa Fa [2010] No. 60 is clear: protecting the scene, rescuing the injured, and reporting to police are legal obligations; once surrender is recognized, the leniency must also be strictly controlled. The actual delta is at the baseline-sentence tier — not running is calculated under the first tier of Criminal Law Article 133; running and then surrendering must use the heavier tier as the baseline. The Note writes it this way, without exaggerating the sentence-reduction benefit of "actively staying."
