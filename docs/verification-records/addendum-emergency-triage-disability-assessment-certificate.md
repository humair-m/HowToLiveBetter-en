# Addendum: How to Queue at the Emergency Department, Foreign Bodies Impaled in the Body and Fractures, Disability Assessment and the Disability Certificate

2026-09-10. Triggered by three reader-raised items: how the hospital treatment flow actually works (in one news story a man with a knife in his head was still queuing at the registration window); what to do on the spot for major injuries and fractures; and whether to get a disability certificate for oneself. Verification showed none of the three had existing entries; they were assigned to Section 13 (on-site first aid) and Section 24 (medical-care flow and post-treatment procedures), with no new section added.

Section 13 adds Entries 41 and 42; Section 24 adds Entries 8, 9, 10, and 11.

## Section 24, Entry 8 (Emergency pre-triage)

| URL | Verified | Verbatim quote |
|---|---|---|
| <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202405/content_6951260.htm> (National Health Commission Office "Notice on Issuing Six Specialty Medical Quality Control Indicators (2024 Edition) Including Emergency Medicine", 国卫办医政函〔2024〕150 号, 2024-04-30) | Yes | Annex 1, indicator 4 "Emergency graded-triage execution rate (EM-ME-04)" explanatory note: "Emergency pre-triage must conduct accurate grading according to scientific criteria; the currently widely used standard classifies patients into four levels by severity of illness: Level I are critically ill patients requiring immediate treatment; Level II are severe patients for whom assessment and treatment often proceed simultaneously; Level III are acute patients requiring diagnosis and treatment within a short time; Level IV are non-acute or subacute patients seen in order in the general treatment area" |
| <http://wjw.beijing.gov.cn/zwgk_20040/ylws/201912/t20191216_1242338.html> (Beijing Municipal Health Commission "Beijing Work Plan for Strengthening Emergency Pre-Triage Grading" and its annex "Beijing Hospital Emergency Pre-Triage Grading Standards (Trial)", December 2019) | Yes | "Adhere to the principle of prioritizing critically ill and severe patients. Arrange patient visit order according to the severity of the patient's condition, following the principle from severe to mild and from rapidly worsening to relatively stable." "Set up an emergency pre-triage area in a prominent location at the entrance to the emergency department"; "Following the principle of 'three areas, four levels'... distinguishing 'critical, severe, acute, non-acute'". Annex adult table: Level 1 critical—"ongoing or imminent life threat or condition deterioration, requiring immediate active intervention"; handling—"immediate assessment and treatment, place patient in resuscitation room or resuscitation bay" (red); Level 2 severe—"immediate vital-sign monitoring, treatment within 10 minutes, place patient in resuscitation bay" (red); Level 3 acute—"prioritized over Level 4 patients, place patient in general treatment area waiting area; if wait time exceeds 30 minutes, re-evaluate" (yellow); Level 4 non-acute—"seen in order; unless condition changes, wait time is longer; if wait time exceeds 4 hours, may re-evaluate" (green). Level 1 manual assessment indicators include "heart rate >180 beats/min or <40 beats/min", "systolic blood pressure <70 mmHg", "SpO2 <80% with respiratory distress", "shock", "confirmed myocardial infarction", "acute disturbance of consciousness / unresponsive or responsive only to pain (GCS<9)" |

Graded A: The four-level triage definition comes from a current National Health Commission document, and the response times at each level come from the Beijing implementation standard; both can be verified word-for-word. Metric: time—this entry buys not being queued behind and delayed, not a quantifiable mortality reduction, so the mortality metric is not applied. Benefit magnitude "medium": no relative-reduction number to apply; by judgment set to medium (severe cases reaching the resuscitation bay within 10 minutes is a substantive difference, but this entry guarantees order, not outcome). Cost money=0 time=few willpower=no, combined as "high".

Not retrieved: The Ministry of Health "Emergency Department Construction and Management Guidelines (Trial)" (卫医政发〔2009〕50 号) is the source of "three areas, four levels", but nhc.gov.cn persistently returns 412 from this machine (both Invoke-WebRequest and headless Chrome fail); the industry standard WS/T 390—2012 "Standardized Procedures for Hospital Emergency Departments" official PDF is a scanned file with no text layer, and pdftotext could not extract text. So neither is placed in the source column; instead the 2024 quality-control indicator (national level, verifiable word-for-word) and Beijing's plan (response times, verifiable word-for-word) are cited. The State Council policy document library does not include NHFPC departmental documents; queries returning no hits are expected.

## Section 24, Entry 9 (Treat first even without money or ID)

|---|---|---|

| <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2014/content_2580977.htm> (National Health and Family Planning Commission Order No. 3 "Pre-hospital Medical Emergency Management Measures") | Yes | Article 13: "The national pre-hospital medical emergency call number is '120'"; Article 22: "May not refuse, shirk, or delay pre-hospital medical emergency services for command-and-dispatch reasons"; Article 23: "Shall transfer patients to medical institutions for treatment according to the principle of nearest, most urgent, meeting professional needs, and taking into account patient wishes"; Article 25: "May not refuse or delay pre-hospital medical emergency services for reasons of fees" |

Graded A: Both are current national documents whose provisions can be verified word-for-word. Metric: money; benefit magnitude "medium"—what is backstopped is the cost of the emergency-care segment, not a 10,000-yuan-class compensation or benefit; caps vary by province, so no amount is stated. All three cost items are zero, combined as "high".

|---|---|---|
| <https://www.moj.gov.cn/pub/sfbgw/zwxxgk/fdzdgknr/fdzdgknrtzwj/201908/P020210316701681025917.pdf> (Supreme People's Court, Supreme People's Procuratorate, Ministry of Public Security, Ministry of State Security, Ministry of Justice "Announcement on Publishing the 'Classification of Human Injury Disability Degrees'", issued 2016-04-18, effective 2017-01-01; Ministry of Justice government information disclosure PDF, with a text layer, extracted and verified word-for-word) | Yes | 4.2 Timing of assessment: "Assessment shall be conducted after the primary injury and its directly related complications have reached the end of treatment or clinical treatment outcomes have stabilized." 4.4: "This standard classifies human injury disability degree into 10 grades, from Level 1 (100% human disability rate) to Level 10 (10% human disability rate), with a 10% difference in disability rate per grade." 4.5: "Based on the degree of damage to human tissue and organ structure and functional impairment, and the degree of dependence on medical and nursing care, with appropriate consideration of the influence of social-interaction and psychological factors due to disability, comprehensively determine the disability degree grade." 5.10.6 Level 10 spine, pelvis, and limb injuries: "Compression fracture of one vertebral body (compression degree reaching 1/3) or comminuted fracture", "two or more pelvic fractures or comminuted fracture with malunion", "any major joint of a limb (except the ankle) with loss of function of 25% or more". 6.2: "For the same site and nature of disability, assessment may not be conducted using two or more clauses of this standard or the same clause two or more times." 4.3 Injury-disease relationship: causation "may be expressed in turn as: complete role, main role, equal role, secondary role, minor role, no role" |

Graded A: The standard text can be verified word-for-word. Metric: money; benefit magnitude "large"—the grade directly determines the disability compensation, and under the metric already verified in Section 9 (the previous year's urban residents' per-capite disposable income multiplied by 20 years), this is at the 10,000-yuan class and above. Cost money=few time=medium willpower=some; cost score 3, combined as "average".

The assessment fee of "several hundred to two thousand yuan" is written in the cost column; this is the common range for judicial expertise fees across regions, with no national unified pricing document to cite, so it does not enter the benefit column and is not stated as a numerical claim; the cost column only states the range and notes that the applicant pays up front.

|---|---|---|

The document number was not retrieved from a reliable source this round (search results mix "残联发〔2017〕34 号" with other numbers; the notice reproduced on the Guangdong provincial government policy library page shows only the issue date with no document number); per project rules, anything uncertain is not written, and the source column shows only the issue and effective dates.

Graded A: The full text of the measures and all time limits and fee provisions can be verified word-for-word. Metric: money; benefit magnitude "medium"—the certificate itself pays nothing; the two subsidies it links to are tens to several hundred yuan per month under the metric already verified in Section 7, Entry 9, not reaching the 10,000-yuan class. Cost money=few time=medium willpower=some; cost score 3, combined as "average".

## Section 13, Entries 41 and 42 (Penetrating foreign body; fracture)

| URL | Verified | Verbatim quote |
|---|---|---|
| <https://www.ifrc.org/sites/default/files/2026-03/IFRC%20International%20First%20Aid,%20Resuscitation%20and%20Education%20Guidelines%202025.pdf> (International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies "International First Aid, Resuscitation and Education Guidelines 2025", official PDF, with a text layer) | Yes | Pages 256–257: "First aid providers should apply pressure around an embedded object (e.g. a knife) to stabilize the object. Avoid removing the object." Pages 267–268: "Impaled objects in the body should be stabilized, and if the object is pulsating, it should be allowed to do so while loosely stabilized." "First aid providers should not push internal organs back into the body." "Do not remove any impaled objects from an abdominal or chest wound, as this may cause serious bleeding and make it more difficult for a medical professional to determine the severity of the wound." "An open chest wound that is not actively bleeding may be left open, without the application of a dressing, because a sealed wound may allow air to build up in the chest, which may lead to the lung partially or fully collapsing (pneumothorax)." "First aid providers should access emergency medical care for all penetrating chest or abdominal wounds." Pages 301–302: "Any injury to an extremity should be approached as a potential bone fracture." "The injury should be stabilized to minimize any movement and pain either manually (e.g. self-splinting) or mechanically (e.g. using a sling and bandages)." "first aid providers may ice or cool the sprained joint or soft tissue injury for up to 20 consecutive minutes, as this may decrease the pain. Cooling for longer than this may damage the skin." "If a long bone is injured, such as the femur, the bleeding from the bone itself can be life-threatening." "The only accurate way to diagnose a fracture is with an X-ray. When in doubt, assume it is a fracture." "Access emergency medical services (EMS) if the person has a lot of pain or swelling, signs of shock, the fracture is in the femur or if the limb is in an abnormal position." "When in a remote environment (or one with limited resources) and the limb below the angulated fracture is cool, pale or the first aid provider is unable to splint for movement, the first aid provider may consider straightening it if trained to do so." "If the injury is an open fracture, your priority is to stop the bleeding and limit infection." Evidence section: The only retrospective cohort on pre-hospital traction fixation of femur fractures showed "a statistically significant decrease in units of blood transfusion needed within the first 24 hours and a statistically significant shorter length of hospital stay", but "a statistically significant decrease in mortality and complications could not be demonstrated, and the evidence was of very low certainty"; for the comparison of reduction versus in-situ fixation, "no evidence was found" |

Both graded B: The guidelines list these as good practice points, with no randomized trial behind them; the traction-fixation item has only one very-low-certainty retrospective cohort, and reduction versus no-reduction has no evidence at all. Entry 41 benefit magnitude "large"—mortality metric, preventing immediate massive hemorrhage and tension pneumothorax after pulling out the foreign body; cost money=0 time=few willpower=some; cost score 1, combined as "high". Entry 42 benefit magnitude "medium"—what is prevented is mainly secondary injury and disability, not death (femur-fracture hemorrhage is the exception, but the main action here is fixation and cold compress); all three cost items zero, combined as "high".

Not adopted: NICE NG37 (complex fractures) returned 403 from this machine; the 2020 ILCOR International First Aid Science Consensus (doi:10.1161/cir.0000000000000897) abstract topics do not include penetrating foreign bodies, only compression bandaging of closed joint injuries, so it is not cited. The Chinese Red Cross first-aid training textbook has no publicly verifiable version, so it is not cited.
