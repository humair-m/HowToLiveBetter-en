# Section 1, Entry 26 "Home Emergency Kit" · Verification Record (2026-09-08)

Task origin: A reader pointed out that "essential items for the home, such as a tourniquet" had no standalone entry. The verification result is that tourniquets are already in Section 13, Entry 4; what is missing is the equipment-list perspective itself: fire extinguishers, fire blankets, escape breathing masks, and the contents of a first-aid kit appear nowhere in the book. Per convention, no new section was opened; one entry was added at the end of Section 1, with detail placed in docs/home-emergency-kit-list.md.

Tool notes: WebSearch was available this round; the China Government Network search API (sousuo.www.gov.cn/search-gov/data) returned code 1001 with no results for both "家庭应急物资储备" (home emergency supplies reserve) and "全国火灾情况" (national fire statistics), so WebSearch was used to locate official URLs and then WebFetch was used to open each one. The National Fire and Rescue Administration site 119.gov.cn consistently returned 412 across the site, consistent with previous records. After downloading the two government-site PDFs of GB 55036—2022, pdftotext could only extract digits and English text; the Chinese fonts had no ToUnicode mapping, and there was no pdftoppm on the machine to render to images, so the regulatory text itself could not be retrieved.

## Benefit evidence

| URL | Result | Verbatim quote |
|---|---|---|
| Europe PMC REST, DOI 10.1002/14651858.CD005014.pub3 | Opened; Kendrick D et al. 2012 Cochrane review, 98 studies, 2,605,044 people | Injury incidence IRR 0.89 (95% CI 0.78 to 1.01); for interventions delivered in the home IRR 0.75 (0.62 to 0.91); for interventions not providing safety equipment IRR 0.78 (0.66 to 0.92); safety behaviors: working smoke alarm OR 1.81 (1.30 to 2.52), escape plan OR 2.01 (1.45 to 2.77), safe medication storage OR 1.53 (1.27 to 1.84), safe cleaning-product storage OR 1.55 (1.22 to 1.96), stair gate OR 1.61 (1.19 to 2.17), outlet cover OR 2.69 (1.46 to 4.96); "lack of evidence that home-safety interventions reduced thermal injury or poisoning rates" |

Grading note: The entry is graded B and marked disputed. The reason is that on the benefit side there is only this single Cochrane review; the confidence interval of its primary result crosses 1, and the effect size of the "no equipment provided" group is comparable to the overall effect, indicating that the reduction in injuries is not primarily attributable to the equipment itself. The official list is an authoritative document but contains no effect sizes. The benefit magnitude applies the relative-reduction threshold against the mortality metric: IRR 0.89 corresponds to roughly 11%, which falls in the 10–20% band, recorded as "medium", not "large". Cost is recorded as money=few, time=few, willpower=no (a kit costs several hundred yuan).
