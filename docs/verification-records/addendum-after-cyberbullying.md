# Addendum: What to Do After Cyberbullying · Verification Record (2026-09-09)

Task origin: After unwinding the sixth layer (secondary spread and cyberbullying) of [docs/遇到陌生人出事该不该停.md](../遇到陌生人出事该不该停.md), the author asked whether to add a separate entry on "what to do when encountering cyberbullying."

Existing coverage: Only the back half of Section 8, Entry 15's sentence "if cyberbullied, preserve evidence first then call police," with that entry's main body being "do not become the rumor-mongering or insulting party"; what to do after being cyberbullied has only half a sentence. A whole-book search for "cyberbullying" hits only that one entry; "personality-rights injunction," "doxxing," "fast evidence preservation" return zero hits, and the Provisions on the Governance of Online Violence Information previously appeared only in the long-form piece above, not in the book/ body. The conclusion is a genuine blank; one entry is added.

Landing point: Section 8 gets Entry 35 appended at the end, not inserted after Entry 14. Two reasons: first, the tail of Section 8 (Entries 30 to 34) is already a "you've been wronged, what to do" cluster — extortion by blackmail, false accusation, presumption of innocence, state compensation, claiming compensation as a victim; cyberbullying fits in there. Second, inserting after Entry 14 would have renumbered 20 entries and required editing 8 external references and 11 "this section, Entry N" self-references; appending at the end is zero renumbering. Entry 14's Note gets one sentence pointing to Entry 35.

## Section 8, Entry 36

| <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/412992.html> (Supreme People's Court official site, Fa Fa [2023] No. 14, Guiding Opinions on Lawfully Punishing Cyberbullying Crimes in Accordance with the Law, full text) | Yes | Item 4: "Organizing 'doxxing,' unlawfully collecting and publishing citizens' personal information to an unspecified majority, where circumstances are serious and meet the requirements of Criminal Law Article 253-1, shall be convicted and punished as the crime of infringing on citizens' personal information"; Item 11: "For cases where the victim files a private prosecution for online insult or defamation, where the people's court upon review finds that the victim genuinely has difficulty providing evidence, it may request the public security organ to provide assistance. The public security organ shall, based on the people's court's request and the specific circumstances of the case, promptly ascertain the actor's identity and collect evidence materials such as the spread and impact of the relevant insulting or defamatory information. The online service provider shall, according to law, provide necessary technical support and assistance to the public security organ for evidence collection. Where, after the public security organ's assisted evidence collection, the conditions for accepting a private prosecution case are met, the people's court shall decide to file the case; where the relevant evidence materials cannot be collected, the public security organ shall provide a written explanation to the people's court"; Item 12 lists five circumstances to be recognized as "serious harm to social order" under Criminal Law Article 246 paragraph 2 (serious consequences such as mental breakdown or suicide with adverse social impact; randomly targeting the general public as victims triggering large numbers of vulgar malicious comments; insulting or defaming multiple persons or disseminating multiple times; organizing or directing personnel to disseminate in large volumes across multiple platforms; others); Item 15: "Where the right-holder has evidence that the actor is engaged in or about to engage in unlawful acts infringing on their personality rights, and failure to promptly stop will cause irreparable harm to their lawful interests, the people's court may, on the basis of Civil Code Article 997 and the specific circumstances of the case, issue a personality-rights injunction ordering the actor to cease the relevant acts" |

Graded A: All four are currently effective official documents, and the provisions themselves give concrete actionable actions and thresholds (which five circumstances trigger conversion to public prosecution; the case must be filed after assisted evidence collection). Benefit tier "medium" — freedom metric; this entry offers the reader cessation of harm and pursuing the other party, at the same tier as Entry 31 (the falsely accused can request pursuit), not graded "large." Cost money=0 time=medium willpower=some, synthesized as "ordinary."

Per this round's set rule "when writing about legal support you must write the process cost," the Note states: the platform is fastest and cheapest, the court is slowest and most expensive, so preserve evidence first and report first; an injunction requires applying to a court, a private prosecution requires the victim to file and prove the case themselves, and conversion to public prosecution requires meeting one of the five circumstances in Item 12; failing to meet them leaves only private prosecution. Two additional "don'ts": don't engage in mutual insults (which would turn you into the one punished under Entry 14), and don't delete content from your own account (it's evidence).

Not written into the body: Quantitative evidence on the association between cyberbullying and suicide (the available meta-analyses focus on adolescents, mismatching the population and scenario); only the exit hotline 12356 is given in the Note (Section 1, Entry 25). The 12377 cyberspace reporting hotline did not have its official page verified word for word this round, so the body only writes "the prominent quick-report entry on the platform," not the hotline number. Civil Code Article 1195 (notice-and-takedown) was not retrieved from the Supreme People's Procuratorate's reproduced page this round, and is not cited; platform liability is instead cited from the Provisions on the Governance of Online Violence Information.
