## Entry 2 (Years of education and adult mortality)

| URL | Verified | Original quotation |
|---|---|---|
| <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(23)00306-7> (Europe PMC REST retrieved title record and abstract full text, PMID 38278172, The Lancet Public Health 2024;9(3):e155-e165) | Yes | "an average reduction in mortality risk of 1·9% (95% uncertainty interval 1·8-2·0) per additional year of education"; "2·9% (2·8-3·0) … for adults aged 18-49 years, compared with a 0·8% (0·6-1·0) reduction for adults older than 70 years"; "603 of which were eligible for analysis and included data from 70 locations in 59 countries, producing a final dataset of 10 355 observations"; "We found no differential effect of education on all-cause mortality by sex or Socio-demographic Index level"; "We identified publication bias (p<0·0001)" |

Authorship is recorded per Europe PMC's authorString as "IHME-CHAIN Collaborators," without writing an individual first author. Graded A (disputed): effect sizes, intervals, and sample sizes are complete, but the authors self-report publication bias and high heterogeneity; all included studies are observational, and education cannot be separated from family-condition confounding; the Note notes this. Benefit tier "large" — the mortality metric by relative reduction threshold does not apply (1.9% per year is a single-year increment; reading to high school is three years more than reading to junior high, cumulatively about 5%–6%); here it is judged "large" on the basis of "the additional years usually count in threes, and the 18-49 age band has 2.9% per year," with the reasoning recorded here. Cost money=0 (tuition accounted for separately in Entry 4) time=much willpower=yes, synthesized as "ordinary" — this is honest: studying for several more years is one of the highest-cost actions in this book.
