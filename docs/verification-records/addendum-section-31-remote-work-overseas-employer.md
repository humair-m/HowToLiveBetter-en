# Addendum: Working Remotely from Home for a Foreign Company · Verification Record (2026-09-20)

Task origin: A reader asked "is there anything about precautions for working remotely in China," and mentioned a saying they had heard — that to work for a foreign employer, the employee must hold that country's nationality.

Existing coverage: Searching the whole book for "remote work," "remote working," "working from home," "digital nomad," "foreign employer," "foreign company," "cross-border employment," and "foreign-related labor" returned not a single entry addressing "the person is in the country, working remotely for a foreign company." The five closest hits were all on a different matter: Section 31, Entry 14 (check the foreign labor cooperation business qualification when going abroad to work) governs the person leaving the country; Section 32, Entries 3 and 4 (working hours and status for international students) presuppose a local study visa; Section 11, Entry 5 (do not take gambling or scam App development jobs) is a red line for accepting work; Section 9, Entries 5 and 6 are moonlighting traps; Section 5, Entry 2 (individual income tax annual reconciliation) only addresses domestic comprehensive income, with no mention of foreign-source income anywhere in the section. The landing point is one new entry in Section 31 (Entry 15); the original Entry 15 (entrepreneurship guaranteed loan) is renumbered to Entry 16.

Placement rationale: Inserted after Entry 14 (going abroad to work); the two entries form the two sides of "working for a foreign employer" — leaving the country and not leaving the country — and read together. Checked the whole book: **no other section refers to specific entry numbers in Section 31**, and the only entry-number reference inside the section is "see Entry 3" in Entry 3's note, which is before the insertion point, so this insertion breaks zero references.

## Grading and selection

- Graded A: All four are verifiable regulations with a complete chain of reasoning — the service is provided within the country (Implementing Regulations Article 3, item 1) → domestic-source income → the payer has no establishment in China and withholding cannot land → triggers self-filing under Individual Income Tax Law Article 10, item 2 → foreign exchange receipt is handled under the current account as non-commercial settlement, going through Article 10, item 7, "employee compensation."
- **Metric and tier**: The metric is money. Benefit tier is judged "medium," **not derived by mechanically applying a threshold** — what this entry buys back is not a specific amount but "do not under-report, do not get stuck on foreign exchange receipt." No monetary threshold applies, so it is judged "medium" by judgment, with the basis recorded here. Cost: money 0, time medium (one annual reconciliation per year, plus bank paperwork), willpower some; by the threshold the cost-effectiveness tier lands at "ordinary."
- **The most counterintuitive point**: ordinary people think "working for a foreign company = foreign-source income," but under the rule that it is determined by the place of service provision, it is in fact **domestic-source income**. The foreign income credit framework (Ministry of Finance and State Taxation Administration Announcement No. 3 of 2020) does not apply, and the foreign-source income exemption for individuals without a domicile (Implementing Regulations Article 4) does not apply either. The "plain-language" summary makes this point.
- **Four items not written** (each could have an actual cost, but no verifiable basis could be found this round, so they were not written): ① whether this income is subject to value-added tax and whether to issue an invoice on behalf of the payer; ② whether long-term operation like this requires a business license or registration as an individual household (个体户); ③ whether labor law rights can be claimed against the foreign employer (annual leave, overtime pay, severance); ④ data export and confidentiality. The Note lists these four items so the reader does not assume they are covered.

## The reader's "foreign employers require employees to hold that country's nationality"

**Not adopted; the body does not write this claim at all** (correction after merging: an earlier draft Note had read "the online claim '...must hold that country's nationality' has no verifiable official basis in this book, so it is not written" — that is quoting a claim the reader has not seen in order to then evaluate it, the same flaw as the "so-and-so is not made up" formulation that is banned from the "plain-language" summary; it has been deleted). Findings from the search: U.S. export control `U.S. person` (citizen, lawful permanent resident, asylee or refugee) does mean that some controlled-technology positions effectively do not hire foreigners, but that is a consequence of export-control "deemed export," not a labor law rule of "employers must hire nationals"; moreover, the materials all discuss scenarios of **being employed within the United States** and disclosing controlled technology to foreign nationals — they do not match "the person sits in China working remotely." **More importantly, all sources retrieved were law firm analytical articles (Covington, Pillsbury, etc.); not a single ITAR/EAR original text or BIS/DDTC official position was found**, which under the project's citation rules cannot be cited, and is therefore not written into the body. If collected in the future, the original ITAR (22 CFR 120.62 et al.) and EAR (15 CFR 772.1) texts on eCFR must first be obtained before deciding.

## Correction after merging (2026-09-20): Classifying the income as "income from personal services" is wrong

The original entry's "plain-language" summary wrote "pay individual income tax domestically as income from personal services," and the Benefit field only cited the definition of personal services in Implementing Regulations Article 6, item (2). This step does not hold up:

- Implementing Regulations Article 6, item (1), reads "income from wages and salaries refers to wages, salaries, bonuses ... obtained by an individual by reason of **employment or engagement**" (verified at <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2018-12/22/content_5351177.htm>, compared word for word). The entry title itself reads "working for a foreign **employer**," the body asks the reader to take an **employment contract** to the bank to settle foreign exchange as "employee compensation," yet files tax under personal services — internally contradictory.
- And the entry itself admits in its Note that "what kind of relationship exists between the foreign employer and the remote worker ... no verifiable basis could be found" — if the relationship is not characterized, the income category cannot be asserted either.
- This is not a wording issue; it lands on money: Individual Income Tax Law Article 6 only allows a 20% deduction from income from personal services, author's remuneration, and royalties before being included in income; wages and salaries are not included (verified at the State Taxation Administration policy and regulation database <https://fgk.chinatax.gov.cn/zcfgk/c100009/c5193028/content.html>, 2018 seventh-amended version, Article 6). The same income computed under two different income categories yields different tax.

Fix: The plain-language summary was changed to "include it in the current year's comprehensive income and file on your own domestically." The income category is split between "signed an employment contract, follow the other side's arrangements = wages and salaries / take work on a project basis and deliver = income from personal services" with one sentence each, and personal services is noted to be computed using only 80% of the income. The Benefit field adds the verbatim quotations of Implementing Regulations Article 6, item (1), and Individual Income Tax Law Article 6. The Source field adds Article 6 to the Individual Income Tax Law article numbers and adds a State Taxation Administration policy and regulation database link. The "four items not written" item ③ in the Note is correspondingly narrowed to "whether labor law rights can be claimed against the foreign employer," no longer mixing it with the income-category question; repository-workflow words like "this round" were changed out. The original reasoning chain (service provided within the country → domestic-source income → no withholding agent → self-filing → foreign exchange settlement as "employee compensation") is unaffected and still holds.
