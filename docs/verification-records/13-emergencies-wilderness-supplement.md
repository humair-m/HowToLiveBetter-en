Verification date: 2026-09-07. This round Section 13 went from 18 to 27 entries, the book from 309 to 318 entries.

The additions cover previously missing common fatal scenarios: anaphylactic shock, epileptic seizures, hypoglycemia, electric shock, carbon monoxide poisoning; and in the wilderness section, which previously only covered getting lost, hypothermia, snakebite, encountering wild animals, and heat illness, lightning, altitude illness, ticks, and wilderness water have been added.

Method consistent with previous rounds: English-language literature was retrieved via Europe PMC REST (`search?query=TITLE:"…"&resultType=core`) to obtain bibliographic records and original abstracts; for open access items, `/{PMCID}/fullTextXML` was used to obtain the full text and verify word by word; official agency pages were retrieved via WebFetch and verbatim quotation required.

## III. Metrics and Benefit Magnitude

Seven of the nine entries have a mortality metric (including health endpoints). The benefit magnitude is set per the prevailing convention of Section 13: those that directly determine life or death (anaphylactic shock, severe hypoglycemia episode, electric shock, carbon monoxide, lightning, high-altitude cerebral edema/pulmonary edema) are set to "large"; those whose benefit is mainly avoiding secondary injury or delay (epilepsy, ticks, wilderness water) are set to "medium". All are zero-cost except the tick entry (which requires a pair of fine-tipped tweezers, money=few); lightning and high-altitude additionally note "willpower=some" — the hard part is not knowing, but being willing to turn back at the first thunderclap, and being willing to descend on the plateau.
