Blood donation itself: zero coverage.

Landing: Section 6, Entry 17. Placed in the negative list because what is being rejected is the claim "blood donation is good for you" (this section collects things that look cheap and effective but whose benefit is near zero), not the act of donating; "should you donate, how to donate" is written in the notes. Metric: mortality (health endpoint); benefit magnitude "small"—all endpoints are surrogate (ferritin, hemoglobin, self-reported symptoms, body temperature, oxygen consumption); per the threshold "surrogate endpoints only = small" applied mechanically. Cost: time medium (about an hour per donation), willpower some, money 0; lands in the average cost-effectiveness tier.

## Grading and selection

- Graded A: The primary evidence is two randomized controlled trials (FeAST 1,277 people, INTERVAL 45,263 people) plus a million-level cohort; all numbers verifiable word-for-word.
- **Not marked "disputed"**: The Kuopio study and the subsequent evidence do not constitute two opposing factions; it is the source of this claim and is itself observational. Ullum quantified the healthy-donor effect in the same population (18.6% → 7.5%); FeAST turned "bloodletting to reduce iron protects the heart" into a randomized trial and got a negative result. The body lays out this chain in order, which is clearer than slapping on a "disputed" label.
- **The cold-intolerance passage is given a separate strength note**: The human evidence is only Beard 1990's 10-person small sample (if standalone, would be grade B); the body note states "this is here for mechanism, not for incidence rate", and does not use it to infer what proportion of donors will become cold-intolerant.
- **Not written**: the incidence of vasovagal reactions (needle phobia, fainting)—no verifiable large-sample Chinese data found this round, so it is not written; nor were any claims like "blood donation can help with weight loss / blood pressure reduction" written, which have no basis in the primary literature.
- Beneficiary: Blood goes to strangers (Tier 4); per the book's rule, the part that can come back to oneself must be written out, so the note lists the Article 14 fee waiver for blood use, the 2024 Notice's 28-province direct reduction and "three exemptions".
