# Section 2, Entry 26 Supplement: Tea Mycotoxins and Green-Tea Extract (2026-09-23)

Task source: GitHub issue #27 (ccpfucker), titled "Against the view that drinking tea is good for health." Original claim: "When you have nothing to do, drink water and imported pure milk; drink less tea," on the grounds that tea may be contaminated with mycotoxins and high-concentration tea extracts are hepatotoxic; the conclusion: "drinking tea is good for health" cannot be a universal conclusion, and tea should be treated as an ordinary beverage. Three papers attached.

## Three Papers, Paper-by-Paper Verification

| URL | Verified | Original abstract key points |
|---|---|---|
| <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fct.2020.111830> (Cui P et al., 2020, Food and Chemical Toxicology; PMID 33127496) | Yes (Europe PMC abstract) | 158 samples of domestic post-fermented dark tea tested for 4 aflatoxins, "Two out of 158 samples were positive… (occurrence rate 1.27%)." Upper-bound exposure for high-exposure populations 9.19 × 10⁻⁶ μg/kg/day, "lower than the JECFA acceptable value of 1.0 ng kg⁻¹ day⁻¹ on liver risk"; the 95th-percentile probabilistic cancer risk "equally below the acceptable carcinogenic risk level" |

## Handling Decision

**No change to conclusion, no change to evidence grade, no change to title.** All three papers' own conclusions are "no risk at normal tea-drinking amounts": the first's exposure is below JECFA's acceptable value, the second explicitly states that the Chinese population has no dietary risk from tea consumption, and the third explicitly states that brewed tea does not produce hepatic adverse events. They do not overturn China-PAR's mortality association; the issue's conclusion goes further than the evidence it cites. The "drink imported pure milk instead" suggestion has no evidence from the issue, not adopted.

**Adopted parts go into the Note.** Readers may indeed be concerned about moldy tea, and some do take green-tea extract capsules as a substitute for "drinking tea"; the entry previously did not address these. So the Note adds two paragraphs: mycotoxin detection status and risk-assessment conclusions, and the distinction between extract and brewed tea, landing at "this entry only counts brewed tea; don't substitute extract supplements for it." All three papers per the rule go into the Source field; the Note has no links.

**"Don't drink tea that's been damp and molded" is a common-sense reminder (C-grade metric), with no separate source.** The issue mentions storage conditions affecting safety, but none of the three papers provides storage-related numbers, so only the action is written, without numbers.

**"Disputed" opening retained, magnitude unchanged.** Benefit field unchanged, Plain-language summary also unchanged.
