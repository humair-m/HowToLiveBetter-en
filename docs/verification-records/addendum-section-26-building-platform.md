# Section 25 "Building a Website or Platform" · Verification Record (2026-09-08)

Task origin: A reader wanted to build a livestreaming website and a platform where users could post things for sale, and asked what procedures to handle, what process to follow, and where to buy servers. The whole book's existing coverage only included ICP filing and classified protection in Section 11, Entry 14; everything else (value-added telecommunications license, EDI, online culture business license, audiovisual license, e-commerce law platform obligations, tax-information reporting, content governance, real-name registration, minors, notice-and-takedown, data export, platform collection and disbursement) was zero coverage. A new Section 26 was agreed with the reader, with 11 entries, plus a long-form piece docs/做平台要办哪些证.md.

Why a new section rather than folding into Sections 11 or 12: the reader of Section 11 is a technical employee; the reader of Section 12 is someone opening a shop or doing business; someone building a platform is neither, and splitting would have scattered the same matter across three sections with readers bouncing between them. The book's table of contents is already scenario-driven, and adding one scenario does not break the structure.

Working method: This round located official original text through four parallel retrieval subtasks (telecom and filing, livestreaming and audiovisual, e-commerce law platform obligations, payment and settlement). The main task verified the key quotations before writing the entries. The "Verified" column in the table below indicates whether the main task personally opened the original page to check.

| <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/201901/t20190131_407161.shtml> | No | 2019 Interpretation of the Supreme People's Court and Supreme People's Procuratorate, Article 1: three circumstances; Article 3: "serious circumstances" means an illegal business volume of more than 5,000,000 yuan or illegal gains of more than 100,000 yuan; Article 4: standards for "particularly serious circumstances" |

Key limitation, written into the entry Note: There is no term "second settlement" (二清) in the official documents. The three circumstances enumerated by the Supreme Court and Supreme Procuratorate interpretation are fabricating transactions for cash-out, cash-out through a unit's bank settlement account, and cash-out by check; it does not list "platform collection and disbursement" as one of the circumstances. The entry therefore only states the metric from the statute and the interpretation, and does not assert that "platform collection and disbursement is itself a crime."

## Two expired entry numbers fixed in passing

The Cybersecurity Law was amended on 2025-10-28 and took effect on 2026-01-01, with article numbering adjusted. The amendment decision states "Article 59 is renumbered as Article 61." Accordingly:

- Section 11, Entry 14 originally cited "Cybersecurity Law (Articles 21, 59)" and has been changed to Articles 23 and 61, with the old article numbers retained for comparison. The URL was switched to the Central Cyberspace Affairs Commission's full amended text at <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm>. Article 23, paragraph (3), "retain relevant network logs for no less than six months," was checked by the main task opening the original page.
- Another entry in Section 11 originally cited "Articles 27, 63." Article 27 (prohibiting illegal intrusion into another's network) is now Article 29, and was verified. The new article number of the former Article 63 in the Liability chapter is not listed in the amendment decision's correspondence table, so it could not be confirmed this round; the Source field states truthfully that "the article numbers in the amended Liability chapter have been adjusted, and were not verified item by item this round."

## Could not open or did not obtain

- The 200-call WebSearch quota for this session was exhausted by subtasks; later-stage positioning relied entirely on numbering-probing of the gov.cn gazette pages and the policy repository search interface.
- For the E-Commerce Law, no usable full-text page was found on the Chinese government website (<https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2018/content_5320991.htm> and <http://www.gov.cn/xinwen/2018-08/31/content_5318220.htm> both 404). The entry was switched to the National Laws and Regulations Database (flk.npc.gov.cn) entry page; that page is front-end rendered and readable in a browser, but scripts cannot extract the body text.
- The official text stating "e-commerce platforms with matchmaking and fund-handling functions must apply for an EDI license" was not obtained. The Ministry of Industry and Information Technology service guidelines only state "apply for the corresponding telecommunications business operating license according to the business definition," and have answered that "ride-hailing platforms only need to complete website filing" and "equity- and bulk-commodity trading platforms only need to complete website filing." The entry therefore only states the B21 definition and does not draw a conclusion.
- A Supreme Court or Supreme Procuratorate judicial interpretation or guiding case directly characterizing "operating commercial Internet cultural activities without a license" or "engaging in Internet audiovisual program services without authorization" as the crime of illegal business operations was not obtained; the existing interpretation (Fa Shi [2000] No. 12) targets unauthorized operation of international or Hong Kong/Macao/Taiwan telecommunications business.
- An official case of someone being penalized by the People's Bank of China or convicted for operating a payment business without a license was not obtained; the People's Bank of China administrative penalty disclosure batches spot-checked did not contain this type of violation.
- npc.gov.cn site-wide HTTPS handshake failure; mps.gov.cn site-wide 521; several provincial communications administration bureau sites had handshake failures or 502.
- The official pages for the Provisions on the Administration of Online Livestreaming Services and the Notice on Strengthening the Standardized Management of Online Livestreaming Tipping both lack document numbers, so the Source field only lists the issuing body and date.
- The Interim Provisions on the Administration of Internet Culture reproduced by the Chinese government website in July 2025 is the unrevised 2011 original text, and its Article 21 still cites the superseded Regulation on the Investigation and Punishment of Business Operations without a License. This section uses the current Ministry of Culture and Tourism text throughout; this trap is recorded here for reference.
