# Audit: Entries That "Only Prohibit, Without a Way Out" · Record (2026-09-18)

Task source: The first draft of Section 24, Entry 12 was called cold-hearted by a user ("My dad did give cigarettes to the doctor who resuscitated me — that doctor worked through the night to save me and never slept... so I shouldn't give anything or even say thank you?"). After it was rewritten, the user asked: "check whether this problem exists elsewhere."

Audit metric (different from the 2026-09-09 round on "immunity overreach / beneficiary mismatch" — this is a third type): the entry negates something done out of a legitimate emotional motive (gratitude, filial piety, caring, mourning, affection, helping, saving face), but only writes "don't, useless, against the rules" without giving that motive a legitimate outlet; or it writes the person's situation as if it were a mistake.

## Audit Scope and Results

Scanned all negative-formulation expressions across all 522 titles ("don't" / "do not" / "less" / "refuse" / "stop"), then filtered for "high-risk" entries whose motive is part of ordinary human nature and read each one's body text. Also searched the full text for "gift, gift money, favors, filial piety, sentiment, gift, present."

## Changes

### 1. Section 6, Entry 10 (health supplements, herbal pastes, tonics) — a real problem, fixed

The entry itself states in the Benefit field that "in gift scenarios, the purchase decision is driven by sentiment, not evidence," thereby naming the motive as filial piety and sentiment — and then the entire entry gives that money nowhere to go, reading like "your little bit of thought toward your parents is an idiot tax." Same flaw as the first draft of Section 24, Entry 12.

Fix (without touching the Benefit field or evidence grade; only changing Plain-language summary and Note; no new sources added): the Plain-language summary ends with a paragraph "the same money, swapped for these" — each item points back to existing entries in the book — flu vaccine for older adults (Section 1, Entry 20), shingles vaccine for those over 50 (Entry 21), pneumococcal vaccine for those over 65 (Entry 22), blood pressure monitor and antihypertensics taken as prescribed (Entry 7), fall-prevention modifications and balance training (Entry 13), age-appropriate cancer screening (Entries 17 to 19), pressure ulcers and long-term care insurance for the bedridden (Section 17, Entries 7 and 8); for things brought to the door, swap for fruit and rice. Note adds: this entry rejects the product, not the sentiment; if the elder is already taking it and it isn't replacing a medication they should be on, there's no need to argue at the door — what really needs blocking is the type that substitutes for a medication, and the "pay-first investment-style eldercare" (Section 17, Entry 5).

### 2. Section 25, Entry 6 (funeral basic-services list) — insufficient warmth, added a sentence

The original Note only taught how to ask for the list and how to negotiate the price. Added: "Wanting to handle a loved one's affairs with dignity is not wrong; this entry doesn't urge simplicity — it only addresses which items have a list and a statutorily-set fee, and which are optional."

### 3. Section 17, Entry 4 (giving an elder a scripted excuse line) — insufficient warmth, added a sentence

The word "scripted excuse" (话术) can read as defending against the elder. Added: "This line isn't for defending against the elder — it's a way to give them a step so they don't have to refuse on the spot; the hardest part of being surrounded by salespeople is never judging whether it's real, it's opening your mouth to refuse in a roomful of people."

## What Was Not Touched

- All three changes added no new entries, no new sources, no change to evidence grade. Entry count 522, A-grade 342, links 1051 — all unchanged.
- Section 3, Entry 16 (reducing relationships that drain you) reads coldly, but its Note already states "which relationships count as 'draining' has no objective standard; only you can judge," and the evidence itself is weak (C grade, cross-sectional); adding a buffer would turn it into persuasion that doesn't match the evidence, so it was left as-is.
