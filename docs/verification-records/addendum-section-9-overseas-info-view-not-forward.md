# Addendum: Overseas Information — View Only, Do Not Save, Forward, or Comment · Verification Record (2026-09-08)

Task origin: A reader raised one matter — content on overseas websites (especially political content) should only be viewed, not saved, forwarded, or commented on, and not credulously followed; doing this yields no substantive personal benefit, but the cost may be a prison term.

Existing coverage: Section 9, Entry 1 covers disaster/epidemic/police-situation rumors (Public Security Administration Punishment Law Article 29 + Criminal Law Article 291-1); Entry 2 covers insults to martyrs and the national flag, anthem, and emblem; neither involves the chapter on crimes against state security; Section 11, Entry 9 covers "selling circumvention tools and setting up nodes for others" under the crime of illegal business operations and Provisional Regulations Article 14, but does not cover what to do after viewing content; Section 8's online speech entry covers reputation rights and defamation. None of the four overlaps; the landing point is one new entry in Section 9 (Entry 3), with the original Entry 3 onward renumbered to Entries 4 through 18.

Placement rationale: This section's ordering metric is "easiest to inadvertently trigger × most serious consequence." Forwarding and posting comments are routine actions (easy to trigger), and Criminal Law Article 105 paragraph 2's "ringleaders or those whose crimes are serious" tier starts at 5 years with no upper bound up to the 15-year fixed-term imprisonment cap, and crimes against state security require supplementary deprivation of political rights and may be concurrently punished with confiscation of property (the consequence is more serious than the dissemination-of-obscene-material entry under Entry 3), so it is placed after the two speech-related Entries 1 and 2 and before the pornography dissemination entry.

Article version check: Criminal Law Articles 103, 105, 106, 107, 111, and 113, as well as Articles 45, 55, and 56, have not been amended since 1997 by any criminal law amendment, so they are cited directly from the Supreme People's Procuratorate reproduced 1997 text (any other entry in this repository citing this page must first confirm the article has not been amended, per the pitfall record in memory). **The Cybersecurity Law has article renumbering after the 2025 amendment**: the prohibition in the 2016 text was Article 12 paragraph 2; after the 2025 amendment it is Article 13 paragraph 2, with the corresponding penalty provision moving from Article 68 to Article 71 item (1). This entry cites by the post-amendment article numbers.

Cases (not adopted): Searches for officially published cases of "incitement to subvert state power" yielded no verifiable judgment documents or typical cases on the Supreme Court, Supreme People's Procuratorate, or Ministry of State Security websites (mss.gov.cn failed TLS handshake on this machine; chinapeace.gov.cn connection was reset). The "Program Thinker" (Ruan Xiaohuan) case raised by the reader was sentenced by the Shanghai Second Intermediate People's Court in February 2023 to seven years' imprisonment for incitement to subvert state power, with two years' deprivation of political rights and confiscation of 20,000 yuan in personal property; the second-instance in December 2024 upheld the original verdict — but this information appears only in overseas media reports (RFA), Wikipedia, and a scan of the judgment released by the family, all of which are second-hand or non-official channels and may not be cited per the project's citation rules. So this entry only writes the statutory provisions without appending cases, and the Note states "no verifiable judgment document from an official channel was obtained," consistent with Entry 1's Note handling of "Ministry of Public Security website inaccessible, no specific case cited."

Related provisions not written into the entry (left for later): The Counter-Espionage Law (2023 revision) provisions on overseas institutions, organizations, and individuals carrying out or funding or directing others to carry out espionage — no verifiable official full text was obtained this round (the corresponding page in the Supreme People's Procuratorate law library did not hit), so the body cites only Criminal Law Articles 107 and 111, not the Counter-Espionage Law. The Public Security Administration Punishment Law (2025 revision) Article 31 item (3) "making and disseminating items, information, or materials propagating cults and sects" with detention of 10 to 15 days is adjacent to this entry's topic but a different category of content, and was not merged.
