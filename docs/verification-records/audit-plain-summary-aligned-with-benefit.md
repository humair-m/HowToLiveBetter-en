# Whole-Book Audit: Plain-Language Summary Aligned with Benefit Field (2026-09-19)

Task source: A user reading Section 6's blood-donation entry picked up the line "'pale complexion, sensitivity to cold' isn't made up" and couldn't understand it — they had no idea who had ever said anything about a pale complexion, and indeed nothing in the book's sources mentions "pale complexion." The user then pointed out the general nature of the problem: "Many readers are used to reading the Plain-language summary directly, without reading the Source or the verification records," and asked for a whole-book audit of similar issues.

## Audit Method

First ran two rounds of mechanical scans (19 rebuttal-style phrases' hits on Plain-language summary lines, and an item-by-item comparison of all Section 6 quotation assertions with the Benefit field), which surfaced only one new problem in the cold-shower entry — indicating that mechanical scanning was insufficient. Six review agents were then dispatched in parallel, each comparing every entry item-by-item, covering all 32 files and 544 entries under book/. Each agent was given the same criteria:

Four categories of problems to look for: **[added number]** — a number in the Plain-language summary that cannot be found in this entry's Benefit field; **[added fact]** — a symptom, outcome, or factual assertion in the Plain-language summary that has no basis in this entry's Benefit field or Source field; **[fabricated mechanism]** — the Plain-language summary imposes a causal or mechanistic interpretation on the Benefit field data that the original research did not make; **[not self-contained]** — the Plain-language summary refers to a claim the reader has never seen and then critiques it.

Six categories of **non-problems** (counter-examples) were also given to prevent false positives: phrasing differs but content matches; jargon popularized; HR/RR converted to everyday terms; self-contained logical turns; well-known statements or statements whose origin is given in-body; closing action recommendations.

104 issues were reported. Three were spot-checked (Section 2 hot-water-drinking control group; Section 24 ER triage Beijing metric; Section 31 targeted medical students' service period) — all confirmed true — and the report was followed item by item.

## What Was Fixed

**Metric written backwards or too narrow (the most critical category)**

| Entry | Original text | What the Benefit field actually says |
|---|---|---|
| Section 31, targeted medical students | Residency training "these 3 years count toward the service period" | "Training years in case of breach **do not** count toward the service period" — opposite meaning |
| Section 24, ER triage | 10 minutes / 30 minutes / 4 hours are universal time limits | "Four-tier standard is a national metric; **response times are Beijing municipal standards**" |
| Section 24, out-of-area medical care | Reimbursement is "a notch lower" | "Maintains **reasonable differences**" — original does not state direction |
| Section 9, rumors | "Forwarding without verifying yourself" can be punished | Both penalty clauses require the element of **knowing** it is false |
| Section 11, mining | "Also **must** be fined 50,000 to 500,000" | "**May** be fined" — the fine is discretionary |
| Section 12, sourcing | "**Directly** deemed to be knowingly involved" | "**May** be deemed (except where evidence proves genuine ignorance)" |
| Section 28, eating disorders | "Looking at each one separately doesn't predict" | "**After adjusting** for prior dieting and psychiatric symptoms" |
| Section 29, unemployment | "Nearly a quarter of the gap is explained by smoking and drinking" | "Studies that controlled for health behaviors had HR 24% lower" — not the same quantity |
| Section 23, child labor | "If something happens, settle privately" | Article 10 of the Provisions on the Prohibition of the Use of Child Labor specifically imposes statutory compensation obligations on the employer |

**Deleted facts, mechanisms, and frequency assertions that could not be verified in the Benefit field**: seatbelt's "smashed into serious injury," helmet's "unbuckled chinstrap equals no helmet," shingles' "rarely fatal" and neuralgia, bloody urine's "often stops on its own," Section 8 wife-murder-insurance-fraud case's search records and skid marks, Section 9 kidney-selling case's "iPad 2" and fabricated amount "four to five hundred thousand," Section 13's "usually two people together fail," "the person breaking up the fight is usually the one finally counted in," "the car is most easily found," Section 13 foreign-body pressure-hemostasis mechanism, Section 17 pressure ulcer's "broken skin turns black," Section 20 vitamin K oral comparison and diaper red-rash cause, Section 21 overseas driver's-license claim denial, Section 25 pension being falsely claimed, Section 27 postpartum follow-up items and hospital costs, Section 28 filler embolism mechanism, Section 29's "transfers over," "active door-knocking," "scammers most concentrated," Section 30 testicular torsion's "often accompanied by nausea and vomiting" and outdoor's "not about which sport," Section 31's one-size-fits-all online-loan advice, Section 32's RMB conversion.

**Added source rather than deleting** (content is accurate; just lacked a citation in this entry):

- Section 6, vitamin C: illness shortened by 8%, RR 0.48 in extreme-exercise populations was already in the Note; both come from the same Cochrane review, moved into the Benefit field.
- Section 7, unemployment insurance: added Article 48 of the Social Insurance Law (enrollment in employee medical insurance during benefit receipt, premiums paid from the unemployment insurance fund, individual pays nothing).
- Section 10, premarital exam: added Article 1053 of the Civil Code (right of revocation must be exercised within one year of knowing or should have known).
- Section 1, HIV testing: originally written as "may be anonymous"; verification only found basis for **confidentiality** (the National HIV Testing Work Management Measures provides that staff must not disclose name, address, or test results), and no official rule on waiving real-name registration was found. Confidentiality is not the same as anonymity, so the entry's title and body text were both changed to "results kept confidential," and the Measures were added to the Source field.

**While at it, fixed misaligned in-section references in Section 7** (additional findings by the agent, unrelated to Plain-language summary): Entry 5 pointed medical assistance to Entry 8 (should be Entry 11); Entry 8 pointed legal aid and subsidized insurance to Entries 2 and 8 (self-referential; should be Entries 3, 10, 11); Entry 11 pointed the 4× LPR cap to Entry 13 (should be Entry 16); Entries 19 and 21 pointed resident medical insurance to Entry 8 (should be Entry 10); Entry 22 pointed relief stations to Entry 3 (should be Entry 4). The whole book was then scanned for overreach references (reference entry number exceeds the section's entry count); no other hits. This type of "in-range but pointing wrong" can only be caught manually.

## Two same-typed errors I made while fixing

Worth recording separately, because they show how easy this error is to make:

1. In the Section 28 medical-aesthetics blindness entry, I rewrote the original embolism mechanism as "in 48 cases, six-tenths occurred at the nasal dorsum, glabella, and forehead" — that "six-tenths" was written from impression. The actual numbers in the Benefit field were nasal 56.3%, glabella 27.1%, forehead 18.8%, nasolabial fold 14.6%, and a single case can involve multiple sites, summing to over 100% — they cannot be combined into "six-tenths." Corrected to copy each item verbatim.
2. In the Section 27 cesarean-section entry, I rewrote WHO's "after exceeding 10% **there is no evidence that** mortality decreases" as "maternal-infant mortality **no longer decreases**." Lack of evidence is not the same as proof of ineffectiveness. Reverted to the original metric.

**Lesson learned**: When rewriting Plain-language summary, you must open the Benefit field on the spot and copy verbatim — you cannot rely on memory of what you just read.

## Cases Judged as No Change Needed

When the Plain-language summary references content from another section but **carries an explicit cross-section annotation** (e.g., Section 3, Entry 20's discussion of red envelopes includes a "(see Section 24…)"), it is not treated as an added fact — the reader will not mistake it for the result of this entry's research; its nature differs from a fabricated assertion like "pale complexion." This metric was written into the judging convention outside of CLAUDE.md and is recorded here for reference.

## Rule Synchronized

The Plain-language summary rule in CLAUDE.md previously only forbade "**numbers** not in the Benefit field"; the vast majority of failures this round were not numbers. Updated to: what cannot be added also includes symptoms, factual assertions, and mechanism interpretations; the Plain-language summary must be self-contained, and formulations that require a prior assertion to be readable — like "'XX' isn't made up" or "'XX' is true" — are forbidden.
