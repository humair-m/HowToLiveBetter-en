## Entry 11 "When parents fund a home purchase, make clear at the time of transfer whether it is a loan or a gift"

### Marriage and Family Part Interpretation (I) Article 29
- Page: <https://www.court.gov.cn/fabu/xiangqing/282071.html> : opened via WebFetch; 法释〔2020〕22 号, effective January 1, 2021
- Quote: "Where, before the parties marry, parents fund the purchase of a home for both parties, the funding shall be deemed a gift to their own child individually, unless the parents explicitly state that it is a gift to both parties. Where, after the parties marry, parents fund the purchase of a home for both parties, the matter shall be handled according to agreement; absent an agreement or where the agreement is unclear, the matter shall be handled according to the principles of Article 1062 (1) (4) of the Civil Code."
- This link is already used by the Section 8 bride-price entry; the page is reachable

### Marriage and Family Part Interpretation (II) Article 8
- Page: <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/452771.html> : opened via WebFetch; 法释〔2025〕1 号, passed at the 1933rd meeting of the Adjudication Committee on November 25, 2024, effective February 1, 2025, 23 articles in total
- Quote (Article 8): "During the marriage, where a home purchased by the spouses is fully funded by one party's parent, if the gift contract explicitly specifies that the gift is only to that parent's own child, the matter shall be handled according to the agreement; absent an agreement or where the agreement is unclear, upon divorce division of spousal joint property, the people's court may rule that the home belongs to the funding parent's child, taking into account the circumstances of joint life and any joint children, divorce fault, contributions to the family, and the market price of the home at the time of divorce, and determine whether the party receiving the home shall compensate the other party and the amount of compensation. During the marriage, where a home purchased by the spouses is partially funded by one party's parent or funded by both parties' parents, if the gift contract explicitly specifies that the corresponding funding is only to that parent's own child, the matter shall be handled according to the agreement; absent an agreement or where the agreement is unclear, upon divorce division of spousal joint property, the people's court may, based on the parties' claims, take into account the source and proportion of funding, the circumstances of joint life and any joint children, divorce fault, contributions to the family, and the market price of the home at the time of divorce, rule that the home belongs to one party, and require the party receiving the home to compensate the other party reasonably."
- Same-batch published typical marriage-and-family dispute cases <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/452761.html> : opened via WebFetch; Case 2 (Fan v. Xu divorce dispute) holding: "where a home purchased during the marriage with full funding by one party's parent is transferred and registered in both spouses' names, upon divorce division of spousal joint property, the court may, based on the funding source of the property, rule that the home belongs to the funding parent's child"; ruled to belong to the funding parent's child, with the other party compensated 70,000 yuan. The case is used only as supporting illustration of the rule; the body does not cite the number
- Civil Code Article 1062 (1) (4) (property inherited or received as a gift is spousal joint property) was already verified in Section 10, Entry 10; see `10-marriage-worth-it.md`
- Not verified: Interpretation (II) contains no provision on the burden of proof for "is parent funding a loan or a gift", and the 23 articles do not include a spousal-joint-debt provision (verified against the full text via WebFetch); so the body does not state conclusions like "courts always reject IOUs"

## Entry 12 "A large debt your spouse takes on, that you did not co-sign or ratify, does not automatically become your debt"

### Civil Code Article 1064
- Page: <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml> : opened via WebFetch
- Quote: "Debts incurred under the joint signature of both spouses or the joint intent expression of one spouse's subsequent ratification, etc., and debts incurred by one spouse in their own name during the marriage for the family's daily living needs, are spousal joint debts. Debts incurred by one spouse in their own name during the marriage in excess of the family's daily living needs are not spousal joint debts; however, where the creditor can prove that the debt was used for the spouses' joint life, joint production and operation, or based on the joint intent expression of both spouses, this exception does not apply."
- Note the wording is "joint signature" not "joint signing"

### Private Lending Provisions Articles 15 and 16
- Page: <http://gongbao.court.gov.cn/Details/94b6623974526df7d2430a3c73f050.html> : WebFetch 502; curl through proxy fetched a 24 KB GBK page and parsed locally
- Version: passed at the 1655th meeting of the Adjudication Committee on June 23, 2015; first amended by the decision of the 1809th meeting on August 18, 2020; second amended by the decision of the 1823rd meeting on December 23, 2020 (article numbering follows the second amended version)
- Quote (Article 15, paragraph 2): "Where the defendant disputes that the lending act has not yet occurred and can provide a reasonable explanation, the people's court shall, taking into account the lending amount, fund delivery, the parties' financial capacity, local or inter-party transaction methods and customs, changes in the parties' property, witness testimony, and other facts and factors, comprehensively judge and verify whether the lending fact occurred."
- Quote (Article 16): "Where the plaintiff brings a private-lending lawsuit based only on a financial-institution transfer voucher, and the defendant disputes that the transfer was repayment of a prior loan or other debt, the defendant shall provide evidence to prove its claim. After the defendant provides corresponding evidence to prove its claim, the plaintiff still bears the burden of proof for the establishment of the lending relationship."
- Both provisions were also verified via WebSearch abstract; text consistent; article numbers hold only for the second amended version; the version is noted in the citation

## Grading notes

- Both entries use the money metric: what is bought back is the attribution of the purchase-money sum (hundreds of thousands to over a million yuan) and the apportionment of debt in divorce property division; per the entry-format amount threshold (10,000-yuan class = large), the benefit magnitude is "large"
- All three cost items are zero for both: writing a one-page explanation and signing it on the day of funding, and not signing a spouse's IOU, cost no money and no time; Entry 12 requires some willpower (refusing a family member's or spouse's signature request), tagged willpower=some; Entry 11 tagged willpower=no
- Both graded A: all sources are currently effective legal and judicial-interpretation provisions whose text itself provides verifiable rules without depending on judgment
- The body does not state "an IOU signed by only one party is never a joint debt": Civil Code Article 1064 (2) leaves the exception "used for the spouses' joint life"; when purchase funds turn into jointly held or co-inhabited housing, creditors can often prove this layer; practice varies across jurisdictions; this section found no official statistics giving proportions, so the body only states the allocation of the burden of proof and the two-layer evidentiary sequence
