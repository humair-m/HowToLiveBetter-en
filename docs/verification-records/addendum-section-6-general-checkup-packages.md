# Section 6 Addendum: Do Not Rely on General Checkup Packages for Disease Prevention (2026-09-19)

Task origin: A user noted that the book does not address "how often ordinary people should get a checkup," and asked whether to add it.

Existing coverage: Individual screening tests are written throughout the book, but scattered in Section 1 — Entry 7 measure blood pressure, Entry 8 fasting blood glucose for overweight people over 35, Entry 14 hepatitis B two-half, Entry 17 breast cancer screening, Entry 18 cervical cancer screening, Entry 19 colorectal cancer screening, Entry 23 Helicobacter pylori, Entry 24 low-dose CT for heavy smokers, Entry 29 HIV testing; Section 6, Entry 7 rules out whole-body PET-CT and tumor-marker panels for asymptomatic people, and Entries 18 and 19 (original numbering) cover what to do when a checkup finds asymptomatic hyperuricemia or asymptomatic gallstones. What is missing is the umbrella principle — "for people who don't know what to check, should you buy a general package every year" as a question itself has no entry answering it, and the README's question table already had the row for Section 6 stating "checkup packages," indicating the topic was originally planned for Section 6.

Landing point: One new entry added to Section 6, numbered Entry 18, inserted before the original Entry 18 (asymptomatic hyperuricemia); the original Entries 18–23 become 19–24. Inserted at this position rather than at the section end because the two following entries are exactly "what to do when a checkup finds something" — "first don't go overboard on testing" reads coherently before "after you do find something, don't over-treat." Five cross-section references and three intra-section self-references were synced: book/03 line 194 (Section 6, Entry 22→23 and 23→24), book/05 line 311 (22→23), book/16 line 77 (20→21), book/16 line 86 (18→19), book/06 Entry 22 Note (this section, 23→24), Entry 24 Note in two places (this section, 22→23). The README question table was not given an extra row: a row "How often should ordinary people get a checkup, and exactly which items should be checked?" pointing to Section 6 was added initially, then retracted after user feedback. That table is above-the-fold navigation; one row per section keeps readers from stopping twice on the same destination, and Section 6's span is not so wide that one row cannot summarize it; the existing row already contains "checkup packages," so searching for "checkup" finds it. The two existing cases of two rows per section in the table are for Sections 8 and 24, an existing convention, and were not touched.

Graded A: The Cochrane systematic review gives pooled risk ratios and confidence intervals for three outcomes; the figures were verified word for word via the Europe PMC record; the content of the free services comes from the current national specification.

Benefit tier "medium," metric "money": This entry's benefit is not a reduction in mortality — quite the opposite, the evidence it cites shows that general checkups have no effect on mortality (RR 1.00). What it actually buys back is the saved out-of-pocket cost of the package, which by the amount threshold falls in the "hundreds to thousands" tier, so it is graded medium; all three cost components are zero, and the cost-effectiveness synthesis is "high." The metric was not set as mortality because under a mortality metric this entry's benefit would be mechanically set to "small" (no relative reduction), which would misrepresent its value.

## Two writing decisions

**The title carries its own way out.** The repository has a verification record `排查-只给禁止不给出路.md` (audit — only prohibit without giving a way out), and this entry follows that conclusion: the title is written as "Do not rely on general checkup packages for disease prevention; do the few evidence-based screenings appropriate to your age and sex," with the second half being the way out; the Note lists the entry numbers of the nine entries in Section 1 so the reader can directly assemble their own list. Just writing "do not buy packages" without offering an alternative would read as "checkups are useless," which is a dangerous misreading.

**The free channels are written separately into the Benefit field.** Part of the real-world answer to "how often to check up" is already free of out-of-pocket cost: free annual blood pressure measurement for those 35 and above, and a free annual checkup for those 65 and above. Writing this out clearly gives the concrete alternative action after "do not buy a package," and incidentally answers the frequency question.

**Scope of non-applicability is drawn explicitly.** The Note states three situations outside this entry's scope: employer-organized or other-paid checkups — go ahead; people who already have a chronic disease or symptoms; tests ordered by a doctor for a condition — that is diagnosis, not screening. The Cochrane included trials were mostly in high-income countries with good baseline medical access; the Note also notes that areas with poor access to medical resources should not transplant this directly.

**Specification version.** The cited National Basic Public Health Service Specification is the 2017 third edition, the current version; the program itself is still being executed (interpretable from the State Council's "Notice on Doing a Good Job with the 2025 Basic Public Health Services Work"), and that notice was not cited this time because the original text of both services comes from the third edition of the Specification.
