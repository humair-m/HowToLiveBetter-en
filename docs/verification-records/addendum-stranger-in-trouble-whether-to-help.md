# Addendum: Should You Step In When a Stranger Is in Trouble · Verification Record (2026-09-09)

Task origin: A reader asked "when you encounter a stranger crying for help on the road, should you rescue them? When you encounter drowning, when someone has fallen and is convulsing, when a group of people are fighting, when an old person has fallen, should you help them up?"

Existing coverage: Two of the five questions were already covered — drowning in Section 13 (originally Entry 25, now Entry 26), and fallen-convulsing in originally Entry 14 (now Entry 15), both with on-scene actions. The genuine blank is the "should you step in" layer: a whole-book search for "good Samaritan" only hits Section 7, Entry 3 (legal aid scope); searching for "Article 184" and "good person" returns zero hits (Section 13, Entry 1's Note mentions Civil Code Article 184 in passing, with no standalone landing point); "help up," "what to do after a fall" returns zero hits — Section 1, Entry 13 only covers how to prevent your own falls. Encountering a fight also returns zero hits: Section 8, Entry 9 covers being caught up in a conflict yourself, not the bystander. Compensation after being injured while rescuing is not covered anywhere in the book.

Landing point: Section 13 gets three new entries. New Entry 2 (what to do when someone has fallen, an old person has fallen) is inserted after the cardiac arrest entry, with original Entries 2 to 36 renumbered to 3 to 37; new Entry 38 (encountering a brawl) is inserted after "extorted by a stranger" and before HIV post-exposure prophylaxis, with the original Entry 37 (HIV) renumbered to Entry 39; new Entry 40 (compensation for good Samaritan injuries) is placed at the end. Placed in Section 13 rather than Section 8 because the three entries answer the question of "what to do in those dozens of seconds on scene"; the legal provisions here are the basis for "dare to act," not a standalone topic.

Graded B: The guideline gives a handling protocol without an effect size for "doing this saves more lives"; the "leading cause" line is an epidemiological conclusion, but this entry's recommended actions (ask first, don't move, call 120 on the spot) have no controlled studies. Benefit tier "large" — mortality metric; improper movement points to hard endpoints like worsening cerebral hemorrhage and spinal injury, and the guideline lists falls as the leading cause of injury death for this age group. Cost money=0 time=few willpower=some (overcoming the fear of being shaken down and of delay), synthesized as "high."

Not written in the body: The "Peng Yu case" judgment could not be obtained in original text this round and is not written; local "good Samaritan laws" (such as Shenzhen and Shanghai's pre-hospital emergency medical care regulations) were not verified locality by locality and are not written. The Note's "if someone flags down your car for help in a dark, isolated place at night, you do not need to get out; report the location to 110" is a judgment, not a quotation, so it is not placed in the Benefit field.

Addendum 3 (same day, after the four-tier beneficiary framework was set): The author questioned "is this entry really appropriate for strangers? Wouldn't simply walking away yield higher returns." On review the challenge holds — the original entry treated strangers as the default scenario and pressed the entire "fear of being shaken down" onto Civil Code Article 184, but Article 184 only exempts damage caused during rescue, not the counter-claim "you knocked me down," which goes through the burden-of-proof rules. This round's change: the title subject is changed to one's own elderly family members and acquaintances, with a tail "for a stranger, at minimum do not move them"; the plain-language summary opens by stating the main line; the Note breaks it down by four tiers, stating that walking away from a stranger is legal, that if you do stop, only do the two zero-cost things "do not move them" and "keep your distance, call out, call 120 and leave," and do not carry the person into a car alone or pay medical bills in advance. A new source is added, verified word for word against the Supreme People's Court official site <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/353651.html> (adopted 2014-12-18, first amended 2020-12-23, second amended 2022-03-22, effective 2022-04-10): Interpretation of the Supreme People's Court on the Application of the Civil Procedure Law of the People's Republic of China, Article 90: "A party that asserts facts on which their claims are based or facts on which they rebut the other party's claims shall provide evidence to prove them, except as otherwise provided by law. Before a judgment is rendered, where a party fails to provide evidence or the evidence is insufficient to prove their factual claims, the party bearing the burden of proof shall bear the adverse consequences."

Searched but not found: The Supreme Court's 2020-05-13 top ten typical civil cases promoting core socialist values did not include a case of "helping a fallen elderly person, sued, dismissed for insufficient evidence" (the closest case on the official Q&A page <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/229251.html> is "the case of a child injured by a hit-and-run driver who collapsed and died after being stopped while leaving," with different circumstances); second-hand articles claim it does, not cited. So the body only lands on the Article 90 burden-of-proof rule, without cases.

Addendum 4 (same day): The author again challenged that the "do not move them + call 120 and leave" minimum combination is still negative for strangers, because calling 120 leaves your number and may lead to follow-up; walking away is more cost-effective. On review the algorithm holds; the entry's previous "at minimum do ..." was assigning tasks rather than accounting. This round's change: the title tail is changed from "for a stranger, at minimum do not move them" to "for a stranger, walking away is legal, but if you stop, do not move them"; the Cost field states that calling 120 leaves your number and may lead to callbacks or questioning; the Note admits that walking away is the lowest-cost option and that ordinary people have no duty to rescue, narrowing the only action without a second answer to "if you decide to stop, do not move them," and corrects the prior version — the reason not to move them is medical (worsening cerebral hemorrhage or spinal injury), not legal; rescue-caused damage is already exempted by Civil Code Article 184, and the prior version's "if you touch them, you'll get 'you knocked me down'" turned a real-world probability into a legal consequence.

A caveat that makes "walking away" no longer free of cost is also added, verified word for word against the Supreme People's Procuratorate reproduced Civil Code full text <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>: Article 1198: "Operators and managers of business premises such as hotels, shopping malls, banks, stations, airports, sports stadiums, and entertainment venues, or organizers of mass activities, who fail to fulfill their safety-protection obligations and cause harm to others, shall bear tort liability. Where the harm to another is caused by a third party's act, the third party shall bear tort liability; where the operator, manager, or organizer fails to fulfill their safety-protection obligations, they shall bear the corresponding supplementary liability." That is, if the reader is the operator, manager, or activity organizer at such a venue, walking away carries tort liability risk, unlike a passerby in a different tier.

## Section 13, Entry 38 (Encountering a group brawl)

|---|---|---|

| <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/251611.html> (Fa Fa [2020] No. 31, self-defense guiding opinions) | Yes | Item 5: unlawful infringement "includes both unlawful infringement against oneself and unlawful infringement endangering the state, public interest, or against others"; Item 9: "Where a dispute arises over trivial matters ... where the party at fault strikes first and the means are clearly excessive, or where one party strikes first and continues the infringement despite the other party's efforts to avoid conflict, the responding party's act shall generally be recognized as defensive" |
| <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml> (Public Security Administration Punishment Law 2025 revision, Articles 30 and 51) | Reusing | The figures reuse the verified framework in Section 8, Entry 9: battery against another person, detention of 5 to 10 days with a fine of 500 to 1,000 yuan; group affray as picking quarrels, detention of up to 15 days with a fine of 2,000 yuan |

Graded B: The consequence provisions are hard, but "step back, call the police, do not intervene physically, do not gather to watch" has no official original text; it is derived from the "actively participate" element of affray and the standards for mutual affray recognition, noted in the Note. Benefit tier "medium" — freedom metric; what is mainly avoided is detention and administrative punishment (only reaching "actively participating in affray" triggers criminal liability, but that requires one's own active involvement, not the typical scenario this entry guards against). Cost money=0 time=few willpower=some, synthesized as "ordinary."

Addendum (same day): A reader questioned "why not run, will calling 110 lead to being pulled in for questioning and wasting time?" On review, the time cost was added to the entry; the source adds the Criminal Procedure Law (2018 revision), verified word for word against the Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region People's Procuratorate reproduced full text <https://www.nx.jcy.gov.cn/zwsy/qwfb/202011/t20201119_608817.html>: Article 62: "Anyone who knows the circumstances of a case has a duty to testify. Persons who are physiologically or mentally defective, or who are young and unable to distinguish right from wrong or to correctly express themselves, may not serve as witnesses"; Article 110 paragraph 1: "Any unit or individual that discovers a criminal fact or a criminal suspect has the right and duty to report or accuse to the public security organ, the people's procuratorate, or the people's court"; Article 111 last two sentences: "The public security organ, the people's procuratorate, or the people's court shall ensure the safety of the reporter, accuser, or informant and their close relatives. Where a reporter, accuser, or informant does not wish to disclose their name and the act of reporting, accusation, or informing, it shall be kept confidential for them"; Article 193: "Where a witness, having been notified by the people's court, fails to appear in court without justified reason, the people's court may compel their appearance, except for the spouse, parents, or children of the defendant. Where a witness fails to appear in court without justified reason or refuses to testify after appearing, they shall be reprimanded, and where the circumstances are serious, with the president's approval, detained for up to ten days." Note on article numbers: in the 2012 version, Articles 60, 108, and 188 became Articles 62, 110, and 193 in the 2018 revision; old article numbers still circulate online; the Supreme People's Procuratorate site's (spp.gov.cn) Criminal Procedure Law full-text page returned only the homepage on this fetch, so the procuratorate reproduced page was used instead. The cost label remains "time=few" — the default path is to make a phone call and leave; being called back for a statement is possible but not certain; changing to "medium" would not change the cost-effectiveness tier (benefit medium, cost of 1 or 2 both synthesize to "ordinary"), but the Cost field's wording states that at most half a day may be lost. The Note points out that this entry's benefit to the reader themselves comes from "not intervening and not watching"; calling 110 is altruistic, and the two are not pretended to be the same account.

Addendum 2 (same day): A reader followed up with "if that person on the ground has zero relationship with me, why should I care." This is a challenge to the entry's premise; the handling is not to argue back, but to make the boundary of the self-interested part clear. So the Note adds "not calling the police is not in itself illegal." Verified against the Supreme People's Procuratorate reproduced Criminal Law full text <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml>: the Criminal Law contains no crime punishing ordinary citizens for knowing of a crime and not reporting (only malfeasance provisions for persons with specific duties, which do not apply to ordinary citizens); Article 305, crime of perjury: "In criminal proceedings, where a witness, expert witness, recorder, or translator intentionally makes false testimony, expert opinion, record, or translation on a circumstance that has an important bearing on the case, intending to frame another or conceal criminal evidence, the sentence is fixed-term imprisonment of not more than three years or criminal detention; where the circumstances are serious, fixed-term imprisonment of not less than three years and not more than seven years"; Article 310, crime of harboring or shielding a criminal: "Whoever, knowing that a person is a criminal, provides them with a hiding place or financial support to help them escape, or makes false certifications to shield them, shall be sentenced to fixed-term imprisonment of not more than three years, criminal detention, or surveillance; where the circumstances are serious, fixed-term imprisonment of not less than three years and not more than ten years." Although Criminal Procedure Law Article 110 states "the right and duty to report," there is no penalty provision; the body accordingly states that walking away is a legal option. Benefit tier and metric unchanged: this entry's benefit to the reader under the freedom metric still comes from "not intervening and not watching"; calling 110 is altruistic, already noted in the Note, not merged into the same account.

Not adopted: The Ministry of Public Security official site (mps.gov.cn) tip page on how to handle a brawl was not retrieved this round, so the body does not use the phrase "police reminder." Judicial cases of "intervening in a fight being characterized as mutual affray" did not yield a citable judgment; the entry only states that the determination depends on whether one engaged in physical action, without citing cases.

## Section 13, Entry 40 (Compensation after being injured as a good Samaritan)

| URL | Verified | Original quotation |
|---|---|---|
| <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml> | Yes | Civil Code Article 183: "Where a person suffers harm while protecting another's civil-rights and interests, the tortfeasor shall bear civil liability, and the beneficiary may provide appropriate compensation. Where there is no tortfeasor, the tortfeasor has fled, or the tortfeasor is unable to bear civil liability, and the victim requests compensation, the beneficiary shall provide appropriate compensation." |
| <https://www.gov.cn/zwgk/2012-07/26/content_2191928.htm> (General Office of the State Council forwarding the Ministry of Civil Affairs and others' "Opinions on Strengthening the Protection of the Rights and Interests of Good Samaritans," Guo Ban Fa [2012] No. 39) | Yes | "For good Samaritans injured in the act, medical institutions shall establish a green channel and adhere to the principle of 'treat first, bill later'"; "Medical expenses incurred from emergency treatment shall be borne by the perpetrator or responsible party where one exists; where there is no perpetrator or responsible party, or the perpetrator or responsible party has fled or is unable to bear the cost, the expenses shall be resolved through the basic medical insurance system as required"; "those meeting the conditions for martyr evaluation shall be evaluated as martyrs according to law, and their families shall enjoy the relevant benefits under the Regulations on Commending Martyrs"; "those falling under the circumstances of death in the line of duty shall be compensated according to the relevant provisions of the Regulations on Pension and Preferential Treatment for Military Personnel"; "those falling under the circumstances of being deemed a work injury shall enjoy the lump-sum work-death subsidy and a special survivor subsidy equivalent to 40 months of the person's own wages"; "those meeting the conditions for enjoying work-injury insurance benefits shall have the corresponding benefits implemented in accordance with the Work-Related Injury Insurance Regulation; those who do not shall have their disability level assessed by the civil affairs department and the corresponding benefits implemented"; "priority inclusion in employment assistance, with key support," and public-welfare positions "shall prioritize placement of good Samaritans who meet the conditions"; "priority inclusion in the housing security system, with priority allocation of public rental housing or sale of affordable housing or issuance of housing rental subsidies" |

Graded B: The benefits framework is fixed by national-level documents, but bonus standards and recognition procedures are set by each province's good Samaritan regulations, with figures varying by locality (not verified province by province this round, so the body does not write bonus amounts). Guo Ban Fa No. 39 has no dedicated chapter on the recognition authority; the body only writes "generally apply to the public security organ at the location of the incident or the good Samaritan work organization (foundation)," with a Note noting no province-by-province verification. Benefit tier "large" — money metric; the lump-sum work-death subsidy plus the 40-month-wage survivor special subsidy is in the 10,000-yuan-class and above. Cost money=0 time=medium willpower=no, synthesized as "high."
