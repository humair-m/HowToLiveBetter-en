## Trade-offs

- **The user's "foreigners, big companies get priority handling" was not written in**: This is rumor, with no official-document support; writing it would be passing off hearsay as fact. The body text instead writes "lawful priorities are written openly" and gives cited examples (ER triage by condition severity), plus a sentence on using complaint and supervision channels when facing obvious differential treatment.
- **The 110 dispatch handling rules not cited**: Not collected by the State Council policy document repository (Ministry of Public Security internal normative document); mps.gov.cn consistently returns 521; original not obtainable, so nothing about "police-call priority handling by emergency degree" is written — only the verifiable deadline and paperwork clauses are kept.
- **Criminal cases have no universal "must decide whether to file within X days" deadline**: The procedure provisions say "shall promptly review"; the 7-day, 30-day, 60-day set was not found in current regulations this round, so the body text only states the 30-day (+30-day) for public-security cases and the reconsideration/review deadlines after non-filing — not the criminal-case filing-review deadline.
- **The bribery entry did not write "can the money sent out be recovered"**: It involves the application of Criminal Law Article 64 and related judicial interpretations, not verified item by item this round, not written.
- **Section 24, Entry 12 was revised once**: The first draft only wrote "no need to give red envelopes" and prohibitory clauses, and the user immediately called it cold-hearted — "My dad gave cigarettes to the doctor who resuscitated me — that doctor worked through the night to save me and never slept… so I shouldn't give anything or even say thank you?" This criticism holds: the first draft collapsed "pre-paying for priority" and "after-the-fact wanting to thank the life-saver" into one thing, only prohibiting without giving an outlet. The revised structure first separates the two, then gives three ways of thanking that actually reach the doctor, and explains that the doctor returning the red envelope is protecting themselves, not finding it too little. Two documents were added (see table above).
- **"Pennants and thank-you letters go into the individual's and department's record" written only as experience**: No document prescribing this was found; the Note states it is experience. The only documented basis is that satisfaction-survey results are included in tertiary-public-hospital performance appraisal.
- **Did not cite the 2014 "Doctor-Patient Joint Signing of No-Gift-Acceptance/No-Gift-Giving Agreement" notice**: Not found in the State Council policy document repository by title; current effectiveness could not be verified verbatim; better not to cite.
- **The umbrella entry set C**: The cognitive bias itself has no direct literature; the two regulations cited support only the half-sentence "deadlines and relief are written down"; the Note states this explicitly.
- The three operational entries cross-reference each other; the umbrella entry points to the three operational ones; Section 8, Entry 39 and Section 24, Entry 12 mutually explain that "giving money to case-handling personnel" and "giving red envelopes to doctors" are subject to two different rule sets.
