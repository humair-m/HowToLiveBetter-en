## Grading and selection

- **Graded A**: LEAP is a randomized controlled trial, significant by intention-to-treat analysis, both cohorts significant, numbers verifiable word-for-word.
- **Benefit "large"**: 13.7% down to 1.9%, relative reduction about 86%, far exceeding the mortality metric "≥20% = large" threshold.
- **Cost "money=few" not 0**: requires buying peanut butter (tens of yuan) and a doctor visit beforehand.
- **Three safety boundaries must be written; missing any makes this entry dangerous**:
  1. **Never give whole peanuts**—explicitly stated by the NHFPC, choking hazard. The form may only be thinned peanut butter or peanut powder mixed into complementary food. The note points to Section 13, Entry 28 (someone choking and unable to speak).
  2. **Must have a doctor's assessment first; do not try at home**—LEAP performed a skin test on every child before enrollment, **those with a wheal larger than 4 mm were excluded from the trial**, and the trial never fed peanuts to these children. The "no measurable wheal" and "1 to 4 mm" cohorts in the abstract are the evidence.
  3. **Only for high-risk children** (severe eczema or existing egg allergy), not for all infants.
- **The general-infant side is honestly written as weaker**: the EAT trial is non-significant by ITT (5.6% vs 7.1%, P=0.32), significant only in the per-protocol analysis. The note states "this kind of calculation easily inflates the effect; the authors themselves state ITT failed to prove efficacy", while preserving the authors' own statement that "early introduction is safe".
- **Not marked "disputed"**: LEAP and EAT are not opposing factions; their populations differ (high-risk vs general) and their conclusions are not contradictory. Per the rules, only opposing evidence is marked disputed; here the applicability scope is instead clarified in the note.
- **The time conflict with Section 20, Entry 4 is handled**: LEAP starts at 4 months; China starts complementary food at 6 months. The note states this difference and leaves the choice to the physician, not the reader. Entry 4's note also added a cross-reference.
- Beneficiary: Tier 2 (children).
