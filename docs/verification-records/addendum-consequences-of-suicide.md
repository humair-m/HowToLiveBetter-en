# Addendum: Three Entries on Suicide · Verification Record (2026-09-18)

Task origin: A user asked "should we add something about not committing suicide? It seems we haven't written how serious the consequences of suicide are."

Existing coverage (whole-book search for "suicide"): Section 1, Entry 25 (restricting lethal means + 12356); Section 3, Entry 19 (what to do first when mood is low); Section 8, Entries 13 and 14 (impulses to harm others, near-relative commitment rights); Section 29, Entry 4 (family risk after suicide bereavement); Section 30, Entries 4 and 7 (school bullying, adolescent depression screening); Section 19 (suicide not recognized as a work injury, only one sentence in the Note). So "how to seek help" and "what families should do" are covered, **what's missing is exactly what the user pointed to: what happens if you go through with it** — the time scale of suicidal ideation, the long-term outcome after a survived attempt, what's left after being rescued, and the money side.

Landing point: Section 1 gets Entries 30 and 31 (appended at the end, not inserted after Entry 25, to avoid renumbering Entries 26–29 and editing 6 existing cross-section and verification record references); Section 29 gets Entry 12 (the money side, following "no irreversible decisions during the stress period"). The Section 1, Entry 25 Note and the Section 29 section-opening cross-section introduction get matching pointers added.

Two lines held in writing: **do not compare lethality rates across methods, do not write any specific method.** Entry 31 only uses the two already present in the book (the "pesticide" in Entry 25, the "carbon monoxide" in Section 13, Entry 18), discussing "what's left after rescue," not "which is more effective." ## Section 1, Entry 30 (Time scale of ideation + long-term outcome after attempt)

| URL | Verified | Original quotation |
|---|---|---|
| <https://doi.org/10.4088/JCP.07m03904> | Yes (Europe PMC abstract) | Deisenhammer 2009: "Eighty-two patients referred to a psychiatric university hospital after a suicide attempt were approached within 3 days after the act"; "Nearly half of the patients (47.6%; N = 39) reported that the period between the first current thought of suicide and the actual attempt had lasted 10 minutes or less"; "Those patients in which this process had taken longer showed a higher suicidal intent (p < .001). Graded B: Deisenhammer has only 82 participants, single-center, relying on retrospective self-report; Owens gives median proportions across 90 heterogeneous studies rather than a pooled effect size. The 47.6% figure was already cited in Section 8, Entry 13 (on the side of "impulses to harm others"); this entry is the proper place for the self-harm side, and additionally adds the 76.8% who had some interpersonal contact — it is the direct basis for this entry's action (tell someone around you), and is the line in the original conclusion that points out "professional helpers have limited room for intervention at this stage, so dissemination must reach the general public."

Benefit tier "large": the metric is mortality, but this entry has no relative reduction to apply (it is not an intervention trial); per CLAUDE.md's "use judgment only when figures are insufficient to decide," it is graded large — what it avoids is death itself. Cost money=0 time=few willpower=some: the willpower point is "speaking up at that moment."

## Section 29, Entry 12 (The money side)

| URL | Verified | Original quotation |
|---|---|---|
| <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf7c4060811> | Yes (back-end API downloaded docx and verified word for word; sxx=3 currently effective, amended 2015-04-24) | Insurance Law Article 44: "For contracts that pay insurance benefits upon the death of the insured, if the insured commits suicide within two years of the contract's establishment or restoration of effectiveness, the insurer shall not be liable for the payment of insurance benefits, unless the insured was a person without civil capacity for civil acts at the time of suicide." | <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1778064.htm> | Reusing Section 19's verified source | Work-Related Injury Insurance Regulation Article 16 item (3): "self-injury or suicide" is not recognized as a work injury or deemed a work injury; Article 39: standards for the three work-death benefits (funeral subsidy of 6 months of the previous year's average monthly wage of workers in the pooling region, dependent relative pension, lump-sum work-death subsidy equal to 20 times the previous year's per capita disposable income of urban residents nationwide) |
| <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff808081729d1efe01729d50b5c500bf> | Yes (same as above; sxx=3) | Civil Code Article 1159: "When dividing the estate, taxes and debts that the decedent should lawfully pay shall be paid off; however, necessary inheritance shall be reserved for heirs who lack the ability to work and have no source of income." Article 1161: "Heirs shall pay off the taxes and debts that the decedent should lawfully pay, to the extent of the actual value of the inheritance they receive. For the portion exceeding the actual value of the inheritance, heirs who voluntarily repay are not subject to this limit." "Heirs who disclaim inheritance may not be liable for payment of the taxes and debts that the decedent should lawfully pay." |

Graded A: All three are currently effective statutory provisions verifiable word for word. Benefit tier "large" by the money metric's amount threshold, mechanically applied — life insurance payouts and the lump-sum work-death subsidy are both in the hundreds of thousands of yuan range.

Two deliberate omissions: ① **Accident insurance is not covered.** "Suicide does not count as accidental injury" is contract law reasoning derived from the "unintentional" element, but no verifiable official definition was obtained this round (the regulatory rules for accident insurance are not in the State Council Policy Document Repository), so the body only writes the two paths with statutory backing: life insurance and work injury. ② **"Article 44 only covers the first two years" is written into the Note.** Not writing it would lead the reader to think suicide is never covered, which is wrong; writing it in the body would risk being read as a timetable, so it goes in the Note with the explicit statement "this is to be complete about the provision, not to give a timetable."

## Section 17, Entry 8 (Pressure ulcers from prolonged bed rest)

| URL | Verified | Original quotation |
|---|---|---|
| <https://doi.org/10.5435/JAAOSGlobal-D-22-00117> | Yes (Europe PMC abstract) | Porter et al. 2022, 19,520 hip-fracture patients (NSQIP 2016–2019): "The presence of a PPU was independently associated with a 21% increase in odds of 30-day mortality (odds ratio (OR) = 1.2, P = 0.004)"; "also independently associated with increased odds of DVT (OR = 1.59, P < 0.001), pneumonia (OR = 1.39, P < 0.001), and unplanned hospital readmission (OR = 1.43, P < 0.001) and a significant increase in the mean length of hospital stay of 0.4 days (P = 0.007)" |
| <https://doi.org/10.1007/s41999-025-01331-8> | Yes (Europe PMC abstract) | Ottaviani et al. 2026, 324 hospitalized elderly (mean age 86), median 18-month follow-up: "both frailty (CFS: HR 1.535, 95% CI 1.314-1.793, p < 0.001) and PU (HR 1.500, 95% CI 1.059-2.126, p = 0.020) were significant predictors of long-term mortality" |
| <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0192707> | Yes (Europe PMC abstract) | Shi et al. 2018, network meta-analysis of 65 randomized trials: "There was moderate-certainty evidence that powered active air surfaces and powered hybrid air surfaces probably reduce pressure ulcer incidence compared with standard hospital surfaces (risk ratios (RR) 0.42, 95% confidence intervals (CI) 0.29 to 0.63; 0.22, 0.07 to 0.66, respectively)" |
| <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD009958.pub4> | Yes (Europe PMC abstract; doi.org redirect returned Wiley 403, publisher anti-crawl) | Cochrane 2026 update, 11 trials, 4,462 participants: turning every 2 hours vs every 4 hours "(RR 1.05, 95% CI 0.79 to 1.39; 4 trials, 1104 participants), but we judged the certainty of the evidence as very low"; "One trial evaluated real-time wearable patient sensor data with visual reminders for 2-hourly repositioning compared to standard care... reported a significant reduction in pressure injuries with the visual warnings from the sensors (RR 0.28, 95% CI 0.10 to 0.75). We judged the certainty of this evidence as moderate"; "One trial (116 participants) compared prone versus supine positioning in ICU patients, and found there may be higher pressure injury incidence in the prone group (RR 4.55, 95% CI 2.31 to 8.98)" |

Graded B: The two mortality findings are observational associations (people with pressure ulcers are themselves more frail and sicker), and cannot be read as "preventing pressure ulcers lowers mortality"; the randomized evidence on turning intervals is very low certainty. This entry therefore phrases things as "what matters is whether turning actually happens, not clock-watching the interval" — the moderate-certainty item is precisely "sensor reminders to actually turn" rather than a specific interval.

The air mattress entry's original text compares against the hospital standard support surface; the Note notes that home use is an extrapolation. Body sites to check and "redness that does not fade when pressed" are routine nursing practice, written in the body with a Note noting no separate literature.

Cost money=few (home electric air mattresses start at a few hundred yuan) time=much (every two to three hours, including at night) willpower=yes, benefit=large, cost-effectiveness tier ordinary — the cost is genuinely high; this entry is not a free lunch.
