## III. Metric and benefit magnitude

- The Section 16 long-term prescription is set as "metric=time, benefit=medium": what it buys back is the half-day per month of trips to the hospital, not money; under the time-metric threshold (hours per week classed as medium) it is medium.
- Family doctor contracting is set as "money, medium": what is avoided is the out-of-pocket service package of several hundred to several thousand yuan.
- Eye and foot screening are set as "mortality, large": the endpoints are blindness and amputation, preventable serious health endpoints.
- Long-term care insurance is set as "money, large": payment ratio 50%–70%, annual cap tied to 50% of per-capita disposable income; in the 10,000-yuan class.
- The two Section 14 entries are set as "metric=free" (this book's "free" metric includes personal information), but the three-tier threshold for benefit magnitude is drawn along criminal liability / administrative penalty / civil dispute, which does not fit personal-information entries. Both are set to "medium" by judgment: they change long-term information-exposure surface, neither involving criminal or administrative consequences nor being only a one-off civil dispute. This is the only place this round where the threshold was not applied mechanically.
