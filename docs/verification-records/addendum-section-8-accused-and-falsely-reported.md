# Section 8 Addendum "After Being Accused or Falsely Reported" 5 Entries · Verification Record (2026-09-09)

Task origin: The reader's original words — "Is there anything on avoiding being falsely accused? Many women in China falsely report rape; one man has been locked up for years, is innocent, has evidence, but it didn't help, and the women can falsely accuse with no consequences at all."

## Handling the reader's premises

The reader's question contains three verifiable assertions, handled separately this round:

1. **"Many women falsely report"** — There are no publicly available national statistics on the filing and handling of false accusations. The State Council Policy Document Repository, the Supreme People's Procuratorate, and the Supreme People's Court all lack any false-accusation data broken down by sex or by charge; the rule of this book is that figures must be findable in the original text, so the body does not write incidence rates or sex distributions, and Entry 31's Note explicitly states this. Likewise, the individual case the reader mentioned could not be located in a verifiable official bulletin this round, so the body does not cite cases.
2. **"Having evidence is useless"** — At the institutional level this is clearly written down; what's missing is enforcement, not the provisions: Criminal Procedure Law Article 55's standard for conviction requires exclusion of reasonable doubt, Article 200 item (3) provides for acquittal when evidence is insufficient, Article 56 requires exclusion of confessions obtained by torture, and Articles 252 and 253 provide channels for appeal and retrial. So this lands as Entry 32, with a Note stating "these are the institutional baselines, not result guarantees," and stating that this book has no verifiable official data on the acquittal rate or appeal-reversal rate of Chinese criminal cases, so no proportions are written.
3. **"Falsely accusing has no consequences at all"** — Inaccurate, but the source of the reader's feeling is real. The Public Security Administration Punishment Law (2025 revision) Article 50 lists fabricating facts to falsely accuse as a public security violation; Criminal Law Article 243 is the crime of false accusation and frame-up. But the same Article's third paragraph states "where the accusation is not intentional framing but a mistaken report, or where the report is factually wrong, the preceding two paragraphs do not apply" — pursuing the other party requires proving "fabrication of facts" plus "intentional framing," rather than proving "I did not do it," and the burden of proof for these two things is very different. Entry 31's body writes out the provision and this boundary, neither exaggerating nor denying.

## Landing point

No new section is opened. Section 8's original Entry 5 "When accused or summoned, hire a lawyer first, do not settle privately, do not delete records" already covers the timing of hiring a lawyer, summons duration, the prohibition on compelled self-incrimination, no private settlement, no record deletion, and the existence of the crime of false accusation and frame-up and state compensation. What is missing is the pre-event side and concrete post-event remedies, so per the section's existing practice, 5 entries (Entries 29–33) are appended at the end, and a sentence pointing to Entries 31, 32, and 33 was added to Entry 5's Note.

## Sourcing and pitfalls

- **Public Security Administration Punishment Law 2025 revision**: The State Council Policy Document Repository only collects the 2005 original text (this repository does not collect new versions of laws enacted by the NPC); the Supreme People's Procuratorate law section also does not. Finally obtained the full text reproduced on the Beijing Municipal Public Security Bureau official site (`gaj.beijing.gov.cn`), verifying the revision adoption date, Article 50 original text, and the effective date 2026-01-01 in Article 144. Following this repository's existing convention, the Source field notes "reproduced full text."
- **Criminal Law Article 274**: The Supreme People's Procuratorate version is the 1997 original; this article was amended by Criminal Law Amendment VIII (adding "multiple extortion" and adding the 10-years-and-above tier and fines), so it cannot be copied directly. This entry uses the same integrated text link already used in Section 13's naked-chat extortion entry (reproduced by the Beijing Municipal Public Security Traffic Management Bureau); the amount standards additionally cite the original text of Fa Shi [2013] No. 10 (Supreme Court official site directly reachable).
- **State compensation daily standard**: The Supreme People's Procuratorate May 19, 2026 release page is directly reachable; verified word for word to 495.94 yuan, effective date 2026-05-18, and the calculation basis. This number is updated each May, so Entry 33's Note states "look up the current year's figure when using it; do not directly transplant this entry's number."
- **Why Entry 29 is graded B**: Criminal Law Article 236 only writes the four characters "other means" (其他手段); "unable to effectively consent while drunk or in a state of stupor" is the judicial consensus, but no verifiable currently effective public interpretation was obtained this round (the Supreme Court/Supreme Procuratorate and two ministries interpretation targeting minors does not apply to the adult side), so the inference from statute to specific circumstance is treated as B-grade, with the Note noting this boundary.

## Addendum: Entry 34 and the Guo Li case (same day, reader raised "a man was arrested for seeking compensation for milk powder")

The reader was talking about the Guo Li case (the brand involved is "Shi En," not Sanlu itself; both belong to the 2008 melamine incident). This case exposed a blind spot in this round's Entry 30: Entry 30 writes that "the other side holding leverage to extort money from you is handled as extortion," which, read literally, would suggest "high compensation demand = extortion," whereas the official conclusion in the Guo Li case is the exact opposite. So Entry 34 is added, and the Notes of Entries 30 and 33 are backfilled.

Verified official original text:

- **Guangdong Provincial People's Procuratorate retrial recommendation** (reproduced by Heyuan Municipal People's Procuratorate, `jcy.heyuan.gov.cn/node/142`; the Guangdong Provincial Procuratorate site `gd.jcy.gov.cn` failed TLS certificate verification on this machine, so the reproduced page is cited and the Source field notes this): Guo Li's daughter's examination result showed "several punctate strong echoes visible within the central collecting system of both kidneys"; in June 2009 the two sides reached an agreement under which Shi En Company compensated 400,000 yuan, and Guo Li issued a written statement that he would no longer pursue claims and waived the right to compensation. After that, the company reported the case to police; the Chaozhou District People's Court sentenced him to five years' imprisonment for extortion. The procuratorate's three reasons are quoted word for word into the body: "the existing evidence is insufficient to prove that Guo Li subjectively had the purpose of illegal possession" "he had the right to seek compensation from the milk powder manufacturer, and his compensation-seeking behavior had a legal basis" "consumers have the right to expose and criticize acts that harm their lawful rights and interests through mass media." On April 7, 2017, the Guangdong Provincial High People's Court declared him not guilty on retrial.
- **State compensation decision** (Guangdong Court Network selected judgment documents, Guangdong Provincial High People's Court Compensation Committee (2018) Yue Wei Pei No. 11): The retrial reversal reason, original text: "The original trial found that Guo Li used illegal possession as the purpose, and used threatening and coercive methods to forcibly extort property, but the facts are unclear and the evidence is insufficient; the nature of Guo Li's conduct does not exceed the scope of civil disputes"; he was detained from July 23, 2009 to July 22, 2014, 1,826 days total; personal liberty compensation of 472,733.14 yuan was calculated at the 2016 national average daily wage of workers of 258.89 yuan; mental distress compensation of 165,450 yuan; Guo Li's claimed ten-year mental distress compensation of 88.88 million yuan and other claims were denied in excess, and lost-wage compensation, life-and-health-right compensation, property loss, attorney fees, and detention center living allowances were determined not to fall within the statutory scope of state compensation.

Writing decisions:

- **Entry 34 graded B**, because it is a single case rather than a generalizable proportion. It proves two things at once — that compensation-seeking is lawful, and that one may still be prosecuted — and the body and Note both state this, not writing "acquittal" as a guaranteed outcome.
- **Two details from the reader's account were not written into the body**: One is personal circumstances like "annual salary in the hundreds of thousands, became lame, lost his wife too" — the official documents have no verifiable record of these; the other is the specific sequence of "Sanlu asked him to come over, and he was arrested upon arrival" — the procuratorate bulletin only writes "Guo Li was apprehended after the company reported the case to police," so the body only retains the operational prompt "be careful when the other party asks you to come to their location; reporting and prosecution typically happen at the other party's location," without recounting details.
- **Entry 33's Note adds the actual calculation from this case**, because it illustrates two easily misunderstood things: the daily standard uses the year standard applicable at the time the compensation decision is made (detention ended in 2014, but the 2016 figure of 258.89 yuan was used); and the statutory scope of state compensation does not include lost wages, attorney fees, or property loss.
