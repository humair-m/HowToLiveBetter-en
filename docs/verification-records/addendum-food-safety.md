Two metric corrections (claims in the author's original wording that need correction): ① "Eating food with hair in it can be claimed at ten times with a 1,000-yuan floor" does not hold. The threshold for tenfold compensation is that the food "does not meet food safety standards"; whether a foreign object reaches that threshold usually requires the market supervision department's determination or testing; and the statute explicitly excludes "label and instruction defects that do not affect food safety and will not mislead consumers"; Fa Shi [2024] No. 9 Article 8 also lists specific unsupported defect circumstances. ② Tenfold is not unlimited: for those who knowingly buy fake goods, buy repeatedly, or sue repeatedly, the court only supports claims within the scope of "reasonable living-consumption needs"; for ordinary purchases for eating, ten times is calculated based on the price actually paid, a layer covered by Judicial Interpretation Article 1 paragraph 2. Both points are written into the body's plain-language summary and Note.

## Grading and tier

All four graded A: Sources are current laws, administrative regulations, and Supreme Court judicial interpretations; the conditions, penalty provisions, and amounts all match verbatim.

- Section 5, Entry 31: Money metric, benefit tier "medium" — by the amount threshold, between hundreds and thousands (1,000-yuan floor, with ten times the price depending on unit price). Cost money=0 time=few willpower=some.
- Section 12, Entries 8, 9, 10: Freedom metric, benefit tier all "medium" — by the threshold "avoiding administrative punishment" is the medium tier (operating without a license, no label, and claiming efficacy are all administrative fines without criminal liability). Although the fine amounts reach the 10,000-yuan-class, the metric is set as freedom rather than money by "what is bought back"; the tier is not converted across metrics. Costs are money=few time=medium willpower=no / money=few time=medium willpower=no / money=0 time=few willpower=some, respectively.

Beneficiary: Section 5's entry is the first tier (oneself and family); Section 12's three entries only apply to people running a food business, also the "oneself" tier but with a narrow applicable scope, so they are placed in Section 12 rather than promoted.
