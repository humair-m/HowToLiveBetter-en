# Cross-Reference Table

This file is generated by `node tools/check-refs.mjs`; do not edit by hand.

In the main text, "Entry X" records only the entry number, not the content. Inserting or deleting entries shifts all
subsequent references out of place, and the shifted entry numbers often still fall within range, so simple out-of-bounds
checks cannot catch them. So each reference's **actually pointed-to title** is laid out here and checked into the
repository: after editing entries, regenerate; in `git diff`, any case where the entry number has not changed but the
title has is a reference that has been shifted out of place.

Scan scope: the entry text and section-intro of each section under `book/`, plus the long reads under `docs/`. Long
reads do not contain "this section"; a bare "Entry N" is always treated as a statute article and skipped, so long-read
references must be written out in full as "Section X, Entry Y." In the "Source" column, an entry writes "Entry N," a
section intro writes "Section intro," and a long read writes the nearest subheading.

A second safeguard is the **anchor**: each reference's surrounding context must contain a word that matches the target
entry title (the "medical aid" in "for medical aid, see Entry 11," or written explicitly as "see Entry 16 (IOUs and
guarantees)"). `node tools/check-refs.mjs --check` will fail any bare entry number without an anchor — once such a
reference is shifted out of place, the diff of the cross-reference table will show no anomaly, and only the anchor can
catch it. Range references ("see Section 8, Entries 11 to 14") are an exception: they refer to an entire block of
entries, and an anchor cannot be paired with every entry in the block, so the diff alone must catch them.

Whether an anchor counts is judged by length and distance: three consecutive Han characters in the same sentence
matching the title ("sugary drinks," "resident health insurance") counts, or two Han characters matching within the
comma-delimited clause containing the reference; merely colliding with two common Han characters ("yourself," "company")
outside the clause does not count as an anchor. This tightening was added on 2026-09-20: when an entry was inserted in
Section 31, "for the loan, see Entry 15 of this section" was shifted onto a new entry, "Working remotely from home for
an overseas company... filing your own income tax," where the "you yourself still" separated by two commas posed as an
anchor, and `--check` reported it as passing at the time.
