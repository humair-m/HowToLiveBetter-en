# 30. School-Age Children

Section 20 covers the first year after birth. Section 18 calculates how much money and time raising a child takes. The dozen-plus years from age 3 to 18 are addressed in this section. The entry on age-graded screen management starts from age 0, because the official breakdown is 0–3, 3–6, and primary/secondary school, and splitting them apart would make it harder to use. This section answers: when "don't disrupt school" and "health" conflict, which things cannot wait. These things have time windows — once passed, they cannot be recovered. Whether academics should be that intense is not discussed here; only windows and numbers are given.

The first eight entries and the last two calculate death and lasting physical harm. Entry 9 (do not buy "cure myopia" products) calculates money. Entry 10 (written rules on sleep, homework, physical activity, and ranking) and Entry 11 (leaving school with student status preserved) calculate time. For drowning, traffic, helmets, and HPV vaccine, see Section 1. For infants and toddlers, see Section 20. For child scams and in-game purchases, see Section 5. For red lines minors themselves should not cross, see Section 9. For how to recognize emergencies and call transport, see Section 13. For children after a parent's death, see Section 29. None of these are repeated here.

### 1. If a child reports sudden severe pain that keeps getting worse, go to the hospital immediately — do not have them wait until after school
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. Take half a day off and bring the child to the emergency department.
- Plain-language summary: Some conditions are counted in hours. A boy with sudden severe pain on one side of the scrotum has testicular torsion — the blood vessel attached to the testicle has twisted and the blood supply is cut off. If treated within 6 hours, 97.2% of testicles are saved. Delayed to 19 to 24 hours, only 42.5% remain. This kind of pain cannot wait until after school, nor until after an exam.
- Benefit: A systematic review pooled 30 case series, 2,116 testicular torsion patients. Of these, 1,283 were tabulated in 6-hour bins. From onset of pain, the testicular salvage rate at 0 to 6 hours was 97.2%. At 7 to 12 hours, 79.3%. At 13 to 18 hours, 61.3%. At 19 to 24 hours, 42.5%. At 25 to 48 hours, 24.4%. Beyond 48 hours, 7.4%. Aggregated into larger blocks: within 12 hours 90.4%, 13 to 24 hours 54.0%, beyond 24 hours 18.1%. The authors' conclusion is that the salvageable window may be longer than the commonly cited 6 to 8 hours. So even if pain has persisted for some time, treatment should still be sought — do not skip it because "it is already too late."
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Mellick LB, et al. (2019). A Systematic Review of Testicle Survival Time After a Torsion Event. Pediatric Emergency Care. <https://doi.org/10.1097/PEC.0000000000001287>
- Note: Tell boys in advance: if sudden severe pain occurs down there, speak up immediately — do not be embarrassed. Testicular torsion is most common in adolescent boys, and many tough it out because of embarrassment. Other conditions counted in hours include acute appendicitis (delayed cases perforate), diabetic ketoacidosis, and meningitis. This section has not separately verified "how many hours of delay causes how much harm" for each of these. Their shared signals are: sudden onset, progressive worsening, unlike any previous episode. For how to recognize emergencies and call transport, see Section 13.

### 2. Do not postpone needed treatment to "wait until after exams"; some windows follow bone age, not the exam schedule
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free of charge; the only decision is to start early or late. Leave from school and follow-up visits take time; the cost of the treatment itself is separate. The hard part is that bracing requires at least 18 hours a day of wear, and the child must endure it throughout.
- Plain-language summary: Scoliosis is sideways curvature of the spine. A brace is a hard-shell vest worn on the body to support the spine. Worn while bones are still growing, 75% of children do not progress to the point of needing surgery; with observation alone, only 42%. The window of usability follows the completion of bone growth, not the middle-school or college entrance exam schedule; waiting until after the exam often leaves surgery as the only option.
- Benefit: A multi-hospital trial enrolled 242 adolescents with idiopathic scoliosis who met the criteria for bracing. Of these, 116 were randomized, and 126 chose whether to wear the brace. Success/failure was prespecified: progression to 50 degrees or more counted as treatment failure; remaining below this threshold at skeletal maturity counted as success. Analysis was by intention-to-treat (by the group initially assigned, regardless of actual brace wear). The randomized bracing group's success rate was 75%, versus 42% for the randomized observation group (OR 4.11, 95% CI 1.85–9.16), meaning the chance of success was about 4 times that of observation alone. Pooling the randomized and self-selected groups, the rates were 72% vs 48% (after smoothing out the two groups' inherent differences, OR 1.93, 1.08–3.46, still about 1.9 times). More hours of daily wear meant higher success rate (P<0.001, this relationship is unlikely to be chance). The trial was stopped early because bracing was indeed effective.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Weinstein SL, et al. (2013). Effects of bracing in adolescents with idiopathic scoliosis. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1307337>
- Note: Scoliosis is used as the example here because it has a randomized trial, a clear time window, and happens to be one of the focal points of student health checkups (see Entry 7). Others in the same category include strabismus, amblyopia, dental caries, and congenital heart conditions where the timing of surgery is elective. Their common feature is that the later the treatment, the worse the effect or the higher the cost. Ask a specialist about the specific window. If worried about missing class, in compulsory education a leave of absence can be taken, for up to 1 year, with student status preserved — see Entry 11.

### 3. If a child is bullied, report it to the school that day and demand written handling; for hitting, taking money, or spreading rumors, call the police directly
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. Speak to the homeroom teacher, and to the school's student bullying governance body. To report to police, call 110. The hard part is withstanding advice to "not make a big deal of it."
- Plain-language summary: Children bullied by peers are about 2.2 times more likely to report suicidal thoughts than those not bullied, and about 2.6 times more likely to have attempted suicide. Schools must investigate upon report and discipline if bullying is confirmed; serious cases must be reported to police and cannot be concealed. So report that day, demand the recognition process, and keep written records.
- Benefit: 34 studies, 284,375 children and adolescents pooled. Being bullied by peers is associated with suicidal thoughts, OR 2.23 (95% CI 2.10–2.37), the chance of suicidal thoughts about 2.2 times that of non-bullied children. Another 9 studies, 70,102 people, found bullying associated with suicide attempts, OR 2.55 (1.95–3.34), about 2.6 times. Results were the same regardless of sex, age, and study quality. Cyberbullying showed a stronger association with suicidal thoughts than offline bullying. Article 21 of the Provisions on School Protection of Minors lists five categories of behavior: beating, kicking, slapping, biting, pushing, pulling; insulting, mocking, giving humiliating nicknames; seizing or extorting property, or deliberately destroying property; malicious exclusion or isolation; spreading fabricated facts or rumors online to defame. The article also provides that where the party with advantages in age, physical strength, or numbers deliberately or maliciously engages in these acts, causing physical injury, property loss, or mental harm, it can be recognized as bullying. Article 22 requires teaching staff who notice abnormal student mood or physical injury to promptly inquire and report. Article 23 requires the school to investigate immediately upon receiving a report, refer it to the bullying governance body for recognition and handling, and notify parents to participate. Where bullying is confirmed, educational discipline or a disciplinary action must be imposed. For serious bullying that violates public security management or constitutes a suspected crime, concealment is prohibited, and the case shall be promptly reported to the public security organ and the education administrative department (national, effective September 1, 2021).
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: van Geel M, et al. (2014). Relationship between peer victimization, cyberbullying, and suicide in children and adolescents: a meta-analysis. JAMA Pediatrics. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2013.4143>; Ministry of Education (2021). Provisions on School Protection of Minors (Ministry of Education Order No. 50, Articles 21, 22, 23). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-06/02/content_5614946.htm>
- Note: When a child has unexplained injuries, suddenly refuses to attend school, or repeatedly loses belongings, first ask along the lines of the signals in Article 22. This pooled analysis of multiple studies is observational and has no control group, so it cannot prove bullying alone causes suicide — but the direction and magnitude of the risk are clear. The Comprehensive Management Plan for Primary and Secondary School Bullying additionally requires that bullying be strictly distinguished from rough play among students. Schools therefore have an obligation to make a determination — they cannot brush it off as "kids playing around."

### 4. Have your child spend 2 hours outdoors every day — currently the only myopia prevention method backed by a randomized trial
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. But daily time must be set aside, mostly squeezed out of homework and online class time. The hard part is doing it every day, not just a few times.
- Plain-language summary: First-graders given an additional 40-minute outdoor class each day had new myopia drop from about 40% to about 30% over three years. The NHC's recommended amount is at least 2 hours outdoors during the daytime each day, or 14 hours per week combined. Cloudy days count; standing outside during recess counts.
- Benefit: First-graders at 12 Guangzhou primary schools were randomized by school (intervention schools 952 students, control schools 951, mean age 6.6). The intervention was the addition of 1 40-minute outdoor activity class per school day, with parents encouraged to take children outdoors more outside class. New myopia over 3 years: intervention group 30.4%, 259 cases among 853 eligible; control group 39.5%, 287 cases among 726 eligible. The difference was −9.1 percentage points (95% CI −14.1 to −4.1, P<0.001), with the true difference roughly between 4 and 14 fewer percentage points. Spherical equivalent change over 3 years was −1.42 D vs −1.59 D, a difference of 0.17 D (P=0.04). The intervention group's progression was slightly less. D is the unit of refractive power. Axial length (front-to-back length of the eye) growth showed no difference between the groups (P=0.07). The Ten Core Knowledge Points for Myopia Prevention and Control in Children and Adolescents requires that children and adolescents engage in no less than 2 hours of outdoor activity during the day each day, or 14 hours per week cumulatively. It notes that outdoor activity has a protective effect even on cloudy days, and recommends avoiding the high-heat, high-UV afternoon period.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: He M, et al. (2015). Effect of Time Spent Outdoors at School on the Development of Myopia Among Children in China: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2015.10803>; General Office of the National Health Commission (2023). Ten Core Knowledge Points for Myopia Prevention and Control in Children and Adolescents (Guo Wei Ban You Er Han [2023] No. 278). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm>
- Note: This trial ran for only 3 years, and the participating children started at age 6. The 9.1 percentage point difference in myopia rates can only spare some from myopia — it does not guarantee no myopia. The same Ten Core Knowledge Points also provides several practices. For reading and writing, "one foot, one fist, one inch" (distance to book, body to desk, fingers to pen tip). After 20 minutes of close work, look into the distance for at least 20 seconds. For primary and secondary school students using electronic screens for non-learning purposes, no more than 15 minutes per session, and no more than 1 hour total per day. "Being outdoors" and "having PE class" are not the same thing — standing outside during recess counts.

### 5. No screens for ages 0 to 3; minimize for ages 3 to 6; for primary and secondary school students, no more than 1 hour per day of non-learning use
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. The hard part is taking the screen out of the child's hands, and the parent modeling less screen use in front of the child.
- Plain-language summary: The NHC requires no screens for ages 0 to 3, minimal exposure for ages 3 to 6, and for primary and secondary school students, no more than 15 minutes per session and no more than 1 hour per day of non-learning screen use. The reason is that prolonged close eye use brings myopia earlier. The evidence on screens and myopia is weaker; what really has shown effect in trials is 2 hours outdoors per day.
- Benefit: Article 4 of the Ten Core Knowledge Points states: "Infants and toddlers aged 0–3 should not use screen-based electronic products such as mobile phones, tablets, or computers; children aged 3–6 should尽量避免 their exposure to and use of screen-based electronic products such as mobile phones, tablets, or computers; for primary and secondary school students using electronic screens for non-learning purposes, single sessions should not exceed 15 minutes, and cumulative daily use should not exceed 1 hour." The same article notes that "prolonged close use of screen-based products easily depletes children's hyperopia reserve and is an important cause of early and high incidence of myopia in children and adolescents." The 2018 implementation plan from the Ministry of Education and seven other departments gives parents the same requirement, with an added note: "the younger the age, the shorter the continuous use of electronic products should be." The research evidence is much weaker: a systematic review included 33 studies, 11 of which entered the meta-analysis, with subjects ranging from 3 months to 33 years. Counting only mobile phones and tablets, the odds ratio for myopia was 1.26 (95% CI 1.00 to 1.60, I²=77%). Combined with computers, 1.77 (1.28 to 2.45, I²=87%). The authors' conclusion states only "possibly associated," and notes that none of the 33 studies had a reliable measurement of screen time.
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: General Office of the National Health Commission (2023). Ten Core Knowledge Points for Myopia Prevention and Control in Children and Adolescents (Guo Wei Ban You Er Han [2023] No. 278). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm> ; Ministry of Education and seven other departments (2018). Implementation Plan for Comprehensive Prevention and Control of Myopia in Children and Adolescents (Jiao Ti Yi [2018] No. 3). <http://www.moe.gov.cn/srcsite/A17/moe_943/s3285/201808/t20180830_346672.html> ; Foreman J, Salim AT, Praveen A, et al. (2021). Association between digital smart device use and myopia: a systematic review and meta-analysis. The Lancet Digital Health, 3(12), e806-e818. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2589-7500(21)00135-7> ; Madigan S, McArthur BA, Anhorn C, Eirich R, Christakis DA (2020). Associations between screen use and child language skills: a systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA Pediatrics, 174(7), 665-675. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2020.0327>
- Note: Rated B because the official guidance is a tiered regulation, not a number derived from trials. The lower bound of the odds ratio in the research sits exactly at 1.00, and the authors state only "possibly associated." So treat it as a household rule that is easy to follow; do not treat it as "no screens, no myopia." What is backed by a randomized trial is Entry 4 (2 hours outdoors). Preschoolers also need eye checks: the same document specifies that children aged 1–3, 4–6, and after age 7 should all receive regular refractive screening to track the hyperopia reserve — see Entry 12 (cycloplegic refraction) for the method. The relationship between screens and child language skills has also been meta-analyzed: 42 studies, 18,905 people — more screen time means weaker language skills (r=−0.14, 95% CI −0.18 to −0.10). But co-viewing and watching educational content were positively correlated (r=0.16 and 0.13). This is association, not causation, and only shows that replacing conversation with the child with a screen is not a worthwhile trade.

### 6. Treat myopia as lasting damage: the higher the prescription, the more retinal detachment, maculopathy, and glaucoma later
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. Nothing to do here on its own; what to do is in Entries 4 and 5 (2 hours outdoors, tiered screen management).
- Plain-language summary: Myopia does not heal itself, and the prescription does not go back down. It is also not just "wear glasses." Compared with non-myopes, the risk of myopic maculopathy is more than ten times higher in mild myopia, and several hundred times higher above 600 degrees (−6.00 D). Retinal detachment is about 3 times higher in mild myopia and about 13 times higher above 600 degrees. So the earlier a child becomes myopic and the faster the prescription rises, the greater the future risk.
- Benefit: A systematic review and meta-analysis pooled studies up to June 2019, stratified into three tiers by prescription: mild (−0.5 to −3.00 D), moderate (−3.00 to −6.00 D), and high (≤−6.00 D, above 600 degrees). Compared with non-myopes, the odds ratios for myopic maculopathy were 13.57 (95% CI 6.18 to 29.79), 72.74 (33.18 to 159.48), and 845.08 (230.05 to 3104.34). For retinal detachment: 3.15 (1.92 to 5.17), 8.74 (7.28 to 10.50), and 12.62 (6.65 to 23.94). For posterior subcapsular cataract: 1.56 (1.32 to 1.84), 2.55 (1.98 to 3.28), and 4.55 (2.66 to 7.75). For open-angle glaucoma: 1.59 (1.33 to 1.91) in mild, and 2.92 (1.89 to 4.52) combining moderate and high. For visual impairment after age 60: 1.71 (1.07 to 2.74), 5.54 (3.12 to 9.85), and 87.63 (34.50 to 222.58). The Ten Core Knowledge Points states the same: above 600 degrees is high myopia, and in this population the incidence of blinding eye diseases such as cataract, open-angle glaucoma, myopic maculopathy, retinal detachment, and myopic optic neuropathy is markedly higher than in other groups, and "myopia is preventable, controllable, and irreversible."
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Haarman AEG, Enthoven CA, Tideman JWL, Tedja MS, Verhoeven VJM, Klaver CCW (2020). The complications of myopia: a review and meta-analysis. Investigative Ophthalmology & Visual Science, 61(4), 49. <https://doi.org/10.1167/iovs.61.4.49> ; General Office of the National Health Commission (2023). Ten Core Knowledge Points for Myopia Prevention and Control in Children and Adolescents (Guo Wei Ban You Er Han [2023] No. 278). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm>
- Note: All studies included are observational, and the odds ratios are unusually large. For high myopia and maculopathy, the confidence interval runs from 230 to 3,104, indicating that the underlying studies differ widely — the figure can only be read by order of magnitude, not as a precise value. Most of these complications manifest in middle to old age. Myopia itself is not a disability, and the vast majority of people have normal vision after correction with glasses. But myopia is not just "a pair of glasses" — it is worth the effort to delay and control it. For follow-up after diagnosis, see Entry 12 (cycloplegic refraction). For not buying products claiming to cure, see Entry 9 (curing myopia).

### 7. Read the annual student checkup report yourself; for abnormal items, take the child to a hospital that year for examination
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. During compulsory education, the cost of the checkup is covered by the school's operating funds; parents do not pay extra.
- Plain-language summary: Primary and secondary schools organize a student health checkup once a year. The key items on the report — overweight, obesity, malnutrition, abnormal spinal curvature, poor vision, and dental caries — are the most easily overlooked. Do not just glance at height and weight and put the report away. If any of these are abnormal, take the child to a specialist that year to clarify.
- Benefit: The Management Measures for Primary and Secondary School Student Health Checkups (2021 Edition) require that primary and secondary schools organize one in-school health checkup per year. Surgical examination covers head, neck, chest, spine, limbs, skin, and lymph nodes. Eye examination covers external appearance, distance vision, and refractive power (degree of myopia or hyperopia). The individual report should include the findings, an overall assessment, and health guidance recommendations. Overweight, obesity, malnutrition, abnormal spinal curvature, poor vision, and dental caries (cavities) must be the focus of guidance. The education administrative department shall establish files for students with health problems and follow up (record and track later). During compulsory education, the cost of student health checkups is paid from the school's operating funds; parents do not pay extra (national, issued September 2021).
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: National Health Commission and Ministry of Education (2021). Management Measures for Primary and Secondary School Student Health Checkups (2021 Edition) (Guo Wei Yi Fa [2021] No. 29). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-10/21/content_5644100.htm>
- Note: Rated B because this is a document regulating how schools act, not a study of outcomes. It guarantees "annual checkups, of these items, follow-up on abnormalities," but does not guarantee that what is found will be acted on. The checkup is only a first screen, not a diagnosis. Poor vision requires cycloplegic refraction at ophthalmology (Entry 12). Abnormal spinal curvature requires orthopedics or spine surgery (Entry 2 — do not delay treatment to wait for exams). Both steps require the parent to take the child themselves.

### 8. Children aged 12 to 18 should receive a depression screening; do not treat the school's psychological assessment as a diagnosis
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0 to a few dozen yuan, completing a screening questionnaire at a community clinic or hospital outpatient department. Calls to the 12355 and 12356 hotlines are free.
- Plain-language summary: US preventive guidelines recommend screening adolescents aged 12 to 18 for major depression; for those 11 and under, evidence is insufficient. Suicide is the second leading cause of death for ages 10 to 19. The school's annual psychological assessment is only a survey, not a diagnosis. If a child says they feel bad, or if eating, sleeping, interests, or grades suddenly change, go directly to psychiatry or clinical psychology.
- Benefit: The US Preventive Services Task Force issued a recommendation statement in 2022. It recommends screening adolescents aged 12 to 18 for major depressive disorder (Grade B recommendation). Grade B means moderate certainty, with moderate net benefit — worth doing. For children 11 and under, evidence is insufficient to screen for major depression (I statement, that is, evidence is insufficient to make a recommendation). For dedicated screening of suicide risk in children and adolescents, evidence on both benefits and harms is insufficient (I statement). The document notes that suicide is the second leading cause of death in those aged 10 to 19. It also notes that major depression in children and adolescents is strongly associated with many later problems. These include recurrent depression in adulthood, other mental disorders, and elevated risks of suicidal thoughts, suicide attempts, and death by suicide. The Ten Measures to Further Strengthen Mental Health Work in Primary and Secondary School Students requires building a three-tier service system of homeroom teachers, full- and part-time mental health teachers, and psychiatrists. Each student receives a psychological assessment once per academic year, generally no more than once. The document also requires advancing referral-to-treatment mechanisms for mental illness and post-recovery return-to-school mechanisms. It calls for leveraging the 12355 youth service hotline and the 12356 psychological assistance hotline (national, issued October 2025).
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: US Preventive Services Task Force (2022). Screening for Depression and Suicide Risk in Children and Adolescents: US Preventive Services Task Force Recommendation Statement. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.16946>; General Office of the Ministry of Education (2025). Ten Measures to Further Strengthen Mental Health Work in Primary and Secondary School Students (Jiao Ji Ting [2025] No. 2). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202510/content_7045528.htm>
- Note: Rated B because the original gives a grade of recommendation strength, not a quantifiable effect. It is also a US recommendation; China has no corresponding official screening recommendation. A positive screening result only means further assessment is needed — it is not a diagnosis. For what to do when suicidal thoughts appear, see Section 1, Entry 25. For how the household should respond, see Section 29.

### 9. Do not buy products and services that claim to "cure myopia" or "reduce prescription"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free of charge, conversely saving this money.
- Plain-language summary: The State Administration for Market Regulation states plainly that "under current medical technology, myopia cannot be cured." Marketing claims of "recovery," "reducing prescription," "curing myopia," or "prescription repair" will be strictly prosecuted. When you see such words, exclude the product outright — no need to compare prices. Eye-protection devices are medical devices, and e-commerce platforms are responsible for verifying sellers' qualifications.
- Benefit: The State Administration for Market Regulation's November 2021 to March 2022 special enforcement action notice states clearly: "Under current medical technology, myopia cannot be cured." The notice requires strict prosecution, in accordance with the law, of marketing practices for children's and adolescents' myopia prevention and control products that use misleading terms such as "rehabilitation," "recovery," "reducing prescription," "myopia cure," "myopia nemesis," and "prescription repair." The notice calls for strengthened enforcement against practices that falsely use the name of traditional Chinese medicine or falsely invoke TCM theory and techniques to deceive consumers. The notice also requires strict action against two categories of false and illegal advertising: using patient names or images for before-and-after treatment comparisons, and making safety or efficacy guarantee commitments. The notice requires e-commerce platforms to strictly carry out qualification review for eye-protection devices and other medical devices and related special goods (verifying whether sellers are qualified) (national).
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: General Office of the State Administration for Market Regulation (2021). Notice on Conducting a Special Enforcement Action Against Illegal Commercial Marketing of Myopia Prevention and Control Products for Children and Adolescents. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-11/12/content_5650400.htm>
- Note: Rated B because the basis is the regulator's prosecutorial position, not a controlled trial of the products. But the statement "myopia cannot be cured" is consistent across the health authority and the regulator: the Ten Core Knowledge Points states "myopia is preventable, controllable, and irreversible." The two things with evidence are Entry 4 (outdoors) and Entry 12 (standardized refraction, glasses, and follow-up). For blue-light glasses, see Section 6.

### 10. Sleep, homework, physical activity, and ranking all have written rules; if the school does not meet them, you can raise the issue
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. Knowing the regulations is enough; if necessary, raise the issue with the school or the local education administrative department. The hard part is speaking up.
- Plain-language summary: The documents specify: primary school students should sleep 10 hours per day, middle school 9 hours, high school 8 hours. Primary school morning classes should generally not start before 8:20, and secondary school before 8:00. In 2025, a new rule requires at least 2 hours of physical activity per day and prohibits ranking by test scores. If a child is chronically sleep-deprived, first check against these rules — do not first blame the child for dawdling.
- Benefit: The Notice on Further Strengthening Sleep Management for Primary and Secondary School Students specifies: primary school students should sleep 10 hours per day, middle school students 9, high school students 8. Primary school morning classes generally no earlier than 8:20; secondary school generally no earlier than 8:00. Schools may not require students to arrive early for unified educational and instructional activities. Primary school students' bedtime generally no later than 21:20; middle school no later than 22:00; high school no later than 23:00. Off-campus training institutions may not end training later than 20:30; live online training may not end later than 21:00. From 22:00 to 8:00 daily, gaming services may not be provided to minors (national, issued March 2021). The Ten Measures to Further Strengthen Mental Health Work in Primary and Secondary School Students adds several provisions: comprehensive daily physical activity for primary and secondary school students no less than 2 hours; encouraging "15-minute recess." It also prohibits ranking students by test scores, strictly prohibits mechanical, repetitive, or punitive homework, and encourages setting one day per week as a "no homework day" (national, issued October 2025).
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: General Office of the Ministry of Education (2021). Notice on Further Strengthening Sleep Management for Primary and Secondary School Students (Jiao Ji Ting Han [2021] No. 11). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-04/02/content_5597443.htm>; General Office of the Ministry of Education (2025). Ten Measures to Further Strengthen Mental Health Work in Primary and Secondary School Students (Jiao Ji Ting [2025] No. 2). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202510/content_7045528.htm>
- Note: Both are documents issued by the competent authority. Execution varies widely across regions and schools, so this entry is rated B. Their use is to let you know what the school should already be doing, so that you have grounds when raising the issue. For adults' sleep ledger, see Sections 2 and 3.

### 11. If a child cannot hold on, they can take a leave of absence; the school must preserve their student status for up to 1 year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. Parents or other legal guardians apply to the school. The hard part is first accepting that the child needs to pause.
- Plain-language summary: Leave of absence has a formal system. A parent or other guardian applies, the school confirms and forwards to the higher authority for approval. One application is for up to 1 year; if more is needed, a new application must be made. During the leave, the school preserves the student's status; on return, they resume studies. Specific procedures are set by each province — ask the school's academic affairs office or the local education bureau.
- Benefit: Article 16 of the Management Measures for Primary and Secondary School Student Status governs leaves of absence. A student's leave is applied for by parents or other legal guardians. The school confirms and forwards to the higher-level student-status authority for approval. On return, the school handles the relevant procedures promptly. A single leave may not exceed 1 year; if more is genuinely needed, a new application must be made. During the leave, the school preserves the student's status (the student's identity and slot are retained). Specific procedures for leave and return are formulated by the provincial education administrative department (national, issued January 2025). The Ten Measures to Further Strengthen Mental Health Work in Primary and Secondary School Students requires advancing mechanisms for referral-to-treatment for mental illness and post-recovery return to school, and instructs schools to develop plans for handling campus psychological crisis incidents.

- Note: Before applying, ask three questions: what medical certificate is required, how the leave will be recorded in the system, and whether return will be to the original grade or one grade lower. Rated B because the conditions, documents, and return procedures are set by each provincial education department; the national level provides only a framework. The Provisions on School Protection of Minors also requires compulsory-education schools to register dropouts, leaves, and long absences — so leave is something schools have processed before, not a special exception for you alone.

### 12. If poor vision is detected, go to a hospital for cycloplegic refraction, then follow up at the interval the doctor gives
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A few dozen to a few hundred yuan for examination and glasses. After cycloplegia, the child will be sensitive to light and have blurred near vision for half a day to a day or two.
- Plain-language summary: Cycloplegic refraction is first applying eye drops that relax the muscle responsible for focusing the eye, then measuring the prescription. This is done at a hospital and is the most accurate method for diagnosing myopia. The NHC states plainly that these drops do not harm a healthy eye and that vision returns to normal after discontinuation. Do not treat a single auto-refraction at a glasses store as a diagnosis, and do not refuse to get the child glasses for fear the prescription will increase.
- Benefit: The Ten Core Knowledge Points explains cycloplegic refraction. First, a cycloplegic agent (a drop that relaxes the focusing muscle of the eye) is applied, then refraction is performed. Cycloplegic refraction is medical refraction performed at a hospital, the "gold standard" (most accurate method) for diagnosing myopia. Cycloplegic drugs do not damage a healthy eye; transient photophobia and blurred near vision may occur after use, and vision returns to normal after discontinuation. Once myopia is diagnosed, timely scientific intervention should begin, including correction with glasses. Properly fitted glasses can effectively correct blurred vision and slow myopia progression. Those with normal vision while wearing glasses: preschool and primary school children should be rechecked every 3 to 6 months, middle and high school students every 6 to 12 months, with replacement decided based on the specific situation. Children should receive regular refractive screening (prescription check) at ages 1–3, 4–6, and after age 7, monitoring the hyperopia reserve (national, issued July 2023).
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: General Office of the National Health Commission (2023). Ten Core Knowledge Points for Myopia Prevention and Control in Children and Adolescents (Guo Wei Ban You Er Han [2023] No. 278). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm>
- Note: Rated B because this is the practice given by an official science-education document, not original research with specific numbers. High myopia can lead to problems such as retinal detachment, so follow-up is not only for replacing glasses. "Poor vision" found in a student checkup is only a screening result; a complete ophthalmology examination at a hospital is still needed — see Entry 7.

### 13. After the permanent molars erupt, get pit and fissure sealants
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A few dozen to a hundred or two hundred yuan per tooth. No anesthesia, no drilling, a few minutes per session. Many regions have free programs for children of eligible age.
- Plain-language summary: Pit and fissure sealants fill the grooves on the chewing surfaces of the back teeth with resin, so food debris cannot get stuck. After two years, the chance of cavities is about one-eighth that of unsealed teeth. Without sealants, 16% of tooth surfaces develop cavities; with sealants, only about 5%. Still effective at four years.
- Benefit: 38 trials, 7,924 children aged 5 to 16, pooled by Cochrane according to standard methods. Resin-based sealants compared with no sealant. For first permanent molars in children aged 5 to 10, the odds ratio for cavities at 24 months after sealing was OR 0.12 (95% CI 0.08–0.19), the chance of cavities about one-eighth that of no sealant. This result is from 7 trials, 1,548 randomized, 1,322 ultimately examined; evidence quality moderate. If the control group's 24-month cavity rate is 16%, the sealed group's rate is 5.2% (3.13%–7.37%). At a control rate of 40%, 6.25% (3.84%–9.63%). At 70%, 19% (12.3%–27.2%). At 48 to 54 months after sealing, OR 0.21 (0.16–0.28), still about one-fifth that of no sealant. The authors conclude that resin sealants produced 11% to 51% fewer cavities than no sealant at 24 months. Across 4 trials that recorded adverse events, none occurred. Evidence is insufficient to determine whether glass ionomer or other sealant materials are superior.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Ahovuo-Saloranta A, et al. (2017). Pit and fissure sealants for preventing dental decay in permanent teeth. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD001830.pub5>
- Note: The first permanent molar (the back tooth that does not fall out) typically erupts around age 6; the second around age 12. Sealants must be applied when the tooth has just erupted and is not yet decayed; once decayed, only a filling is possible. Many regions list pit and fissure sealants for age-eligible children as a free program; ask the local Center for Disease Control and Prevention or community health service center. This section did not find a verifiable national-level document, so coverage and age ranges are not given. Dental caries is also a focus of student checkup guidance — see Entry 7.
