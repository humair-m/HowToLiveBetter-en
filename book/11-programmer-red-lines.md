# 11. Red Lines Programmers and Tech Workers Easily Cross

This section's metric is personal freedom and money — what it returns is "don't get locked up" and "don't pay money out". People who write code and do tech often treat the things below as "just technical issues", but they correspond to specific crimes in the Criminal Law, or to specific sums payable in civil damages. Each entry gives the numbers from the statute, plus cases published on the official websites of the Supreme People's Procuratorate and the Supreme People's Court. The Criminal Law cited is the consolidated text "revised according to Criminal Law Amendment (XI)" reposted by the Beijing Municipal Public Security Traffic Management Bureau. Entries are ordered from highest to lowest by "tech workers most likely to think it's no big deal × heaviest consequence". Entry 1 is a general one: how to judge before acting, and what to do first after a problem arises.

### 1. Before acting, ask three questions: whose interests are harmed, how capable the other party is of pursuing it, how much evidence I left behind; once pursued, get a criminal lawyer immediately
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=some benefit=large metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Before acting, replace "can it be done technically" with another question: will the vendor, platform, or company file a police report. The hard part is resisting the "this little thing, no one cares" thought, because server logs, payment records, and chat records are all pulled out by default during case handling.
- Plain-language summary: Before acting, ask one question: will the vendor, platform, or company file a police report. Server logs, payment records, and chat records can all be pulled during case handling. If you are criminally detained, the decision on whether to formally arrest is made within 37 days at the longest. From the first interrogation you can retain a lawyer; these 37 days are when a criminal lawyer is most useful.
- Benefit: Any unit or individual who discovers a criminal fact has the right to file a report. If the investigating organ believes a criminal fact exists, it must file the case. After criminal detention, public security must submit a request for approval of arrest (applying to the procuratorate for formal arrest) within 3 days. Under special circumstances it can be extended by 1 to 4 days. For itinerant crime (committing crimes across locations) and similar situations, it can be extended to 30 days. The procuratorate then takes 7 days to decide. In total, the decision on whether to arrest is made within 37 days at the longest. From the first interrogation, or from the moment coercive measures are taken (detention, arrest, etc.), you may retain a lawyer. While the case is still in the public-security investigation stage, only a lawyer may be retained. When the lawyer requests a meeting, the detention center must arrange it within 48 hours at the latest. Those who may be sentenced to public surveillance or criminal detention (lighter than prison), or only to supplementary penalties with no principal penalty, can be released on guarantee pending trial: paying a bond or finding a guarantor and going home to wait. Release on guarantee lasts up to 12 months (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: C
- Source: National People's Congress (2018). Criminal Procedure Law (2018 revision, Articles 34, 39, 67, 79, 91, 110, 112). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/zdgz/201810/t20181027_396818.shtml>
- Note: These 37 days count from detention to the procuratorate's decision on whether to approve arrest — they are not the upper limit of custody. After arrest approval, there is also the custody period during investigation, which is added on. A competent lawyer is most useful in exactly these 37 days: meeting the person, submitting an opinion to the procuratorate that arrest should not be approved, and applying for release on guarantee pending trial. Waiting until after prosecution to find a lawyer is too late. The remaining entries in this section are all about the same thing: whether the other party has reason to file a report.

### 2. Don't make or sell game cheats and assistants, including "screen-read-only, no-memory-touch" AI assistants
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=some benefit=large metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. The price is giving up the income from such orders and "little tools". The hard part is that this money comes in fast and you can't see who would come after you.
- Plain-language summary: Selling a cheat to 20 users, or earning 5000 yuan, is enough for up to 3 years; in especially serious cases, 3 to 7 years. Someone made wallhack and aimbot cheats, sold over 80,000 yuan in 3 months, and was sentenced to 1 year with 1 year probation and a 5000 yuan fine. Someone took server-side code to run a private server, took in over 20 million yuan in recharge, and the heaviest sentence was 4 years with fines up to 10 million yuan.
- Benefit: Providing programs or tools specifically for invading or illegally controlling computer information systems, in serious circumstances, is punished with up to 3 years and a concurrent or sole fine. "Concurrent" means money is fined alongside the sentence; "sole" means only money is fined. Especially serious circumstances carry 3 to 7 years and a fine. The threshold for "serious circumstances" is providing tools to 20 or more users, or illegal gains of 5000 yuan or more. Illegal gains refers to money earned from this activity. Intentionally circumventing or destroying technical protection measures set by the rights holder may also constitute the crime of copyright infringement, up to 3 years; in serious cases, 3 to 10 years. Official cases: Wang and others made and sold cheats that read and modified game memory to enable wallhack and aimbot. In 3 months they sold over 80,000 yuan and profited over 30,000 yuan. Sentenced to 1 year with 1 year probation and a 5000 yuan fine. "1 year probation" means sentenced to 1 year but not incarcerated first; if no new offense is committed during this year, no prison time is served. Xiang and others sold "TZ"-series cheats with sales of over 200,000 yuan; Xiang was sentenced to 3 years with 3 years probation and a 20,000 yuan fine. Liu and others obtained the game's server-side program, modified parameters to run a private server, and took in over 20 million yuan in recharge. Sentenced to 4 years down to 2 years 6 months, with fines from 10 million yuan down to 1.3 million yuan (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law (revised according to Criminal Law Amendment (XI), Article 285 paragraph 3, Article 217 item 6). <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>; Supreme People's Court, Supreme People's Procuratorate (2011). Interpretation on Several Issues Concerning the Application of Law in Criminal Cases Endangering Computer Information System Security (Article 3). <https://ga.sz.gov.cn/ZWGK/ZCFG/ZCJD/content/post_1304363.html> (reposted by Shenzhen Municipal Public Security Bureau); Supreme People's Procuratorate (2026). Shanghai Jing'an: severely punishing the cheat-making black-market industry to jointly protect a clean cyberspace. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/zdgz/202608/t20260825_735480.shtml>; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2021). 2020 typical cases of procuratorial organs protecting intellectual property (Case 5). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/xwfbh/wsfbt/202104/t20210425_516525.shtml>
- Note: The published cheat cases all involve reading and writing game memory or bypassing anti-cheat. "Screenshot-only, key-simulation-only" assistants have no published official cases yet. But the ticket-snatching case in Entry 3 shows that not touching memory and only sending requests can still be convicted as the crime of illegally obtaining data. Whether it constitutes a crime and which crime it is are determined by the investigating authority based on the materials the vendor submitted when filing the report — not by the author. Selling to 20 users is enough for "serious circumstances"; this is a small number.

### 3. Don't write or sell ticket-snatching, flash-sale, click-farming, or wool-pulling scripts, even if they only "automate button clicks"
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=none benefit=large metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free, takes no time.
- Plain-language summary: A script that does not modify memory or crack anything, but only sends requests faster than a human, can still be convicted; earning 5000 yuan or causing the other party a loss of 10,000 yuan is enough. Someone used purchased 12306 accounts to grab over 200 tickets and earned over 20,000 yuan; sentenced to 1 year 6 months with 1 year 6 months probation and a 5000 yuan fine. Selling ticket-snatching software on an online store was held to constitute unfair competition and was ordered to pay 20,000 yuan in damages.
- Benefit: Invading a computer information system, or obtaining data inside by other technical means, in serious circumstances is punished with up to 3 years with a concurrent or sole fine; in especially serious cases, 3 to 7 years. The threshold for "serious circumstances" is illegal gains of 5000 yuan or more, or causing a loss of 10,000 yuan or more. Official cases: Someone used a cheat to bulk-import purchased 12306 accounts and submitted purchase requests intensively across multiple threads. They grabbed over 200 train tickets and profited over 20,000 yuan. The court convicted them of the crime of illegally obtaining computer information system data, sentencing them to 1 year 6 months with 1 year 6 months probation and a 5000 yuan fine. Zheng sold ticket-snatching software for a ticketing app on an online store and was held to constitute unfair competition, ordered to pay 20,000 yuan in damages (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law (revised according to Criminal Law Amendment (XI), Article 285 paragraph 2). <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>; Supreme People's Court, Supreme People's Procuratorate (2011). Interpretation on Several Issues Concerning the Application of Law in Criminal Cases Endangering Computer Information System Security (Article 1). <https://ga.sz.gov.cn/ZWGK/ZCFG/ZCJD/content/post_1304363.html>; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2026). Shanghai Jing'an: handling a case of using cheat software to bulk-grab train tickets for profit. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/dfjcdt/202607/t20260707_731426.shtml>; Supreme People's Court (2025). 2024 People's Court typical intellectual-property cases (Case 7, ticket-snatching software unfair-competition case). <https://enipc.court.gov.cn/zh-cn/news/view-4205.html>
- Note: The script in the ticket-snatching case did not modify memory or crack anything; it was only "faster than a human", and was still convicted under Criminal Law Article 285. Both taking money to grab tickets and selling scripts count as profit-seeking. For personal one-time use, no official cases were found.

### 4. Crawlers should only crawl public pages that require no login; do not bypass anti-crawl, do not touch personal information, do not sell crawled data
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=some benefit=large metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. A crawler is a program that automatically pages through websites and scrapes data off them. Before crawling, look at the robots file and the interface authorization description; robots is the file a site places in its root directory specifying which pages may not be scraped. Data that requires login, a token (a temporary access credential string), or signature cracking to obtain — do not crawl. The hard part is that such data is technically easy to grab; restraint depends entirely on self-discipline.
- Plain-language summary: Data that requires login, a token, or signature cracking to obtain constitutes bypassing protection — up to 3 years; in serious cases, 3 to 7 years. Crawled personal information is even more trouble. 50 records of whereabouts, communications, credit, or property data already counts as "serious circumstances". 500 records of accommodation, health, or transaction data, or 5000 records of other categories, or earning 5000 yuan, also count.
- Benefit: Bypassing protection to obtain data from a system carries the same sentencing as Entry 3 (ticket-snatching, flash-sale, click-farming scripts): up to 3 years, or 3 to 7 years. Illegally obtaining, selling, or providing citizens' personal information reaching any of the following constitutes "serious circumstances": 50 or more records of whereabouts, communication content, credit, or property information; 500 or more records of accommodation, communication records, health, or transaction information; 5000 or more records of other personal information; illegal gains of 5000 yuan or more. Serious circumstances carry up to 3 years with a concurrent or sole fine; especially serious, 3 to 7 years. Official cases: Software developer Li disguised a feature that stole phone-album contents as a "face-rating detection" app, obtained 1751 face photos, and also resold social-engineering-database materials; sentenced to 3 years with 3 years probation. A social-engineering database (社工库) is a database that aggregates personal information leaked from various sources. A network-technology company used a crawler to obtain a food-delivery platform's store and order data, causing over 40,000 yuan in losses, and was prosecuted for the crime of illegally obtaining computer information system data (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law (revised according to Criminal Law Amendment (XI), Article 285 paragraph 2, Article 253-1). <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>; Supreme People's Court, Supreme People's Procuratorate (2017). Interpretation on Several Issues Concerning the Application of Law in Criminal Cases of Infringing Citizens' Personal Information (Articles 3, 4, 5). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/201705/t20170509_190088.shtml>; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2022). Typical cases of procuratorial organs lawfully punishing crimes of infringing citizens' personal information (Case 2). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/xwfbh/wsfbt/202212/t20221207_594915.shtml>; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2025). Procuratorial organs lawfully punishing crimes against enterprise data security, serving and safeguarding enterprise innovation and development (Case 1). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbh/202501/t20250124_680670.shtml>
- Note: The 2017 judicial interpretation provides that "providing lawfully collected information to others without the consent of the data subject" also counts as "providing". A company exporting its user list to an external partner falls under this same sentence. 5000 records is just the size of a small table.

### 5. Don't take development contracts for gambling, fraud, or pornography apps; don't build payment channels or unblock accounts for "clients"
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=some benefit=large metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. The price is giving up outsourcing work with high prices and unclear requirements. The hard part is that the money offered is often a big step above normal rates.
- Plain-language summary: These high-priced, vaguely specified contracts, once accepted, make you an accomplice of the gambling gang. Convicted as running a casino: up to 5 years; in serious cases, 5 to 10 years — heavier than the maximum 3 years for merely "helping". An esports-betting app had 28 coders sentenced to 1 year to 3 years 6 months. Someone unblocking QQ accounts for a fraud gang was sentenced to 1 year 6 months with a 20,000 yuan fine.
- Benefit: Knowing that another is using the network to commit crimes, and providing technical support, payment settlement, or similar assistance, in serious circumstances is punished with up to 3 years and a concurrent or sole fine. Where another crime is concurrently constituted, the heavier punishment applies. Running a casino: up to 5 years with a fine; serious circumstances, 5 to 10 years. Official cases: The Zhang Sheng group developed gambling games and then "packaged and exported" them, collecting site-building and reporting fees. The case involved nearly 7 billion yuan. Zhang Sheng was sentenced to 9 years 9 months with a 50 million yuan fine. Wang Qiang and 78 others responsible for outsourced development were sentenced to between 6 months and 9 years 9 months. Chen Dong and others developed and operated an esports-betting app, with the tech team writing code in Hainan; 28 people were convicted of running a casino, sentenced from 1 year to 3 years 6 months with fines. Zhang ran a studio unblocking QQ accounts for a fraud gang and was sentenced to 1 year 6 months with a 20,000 yuan fine (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law (revised according to Criminal Law Amendment (XI), Article 287-2, Article 303 paragraph 2). <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2023). Building gambling websites and selling them, involving nearly 7 billion yuan. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/zdgz/202307/t20230711_621248.shtml>; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2022). Shanghai Minhang District: handling a new type of online casino case. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/zdgz/202208/t20220805_569952.shtml>; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2025). Typical cases of lawfully punishing the crime of assisting information-network criminal activities and related crimes (Case 1). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/202507/t20250728_702377.shtml>
- Note: "Knowing" does not require the other party to state the purpose. A price clearly above market, a feature set of recharge-withdraw-and-odds, and a client who won't disclose the purpose — all are used during case handling to infer that you knew. Tech personnel developing gambling apps are charged as accomplices in running a casino, not capped at the 3-year maximum for the crime of assisting information-network criminal activities. That crime is abbreviated as "帮信罪" (the crime of assisting information-network criminal activities).

### 6. When leaving, hand over account permissions; don't drop the database, don't leave backdoors, don't lock the system by changing passwords — even if the company owes you money
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=some benefit=large metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. If the company owes you wages, recover them through labor arbitration; don't take matters into your own hands (labor arbitration is in Section 7). The hard part is holding back from touching the system in the heat of anger.
- Plain-language summary: The account password was given by the company; that does not mean you can still use it after leaving. Logging in counts as illegal control, up to 3 years. Deleting data to make the system unusable: up to 5 years; in especially serious cases, 5 years or more. A senior engineer who was fired used his former administrator account to log into the company's server and delete data and logs, causing over 120,000 yuan in losses; sentenced to 3 years with 5 years probation and a 30,000 yuan fine.
- Benefit: Illegal control of a computer information system, in serious circumstances: up to 3 years with a concurrent or sole fine; especially serious, 3 to 7 years. Deleting or modifying system functions or data, causing the system to be unable to operate normally, with serious consequences: up to 5 years; especially serious, 5 years or more. Official cases: Senior IT engineer Lu, after being fired, used his former administrator account to log into the company's shared server. He changed the password and deleted data and logs, causing over 120,000 yuan in losses. Sentenced to 3 years with 5 years probation and a 30,000 yuan fine. Software-company employee Bai deliberately inserted erroneous code into an upgrade program, causing clients' systems to be unable to log in, with losses over 40,000 yuan; prosecuted for the crime of destroying computer information systems (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law (revised according to Criminal Law Amendment (XI), Article 285 paragraph 2, Article 286). <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2024). Procuratorial organs' "lawfully punishing cybercrime, supporting comprehensive governance of cyberspace" typical cases (Case 6). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbh/202402/t20240223_644505.shtml>; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2025). Procuratorial organs lawfully punishing crimes against enterprise data security, serving and safeguarding enterprise innovation and development (Case 7). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbh/202501/t20250124_680670.shtml>
- Note: The account password was given by the company; that does not mean you can still use it after leaving. Logging in with the former account counts as "illegal control". Losses are calculated by data-recovery costs; tens of thousands of yuan is enough for a case to be filed.

### 7. Don't take source code, customer lists, or technical documents with you when leaving; don't upload them to personal cloud storage; don't reuse them at the next job
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=some benefit=large metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Before leaving, delete all company materials from your personal computer and cloud storage. The hard part is that some files were written line by line by yourself and are hard to part with.
- Plain-language summary: Code you wrote yourself has copyright that belongs to the company; customer lists also count as trade secrets. Taking them means taking the company's property. A former employee who took the source code of a security-screening system to a new company was sentenced to 5 years and fined 500,000 yuan; the new company was fined 4 million yuan. Someone exploited a system vulnerability to download over 33,000 design files and was sentenced to 1 year 10 months. The company can also sue you separately for damages.
- Benefit: Violating confidentiality obligations, disclosing trade secrets or using them yourself, in serious circumstances: up to 3 years with a concurrent or sole fine; especially serious, 3 to 10 years with a fine. The Anti-Unfair Competition Law also lists two acts as infringement: obtaining trade secrets by "electronic intrusion" (technical means of intruding into a system); instigating or helping others to violate confidentiality obligations. Official cases: Sun and other former employees took the source code and core algorithms of a security-screening system and used them in the new company's products; Sun was sentenced to 5 years and fined 500,000 yuan, and the new company was fined 4 million yuan. Tian exploited a system vulnerability to download over 33,000 design files and was sentenced to 1 year 10 months with a 100,000 yuan fine. Engineer Yang stored an app module's source code in his personal computer and the cloud, then moved to a competitor to build a similar app; prosecuted for the crime of trade-secret infringement (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A

Taking it means taking the company's property. Customer lists are operating information and are also trade secrets. ### 8. Don't run your own programs on other people's computers, servers, or cameras; don't use company machines to mine cryptocurrency
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=none benefit=large metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free, takes no time.
- Plain-language summary: Controlling someone else's computer, server, or camera without permission reaches 20 units for up to 3 years; in especially serious cases, 3 to 7 years. If it does not reach the criminal threshold, detention up to 5 days, with possible additional fines of 50,000 to 500,000 yuan. Anyone who has received criminal punishment is permanently barred from key positions in cybersecurity management and network operations.
- Benefit: Illegal control of a computer information system, in serious circumstances: up to 3 years with a concurrent or sole fine; especially serious, 3 to 7 years. Illegal control of 20 or more units counts as "serious circumstances". Cases not reaching the criminal threshold: public security confiscates illegal gains, detains up to 5 days, and may impose a fine of 50,000 to 500,000 yuan. More serious circumstances: detention 5 to 15 days, with possible fine of 100,000 to 1,000,000 yuan. Those who have received administrative penalties within 5 years, or criminal punishment at any time, are barred from key positions in cybersecurity management and network operations. Official cases: Li bought the IPs of network cameras and used software to control 105 cameras for peeping; sentenced to 3 years with 5 years probation and a 20,000 yuan fine. Wu and others implanted Trojans into phone motherboards to control over 3.3 million phones to relay verification codes; sentenced from 2 years to 4 years 6 months (nationwide)

### 9. Don't test other people's systems without written authorization; "good intentions" and "after-the-fact reporting" are not grounds for non-prosecution
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=some benefit=large metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Before participating in a vendor's SRC or crowdsourced testing, first obtain a document specifying the scope of authorization, the target list, and the testing time window. SRC (Security Response Center) is the vendor's own security emergency response center, specifically for receiving vulnerabilities reported from outside. Stop as soon as the vulnerability is verified to exist. The hard part is forgoing the "just try it on the side" attempt.
- Plain-language summary: Whether you are convicted depends on whether there was authorization and how much you obtained, not on your intent. Obtaining 500 sets of ordinary account passwords, or earning 5000 yuan, or causing the other party a loss of 10,000 yuan, is enough for up to 3 years. To prove a vulnerability, pulling just one user table often crosses the 500-set threshold at once. With account credentials in hand, using them outside the authorized scope also counts as intrusion.
- Benefit: Systems of ordinary companies and individuals are computer information systems outside the areas of state affairs, national defense, and cutting-edge science and technology. Intruding into such systems, or using other technical means to obtain data stored, processed, or transmitted inside, in serious circumstances: up to 3 years of fixed-term imprisonment or criminal detention, with a concurrent or sole fine. Especially serious: 3 to 7 years with a fine. "Serious circumstances" has several criteria: obtaining 10 or more sets of identity authentication information (account passwords, etc.) for online financial services such as payment settlement, securities trading, or futures trading; obtaining 500 or more sets of other identity authentication information; illegally controlling 20 or more computer information systems; illegal gains of 5000 yuan or more, or causing economic losses of 10,000 yuan or more. Five or more times these numbers constitutes "especially serious circumstances". Official case: Procuratorial Guiding Case No. 36 established the proposition that "using an account and password beyond the scope of authorization to log into a computer information system constitutes intrusion into a computer information system". That means having account credentials in hand but using them outside the authorized scope itself counts as intrusion. In that case, three individuals used accounts, passwords, and tokens they had obtained through work to remotely log into the company's internal systems, downloaded data outside their work scope, and sold it online, with illegal gains of 37,000 yuan. The three were respectively sentenced to 4 years, 3 years 9 months, and 4 years, each fined 40,000 yuan (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law (revised according to Criminal Law Amendment (XI), Article 285 paragraph 1, paragraph 2). <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>; Supreme People's Court, Supreme People's Procuratorate (2011). Interpretation on Several Issues Concerning the Application of Law in Criminal Cases Endangering Computer Information System Security (Article 1). <https://ga.sz.gov.cn/ZWGK/ZCFG/ZCJD/content/post_1304363.html> (reposted by Shenzhen Municipal Public Security Bureau); Supreme People's Procuratorate (2017). Ninth batch of guiding cases (Procuratorial Guiding Case No. 36, Wei Menglong, Gong Xu, Xue Dongdong illegal acquisition of computer information system data case). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/jczdal/201710/t20171017_202593.shtml>
- Note: Procuratorial Guiding Case No. 36 was itself a case of selling data for profit. It is cited only for the proposition that "logging in beyond the scope of authorization also counts as intrusion" — not to suggest that well-intentioned testing gets sentenced as heavily. What you intended and whether you reported afterward cannot cancel the charge; they can only affect how heavy the sentence is or whether prosecution is brought. A vendor thanking you first and then filing a report later are not contradictory; thanks do not equal authorization. At the time of writing, no verbatim-verifiable well-intentioned-testing cases were found on the Supreme People's Court or Supreme People's Procuratorate websites, so only the statute and the punishment standards are written. If you want to do this, use the vendor's public SRC, or commission testing with a written contract that specifies scope, targets, and time window. Use the minimum evidence to prove the vulnerability exists; do not extract data in bulk. Cases that do not reach the criminal threshold still incur administrative penalties, plus an industry ban; the fine amounts and years are the same as Entry 8 (don't run your own programs on other people's machines). For how to handle a vulnerability after discovery, see Entry 10 (report vulnerabilities as required).

### 10. Report vulnerabilities as required; do not publish details, exploit tools, or hand them to overseas parties before they are patched
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=none benefit=medium metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Report the vulnerability to the product provider, or to an official platform. Before the vendor patches it, do not publish details and do not publish verification code.
- Plain-language summary: Individuals are also governed by the Network Product Security Vulnerability Management Provisions. Before the vendor releases a patch, you may not publish the vulnerability, nor may you publish details of vulnerabilities in systems currently in use. You may not release programs or tools specifically for exploiting the vulnerability; when publishing, you must simultaneously explain how to fix it. Unpublished vulnerabilities may not be given to overseas organizations or individuals outside the vendor.
- Benefit: These provisions govern three categories of targets: network product providers within China, network operators, and organizations or individuals engaged in vulnerability discovery, collection, release, and other activities. No organization or individual may use vulnerabilities to engage in activities endangering cybersecurity, nor may they illegally collect, sell, or publish vulnerability information. Publishing vulnerability information to the public must follow five rules. First, it must not be published before the product provider releases remedial measures. Second, it must not publish details of vulnerabilities in networks, information systems, and equipment currently in use by network operators. Third, it must not deliberately exaggerate harm and risk, nor use vulnerability information for malicious hype or fraud. Fourth, it must not publish or provide programs or tools specifically for exploiting vulnerabilities to engage in activities endangering cybersecurity. Fifth, when publishing, remedial or preventive measures must be published simultaneously. Additionally, unpublished vulnerability information may not be provided to overseas organizations or individuals other than the product provider. The provisions encourage reporting vulnerabilities to four official platforms: (1) the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology cybersecurity threat and vulnerability information sharing platform; (2) the National Network and Information Security Information Notification Center vulnerability platform; (3) the National Computer Network Emergency Response Technical Coordination Center (CNCERT) vulnerability platform; (4) the China Information Technology Security Evaluation Center vulnerability database. Anyone who collects or publishes vulnerability information in violation of these provisions is handled by the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology and the Ministry of Public Security according to their respective duties. Where the circumstances constitute those specified in the Cybersecurity Law, punishment is imposed under that law. Publishing network security information such as system vulnerabilities to the public must comply with relevant national provisions. Violators are ordered to correct by the relevant competent department, given a warning, and may be fined between 10,000 and 100,000 yuan. Those who refuse to correct or whose circumstances are serious are fined between 100,000 and 1,000,000 yuan. They may also be ordered to suspend relevant business, undergo business rectification, or close the website or application. Relevant business licenses may also be revoked, or the business license revoked. The persons directly in charge and other persons directly responsible are fined between 10,000 and 100,000 yuan (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, State Internet Information Office, Ministry of Public Security (2021). Network Product Security Vulnerability Management Provisions (MIIT Lian Wang An [2021] No. 66, Articles 2, 4, 9, 10, 14; in force September 1, 2021). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2021/content_5641351.htm>; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2025). Cybersecurity Law (2025 revision, in force January 1, 2026, Articles 28, 65; the 2016 text had Articles 26, 62; Article 14 of the Provisions cites the article numbers of the 2016 text). <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm>
- Note: The provisions explicitly include individuals in the governed targets; "I'm just an amateur hobbyist" is not an exception. Submitting domestic-system vulnerabilities to overseas bug-bounty platforms falls squarely under "unpublished vulnerabilities may not be provided overseas". Whether you have qualification to test is in Entry 9 (don't test other people's systems without written authorization); that is separate from how to handle a vulnerability after obtaining it. A lawfully discovered vulnerability can still be penalized for improper release; a vulnerability discovered without authorization has problems on both the testing and release sides. Among the reporting channels, the MIIT platform and CNCERT are used most often. The provisions do not specifically address "the vendor was notified but won't patch"; in practice, document the negotiation and reporting process, and report to the official platform at the same time.

### 11. Don't sell wall-climbing tools or VPN accounts; don't set up such nodes for others
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=none benefit=large metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free, takes no time.
- Plain-language summary: Self-provisioning a line out of the country, or using international channels other than the state's public channel, can be ordered by public security to stop networking, given a warning, and fined up to 15,000 yuan with confiscation of illegal gains. Operating this as a business can be treated by the investigating authority as the crime of illegal business operations; in serious circumstances, up to 5 years. This entry covers selling and setting up nodes. For personal use, the maximum penalty is warning plus fine.
- Benefit: Direct international networking must use the international gateway channel provided by the state public telecommunications network. No unit or individual may establish or use other channels: neither self-provisioning a line out of the country nor using channels established by others. Violators are ordered by public security to stop networking, given a warning, and may be fined up to 15,000 yuan concurrently, with confiscation of illegal gains. Operating this as a business can be treated by the investigating authority as the crime of illegal business operations. In serious circumstances, up to 5 years with a concurrent or sole fine of 1 to 5 times the illegal gains. Especially serious: 5 years or more (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: State Council (1997). Interim Provisions on the Administration of International Networking of Computer Information Networks (Articles 6, 14). <https://nyncw.cq.gov.cn/wsdw/zz/zhfb/wlyxxaq/202406/t20240619_13306645.html> (reposted by Chongqing Municipal Agriculture and Rural Affairs Committee); National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law (revised according to Criminal Law Amendment (XI), Article 225 item 4). <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>
- Note: At the time of writing, no published wall-climbing judgments were found on the Supreme People's Court or Supreme People's Procuratorate websites, and local handling varies considerably, so this entry is graded B. For personal use, the maximum penalty is the warning plus fine written in Article 14 of the Interim Provisions.

### 12. If you signed a non-compete agreement and the company doesn't pay compensation monthly after you leave, send a written demand; if no payment for 3 months you can terminate; for positions that never touched trade secrets, you may request that the clause be ruled invalid
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=none benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. When leaving, retain the original non-compete agreement and evidence that the company did not pay monthly.
- Plain-language summary: Non-compete restriction (竞业限制) means that for a period after leaving, you may not work for a competitor, up to 2 years at most, and the company must pay compensation monthly. If no amount is specified, the rate is 30% of the average salary over the 12 months before leaving, no less than the local minimum wage. If the company is at fault for 3 months of non-payment, you can request termination. From 2025 onward, for positions that never knew or touched trade secrets, you may request that the clause be ruled invalid.
- Benefit: Non-compete restrictions may only bind senior executives, senior technical personnel, and others with confidentiality obligations. The maximum term is 2 years. The company must pay financial compensation monthly during this period. If no compensation amount was specified, the rate is 30% of the average salary over the 12 months before leaving, and no less than the local minimum wage. If the company itself is at fault for 3 months of non-payment, the worker may request termination of the non-compete restriction. If the company voluntarily terminates, it must pay an additional 3 months of compensation. From 2025 onward, a worker whose position never knew or touched trade secrets may request that the clause be ruled invalid. Where the agreed scope exceeds the portion of secrets the worker actually knew, the excess is invalid. Official case: A pharmaceutical company sued its chief technology officer Zheng, claiming 7.1 million yuan in liquidated damages. The court found that his new employer's product was not substitutable with the original company's; the non-compete scope should be limited to the secrets he actually knew, and the claim was dismissed in full (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2012). Labor Contract Law (2012 revision, Articles 23, 24). <https://yjglj.fushun.gov.cn/008/20260622/d846d3c2-df3d-4c4c-af16-898ede43e1ee.html> (reposted by Fushun Municipal Emergency Management Bureau); Supreme People's Court (2020). Interpretation (I) on Several Issues Concerning the Application of Law in the Trial of Labor Dispute Cases (Articles 36–40). <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/282121.html>; Supreme People's Court (2025). Interpretation (II) on Several Issues Concerning the Application of Law in the Trial of Labor Dispute Cases (Fa Shi [2025] No. 12, Articles 13–15). <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/472691.html>; Supreme People's Court (2025). Typical labor dispute cases (Case 4). <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/472681.html>
- Note: Conversely, if the agreement is valid and the company has paid monthly, and you go to work for a competitor anyway, you must pay the agreed liquidated damages. After paying, you may still be required to serve out the remaining non-compete period. Non-compete agreed during employment is binding even without separate payment.

### 13. Code written during working hours using company resources belongs to the company; do personal open-source projects on your own time and equipment; don't mix in company code
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=some benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. When joining, read the intellectual-property clause in the labor contract carefully. The hard part is that personal projects and company work are often mixed on the same computer and must be separated.
- Plain-language summary: Software primarily created using the company's equipment, intranet, or business data, and for which the company bears responsibility, has copyright belonging to the company; you retain only the right of authorship. The same applies if the contract so specifies. Even code written outside working hours, if it used company equipment or business data, may also belong to the company. Inventions made using company resources have patents that also belong to the company.
- Benefit: Works created to fulfill a unit's work tasks are service works (things produced during work). Under general circumstances, the copyright belongs to the author, the unit has the right of first use within its business scope, and for 2 years the author may not license a third party to use the work in the same manner. But two categories are different. One is engineering designs, software, and similar works mainly created using the unit's material and technical conditions and for which the unit bears responsibility. The other is works that the contract specifies belong to the unit. For these two categories, the author retains only the right of authorship. The unit's material and technical conditions refer to the company's equipment, intranet, business data, and so on. Inventions and creations made in the execution of the unit's tasks, or mainly using the unit's material and technical conditions, are service inventions; the right to apply for a patent and the patent right obtained both belong to the unit (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2020). Copyright Law (2020 revision, Article 18). <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202011/t20201119_308796.html>; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2020). Patent Law (2020 revision, Article 6). <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202011/t20201119_308800.html>
- Note: Software falls directly into the "copyright belongs to the unit" category; programmers can hardly fit into the "copyright belongs to the author, unit has first use for 2 years" tier. "Written off-hours", if it used a company computer, intranet, or business data, may also be deemed to have been made mainly using the unit's equipment and materials. To keep personal projects clean, use your own machine, do not touch the company's repository, and do not overlap with the company's business.

### 14. When using GPL and other open-source code in a product, follow the license by open-sourcing or changing the license; don't act like "nobody cares"
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=some benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. Before using someone else's open-source code, look at its license (the usage conditions stated by the author). The GPL family requires products that use it to also release their source code, which conflicts with closed-source commercial sale; if conflict, switch. The hard part is giving up a library you've gotten used to.
- Plain-language summary: Copyright infringement is compensated by the other party's loss or your gains; intentional and serious circumstances can bring 1 to 5 times damages. A company hired the opponent's former employees and produced substantially similar software, arguing that the opponent's software used GPLv2 and should have been open-sourced. The Supreme People's Court held that whether the opponent open-sources does not affect its copyright, and ordered cessation of infringement and 500,000 yuan in damages.
- Benefit: Copyright infringement is compensated by the rights holder's actual loss, or by the infringer's gains. Where neither can be clearly computed, the amount is referenced to what would normally be paid to license that authorization. Intentional infringement with serious circumstances can bring 1 to 5 times damages. Where the public interest is harmed, illegal gains may also be confiscated and a fine imposed. Official case: (2021) Supreme People's Court Zhi Min Zhong No. 51. The defendant hired the plaintiff's former employees and developed gateway software substantially similar to the plaintiff's. The defendant argued that the plaintiff's software was based on GPLv2 and should have been open-sourced. The Supreme People's Court held that "whether the developer itself violates the GPLv2 agreement and whether it enjoys the software copyright are two independent issues", and ordered cessation of infringement and 500,000 yuan in damages (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2020). Copyright Law (2020 revision, Articles 53, 54). <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202011/t20201119_308796.html>; Supreme People's Court Intellectual Property Tribunal (2022). "Open-source software" copyright infringement case ((2021) Supreme People's Court Zhi Min Zhong No. 51). <https://ipc.court.gov.cn/zh-cn/news/view-3042.html>
- Note: This case shows that "the other party used GPL code" cannot serve as a shield for copying them. Conversely, GPL authors suing non-open-source commercial parties have effective judgments in China; this section did not find the original text on the Supreme People's Court website, so it is not cited. The reason for grade B is that compliance with open-source licenses can only be governed by general contract and copyright law; there is no dedicated statute.

### 15. Apps should collect only the personal information necessary for the business; those under 14 require guardian consent; write the privacy policy clearly
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=some benefit=medium metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Before launch, match the app's requested permissions list against the privacy policy; cut any permissions that are not needed. The hard part is that the product side always wants to retain more "data that might be useful later".
- Plain-language summary: Collect only information necessary for the business; those under 14 require guardian consent. Violations are first ordered to be corrected, and the app may be suspended. Refusal to correct brings a fine up to 1,000,000 yuan, and the person in charge is fined 10,000 to 100,000 yuan. Serious cases bring fines up to 50,000,000 yuan or 5% of the previous year's turnover, and the person in charge is fined 100,000 to 1,000,000 yuan; coders and product staff may also count as persons in charge.
- Benefit: Processing personal information must have a clear and reasonable purpose, be directly related to that purpose, and adopt the means with the least impact on personal rights. Processing personal information requires the individual's consent, or a statutory circumstance. For those under 14, the consent of a parent or guardian must be obtained. Violations are handled by the supervisory department ordering correction, giving a warning, confiscating illegal gains, and ordering the violating app to suspend or terminate service. Refusal to correct brings a fine up to 1,000,000 yuan, and the person directly in charge and other persons directly responsible are fined 10,000 to 100,000 yuan. Serious circumstances bring fines up to 50,000,000 yuan, or up to 5% of the previous year's turnover. Responsible persons are fined 100,000 to 1,000,000 yuan. These persons may also be barred from serving as directors, supervisors, or senior managers of relevant enterprises for a certain period. Where selling or providing personal information constitutes a crime, see Entry 4 (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2021). Personal Information Protection Law (Articles 6, 13, 31, 66). <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202108/t20210820_313088.html>
- Note: "Persons directly responsible" in practice often falls on coders and product staff. The Ministry of Industry and Information Technology periodically announces and removes violating apps; this section did not verify which specific bulletin, so it is not cited.

### 16. Before launching a website or app, first complete the ICP filing; keep logs for at least 6 months per the graded-protection requirements
<!-- cost-tag: money=little time=medium willpower=none benefit=medium metric=freedom -->
- Cost: ICP filing is free; the cloud-vendor process takes a few weeks. Graded-protection assessments charge by level. Graded protection, abbreviated "dengbao" (等保), is the state's classification of systems by importance and the set of security-management actions required of websites and systems; small sites generally do not need an assessment.
- Plain-language summary: ICP filing is free; the cloud-vendor process takes a few weeks. Without ICP filing, a domestic server's domain name cannot resolve, and refusal to correct results in the site being shut down. Not displaying the filing number on the homepage brings a fine of 5000 to 50,000 yuan. Charging fees when filing is supposed to be free brings a fine of 100,000 to 1,000,000 yuan. Logs must be kept for 6 months; refusal to correct or harm caused brings a fine of 10,000 to 100,000 yuan.
- Benefit: Commercial internet information services are subject to licensing; non-commercial ones are subject to filing. Without a license or filing, the service may not be operated. Without filing, the telecommunications management authority orders correction; refusal to correct results in an order to close the website. Operating without a license: confiscation of illegal gains, plus a fine of 3 to 5 times the illegal gains, or a fine of 100,000 to 1,000,000 yuan. Failure to display the filing number on the website homepage: fine of 5000 to 50,000 yuan. Network operators must, per the graded-protection system (dengbao), formulate security-management systems, defend against attacks, retain network logs for no less than 6 months, and classify, back up, and encrypt data. Non-compliance: ordered to correct, given a warning. Refusal to correct or consequences caused: fine of 10,000 to 100,000 yuan, and the person in charge is fined 5000 to 50,000 yuan (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: State Council (2011). Internet Information Service Management Measures (2011 revision, Articles 4, 19, 22). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1860864.htm>; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2025). Cybersecurity Law (2025 revision, in force January 1, 2026, Articles 23, 61; the 2016 text had Articles 21, 59). <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm>
- Note: Without ICP filing, a domestic server's domain name cannot resolve at all. Common pitfalls are of two kinds: filing as a personal non-commercial site while actually charging fees; or changing the domain name or entity without updating the filing. Keeping logs for 6 months is both an obligation and self-protection; if something happens, the logs can prove it wasn't you.

### 17. Before providing generative-AI services to the public, conduct a security assessment and file the algorithm as required; mark generated content
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=medium willpower=none benefit=medium metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free, but it goes through the cyberspace-administration process and takes some time. Small tools individuals build that are not open to the public do not trigger this set of requirements.
- Plain-language summary: Generative-AI services that face the public and can influence public opinion or mobilize people must undergo a security assessment before launch, file the algorithm, and complete change or cancellation procedures when modified or stopped. Generated images and videos must be marked. Self-use small tools not open to the public do not need to comply. Violations are punished under the Cybersecurity Law and other laws.
- Benefit: Providers of generative artificial intelligence services with public-opinion attributes or social-mobilization capability shall, per national provisions, conduct security assessments, file the algorithm under the algorithmic recommendation management provisions, and complete subsequent change or cancellation procedures. "Public-opinion attributes or social-mobilization capability" refers to the ability to influence public views or to organize and mobilize people. Providers shall mark generated content such as images and videos. The measures took effect on August 15, 2023 (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: State Internet Information Office and six other departments (2023). Interim Measures for the Management of Generative Artificial Intelligence Services (Articles 12, 17). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891752.htm>
- Note: These measures do not themselves specify fine amounts. Violations are referred back to the Cybersecurity Law, Data Security Law, and Personal Information Protection Law for penalties, so this entry is graded B. Civil cases of AI-generated content infringing others' copyright or portrait were not verified in original text on the Supreme People's Court website, so they are not cited.
