# 14. Account and Information Security

Metric: money and personal information. When someone else logs into your account, money is the first thing lost. The other party will also use your account to scam people in your contact list. Your identity is effectively taken away too.

### 1. Turn on two-factor authentication for email, payment, and social accounts; prefer phone pop-up confirmation, with SMS verification codes as the secondary option
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=none benefit=large metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. Set up each account once; two or three minutes per setup.
- Plain-language summary: Two-factor authentication means that at login, in addition to the password, you confirm once more that it's really you. The method of a phone pop-up that you tap to confirm stops more than 90% of phishing-based account takeovers. Relying on old methods like "where did you last log in" and "what's the backup email" stops only about 10% of phishing takeovers.
- Benefit: Google tallied 350,000 real account-takeover attempts (also called account hijacking). One category is device-based verification, such as a phone pop-up asking you to confirm, or plugging in a security key. This category blocked "over 94% of phishing-based hijacking attempts and 100% of automated hijacking attempts". Phishing means tricking you into entering your password on a fake site; automated means machines trying leaked passwords in bulk. The other category is question-based verification, such as asking where you last logged in or what your backup email is. This category "only blocked as little as 10% of phishing hijackings and 73% of automated hijackings"
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Doerfler P, Thomas K, Marincenko M, et al. (2019). Evaluating Login Challenges as a Defense Against Account Takeover. The World Wide Web Conference (WWW '19). <https://doi.org/10.1145/3308558.3313481>
- Note: The same study also found that such verification sometimes locks out the real user. 52% of genuine users failed to log in on the first attempt, but ultimately 97% got in. Turn this on for your email first, because most other accounts can use email to recover the password.

### 2. Your email password should be unique and not reused on any website
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=some benefit=large metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. Use a password manager and you don't have to remember it. The hard part is breaking the old habit of using one password everywhere.
- Plain-language summary: Passwords stolen from other sites will be tried directly on your email. Once your email is logged into, every account that uses email for password recovery is lost with it. So your email password must be unique and never used anywhere else.
- Benefit: Trying leaked accounts and passwords one by one to log in is called credential stuffing, and it's the easiest attack method; your email will be tried this way too. Once your email is logged into, all accounts that use it for password recovery are lost together. The US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency recommends: use a different strong password for each account, at least 16 characters, stored in a password manager
- Evidence grade: C
- Source: US CISA. Use Strong Passwords. <https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world/use-strong-passwords>
- Note: If you can't remember them, use your browser's built-in password manager. It remembers each site's password for you, far better than using one password everywhere. Don't store passwords in WeChat favorites or memo apps.

### 3. Set a phone lock-screen password; set a PIN on the SIM card
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=none benefit=large metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. Set the lock-screen password and the PIN once each.
- Plain-language summary: The SIM card is the small card inside the phone; SMS verification codes are received through it. If the phone is lost, whoever picks it up will pull this card out, insert it into another phone, receive verification codes, and reset your accounts one by one. Set a PIN on the card, and when the card is moved to another phone, the PIN must be entered at startup — this path is cut off.
- Benefit: After a phone is lost, the fastest path for whoever finds it is to insert the SIM card into another phone and receive your SMS verification codes. With verification codes, they can reset your accounts one by one. If the SIM card has a PIN, moving it to another phone requires entering the password at startup, so the finder cannot use it to receive verification codes
- Evidence grade: C
- Source: Author experience, no direct literature
- Note: The PIN is set in the phone's settings under "SIM card lock". The default factory code is usually 1234 or 0000. After three consecutive wrong entries, you must use the PUK code from the carrier to unlock it. So after setting it, write down the code on paper first.

### 4. If your phone is lost, follow this order: report SIM card lost, remotely lock, change passwords, report to police, freeze bank cards
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=none benefit=large metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. The whole sequence takes a dozen or so minutes.
- Plain-language summary: Order matters more than speed. Step one, report the SIM card lost, and the lifeline of verification codes is cut. Next, remotely lock the phone, then use a computer to change email and payment passwords, then report to the police for a receipt, and finally freeze bank cards as needed. If the phone isn't on you, borrow someone else's phone and call the carrier's customer service to report the loss.
- Benefit: Order matters more than speed. Step one, report the SIM card lost, and others can no longer receive your verification codes. Step two, remotely lock the phone and wipe its contents. Step three, change email and payment passwords from a computer. Step four, report to the police and get a receipt. Finally, freeze bank cards as needed. The US Federal Communications Commission also advises: even if you think it's just lost, remotely lock it. If stolen, report immediately, give the model and the IMEI serial number (the phone's identity number), and immediately notify the carrier
- Evidence grade: C
- Source: US FCC. Protect Your Smart Device. <https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/protect-your-mobile-device>; step order is author experience; for ID card replacement, see Section 7; for impersonation loans, see Section 8's entry on credit reports
- Note: Write down the three carriers' customer service numbers in advance: China Mobile 10086, China Unicom 10010, China Telecom 10000. Also note which city your own phone number was registered in — customer service will ask. You can call customer service from someone else's phone to report the loss.

### 5. For a stolen card, first report and freeze, then go to police, then demand the bank pay: proving "you swiped it yourself" is the bank's responsibility
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=some benefit=large metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. If you see unusual activity on the card, immediately report or freeze it. Keep the police report, loss report, and transaction notifications from the bank. If the card is still in your possession, make a small balance inquiry or deposit nearby to leave a record showing that the real card was in your hands at the time of the incident. The hard part is resisting the urge to argue with customer service first — freeze first.
- Plain-language summary: For a stolen card, you don't have to prove "I didn't swipe this". Conversely, the bank must produce evidence that this transaction was made by you personally; if it cannot, it must compensate you. The premise is that you report and freeze as soon as you notice. If you delay, the additional losses that pile up afterward are yours to bear.
- Benefit: The Supreme People's Court's provisions separate "who provides the evidence". You claim it's a counterfeit-card or online-card fraud; you must first provide evidence. Counterfeit-card fraud means the card was copied and used. Evidence includes: effective legal documents, where the real card was at the time of the transaction, where the transaction occurred. Also account transaction details, transaction notifications, police reports, loss reports, etc. **Conversely, if the issuing bank or non-bank payment institution (third-party payment) claims the transaction was made by the cardholder or with their authorization, they must produce the evidence**. After you notify the bank, if the bank fails to verify in time, or fails to provide and preserve transaction slips and surveillance footage in time, and as a result the evidence cannot be obtained, the consequences of failing to produce evidence are borne by the bank. After the finding is established: for debit card (savings card) holders, the issuing bank may be required to pay you the stolen deposit principal and interest, and to compensate for losses. For credit card holders, the deducted overdraft principal, interest, and penalties may be refunded, and the bank may be required to compensate for losses; if the bank in turn demands you repay the overdraft, the court will not support it. You may also require the issuing bank to promptly revoke the corresponding adverse credit records (nationwide, in force from May 25, 2021)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Supreme People's Court (2021). Provisions on Several Issues Concerning the Trial of Civil Disputes over Bank Cards (Articles 4, 5, 7, 14, 15). <https://www.court.gov.cn/fabu/xiangqing/304771.html>
- Note: There are two situations in which you bear liability yourself. First, you failed to safeguard the bank card, password, or verification code, and you were at fault (the original text is "failed to fulfill the duty of proper custody and was at fault"), with liability proportional to fault. So don't tell anyone the password, don't forward verification codes to others (see Entry 1, prefer phone pop-up for two-factor verification). Second, if you failed to report in time and allowed the losses to expand, you bear the additional portion yourself. So the first step is always to report and freeze; don't argue with customer service first. Third-party payment institutions also fall under this set of rules. If their promotional materials state "advance compensation" and they make specific, clear commitments, you can demand that they pay first. If you were scammed into transferring the money yourself, a different procedure applies; see Section 8, Entry 2 (if you discover you've been scammed, immediately call 110 or 96110 to request a stop payment).

### 6. Periodically check your account's login devices and authorized apps; remove the ones no longer in use
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=some benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. A few minutes per check. The hard part is that no one reminds you; you have to remember to look.
- Plain-language summary: "Login devices" are the phones and computers that can still use your account. Account thieves often lay low for a while before acting. If an unfamiliar device shows up in the list, or long-unused software is still connected to your account, log out all sessions and change the password.
- Benefit: Account theft is often not acted on immediately; the other side lays low for a while. The login device list in your account records the phones and computers that can still use this account. The authorized-app list records other software you have permitted to log in with this account. Unfamiliar devices in the list, and long-unused third-party software, are the easiest traces to spot
- Evidence grade: C
- Source: Author experience, no direct literature
- Note: WeChat, Alipay, email, Apple accounts, and Android accounts all have this entry point. If you find an unfamiliar device, click log out of all sessions, then change the password.

### 7. Don't click "agree to all" just to use an app: for non-essential information, refusing consent cannot be used to deny you service
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=some benefit=medium metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is resisting the urge to click "agree to all".
- Plain-language summary: When an app asks for your information, if it's not essential to providing the service, your refusal to consent cannot be used as a reason to deny you the service. What it can collect is also limited to what is needed. A map app asking for your location is necessary; a flashlight asking for your contacts is not.
- Benefit: The law explicitly states two rules. First, refusal to provide a product or service on the grounds that the individual does not consent or withdraws consent is prohibited, except where processing such information is necessary to provide the service. Second, collection shall be limited to the minimum scope necessary to achieve the processing purpose; only what is needed may be collected
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2021). Personal Information Protection Law. China NPC Network. <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202108/t20210820_313088.html>: Article 6 "Collection of personal information shall be limited to the minimum scope necessary to achieve the processing purpose, and excessive collection of personal information is prohibited"; Article 16 "Personal information processors shall not refuse to provide products or services on the grounds that the individual does not consent to the processing of their personal information or withdraws consent, except where the processing of personal information is necessary to provide the products or services"; Article 15 "Where personal information is processed based on individual consent, the individual has the right to withdraw their consent. The personal information processor shall provide a convenient means of withdrawing consent"
- Note: The criterion is whether the information is necessary to provide the service. A map app asking for location is necessary; a flashlight app asking for contacts is not. After installing an app, first go to the application permissions page in your phone's settings and turn off non-essential permissions. When actually needed, choose "allow only this once".

### 8. You have the right to view, copy, correct, and delete your own personal information; if refused, you can sue
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=some benefit=medium metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Only if the other party drags its feet will you need to file a complaint or sue. A real lawsuit is months at minimum, and you pay your own legal fees, so complaining first is more cost-effective. The hard part is repeatedly pressing when the other party drags its feet.
- Plain-language summary: You have the right to require a company to let you view, copy, correct, and delete your information. When a service ends, retention period expires, or you withdraw consent, the company should delete it on its own. If it refuses you, it must explain the reason; if it does not act, you can sue it directly in court. Canceling the account and deleting information are two different things; after canceling, you still need to separately request deletion.
- Benefit: There are several situations in which a company should proactively delete: the service has ended, the agreed retention period has expired, you withdraw consent, the original collection purpose has been achieved, etc. If it does not delete, you can request deletion. If it refuses your exercise of these rights, it must state the reason. You can sue directly in court
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2021). Personal Information Protection Law. China NPC Network. <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202108/t20210820_313088.html>: Article 45 "The individual has the right to consult and copy their personal information from the personal information processor... When the individual requests to consult or copy their personal information, the personal information processor shall provide it in a timely manner"; Article 46 correction and supplementation rights; Article 47 lists five situations requiring proactive deletion, including "(1) the processing purpose has been achieved, cannot be achieved, or is no longer necessary to achieve the processing purpose", "(2) the personal information processor stops providing the product or service, or the retention period has expired", "(3) the individual withdraws consent", "where the personal information processor has not deleted, the individual has the right to request deletion"; Article 50 "The personal information processor shall establish a convenient mechanism for accepting and processing individuals' applications to exercise their rights. Where a request by an individual to exercise their rights is refused, the reason shall be stated", "the individual may file a lawsuit with the people's court according to law"
- Note: Canceling the account and deleting personal information are two different things; after canceling, you still need to separately request deletion. Before changing phones or selling an old phone, first log out of all accounts and unbind on the old phone, then factory reset. The law gives you an after-the-fact right to delete; it cannot take back what has already leaked.

### 9. Facial recognition is not something you must accept: when other methods exist, they cannot force you to use facial recognition only; if you refuse, they must provide other methods
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=some benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. When asked to scan your face, ask "is there another verification method?". If the other party says no, require them to provide one. The hard part is asking out loud on the spot.
- Plain-language summary: As long as other methods can accomplish the same thing, the other party cannot force you to use only facial recognition. If you refuse facial recognition, they must provide alternatives such as card swipe, password, or ID card, and they may not pressure you with "then we can't process the business". Hotel guest rooms, public bathhouses, changing rooms, and restrooms — no one may install facial recognition equipment in these places.
- Benefit: The Facial Recognition Technology Application Safety Management Measures state: "Where the same purpose can be achieved or the same business requirement met by other non-facial-recognition technical means, facial recognition technology may not be used as the sole verification method. Where an individual does not consent to identity verification through facial information, other reasonable and convenient means shall be provided." The Measures also state: "No organization or individual may, on grounds such as handling business or improving service quality, mislead, defraud, or coerce an individual into accepting facial recognition technology for identity verification." If facial information is processed based on your consent, "voluntary and explicit separate consent made under fully informed conditions" must be obtained: a separate question on this matter alone, with your separate nod required. You have the right to withdraw consent, and the processor must provide a convenient means of withdrawal. Processing the facial information of minors under fourteen must obtain the consent of parents or other guardians. Installing facial recognition equipment in public places "must be necessary for maintaining public safety", and prominent signage must be displayed. Inside the private spaces of public places such as hotel guest rooms, public bathhouses, public changing rooms, and public restrooms, no organization or individual may install. Facial information shall be stored within the facial recognition device and may not be transmitted externally over the internet. Two exceptions: where laws or administrative regulations provide otherwise, or where separate consent has been obtained (nationwide, in force from June 1, 2025)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: State Internet Information Office, Ministry of Public Security (2025). Facial Recognition Technology Application Safety Management Measures (Order No. 19, Articles 10, 12, 13, in force June 1, 2025). <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-03/21/c_1744174262156096.htm>
- Note: The most common situations are residential access control, rental platforms, gyms, and hotels asking you to enroll your face. When the other party says "the system only supports facial recognition", recite the original text of the Measures to them. The original text: "Where the same purpose can be achieved or the same business requirement met by other non-facial-recognition technical means, facial recognition technology may not be used as the sole verification method". Then demand that they provide alternatives such as card swipe, password, or ID card. If still refused, report to the local cyberspace administration. Where the state has separate provisions for facial recognition identity verification, such as in some financial and government-service scenarios, follow those provisions. The biggest difference between face and password is that once leaked, it cannot be changed, so it warrants more caution than a password. Units storing facial information of 100,000 people or more must file with the cyberspace administration at or above the provincial level within 30 working days; this is also a question you can use to judge whether the other party is legitimate. The right to view, correct, and delete your personal information is in Entry 8.
