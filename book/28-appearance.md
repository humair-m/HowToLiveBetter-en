# 28. Do Not Wreck Your Body for the Sake of Appearance

This section covers several approaches to "doing something to your body to change your appearance": starving, taking pills, injections, and surgery. These are decisions mostly made after comparing with others, but the cost falls entirely on yourself, and a considerable portion cannot be undone.

This section does not judge anyone's desired appearance — that is each person's own business. The same goal can be pursued through "prescription, qualified provider, follow-up," or through "online shopping, beauty parlors, self-dosing at higher amounts." This section only compares how much the risk differs between these two approaches. The ledger mainly looks at what actually goes wrong in the body — blindness, hospitalization, death. The two medical-aesthetics entries also touch on the criminal liability of practicing medicine without a license.

### 1. Do not use extreme dieting, fasting, or vomiting to control weight; if you want to lose weight, start from the exercise side
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. Conversely, it saves the money spent on weight-loss products.
- Plain-language summary: People with anorexia nervosa have a mortality rate about 5.9 times that of peers their age. Per 1,000 people followed for one year, 5.1 die. Of those who die, one in five dies by suicide. And its entry point is dieting: tracking students at 44 secondary schools for three years, the probability of severe dieters developing an eating disorder was 18 times that of non-dieters. If you want to control your weight, exercise more — do not eat so little that you starve.
- Benefit: 36 studies pooled together. Those with anorexia nervosa in this set were followed for a cumulative 166,642 person-years (each person's years of follow-up summed). Their standardized mortality ratio was 5.86, meaning the probability of death was about 5.9 times that of peers their age. Mortality was 5.1 deaths per 1,000 person-years, that is, 5.1 deaths per 1,000 people followed for one year. About 1/5 of those who died did so by suicide. The standardized mortality ratio for bulimia was 1.93, and for other eating disorders 1.92, meaning each was about 1.9 times that of peers their age. Another study conducted at 44 secondary schools in Victoria, Australia, followed students aged 14–15 for 3 years, surveying the same cohort across 6 waves over those three years. New eating disorders in girls occurred at 21.8/1,000 person-years, meaning 21.8 new cases per 1,000 girls followed for one year. The risk of developing an eating disorder was 18 times higher in girls who severely dieted than in non-dieters, and 5 times higher in moderate dieters. After further adjustment for prior dieting and psychiatric symptoms — that is, removing the influence of "already dieted before" and "already had psychiatric symptoms" — BMI (the body-mass index, weight divided by height squared, used as a fatness/thinness metric), exercise volume, and sex no longer predicted new eating disorders. The authors' conclusion was that "in adolescents, controlling weight through exercise rather than through food restriction appears to lower the risk of eating disorders."
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Arcelus J et al. (2011). Mortality rates in patients with anorexia nervosa and other eating disorders. A meta-analysis of 36 studies. Arch Gen Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1001/archgenpsychiatry.2011.74>; Patton GC et al. (1999). Onset of adolescent eating disorders: population based cohort study over 3 years. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.318.7186.765>
- Note: "Severe dieting" here refers to chronically reducing intake to far below what the body needs — not skipping a meal or skipping a late-night snack. If you have already begun vomiting, binge eating followed by self-blame, or uncontrollable repeated weighing, see a psychiatrist or the clinical psychology department of a general hospital. This is a disease with established treatment methods, not a matter of willpower. The relationship between BMI and mortality is discussed in Section 2, Entry 33 — that entry covers long-term weight ranges, not weight-loss methods.

### 2. Before injections, thread embedding, or surgery, check two things: whether the institution's license lists "medical aesthetics," and whether the practitioner is an attending physician
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. Checking these two certificates takes only a few minutes. The institution should display its Medical Institution Practice License in the lobby. A physician's qualifications can be verified through the health commission's licensed physician lookup.
- Plain-language summary: Needles, scalpels, and medical devices that break the skin or enter the body all count as medical care. They may only be performed at institutions whose license lists "medical aesthetics," and the practitioner must be a registered licensed physician. Doing these in a beauty parlor, studio, or hotel room constitutes illegal practice of medicine. In serious cases, the practitioner is sentenced to up to 3 years; if injury results, 3 to 10 years; if death results, 10 years or more.
- Benefit: Article 2 of the Measures for the Administration of Medical Aesthetics Services defines medical aesthetics. The original text reads "the repair and reshaping of human appearance and body parts through the use of surgery, drugs, medical devices, and other medical technical methods that are traumatic or invasive." In plain terms, anything that breaks the skin, inserts a needle, or enters the body is treated as medical care. Articles 8 and 24 require first obtaining a Medical Institution Practice License and registering the medical aesthetics clinical specialty with the registration authority before providing such services. If the license does not list "medical aesthetics," the service cannot be provided. Article 11 requires that the attending physician (the doctor performing the procedure) hold a licensed physician qualification and be registered. Those performing aesthetic surgery must have 6 or more years of clinical experience in the relevant specialty, aesthetic dentistry 5 or more years, and aesthetic traditional Chinese medicine and aesthetic dermatology 3 or more years each. Article 20 requires that, before performing the procedure, written informed consent be obtained after disclosing indications, contraindications (when the procedure is appropriate and when it is not), medical risks, and precautions. Article 336 of the Criminal Law governs the illegal practice of medicine. Illegal practice of medicine refers to a person without a medical practice qualification giving injections, performing surgery, or treating patients. In serious cases, the penalty is up to 3 years of fixed-term imprisonment, criminal detention, or public surveillance, with a fine imposed concurrently or separately. Where the serious harm to the patient's health results, the penalty is 3 to 10 years of fixed-term imprisonment with a fine. Where the patient dies, the penalty is 10 or more years of fixed-term imprisonment with a fine.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Ministry of Health (2002). Measures for the Administration of Medical Aesthetics Services (Ministry of Health Order No. 19, Articles 2, 8, 11, 16, 20, 24). <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2003/content_62198.htm>; National People's Congress (1997). Criminal Law (Article 336). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml>
- Note: Article 16 of the same Measures also provides that medical aesthetics procedures may only be performed at medical aesthetics institutions, or at hospitals and clinics with a medical aesthetics department. So injections at home or operations in private studios are non-compliant based on location alone. Article 20 specifies that medical aesthetics on those who cannot yet decide for themselves, such as minors, requires the guardian's consent — parents must sign. Facials, massage, and manicures are classified as "life aesthetics" and are not governed by this set of rules. But anything involving a needle or scalpel counts as medical aesthetics.

### 3. Avoid nasal dorsum, glabella, and forehead for facial filler injections; do not seek cut-rate non-formal injections
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. Just inject elsewhere, or do not inject.
- Plain-language summary: Among those who went blind after filler injections, more than half had been injected in the nose, followed by the glabella, forehead, and nasolabial folds; over 80% involved hyaluronic acid. Of those who lost vision, only 20% fully recovered, just over 10% partially recovered, and nearly 20% also suffered brain injury. No rescue method currently works reliably — the only option is to avoid these areas in advance.
- Benefit: Pooling all cases reported in journals worldwide. From January 2015 to September 2018, 48 new cases of partial or complete vision loss after filler injection were added. The most common site was the nose, at 56.3%. Next were the glabella 27.1%, forehead 18.8%, and nasolabial folds (the crease beside the nostrils) 14.6%. In 81.3% of cases the filler was hyaluronic acid. In 43.8% skin changes appeared. In 18.8% central nervous system complications occurred (the brain was affected). 10 cases (20.8%) had complete vision recovery, 8 cases (16.7%) partial recovery, and the rest did not recover. The authors concluded that approaches vary widely across centers, and no treatment has been proven consistently effective.
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Beleznay K et al. (2019). Update on Avoiding and Treating Blindness From Fillers: A Recent Review of the World Literature. Aesthet Surg J. <https://doi.org/10.1093/asj/sjz053>
- Note: These figures count each published case one by one. They include only the number of people harmed, not the total number of injections. So one cannot compute "what is the probability of blindness per injection" — one can only see which sites have more incidents and that recovery is mostly impossible afterward. The evidence is rated Grade B for this reason as well. The standard risk-reduction practices at legitimate institutions are routine: first map out the underlying blood vessels at the injection site, use blunt-tipped needles rather than sharp ones, inject small amounts while withdrawing the needle, and have hyaluronidase ready in advance. Each step requires the practitioner to be a trained physician — this is another reason not to use informal channels. Before undergoing the procedure, verify per Entry 2 of this section (check the institution license and the attending physician).

### 4. Do not buy weight-loss drugs, diet coffee, slimming candy, or enzyme plum products that promise "rapid weight loss"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. Conversely, it saves the money spent on these products. The hard part is accepting that "lose several jin in a week" does not exist.
- Plain-language summary: The substance most commonly detected as secretly added to such products is sibutramine, originally a weight-loss drug withdrawn in China in 2010. In overweight people with heart disease or diabetes who took it for over three years, non-fatal heart attacks rose about 30%, and strokes about 36%. Weight loss was only an additional 1.7 kg. By the time it shows up in inspections, you have already taken it.
- Benefit: The SCOUT trial enrolled 9,804 people. All were 55 or older, overweight or obese, and had cardiovascular disease or type 2 diabetes. Participants were randomized into drug and no-drug groups, with an average treatment of 3.4 years. The primary endpoint was a prespecified set of adverse events: non-fatal myocardial infarction, non-fatal stroke (cerebrovascular accident), resuscitated cardiac arrest, and cardiovascular death. The incidence of these events was 11.4% vs 10.0%. HR 1.16 (95% CI 1.03–1.31, P=0.02), with the drug group's event probability about 16% higher. The true value falls roughly between 3% higher and 31% higher. Non-fatal MI 4.1% vs 3.2%, HR 1.28 (1.04–1.57), about 30% higher. Non-fatal stroke 2.6% vs 1.9%, HR 1.36 (1.04–1.77), about 36% higher. Cardiovascular death and all-cause death were not increased; all-cause means all causes combined. After randomization, the sibutramine group lost an additional mean of 1.7 kg. The former State Food and Drug Administration, based on its safety assessment, concluded that "the risks of weight-loss treatment outweigh the benefits," and in October 2010 ordered the cessation of production, sale, and use of sibutramine preparations and the raw material. Sibutramine and phenolphthalein are both prohibited from addition to foods, including health foods.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: James WPT et al. (2010). Effect of sibutramine on cardiovascular outcomes in overweight and obese subjects. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1003114>; Tianjin Municipal Market Supervision and Administration Commission. Beware of Illegal Addition of "Sibutramine Hydrochloride" and "Phenolphthalein" in Some Foods. <https://scjg.tj.gov.cn/tjsscjdglwyh_52651/hdpt/cjwtyxfts/202007/t20200720_2972549.html>
- Note: SCOUT enrolled high-risk individuals who already had heart disease or diabetes. This trial did not prove that healthy young adults would have the same outcomes. But precisely because one cannot distinguish who is high-risk, the regulator simply withdrew it from the market. Another commonly added substance is phenolphthalein, originally a laxative; long-term excess use disrupts the body's electrolytes and can induce arrhythmia. How to tell: legitimate weight-loss drugs are prescription drugs with approval numbers traceable at pharmacies and hospitals. Products sold under the banner of "coffee," "candy," "plum," or "meal replacement" that promise to drop several jin in a week are not supposed to have any drug effect by definition — if they do, it means something has been added. The original text of Guo Shi Yao Jian Ban [2010] No. 432 is on the National Medical Products Administration website; opening it from this machine kept returning a 412 error, so the provincial regulator's reproduced page is cited instead. The phrases "risks outweigh benefits" and "ceased production, sale, and use in October 2010" can be verified verbatim there.

### 5. Do not use anabolic steroids ("muscle-building injections," "oral drugs") to build muscle
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free of charge — also saves the money spent on drugs. The hard part is accepting that muscle grows slowly.
- Plain-language summary: Men who tested positive for steroids at gyms had a mortality risk 3 times that of peers their age, and more than double the annual number of hospital visits. Acne, gynecomastia, and erectile dysfunction each affect at least one in ten users. These drugs are prescription drugs in China, so any channel that sells them to you is breaking the law.
- Benefit: Denmark went back through records and matched each user with same-age controls. Users were 545 men who tested positive for androgenic steroids at gym doping tests between 2006-01-03 and 2018-03-01, matched with 5,450 controls. A further 644 men who refused testing and 6,440 controls were used to replicate the analysis. Results: user mortality was 3 times that of controls, HR 3.0 (95% CI 1.3–7.0), with the true multiplier roughly between 1.3 and 7. The median annual number of hospitalizations or visits was 0.81 vs 0.36 (P<0.0001). The median is the value above which half fall and below which half fall. This P value indicates the difference is almost certainly not chance. Acne, gynecomastia, and erectile dysfunction each affected over 10% of users, meaning at least one in ten users was affected — all significantly higher than controls (P<0.0001). The replication analysis was consistent. Article 7 of the Anti-Doping Regulations: the state strictly manages prohibited substances listed in the doping catalog; no unit or individual may illegally produce, sell, import, or export them. The same regulations provide that protein anabolic agents and peptide hormones are managed as prescription drugs, that is, they may only be purchased with a doctor's prescription. "Protein anabolic agent" is the regulatory term for this class of drugs.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Horwitz H et al. (2019). Health consequences of androgenic anabolic steroid use. J Intern Med. <https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.12850>; State Council (2004). Anti-Doping Regulations (State Council Order No. 398, Articles 7, 9). <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2004/content_63129.htm>
- Note: Controversy: This is a study that followed a group of people to observe outcomes — not a randomized controlled trial. Users may themselves be more risk-taking in other ways, and the 3-times figure cannot all be attributed to the drugs. The true multiplier could be as low as 1.3 or as high as 7.0, a wide range. But "acne, gynecomastia, and erectile dysfunction each affect over 10% of users" are visible side effects, less affected by such bias. These drugs are referred to in the regulations as "protein anabolic agents" — those four characters refer to this class. Ordinary protein powder and creatine are not covered by this entry. The trouble is that products "claiming to build muscle very fast" may have substances secretly added. The way to judge is the same as for weight-loss products (Entry 4, do not buy weight-loss drugs that promise rapid loss): legitimate drugs are prescription drugs with traceable approval numbers. Products sold under the name of "muscle powder" or "nutrition supplement" that promise effects within weeks — if they really work, something has been added.

### 6. If you want to take weight-loss medication, get a prescription from a hospital; do not buy from online stores that ship without one
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: One consultation fee, plus a round trip. A few dozen yuan, half a day.
- Plain-language summary: Prescription drugs must be sold with a prescription, and online sales require a real prescription and real-name registration. Sellers that do not ask questions, do not require a prescription, and ship on order either are not selling the drug they claim, or are operating in violation. If something goes wrong, no one is responsible for your dosage or contraindications. A consultation costs a few dozen yuan and gets a doctor to take on this responsibility for you.
- Benefit: The Measures for the Supervision and Administration of the Quality of Drug Distribution and Use require that retail drug enterprises comply with the prescription drug and nonprescription drug classification system. This system divides drugs into two categories: one requiring a doctor's prescription to purchase, and one that can be purchased by the consumer. Pharmacies must sell prescription drugs with a prescription as required, and prescriptions must be retained for at least five years. When pharmacists or other pharmaceutical technical personnel are not on duty, a notice must be displayed, and prescription drugs may not be sold without their review. Prescription drugs may not be sold on open shelves and cannot be placed on shelves for customers to pick up themselves. Article 9 of the Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Online Drug Sales requires that when selling prescription drugs online to individuals, the source of the prescription must be verified as genuine and reliable, with real-name registration. Article 10 requires each drug display page to prominently display risk warning information. Before a prescription is reviewed, the package insert may not be displayed, and purchase services may not be provided — if the prescription has not been reviewed, the page should not even have a buy button. Article 8 provides that drugs under special state management may not be sold online; these include vaccines, blood products, narcotic drugs, psychotropic drugs, toxic drugs for medical use, radioactive drugs, and precursor chemicals for pharmaceutical use.
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: State Administration for Market Regulation (2023). Measures for the Supervision and Administration of the Quality of Drug Distribution and Use (Order No. 84). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2023/issue_10846/202311/content_6917322.html>; State Administration for Market Regulation (2022). Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Online Drug Sales (Order No. 58, Articles 8 to 10). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2022/content_5717002.htm>
- Note: The legal provisions can be verified verbatim, but no quantitative study has been done on "how many fewer incidents occur when taking by prescription versus buying on your own," so it is rated Grade B as "difficult to quantify." The steroids and sex hormones in Entries 5 and 7 of this section are subject to the same rules. When weight loss genuinely requires medication, a doctor first reviews the body mass index, other existing conditions, and any contraindications, then decides whether to use medication and which kind. This judgment cannot be purchased.

### 7. Sex hormone drugs should only be used with a doctor's prescription and regular follow-up; do not buy online, do not self-increase the dose
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Periodic consultation fees plus blood tests for follow-up. A few hundred yuan per year, taking up several half-days.
- Plain-language summary: This entry does not discuss whether to use; it only discusses how to use. Transgender women taking estrogen have significantly more blood clots than peers their age — about 4 more per 1,000 people over 2 years, and about 17 more over 8 years. This kind of risk must be monitored through regular follow-up. Buying drugs online and adjusting your own dose means no one is checking for you.
- Benefit: Researchers followed a cohort from the electronic medical records of the Kaiser Permanente medical group. Among them were 2,842 transgender women and 2,118 transgender men. Average follow-up was 4.0 years and 3.6 years, respectively. Each was matched to about 10-fold cisgender controls by birth year, race, site of care, and enrollment date — a total of 48,686 cisgender men and 48,775 cisgender women. Venous thromboembolism (blood clots blocking vessels) was higher in transgender women than controls. Compared with cisgender men, 2 years: 4.1 per 1,000 (95% CI 1.6–6.7). 8 years: 16.7 (6.4–27.5). That is, per 1,000 people, 4.1 additional cases over 2 years, 16.7 additional over 8 years. Compared with cisgender women, the figures were 3.4 (1.1–5.6) and 13.7 (4.1–22.7). Ischemic stroke (the vessel-blocking kind of stroke) and myocardial infarction were overall similar across groups. But the differences in venous thromboembolism and ischemic stroke were more pronounced in transgender women who began hormone therapy during the follow-up period. The evidence was insufficient for transgender men to draw a conclusion. The authors concluded that "these results may suggest a need for long-term vigilance regarding the vascular side effects of estrogens in transgender hormone therapy."
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Getahun D et al. (2018). Cross-sex Hormones and Acute Cardiovascular Events in Transgender Persons: A Cohort Study. Ann Intern Med. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M17-2785>; State Administration for Market Regulation (2022). Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Online Drug Sales (Order No. 58, Article 9). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2022/content_5717002.htm>
- Note: The researchers themselves noted the study's limitations: how many people were obtaining drugs outside the medical system could not be captured, so the "whether using" line is not cleanly drawn. This is why use should be placed within regular follow-up. This study examined only whether vascular events occurred — it does not address whether hormone therapy should be used or what benefits it has; that is a different body of evidence. The same applies in all contexts involving sex hormones, including contraceptives, menopausal hormone therapy, and testosterone supplementation. Anyone who buys online, adjusts doses based on internet posts, and never has thrombosis, liver, or kidney indicators checked bears risk in the "no one is watching" segment — regardless of why the hormone is used. The proper domestic pathway is to go to the endocrinology department or the relevant specialty at a legitimate medical institution for evaluation, prescription, and follow-up.

### 8. If "feeling ugly" reaches the point of repeatedly checking the mirror and repeatedly wanting to change, do a body image assessment before surgery
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: One consultation at psychiatry or clinical psychology, a few dozen yuan. The hard part is going before scheduling surgery.
- Plain-language summary: About 15% of those seeking plastic surgery have body dysmorphic disorder — magnifying flaws that others barely notice until they affect daily life. About 13% of those seeking aesthetic treatment at dermatology have it; in both groups, over 70% are women. Such patients, after fixing one feature, fixate on the next; surgery does not solve it. Get one consultation first to see whether you fall into this category, then decide whether to operate.
- Benefit: A systematic review with meta-analysis pooled 33 studies. Among plastic surgery patients, the prevalence of body dysmorphic disorder (magnifying flaws others barely notice until they affect daily life) was 15.04%, with individual studies ranging from 2.21% to 56.67%. In headcount, about 15 per 100 plastic surgery patients met criteria; individual studies ranged from 2 to 57. The mean age was 34.54±12.41 years — the latter number is the spread — and 74.38% were women. For dermatology patients, prevalence was 12.65%, with individual studies ranging from 4.52% to 35.16%; mean age 27.79±9.03 years, 76.09% women. The authors recommended that plastic surgeons and dermatologists actively assess and identify high-risk patients and arrange multidisciplinary management.
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Ribeiro RVE (2017). Prevalence of Body Dysmorphic Disorder in Plastic Surgery and Dermatology Patients: A Systematic Review with Meta-Analysis. Aesthetic Plast Surg. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s00266-017-0869-0>
- Note: The proportions measured by individual studies range from 2.21% to 56.67% — a very large spread. The reason is that the questionnaires and populations studied differ. So one can only read out "this proportion is not low and worth screening once" — do not treat 15.04% as a precise number. The Grade B is for this reason as well. The vast majority of those seeking medical aesthetics do not have a psychological issue. This entry targets the small subset whose preoccupation is "repeated, persistent, and already affecting work and social life" — surgery cannot solve their problem. Clues for identifying this: spending long stretches of the day inspecting or concealing a particular body part, avoiding social situations or photographs as a result, and fixating on the next feature after one is "fixed."
