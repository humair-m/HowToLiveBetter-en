# 2. Don't Die Slowly

This section collects only two kinds of chronic risks: those that have a large impact on total mortality, and those with hard evidence. Entries are ordered by cost-effectiveness from high to low. A few are marked "randomized trial," meaning people were randomly split into two groups for comparison — these results are the most credible. The rest of the numbers come from studies that only track and record, without doing the grouping. The HR, RR, and other risk ratios computed by these kinds of studies mix in two confounders: these people were already different to begin with, and some did these things only because they were already sick. So these numbers indicate only direction and rough magnitude — they do not mean that doing the same will reduce your risk by that much.

### 1. Quit smoking, the earlier the better
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free, and on the contrary saves money. A pack a day is about 20–30 yuan; quitting leaves that money in your pocket. The hard part is getting through withdrawal, which typically lasts a few weeks to a few months.
- Plain-language summary: Smokers on average live more than ten years less than non-smokers. Quitting before age 40 can wipe out about 90% of the mortality risk from continuing to smoke. The earlier you quit, the more you get back.
- Benefit: A U.S. cohort study (recording only, no grouping) found: current smokers have a life expectancy more than 10 years shorter than never-smokers. Quitting before age 40 can eliminate about 90% of the mortality risk from continuing to smoke. Quitting at 25–34 yields about 10 more years of life, at 35–44 about 9 more years, at 45–54 about 6 more years (the original paper reports in life-years). A comparable Chinese study: in the 2010s, urban male smokers had a mortality risk 1.65 times that of non-smokers (RR 1.65), and rural males 1.22 times. After 10 years of quitting on one's own initiative, this smoking-related risk essentially disappears.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Jha P et al. (2013). 21st-century hazards of smoking and benefits of cessation in the United States. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMsa1211128>; Chen Z et al. (2015). Contrasting male and female trends in tobacco-attributed mortality in China: evidence from successive nationwide prospective cohort studies. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(15)00340-2>; Oberg M et al. (2011). Worldwide burden of disease from exposure to second-hand smoke: a retrospective analysis of data from 192 countries. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61388-8> (the two second-hand smoke figures)
- Note: Other people's smoke also kills: in 2004 about 603,000 people worldwide died from second-hand smoke, about 1% of global deaths that year. Even if you don't smoke, avoid other people's smoke, and especially don't let children breathe it. How to quit — the specific methods are in the next four entries: Entry 3 (smoking-cessation drugs), Entry 4 (set a quit date), Entry 5 (go to a smoking-cessation clinic), Entry 6 (e-cigarettes).

### 2. Don't smoke at home or in the car, and don't let guests smoke at home
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free, and takes no time. The hard part is opening your mouth to ask family and guests not to smoke at home.
- Plain-language summary: About 600,000 people die each year from other people's smoke, and nearly 30% of them are children. Long-term second-hand smoke exposure raises the risk of high blood pressure, heart disease, and stroke by about 30%. Smoke inhaled at home is more harmful than smoke inhaled outside.
- Benefit: In 2004, 603,000 people died from second-hand smoke worldwide, about 1.0% of global deaths, of which 28% were children. A pooled analysis of 57 studies: the chance of high blood pressure in those exposed to second-hand smoke was about 1.28 times that of those not exposed (odds ratio). Heart disease 1.39 times, myocardial infarction 1.50 times, stroke 1.36 times. The risk of second-hand smoke exposure at home was higher than outside the home.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Öberg M, Jaakkola MS, Woodward A, Peruga A, Prüss-Ustün A (2011). Worldwide burden of disease from exposure to second-hand smoke: a retrospective analysis of data from 192 countries. Lancet: "603,000 deaths were attributable to second-hand smoke in 2004, which was about 1·0% of worldwide mortality. 47% of deaths from second-hand smoke occurred in women, 28% in children, and 26% in men", "61% of DALYs were in children". <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61388-8>; (2026). The Associations Between Secondhand Smoke Exposure and Various Cardiovascular Diseases: A Meta-Analysis. Nicotine & Tobacco Research: 57 studies, "hypertension (OR: 1.28, 95% CI: 1.15 to 1.40), heart disease (OR: 1.39, 95% CI: 1.28 to 1.50), myocardial infarction (OR: 1.50, 95% CI: 1.17 to 1.84), stroke (OR: 1.36, 95% CI: 1.18 to 1.54)" "Home exposure has a higher risk of CVD than non-home exposure". <https://doi.org/10.1093/ntr/ntaf111>
- Note: Tend to home first, because that's where you spend the most time, and pooled studies show that second-hand smoke inhaled at home is more harmful than outside. Of the health losses caused by second-hand smoke, 61% falls on children. For quitting smoking yourself, see Entry 1 of this section.

### 3. Don't quit smoking by willpower alone — get medication first: success rate more than doubles
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Nicotine patches and gum are over-the-counter, available at pharmacy counters. A course of 8 to 12 weeks costs several hundred to over a thousand yuan. Varenicline and bupropion are prescription drugs and must be prescribed at a smoking-cessation clinic or respiratory medicine department. The cost roughly cancels out with the cigarettes you no longer buy over the same period.
- Plain-language summary: Most people cannot quit by willpower alone. Those taking varenicline quit at more than twice the rate of those taking placebo. Those using nicotine replacement products like patches and gum have a rate about 50% higher than those taking no medication. Patch plus gum pushes it up another 20% or so.
- Benefit: 41 randomized trials and 17,395 people pooled: the quit-success rate in the varenicline group was 2.32 times that of the placebo group (RR 2.32, 95% CI 2.15–2.51, the confidence interval; high-certainty evidence). Varenicline was also higher than bupropion (RR 1.36, about 36% higher) and higher than a single nicotine replacement product alone (RR 1.25, about 25% higher). Nicotine replacement vs no medication: 133 trials, 64,640 people, success rate about 55% higher (RR 1.55, 95% CI 1.49–1.61). A patch plus a fast-acting formulation (gum, lozenge) was about 27% higher than a single formulation alone (RR 1.27, 95% CI 1.17–1.37, 16 trials, 12,169 people, high-certainty evidence).
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Livingstone-Banks J, Fanshawe TR, Thomas KH, et al. (2023). Nicotine receptor partial agonists for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 5, CD006103. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD006103.pub8>; Hartmann-Boyce J, Chepkin SC, Ye W, Bullen C, Lancaster T (2018). Nicotine replacement therapy versus control for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 5, CD000146. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD000146.pub5>; Theodoulou A, Chepkin SC, Ye W, et al. (2023). Different doses, durations and modes of delivery of nicotine replacement therapy for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 6, CD013308. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013308.pub2>; Shanghai Municipal Health Commission (2021). Choose the Right Medication to Make Quitting Smoking Easier: "The smoking-cessation drugs available on the market are mainly of three types, namely nicotine replacement therapy drugs, bupropion, and varenicline", "Nicotine replacement therapy drugs are over-the-counter (OTC) and can be purchased at pharmacy counters; bupropion and varenicline are prescription drugs and require a visit to a hospital smoking-cessation clinic or respiratory medicine department, with a doctor's prescription filled by a pharmacist." <https://wsjkw.sh.gov.cn/jtyx/20211119/df50681e01ba49f896d54f771d8176ae.html>
- Note: The smoking-cessation drugs sold in China are exactly the three types above; only nicotine replacement products are OTC. Common reactions to varenicline include nausea, vivid dreams, and poor sleep; those with a psychiatric history should tell their doctor. Nicotine replacement courses are generally 8 to 12 weeks — don't stop suddenly on your own; taper as your doctor instructs. Medication only solves the discomfort of the withdrawal weeks; it does not solve the situations where "you want a cigarette." So it must be used together with Entry 4 (set a quit date) and Entry 5 (go to a smoking-cessation clinic) of this section.

### 4. Set a quit date and stop all at once on that day — don't taper down first
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. Just pick a day on the calendar and tell your family and colleagues in advance.
- Plain-language summary: Many people plan to smoke a few fewer each day and slowly taper to zero. But by the sixth month, 22% of those who set a date and stopped all at once had not relapsed, vs 15.5% of those tapering. So set a date and stop all at once.
- Benefit: 697 smokers in the U.K. were randomized into two groups. One group stopped all at once on their quit date; the other cut cigarette consumption by 75% in the two weeks before the quit date. Both groups had nurse behavioral support and used nicotine replacement around the quit date. At week 4, the abrupt-stop group had 49.0% (95% CI 43.8–54.2, the confidence interval) not relapsed, vs 39.2% (34.0–44.4) for the tapering group; relative risk ratio 0.80 (0.66–0.93). By month 6, the abrupt-stop group was at 22.0% (18.0–26.6) and the tapering group at 15.5% (12.0–19.7); relative risk ratio 0.71 (0.46–0.91). Among those who originally preferred tapering, week-4 success was also lower than among those who preferred abrupt stopping (38.3% vs 52.2%).
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Lindson-Hawley N, Banting M, West R, Michie S, Shinkins B, Aveyard P (2016). Gradual Versus Abrupt Smoking Cessation: A Randomized, Controlled Noninferiority Trial. Annals of Internal Medicine, 164(9), 585–592. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M14-2805>; Theodoulou A, Chepkin SC, Ye W, et al. (2023). Different doses, durations and modes of delivery of nicotine replacement therapy for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 6, CD013308 (the section on using nicotine replacement in advance). <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013308.pub2>
- Note: Controversy. Cochrane previously had a review specifically comparing the two methods, concluding they were similar (RR 0.94, 95% CI 0.79–1.13, 10 trials, 3,760 people), but that review was withdrawn in 2019 and is no longer updated. So the best current evidence is the randomized trial above, and the direction supports abrupt stopping. In addition, both groups in that trial started using nicotine replacement before the quit date, and this practice itself has evidence: starting earlier than the quit date yields about 25% higher success rate than starting on the quit date (RR 1.25, 95% CI 1.08–1.44, 9 trials, 4,395 people, moderate-certainty evidence). So the reasonable schedule is: set the date, start the patch two weeks ahead, and stop all at once on the date.

### 5. Go to a smoking-cessation clinic, or call 12320 to ask whether local smoking-cessation services exist
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Registration is a few yuan to a few dozen yuan. Behavioral support is usually 4 to 8 face-to-face sessions, half an hour to an hour each, totaling under 5 hours. The hotline is free.
- Plain-language summary: Medication and "someone checking in on you regularly" are two different things, and combining them works best. Those who took medication while having a dedicated person follow up quit at nearly twice the rate of those who only heard a brief piece of advice. Phone calls also count: a quitline that proactively calls back can boost the success rate by another 20–30%.
- Benefit: 52 trials and 19,488 people pooled: the quit-success rate in the medication-plus-behavioral-support group was 1.83 times that of usual care or brief-advice groups (RR 1.83, 95% CI 1.68–1.98, the confidence interval; high-quality evidence). In the 43 trials recruited at medical institutions, it was 1.97 times (1.79–2.18). Telephone counseling alone: among those who had themselves called a quitline, those who received multiple proactive callbacks had about 38% higher success than those who only got materials or a single conversation (RR 1.38, 95% CI 1.19–1.61, 14 trials, 32,484 people); among those who had not called the quitline themselves but were provided telephone counseling by the study, about 25% higher (RR 1.25, 95% CI 1.15–1.35, 65 trials, 41,233 people). Both are moderate-certainty evidence.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Stead LF, Koilpillai P, Fanshawe TR, Lancaster T (2016). Combined pharmacotherapy and behavioural interventions for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 3, CD008286. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD008286.pub3>; Matkin W, Ordóñez-Mena JM, Hartmann-Boyce J (2019). Telephone counselling for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 5, CD002850. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD002850.pub4>; Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention (2021). "China Smoking-Cessation Platform" WeChat Mini Program Officially Launched: "Under the guidance of the Tobacco Control Working Group of Healthy China Action, the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention and the WHO Representative Office in China jointly produced the authoritative Chinese smoking-cessation resource library, the 'China Smoking-Cessation Platform' WeChat mini program. The mini program mainly includes: smoking-cessation hotlines, smoking-cessation clinic details, and online smoking-cessation service resources"; the same site's footer notes "Health consultation phone: 12320." <https://www.chinacdc.cn/jkyj/yckz/gzdt/202203/t20220310_296389.html>
- Note: How to find one in China: search the "China Smoking-Cessation Platform" (中国戒烟平台) WeChat mini program, which contains lists of local smoking-cessation hotlines and clinics; you can also call 12320 to ask whether your area has a smoking-cessation clinic. The trials above were mostly conducted abroad; the intensity of services at local smoking-cessation clinics varies widely, so treat the numbers as direction only. For the medications to pair with, see Entry 3 of this section (smoking-cessation drugs).

### 6. Consider e-cigarettes only if you cannot quit otherwise; people who don't already smoke should not touch them
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Device plus pods cost tens to hundreds of yuan per month. In China only tobacco-flavored products can be sold, and only at shops holding a tobacco retail monopoly license.
- Plain-language summary: Switching to nicotine-containing e-cigarettes yields about 60% more quitters than nicotine patches, and this conclusion is robust. But it is not harmless, and long-term inhalation safety data do not yet exist. People who don't already smoke get zero benefit from it.
- Benefit: A continually updated review included 80 randomized trials and 29,861 people. Compared with nicotine replacement therapy, nicotine-containing e-cigarettes had about 61% higher cessation success rate (RR 1.61, 95% CI 1.23–2.12, the confidence interval; high-certainty evidence, 11 trials, 4,114 people). In absolute terms, about 4 more per 100 people quit (1 to 7 more). The proportion with serious adverse events was similar in the two groups (risk difference 0.01, 95% CI −0.01 to 0.02, moderate-certainty evidence). Compared with behavioral support only or no support, about 75% higher (RR 1.75, 95% CI 1.39–2.20, low-certainty evidence).
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Lindson N, Livingstone-Banks J, Butler AR, et al. (2026). Electronic cigarettes for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 8, CD010216. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD010216.pub11>; State Tobacco Monopoly Administration (2022). Measures for the Administration of Electronic Cigarettes (State Tobacco Monopoly Administration Announcement No. 1 of 2022, effective May 1, 2022), Article 18: Engaging in e-cigarette retail business requires applying for a tobacco retail monopoly license from the tobacco monopoly administrative department. Article 22: "It is prohibited to sell e-cigarette products to minors." Article 23, paragraph 2: "No individual, legal person, or other organization may, through information networks other than the e-cigarette transaction management platform specified in these Measures, sell e-cigarette products, aerosols, or nicotine for e-cigarettes." Article 26: "It is prohibited to sell flavored e-cigarettes other than tobacco flavor, and e-cigarettes to which aerosols may be self-added." <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2022/content_5697988.htm>
- Note: Controversy. The WHO does not recommend e-cigarettes as a smoking-cessation tool. The reasons are insufficient long-term safety data and that they can get young people started on nicotine. The U.K. has incorporated them into smoking-cessation services. China does not ban e-cigarettes outright, but regulates them strictly under the Measures for the Administration of Electronic Cigarettes. Only tobacco flavor may be sold; fruit flavors and self-refillable oils may not be. They can only be purchased at physical shops holding a tobacco retail monopoly license. Selling on websites, online shops, and social apps other than the national unified e-cigarette transaction management platform is illegal. Sales to minors are also prohibited. So fruit-flavored e-cigarettes still available come through illegal channels and outside regulation. "Spice" e-cigarettes laced with synthetic cannabinoids leak out this way — see Section 22, Entry 4 (don't accept pods handed to you by others). The e-liquids tested in the review are regulated, nicotine-containing products; oils of unknown origin are not included. In order of priority, try Entry 3 of this section (smoking-cessation drugs) first — its evidence is harder and it is cheaper.

### 7. Don't drink sugary drinks — switching to sugar-free doesn't solve the problem
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free, and takes no time. Switching to plain water or unsweetened tea also saves money. The hard part is breaking the sweet-drink habit, including the sugar-free ones.
- Plain-language summary: People who drink two or more bottles of sugary drinks a day have about a 20% higher probability of dying during the same period than those who hardly drink any. Those who drink one to two a day are more than 10% higher. Switching to sugar-free doesn't show a benefit either: people who drink two or more glasses of artificially sweetened drinks a day are about 25% higher.
- Benefit: Two large U.S. cohort studies. These only record, no grouping, about 118,000 people and 36,000 deaths. Those who drink 2 or more servings a day have about 21% higher mortality risk than those who drink fewer than 1 serving per month (HR 1.21). Those who drink 1–2 servings a day are about 14% higher (HR 1.14). The European EPIC cohort, recording only, no grouping, 450,000 people across 10 countries. Comparing those who drink 2+ glasses a day with those who drink fewer than 1 glass per month: sugar-sweetened soft drinks about 8% higher (HR 1.08, 95% CI 1.01–1.16). Artificially sweetened beverages (sugar-free versions) about 26% higher (HR 1.26, 1.16–1.35). All soft drinks pooled about 17% higher (HR 1.17, 1.11–1.22).
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Malik VS et al. (2019). Long-Term Consumption of Sugar-Sweetened and Artificially Sweetened Beverages and Risk of Mortality in US Adults. Circulation. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.118.037401>; Mullee A, Romaguera D, Pearson-Stuttard J, et al. (2019). Association Between Soft Drink Consumption and Mortality in 10 European Countries. JAMA Internal Medicine, 179(11), 1479-1490. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2019.2478>
- Note: The controversy is over sugar-free drinks. The association for artificially sweetened drinks is actually stronger than for sugar-sweetened ones — this is very likely reverse causation: people who are already overweight or have diabetes switch to sugar-free. So don't use this to claim that artificial sweeteners are more toxic. But it does at least show that "switching to sugar-free fixes it" has no evidentiary support. Switching to plain water or unsweetened tea is the most reliable choice. Also, these studies are tracking only, without grouping trials, and people who drink more sugary drinks tend to have worse lifestyles overall, so the numbers may be inflated.

### 8. Don't chew betel nut
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free, takes no time, and saves the money spent on betel nut. The hard part is breaking the chewing habit.
- Plain-language summary: People who chew betel nut have about a 20% higher probability of dying during the same period than non-chewers. The risk of diabetes and metabolic syndrome is nearly 50% higher. Oral and esophageal cancer risks are counted separately.
- Benefit: A pooled analysis of 17 Asian studies, 388,000 people. Betel-nut chewers had about 21% higher mortality risk than non-chewers (relative risk 1.21, P=0.02). The mortality figure was based on 179,600 of those people. The risk of diabetes was about 47% higher (1.47). The risk of metabolic syndrome was about 51% higher (1.51). Betel nut itself is also a known risk factor for oral cancer and esophageal cancer.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Yamada T, Hara K, Kadowaki T (2013). Chewing betel quid and the risk of metabolic disease, cardiovascular disease, and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis. PLoS One, 8(8), e70679. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0070679>
- Note: The longer you chew and the more you chew each day, the higher the oral-cancer risk. After quitting, the risk declines over time. Betel nut containing tobacco is even more dangerous.

### 9. Switch household table salt to low-sodium salt (potassium salt)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A bag costs a few yuan more than ordinary salt. Switch when you buy it — takes no extra time. The taste is nearly unchanged.
- Plain-language summary: People who have had a stroke or who are over 60 with high blood pressure, switching household salt to low-sodium salt reduces the probability of death within five years by about 12%, and stroke by about 14%.
- Benefit: A randomized trial done in rural China, 20,995 people, all of whom had had a stroke or were hypertensive patients over 60, followed for 4.74 years. Result: the low-sodium-salt group had about 12% lower mortality risk than the regular-salt group (RR 0.88). Stroke about 14% lower (RR 0.86). Major cardiovascular events about 13% lower (RR 0.87). High-blood-potassium events showed no statistical difference between the two groups.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Neal B et al. (2021). Effect of Salt Substitution on Cardiovascular Events and Death. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2105675>; O'Donnell M et al. (2014). Urinary sodium and potassium excretion, mortality, and cardiovascular events. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1311889> (the controversy side, PURE)
- Note: Controversy. The PURE study only tracks long-term, no grouping. It reports: those excreting less than 3 g of sodium per day have about 27% higher risk of death plus cardiovascular events. Those excreting more than 7 g per day are about 15% higher. By its results, both too little and too much are bad; the middle is best. But in the Chinese rural randomized trial cited here, low-sodium salt replaces only part of the salt, so it does not push sodium that low. Also note that the trial subjects were high-risk older adults. Healthy young people get much less benefit from switching salt. Those with impaired kidney function, or those taking potassium-sparing drugs, should consult a doctor before switching.

### 10. Brush carefully, clean between teeth once a day, and replace missing teeth promptly
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Dental floss or interdental brushes cost tens of yuan per year; a professional cleaning is one or two hundred yuan each time. An extra two or three minutes a day. The hard part is making interdental cleaning a daily habit.
- Plain-language summary: People who use floss or interdental brushes daily have about a 10% lower probability of dying during the same period. Older adults who have lost all their teeth have about a 90% higher probability of dying. Those with fewer than 20 teeth left are about twice as high.
- Benefit: A Japanese study, 9,676 people, followed for 6 years. Those who used interdental cleaning tools had about 11% lower mortality risk (hazard ratio 0.89). Those who used tongue-coating cleaning tools were about 23% lower (0.77). Separately, pooled studies on older adults living in the community. Those with no teeth at all had about a 1.87-times higher chance of dying (odds ratio, 95% CI 1.35–2.59, the confidence interval). Those with fewer than 20 teeth were about 2.04 times (1.67–2.49).
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Wang K, Matsuyama Y, Kiuchi S, et al. (2026). Routine oral health practices and all-cause mortality. Journal of Dentistry. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdent.2026.106789>; Ko MJ, Seo S, So JS, et al. (2026). Deteriorated oral health and function as risk factors for physical disability and mortality in community-dwelling older adults: a systematic review and meta-analysis. European Geriatric Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s41999-025-01319-4>
- Note: Controversy. These studies only track and record. People with bad teeth tend to have worse overall health and economic status. Whether teeth dragged down the body or the body dragged down the teeth is unclear. But this action costs very little, and periodontitis and missing teeth themselves affect eating.

### 11. Walk 7,000–8,000 steps a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. 60–90 minutes a day, which can be split across commuting and grocery shopping. The hard part is doing it every day.
- Plain-language summary: People who walk 5,800 steps a day have about a 40% lower probability of dying during the same period than those who only walk 3,500. Reaching 7,800 steps is about 45% lower. Going higher still lowers it a bit more, but at a certain step count it stops dropping. For those over 60, 6,000–8,000 steps is enough.
- Benefit: A pooled analysis of 15 cohort studies, recording only, no grouping. These 15 studies total 47,471 people and 3,013 deaths. People were grouped into four tiers by daily steps; the lowest tier's median was 3,553 steps/day. The medians of the other three tiers were 5,801, 7,842, and 10,901 steps/day. These three tiers had about 40%, 45%, and 53% lower mortality risk than the lowest (HR 0.60, 0.55, 0.47). For those over 60, after reaching 6,000–8,000 steps, more walking yields diminishing returns. For those under 60, returns diminish after 8,000–10,000 steps. Another pooled analysis: benefit starts at about 3,867 steps/day. After that, each additional 1,000 steps/day reduces mortality risk by about 15%.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Paluch AE et al. (2022). Daily steps and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis of 15 international cohorts. Lancet Public Health. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(21)00302-9>; Banach M et al. (2023). The association between daily step count and all-cause and cardiovascular mortality: a meta-analysis. European Journal of Preventive Cardiology. <https://doi.org/10.1093/eurjpc/zwad229>
- Note: The 4,000-to-7,000 step range is the most cost-effective stretch — you don't have to force 10,000. Note that these studies only track and record: among those who walk the least are mixed in people who are already sick and can't walk much. They walk less because they're nearly done for, not dying early because they walk less. So the calculated gap is often inflated. But the trend of more-walking-is-better is clear. This entry and Entry 14 (150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity exercise per week) are about the same thing, just counted differently — meeting either one counts.

### 12. If you have high blood pressure or high cholesterol, take medication regularly as prescribed — don't stop on your own
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Centralized-procurement blood pressure medications and statins cost a few to a few dozen yuan per month. Taking them once a day takes little time. The hard part is continuing to take them — you can't stop just because you feel fine.
- Plain-language summary: For every 10 mmHg drop in blood pressure, the probability of dying drops about 13%, and stroke drops about 27%. Statins, for every 1-unit drop in LDL cholesterol, the probability of dying drops about 10%. Those who take their medication on schedule have a 30%–45% lower probability of dying than those who often miss doses.
- Benefit: Pooled randomized trials: for every 10 mmHg drop in systolic pressure, mortality risk drops about 13% (RR 0.87). Systolic pressure is the higher number in blood pressure. Major cardiovascular events drop about 20% (RR 0.80). Stroke drops about 27% (RR 0.73). Heart failure drops about 28% (RR 0.72). Statins lower low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL). For every 1.0 mmol/L drop in LDL, mortality risk drops about 10% (RR 0.90). The same drop yields about 22% fewer major vascular events (RR 0.78). Separately, pooled cohort studies, recording only, no grouping. Those who took at least 80% as prescribed had lower mortality risk than those who often missed doses. Statins about 45% lower (RR 0.55). Blood pressure medications about 29% lower (RR 0.71).
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Ettehad D et al. (2016). Blood pressure lowering for prevention of cardiovascular disease and death: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(15)01225-8>; Cholesterol Treatment Trialists' (CTT) Collaboration (2010). Efficacy and safety of more intensive lowering of LDL cholesterol: a meta-analysis of data from 170 000 participants in 26 randomised trials. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61350-5>; Chowdhury R et al. (2013). Adherence to cardiovascular therapy: a meta-analysis of prevalence and clinical consequences. European Heart Journal. <https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/eht295>
- Note: Only those whom a doctor has judged need medication should follow this — healthy people don't need to. The "sticking with medication" figures come from tracking records, and people who can stick with medication are themselves more disciplined, so the benefit is overstated. The earlier two sets of randomized-trial figures are more reliable. This section does not separately list a glucose-control entry: the benefit of pushing glucose lower on total mortality is less consistent than for blood pressure and cholesterol.

### 13. Sleep about 7 hours a night, with a fixed schedule
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. You need to set aside time to sleep; most people swap phone-scrolling time for sleep. The hard part is fixing your bedtime and wake time.
- Plain-language summary: People who chronically sleep less than 7 hours have about a 10% higher probability of dying during the same period. The more irregular the schedule, the higher the risk. The most regular sleepers are 20%–nearly 50% lower than the most irregular. That is, schedule regularity predicts mortality better than sleep duration.
- Benefit: A pooled analysis of 16 cohort studies, recording only, no grouping, 1.38 million people and 113,000 deaths. Short sleepers had about 12% higher mortality risk (RR 1.12). Long sleepers about 30% higher (RR 1.30). Another pooled analysis stratified by sleep duration: the 7-hour tier had the lowest risk. Below 7 hours, each hour less raises about 6% (RR 1.06). Above 7 hours, each hour more raises about 13% (RR 1.13). The UK Biobank has wristwatch records from about 61,000 people, recording only, no grouping. People were grouped into five tiers by schedule regularity. The four more-regular tiers had 20%–48% lower mortality risk than the most-irregular tier. Schedule regularity predicts mortality better than sleep duration. Another UK Biobank study used wristwatches to measure sleep timing in 51,562 people, of whom 3,853 developed cardiovascular disease during follow-up. "Social jet lag" refers to the difference in sleep midpoint between workdays and weekends. Those with social jet lag of 2 hours or more had about 30% higher cardiovascular disease risk (HR 1.30, 95% CI 1.11–1.54). This association also exists in people with normal sleep duration.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Cappuccio FP et al. (2010). Sleep duration and all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective studies. Sleep. <https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/33.5.585>; Yin J et al. (2017). Relationship of Sleep Duration With All-Cause Mortality and Cardiovascular Events: A Systematic Review and Dose-Response Meta-Analysis of Prospective Cohort Studies. JAHA. <https://doi.org/10.1161/JAHA.117.005947>; Windred DP et al. (2024). Sleep regularity is a stronger predictor of mortality risk than sleep duration: A prospective cohort study. Sleep. <https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsad253>; Kumar N, Krishnamurthy S (2026). Social jet lag is associated with incident cardiovascular disease independent of sleep duration and cardiac genetic risk. Journal of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.70133>
- Note: What needs changing is sleeping too little and schedule irregularity — no need to deliberately compress sleep time. The higher risk in long sleepers is mostly reverse causation: depression, chronic illness, and sleep apnea make people sleep more. Also, schedule regularity is supported by two cohort studies so far, both recording only, no grouping — alone they would be B-grade evidence. For how to recover after staying up late, see Entry 39 of this section (catch up the next night after staying up late).

### 14. Accumulate 150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity exercise per week — brisk walking counts
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. 20–45 minutes a day. The hard part is sticking with it long term.
- Plain-language summary: People who walk briskly 150–300 minutes per week have about a 30% lower probability of dying during the same period than those who don't exercise. Even those who don't meet the target still come in 20% lower. Three-to-five times the minimum is where the drop bottoms out, at about 40% lower. Going higher doesn't go lower, but does no harm.
- Benefit: A pooled analysis of cohort studies (recording only, no grouping): those at 1–2 times the guideline minimum had about 31% lower mortality risk than those who did not exercise (HR 0.69). This amount is 7.5–15 MET·h/week, roughly equivalent to brisk walking 150–300 minutes/week. Those below the minimum were also about 20% lower (HR 0.80). Those at 3–5 times the minimum were about 39% lower (HR 0.61) — the peak of the reduction. Going higher doesn't go lower, but does no harm (10+ times is HR 0.69). Another pooled analysis using accelerometer measurements: the most-active quarter had about 48% lower mortality risk than the least-active quarter (HR 0.52). An accelerometer is a device worn on the body that measures actual activity.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Arem H et al. (2015). Leisure time physical activity and mortality: a detailed pooled analysis of the dose-response relationship. JAMA Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2015.0533>; Ekelund U et al. (2019). Dose-response associations between accelerometry measured physical activity and sedentary time and all cause mortality: systematic review and harmonised meta-analysis. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.l4570>
- Note: This entry and Entry 11 (walk 7,000–8,000 steps a day) — pick one to do. Note that these studies only track and record. The studies that use instruments to measure activity have shorter follow-up and older participants, and the numbers mix in the "sick first, then inactive" component. The real benefit is not as large as "risk down to 0.52 times."

### 15. Play racket sports three times a week, 45 minutes each time
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Court fees are tens of yuan per session. About 2 hours per week. The hard part is rounding up people and a court, and doing it every week.
- Plain-language summary: People who regularly play tennis, badminton, or table tennis have about half the probability of dying during the same period as those who don't, and cardiovascular mortality is about 56% lower. Swimmers are about 28% lower. Running and soccer showed no difference in this dataset.
- Benefit: A UK cohort study of 80,306 people, comparing people who did a particular sport with people who did not. Those who played racket sports had about 47% lower mortality risk (hazard ratio 0.53, 95% CI 0.40–0.69, the confidence interval). Cardiovascular mortality about 56% lower (0.44, 0.24–0.83). Racket sports means tennis, badminton, table tennis. Swimmers had 0.72 and 0.59 for the two figures, aerobics 0.73 and 0.64, cycling 0.85. Running and soccer showed no statistically significant difference.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Oja P, Kelly P, Pedisic Z, et al. (2017). Associations of specific types of sports and exercise with all-cause and cardiovascular-disease mortality: a cohort study of 80 306 British adults. British Journal of Sports Medicine, 51(10), 812-817. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2016-096822>
- Note: Controversy. These studies only track and record. People who play sports are themselves healthier and more social. The running group showed no difference, which also suggests that what matters is "what kind of person picks what sport." Don't take this entry to mean running is useless — this section has another entry computed by total exercise volume.

### 16. Accumulate four or five minutes a day of fragmented vigorous activity like climbing stairs and walking fast to catch a ride
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free, and no need to carve out exercise time. The hard part is remembering to take the stairs and walk a bit faster, not to take the easy way.
- Plain-language summary: For people who otherwise don't exercise at all, just three bouts of one or two minutes of vigorous activity a day brings about a 40% lower probability of dying during the same period than those with zero, and cardiovascular mortality is nearly halved. Vigorous activity means things like climbing stairs and walking fast to catch a ride.
- Benefit: A UK Biobank study of 25,200 people who did not specifically exercise. Average follow-up 6.9 years, of whom 852 died. Some had 3 bouts of 1–2 minutes of fragmented vigorous activity per day. Compared to those with none, their mortality risk and cancer mortality risk fell 38%–40%. Cardiovascular mortality risk fell 48%–49%. Those who accumulated 4.4 minutes per day of this kind of activity had 26%–30% lower mortality risk and cancer mortality risk. Cardiovascular mortality risk was 32%–34% lower.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Stamatakis E, Ahmadi MN, Gill JMR, et al. (2022). Association of wearable device-measured vigorous intermittent lifestyle physical activity with mortality. Nature Medicine, 28, 2521-2529. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-022-02100-x>
- Note: Controversy. This study used wearable devices to measure activity, which is more reliable than self-reported questionnaires. But it still only tracks and records, with no grouping trial, and only 6.9 years of follow-up. The subjects were people who did not exercise at all — those already exercising regularly do not apply.

### 17. Do 30–60 minutes of strength training per week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Bodyweight squats and pushups are free. 1–2 sessions a week, 20–30 minutes each. The hard part is working it into your weekly schedule.
- Plain-language summary: People who do 30–60 minutes of strength training a week have a 10%–20% lower probability of dying during the same period than those who don't. Doing more doesn't help further. Combining strength and aerobic is most effective.
- Benefit: A pooled analysis of cohort studies (recording only, no grouping): those who did strength training had 10%–17% lower mortality risk than those who did not. More is not better — about 30–60 minutes per week gives the largest reduction, about 10%–20%, with no added benefit beyond that. Those who did both strength and aerobic had lower mortality risk than those who did neither.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Momma H et al. (2022). Muscle-strengthening activities are associated with lower risk and mortality in major non-communicable diseases: a systematic review and meta-analysis of cohort studies. British Journal of Sports Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2021-105061>
- Note: These studies only track and record, and exercise volume is self-reported. The "training too much backfires" segment has weak evidence — don't limit training volume on that basis. Strength training also benefits older adults through fall prevention and muscle preservation, which are covered in Section 1.

### 18. Don't sit too long without moving — get up and move once in a while
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free, and standing up a few times takes little time. The hard part is forgetting time once seated — set a reminder.
- Plain-language summary: Those who sit the most have 2.6 times the probability of dying during the same period as those who sit the least. Sitting for long unbroken stretches adds a separate risk. But 60–75 minutes of moderate-intensity activity per day essentially cancels out the excess risk from prolonged sitting.
- Benefit: A U.S. cohort study, recording only, no grouping, measured by accelerometer. People were grouped into quartiles by total daily sedentary time. The most-sedentary quartile had 2.63 times the mortality risk of the least-sedentary quartile (HR 2.63). Grouped into quartiles by duration of unbroken sitting: the longest-unbroken-sitting quartile was 1.96 times the shortest (HR 1.96). So total sitting and unbroken sitting are each independently associated. Another pooled analysis of over a million people, recording only, no grouping. Those who sat more than 8 hours a day and barely exercised had 1.59 times the mortality risk of those who sat under 4 hours and were most active. About 60–75 minutes of moderate-intensity activity per day can offset the excess risk from prolonged sitting. Even in the most-active group sitting more than 8 hours a day, HR was only 1.04, with no statistical difference.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Diaz KM et al. (2017). Patterns of Sedentary Behavior and Mortality in U.S. Middle-Aged and Older Adults: A National Cohort Study. Annals of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M17-0212>; Ekelund U et al. (2016). Does physical activity attenuate, or even eliminate, the detrimental association of sitting time with mortality? A harmonised meta-analysis of data from more than 1 million men and women. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)30370-1>
- Note: The reliable conclusion is: how much you sit matters less than how much you move, and when activity is sufficient the risk from prolonged sitting essentially disappears. Note that these studies only track and record, and those who sit the most include many who are already sick — "2.63 times risk" clearly contains "sick first, then sedentary." Also, in the same pooled analysis, the risk of more than 3 hours of TV per day exists across all activity levels; in the most active group, the threshold just shifts to 5 hours.

### 19. Eat less processed meat (ham, bacon, sausage, luncheon meat)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free, even saves money, and takes no time. The hard part is cutting back on a food you love.
- Plain-language summary: Those who eat the most ham, bacon, and sausage have about a 20% higher probability of dying during the same period than those who eat the least. Each additional daily serving of processed meat also raises the probability by about 20%.
- Benefit: A pooled analysis of cohort studies (recording only, no grouping): the group eating the most processed meat had about 23% higher mortality risk than the group eating the least (RR 1.23). Total red meat about 29% higher (RR 1.29). Unprocessed red meat about 10% higher (RR 1.10), but this item showed no statistical difference. Another pooled analysis computed by daily intake. Each additional daily serving of processed meat raised mortality risk by about 23% (RR 1.23). Each additional daily serving of red meat raised it about 10% (RR 1.10).
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Larsson SC, Orsini N (2014). Red meat and processed meat consumption and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis. American Journal of Epidemiology. <https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwt261>; Schwingshackl L et al. (2017). Food groups and risk of all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective studies. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition. <https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.117.153148>; Johnston BC et al. (2019). Unprocessed Red Meat and Processed Meat Consumption: Dietary Guideline Recommendations From the NutriRECS Consortium. Annals of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M19-1621> (the controversy side, NutriRECS guideline)
- Note: Controversy. The NutriRECS guideline used a widely adopted evidence-grading method (GRADE) to rate these studies as "not very reliable." It gave only a very weak recommendation: keep eating as you do. The two sides disagree on whether the evidence is hard enough, not on direction. No study finds processed meat beneficial. Unprocessed red meat has a small effect and no statistical difference. What to control is processed meat.

### 20. Drink less or no alcohol
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free, even saves money, and takes no time. The hard part is the slight awkwardness of declining drinks at social occasions.
- Plain-language summary: Above 100 g of pure alcohol per week, the more you drink, the earlier you die. 100 g of pure alcohol is about 2.5 L of beer. At age 40, those who drink 100–200 g per week lose about half a year; those drinking 200–350 g lose 1–2 years; above 350 g lose 4–5 years. For overall health, the most cost-effective amount is zero.
- Benefit: A pooled analysis of 83 prospective studies, recording only, no grouping, 600,000 current drinkers. The mortality-risk nadir is at no more than 100 g of pure alcohol per week. This is about 2.5 L of 5% beer, or 300 mL of 40% liquor. At age 40, those who drink 100–200 g per week lose about 6 months of life expectancy. Those drinking 200–350 g lose 1–2 years. Above 350 g lose 4–5 years. The above three are reported in life-years. The Global Burden of Disease Study GBD 2016, combining all health losses, finds the lowest-risk alcohol intake is 0 drinks/week. Another pooled analysis corrected for study-design bias. Those drinking 1.3–24 g per day had about 7% lower mortality risk than lifetime non-drinkers (RR 0.93). But this item showed no statistical difference. Those drinking 45–64 g per day were about 19% higher (RR 1.19). Those drinking 65 g or more per day were about 35% higher (RR 1.35).
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Wood AM et al. (2018). Risk thresholds for alcohol consumption: combined analysis of individual-participant data for 599 912 current drinkers in 83 prospective studies. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)30134-X>; GBD 2016 Alcohol Collaborators (2018). Alcohol use and burden for 195 countries and territories, 1990–2016: a systematic analysis for the Global Burden of Disease Study 2016. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)31310-2>; Zhao J et al. (2023). Association Between Daily Alcohol Intake and Risk of All-Cause Mortality: A Systematic Review and Meta-analyses. JAMA Network Open. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2023.6185>; Di Castelnuovo A et al. (2006). Alcohol dosing and total mortality in men and women: an updated meta-analysis of 34 prospective studies. Archives of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/archinte.166.22.2437> (the controversy side)
- Note: Controversy. The side arguing "a little drinking helps" is a pooled analysis of 34 long-term cohort studies. It reported: light drinkers' total mortality is up to 17%–18% lower. It gave amounts of no more than 4 drinks per day for men and 2 for women. Within this amount, heavier drinkers had lower mortality. The other side argues that the "non-drinking" group is contaminated with people who quit drinking for health reasons and people who are already in poor health. One pooled analysis specifically removed these confounders, and after adjustment the protective effect disappeared. The Global Burden of Disease Study also gives 0 as safest. The sound reading is: drinking a little does not noticeably shorten lifespan, drinking a lot definitely does. "Starting to drink for health" has no basis. For how to drink less, see Entry 22 of this section (count drinks first, then talk to a doctor). Daily drinkers cannot quit cold turkey on their own — see Entry 21 of this section (don't quit cold turkey).

### 21. Daily drinkers whose hands shake and heart races when they stop should not quit cold turkey
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: One psychiatry or addiction-medicine registration, a few yuan to a few dozen yuan. Severe cases need a few days of hospitalization.
- Plain-language summary: Heavy daily drinkers who suddenly stop experience withdrawal. Mild cases: shaking hands, sweating, racing heart, inability to sleep. Severe cases: seizures or delirium tremens — a medical emergency that can kill. Norway tracked over 30,000 people; those who had had delirium tremens had an 8% annual death rate afterward. If you want to quit, go to the hospital first, and tell them how much you drink each day.
- Benefit: A 2026 clinical review states: long-term heavy drinkers who suddenly stop or sharply reduce intake experience withdrawal syndrome, with manifestations including tremor, autonomic hyperactivity, anxiety, insomnia, perceptual disturbances, seizures, and delirium tremens — "common and potentially life-threatening" in ordinary medical practice. The Norwegian national registry cohort included 36,287 people diagnosed with alcohol dependence, alcohol withdrawal state, or delirium tremens from 2009 to 2015: annual mortality in the delirium tremens group 8.0%, withdrawal state group 5.0%, alcohol dependence group 3.6%. The standardized mortality ratio in the delirium tremens group was 9.8 (95% CI 8.9–10.7, the confidence interval), meaning the mortality risk is about 9.8 times that of the same-age general population.
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Caputo F, Lungaro L, Costanzini A, De Giorgio R, Addolorato G (2026). Alcohol withdrawal syndrome in hospitalized patients: a practical review. European Journal of Internal Medicine, 107103. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejim.2026.107103>; Bramness JG, Heiberg IH, Høye A, Rossow I (2023). Mortality and alcohol-related morbidity in patients with delirium tremens, alcohol withdrawal state or alcohol dependence in Norway: A register-based prospective cohort study. Addiction, 118(12), 2352–2359. <https://doi.org/10.1111/add.16297>
- Note: How to judge yourself: drinking almost every day, hands shaking and sweating and heart racing and can't sleep if you skip a day, needing a drink in the morning to feel right — if any of these is true, don't stop cold turkey on your own. Hospital routine is to use benzodiazepines to get through these days, while supplementing thiamine (vitamin B1). Graded B because the Norwegian cohort is about "what happens later to people who had delirium tremens," not "what happens if you quit cold turkey" — the connection is inferred. The alcohol still needs to be quit, just at a hospital. For how much per week counts as "a lot," see Entry 20 of this section (drink less or no alcohol); for how to drink less, see Entry 22 of this section (count drinks first, then talk to a doctor).

### 22. To drink less, first count how much you drank in a week, then talk to a doctor for a few minutes
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. Just record your drinks each day. Asking in clinic takes no extra money either.
- Plain-language summary: A doctor spends a few minutes reviewing your drinking, explains the risks, and helps set a goal — after a year, on average you drink 20 g less pure alcohol per week, about half a liter of beer. Talking longer does not add benefit. For those already unable to stop, two drugs are proven abroad to work: roughly one in every 12 treated no longer returns to heavy drinking.
- Benefit: 34 randomized trials and 15,197 people pooled: those receiving brief intervention drank 20 g less pure alcohol per week one year later than those receiving minimal or no intervention (95% CI 12 to 28 g less, the confidence interval; moderate-quality evidence). Baseline drinking in these studies averaged 244 g per week. Brief intervention means advice or lifestyle counseling of no more than 5 sessions totaling under 60 minutes; talking longer did not add benefit. On medication: 122 randomized trials and 22,803 people pooled, acamprosate: for every 12 treated (95% CI 8 to 26), one additional person did not return to any drinking; oral naltrexone 50 mg per day: for every 12 treated (8 to 26), one additional person did not return to heavy drinking.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Kaner EF, Beyer FR, Muirhead C, et al. (2018). Effectiveness of brief alcohol interventions in primary care populations. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 2, CD004148. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004148.pub4>; Jonas DE, Amick HR, Feltner C, et al. (2014). Pharmacotherapy for adults with alcohol use disorders in outpatient settings: a systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA, 311(18), 1889–1900. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2014.3628>
- Note: The benefit is graded "small" because the outcomes measured in these two pieces of evidence are how much was drunk, not mortality — by this book's rule, only surrogate endpoints means "small." Most of the medication trials were conducted abroad; subjects had mostly already met diagnostic criteria for alcohol dependence, and the trials all provided psychosocial interventions alongside, so the figures are "the additional benefit on top of that." Whether these two drugs are approved and can be prescribed in China depends on the doctor and the drug label — don't buy online yourself. For those already experiencing withdrawal, see Entry 21 of this section (don't quit cold turkey).

### 23. Eat a small handful of nuts every day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 28 g per day, about two to three hundred yuan per year. Eaten on the spot, takes no time.
- Plain-language summary: People who regularly eat nuts have about a 20% lower probability of dying during the same period than those who don't. The difference shows up at once a week, about 10% lower. Reaching a handful a day is where the difference is most pronounced.
- Benefit: Two U.S. cohort studies, 119,000 people, over 3 million person-years. "Person-years" means the number of people multiplied by their respective follow-up years. Compared to non-nut eaters: those eating less than once a week had about 7% lower mortality risk (hazard ratio 0.93, 95% CI 0.90–0.96, the confidence interval). Once a week about 11% lower (0.89, 0.86–0.93). 2–4 times a week about 13% lower (0.87, 0.83–0.90). 5–6 times a week about 15% lower (0.85, 0.79–0.91). 7+ times a week about 20% lower (0.80, 0.73–0.86).
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Bao Y, Han J, Hu FB, et al. (2013). Association of nut consumption with total and cause-specific mortality. New England Journal of Medicine, 369(21), 2001-2011. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1307352>
- Note: Buy plain, unsalted. Nuts are not low in calories — don't snack on them freely. Note that these studies only track a group long term, and nut eaters overall have better lifestyles, so discount the numbers somewhat.

### 24. Replace some red meat with fish and poultry
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free, and takes no time. This is swapping one meat for another, not buying more. The hard part is adjusting taste preferences.
- Plain-language summary: Two additional servings per week of processed meat or red meat raises the probability of dying during the same period by about 3%. Swapping for the same number of servings of fish or poultry shows no increase. This entry's effect is on the small side in this section — it's a swap to make in passing, not a battle to fight.
- Benefit: A pooled analysis of 6 U.S. cohort studies, nearly 30,000 people. Two additional weekly servings of processed meat raises mortality risk by about 3% (hazard ratio 1.03, 95% CI 1.02–1.05, the confidence interval). Two additional weekly servings of unprocessed red meat also about 3% higher (1.03, 1.01–1.05). The same intake swapped to poultry is 0.99 (0.97–1.02); swapped to fish is 0.99 (0.97–1.01). Neither showed a statistical difference.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Zhong VW, Van Horn L, Greenland P, et al. (2020). Associations of Processed Meat, Unprocessed Red Meat, Poultry, or Fish Intake With Incident Cardiovascular Disease and All-Cause Mortality. JAMA Internal Medicine, 180(4), 503-512. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2019.6969>
- Note: The difference from two servings per week is very small — don't count on swapping meat to add years. Spending the same money on meat, prioritize fish and poultry.

### 25. Replace some refined rice and white flour with whole grains
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Brown rice, oats, and whole-wheat flour cost a bit more than refined rice and white flour; takes no time. The hard part is adjusting to the texture.
- Plain-language summary: People who eat 90 g more whole grains per day have about a 17% lower probability of dying during the same period. 90 g is about three servings. Up to about 200 g per day, the decline continues.
- Benefit: A pooled analysis of cohort studies (recording only, no grouping): each additional 90 g of whole grains per day lowers mortality risk by about 17% (RR 0.83). 90 g is about 3 servings. This benefit continues up to 210–225 g per day. Another pooled analysis: each additional serving per day lowers about 8% (RR 0.92).
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Aune D et al. (2016). Whole grain consumption and risk of cardiovascular disease, cancer, and all cause and cause specific mortality: systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis of prospective studies. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.i2716>; Schwingshackl L et al. (2017). Food groups and risk of all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective studies. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition. <https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.117.153148>
- Note: No need to swap all to whole grains — swapping half puts you already in the most-benefit stretch. Note that these studies only track and record, and whole-grain eaters are healthier to begin with, so the numbers are inflated. Results across studies also vary widely (I² 83% — the higher this number, the more inconsistent the conclusions across studies).

### 26. Drink tea three or more times per week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Tens to hundreds of yuan per year. Brewing a cup takes a few minutes, not much time.
- Plain-language summary: People with a tea-drinking habit have about a 15% lower probability of dying during the same period. Counting from age 50, they live an average of 1.26 years longer and gain 1.41 years free of cardiovascular disease.
- Benefit: A Chinese study of 100,900 people (China-PAR), with half followed for at least 7.3 years. Those with a tea-drinking habit had about 15% lower mortality risk (hazard ratio 0.85, 95% CI 0.79–0.90, the confidence interval). Counting from age 50: those with a tea-drinking habit had 1.41 more years free of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, and 1.26 years more life expectancy.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Wang X, Liu F, Li J, et al. (2020). Tea consumption and the risk of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease and all-cause mortality: The China-PAR project. European Journal of Preventive Cardiology, 27(18), 1956-1963. <https://doi.org/10.1177/2047487319894685>; tea mycotoxins: Cui P et al. (2020). Quantitative analysis and dietary risk assessment of aflatoxins in Chinese post-fermented dark tea. Food and Chemical Toxicology. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fct.2020.111830>; Zhou H et al. (2022). Mycotoxins in Tea (Camellia sinensis (L.) Kuntze): Contamination and Dietary Exposure Profiling in the Chinese Population. Toxins. <https://doi.org/10.3390/toxins14070452>; green tea extract and liver injury: Hu J et al. (2018). The safety of green tea and green tea extract consumption in adults - Results of a systematic review. Regulatory Toxicology and Pharmacology. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.yrtph.2018.03.019>
- Note: The controversy: these studies only track and record, and among Chinese male tea drinkers the proportions who smoke and drink are high. The authors adjusted for some of this, but not cleanly. Also, don't drink it scalding — for hot drink temperature see the entry on hot drink temperature in this section. Some worry about mycotoxins in tea. Domestically, 158 dark-tea samples were tested, and 2 had detectable aflatoxin; at the amounts people drink, intake was below international acceptable limits. Another study tested 352 tea samples for 16 mycotoxins; only ochratoxin A in dark tea exceeded the limit on average. At the amounts Chinese people drink, neither study found dietary risk. Store tea in a dry place; don't drink damp, moldy tea. Green tea extract capsules are a different matter: a high concentration of catechins swallowed at once can injure the liver, while brewed tea does not have this problem. So this entry refers only to brewed tea — don't substitute extract supplements.

### 27. Drink three to four cups of coffee a day, without sugar and without whipped cream
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Brewing it yourself costs one or two yuan per day. Brewing a cup takes a few minutes, not much time.
- Plain-language summary: People who drink three to four cups of coffee a day have about a 17% lower probability of dying during the same period than those who don't drink coffee.
- Benefit: A review combined 201 meta-analyses, whose underlying original studies all only record, no grouping. Result: those who drink 3–4 cups of coffee per day have 17% lower mortality risk than non-drinkers (relative risk 0.83, 95% CI 0.79–0.88, the confidence interval).
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Poole R, Kennedy OJ, Roderick P, et al. (2017). Coffee consumption and health: umbrella review of meta-analyses of multiple health outcomes. BMJ, 359, j5024. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.j5024>
- Note: The controversy: the original authors note that the evidence is almost entirely from tracking records, and add that "reliable randomized controlled trials are needed to determine causality." Meaning you'd need to randomly split people into two groups to compare — tracking records alone can't establish causation. When implementing, drink black coffee — adding sugar and whipped cream cancels out the benefit. Pregnant women, and people with arrhythmia, anxiety, or insomnia, do not apply.

### 28. Eat enough fruits and vegetables — 5 servings (about 400 g) a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A few yuan to a dozen yuan per day. Some time each day for washing and cutting. The hard part is buying every day and eating enough every day.
- Plain-language summary: Each additional 200 g of fruits and vegetables per day lowers the probability of dying during the same period by about 10%. Those who eat 5 servings a day are about 13% lower than those who eat only 2. Going higher doesn't lower further.
- Benefit: A pooled analysis of cohort studies (recording only, no grouping): each additional 200 g of fruits and vegetables per day lowers mortality risk by about 10% (RR 0.90). This benefit continues up to 800 g per day. Two large U.S. cohort studies plus a pooled analysis of 26 studies: those who eat 5 servings a day have about 13% lower mortality risk than those who eat only 2 (HR 0.87). The best mix is 2 servings of fruit plus 3 servings of vegetables; more does not lower further.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Aune D et al. (2017). Fruit and vegetable intake and the risk of cardiovascular disease, total cancer and all-cause mortality: a systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis of prospective studies. International Journal of Epidemiology. <https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyw319>; Wang DD et al. (2021). Fruit and Vegetable Intake and Mortality: Results From 2 Prospective Cohort Studies of US Men and Women and a Meta-Analysis of 26 Cohort Studies. Circulation. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.120.048996>
- Note: Eating 5 servings is enough; no need to push to 10. The two studies agree on this. Note that these studies only track and record, with obvious confounding: those who eat more vegetables differ in income, education, and exercise. So "about 10% lower risk" is already the upper bound.

### 29. Eat less ultra-processed food (chips, instant noodles, pastries, fast food)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free, just swap what you buy. You have to cook yourself or spend time picking less-processed ingredients, which takes time every day. The hard part is fighting the "convenient is best" inertia long term.
- Plain-language summary: Those who eat the most ultra-processed food have about a 20% higher probability of dying during the same period than those who eat the least, and cardiovascular mortality is 50% higher. However, this evidence is rated as uncertain, so discount the numbers.
- Benefit: A review combining multiple pooled analyses, whose underlying original studies all only record, no grouping. Result: those who eat more ultra-processed food have about 21% higher mortality risk than those who eat less (RR 1.21), and about 50% higher cardiovascular mortality (RR 1.50). The evidence grading for these two was "highly suggestive" and "convincing" respectively. But by the standard evidence-grading method (GRADE), certainty is low or very low.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Lane MM et al. (2024). Ultra-processed food exposure and adverse health outcomes: umbrella review of epidemiological meta-analyses. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-077310>
- Note: Controversy on three points. First, this classification (NOVA) lumps foods with very different nutrition into one category. Second, it overlaps heavily with sugary drinks and processed meat (Entries 7 and 19), so it's unclear which is doing the work. Third, by the standard evidence-grading method (GRADE), the level is also low. So how much additional benefit this entry brings after doing those two is uncertain. The other side's evidence is the low grading itself from the same review; no original study reaches the opposite conclusion. So first do Entries 7 and 19 (no sugary drinks, less processed meat), then look at this entry.

### 30. Don't burn coal or wood for cooking or heating — switch to electric or gas
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Rural households need to replace stoves plus fuel costs, a few hundred to over a thousand yuan per year. A one-time switch, no ongoing time cost. Urban households are mostly already electric or gas.
- Plain-language summary: People who burn coal or wood for cooking have about a 10% higher probability of dying during the same period; for heating with coal or wood about 14% higher. Those who have already switched to electric or gas have a 13%–33% lower probability of dying than those still burning coal or wood.
- Benefit: A Chinese chronic-disease cohort study, recording only, no grouping, 271,000 adults without cardiovascular disease. Those who used solid fuels like coal or wood for cooking had about 11% higher mortality risk than those using electric or gas (HR 1.11). Those who used solid fuels for heating about 14% higher (HR 1.14). Those who had switched from solid fuels to clean fuels, compared with those still burning, were about 13% lower for cooking (HR 0.87) and about 33% lower for heating (HR 0.67). Outdoor PM2.5 is a separate account. A pooled analysis of 104 cohort studies: each 10 µg/m³ increase in long-term exposure concentration raises natural-cause mortality risk by about 8% (RR 1.08). Natural-cause means non-accidental disease death.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Yu K et al. (2018). Association of Solid Fuel Use With Risk of Cardiovascular and All-Cause Mortality in Rural China. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.2151>; Chen J, Hoek G (2020). Long-term exposure to PM and all-cause and cause-specific mortality: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Environment International. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envint.2020.105974>
- Note: These studies only track and record. Households that switched fuel also tend to be wealthier, so "risk down to 0.67 times" mixes in household-income effects. As for outdoor PM2.5, individuals can do little — basically move, wear a mask, use an air purifier. Air purifiers have no mortality-endpoint study, so this section gives no numbers.

### 31. Let hot things cool before drinking — don't drink scalding tea, soup, or coffee
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free, two or three extra minutes each time. The hard part is holding back when craving the drink.
- Plain-language summary: Drinking very hot tea gives 8 times the risk of esophageal cancer compared with drinking warm tea. Even just "hot" gives 2 times. Drinking within less than 2 minutes of pouring carries 5 times the risk of waiting 4+ minutes.
- Benefit: In a high-incidence esophageal-cancer area of northern Iran, 300 patients were compared with 571 people who did not have the disease. Below, all comparisons are with drinkers of warm tea. Drinkers of "hot" tea had about 2.07 times the chance of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (odds ratio, 95% CI 1.28–3.35, the confidence interval). Drinkers of "very hot" about 8.16 times (3.93–16.9). Those who drank within less than 2 minutes of pouring had about 5.41 times the rate of those who waited 4+ minutes (2.63–11.1).
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Islami F, Pourshams A, Nasrollahzadeh D, et al. (2009). Tea drinking habits and oesophageal cancer in a high risk area in northern Iran: population based case-control study. BMJ, 338, b929. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.b929>; Loomis D, Guyton KZ, Grosse Y, et al. (2016). Carcinogenicity of drinking coffee, mate, and very hot beverages. Lancet Oncology, 17(7), 877-878. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(16)30239-X>
- Note: The International Agency for Research on Cancer classifies beverages above 65 °C as Group 2A, meaning probably carcinogenic. The same assessment found coffee itself not carcinogenic. China's Chaoshan and Taihang Mountain regions have high esophageal cancer rates, linked to the habit of drinking things scalding hot.

### 32. Get some sun during the day — don't go all day without light
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. Ten-plus minutes a day, on the commute or during lunch break is enough.
- Plain-language summary: Women who deliberately avoid the sun have a life expectancy 0.6 to 2.1 years shorter than those who get the most sun. Non-smokers who avoid the sun have a life expectancy similar to the heaviest smokers among those who get the most sun.
- Benefit: A Swedish study of 29,500 women, followed for 20 years. Compared with the group that got the most sun, those who deliberately avoided the sun had a life expectancy 0.6 to 2.1 years shorter. The authors wrote "non-smokers who avoided the sun had a life expectancy similar to the smokers in the most sun-exposed group."
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Lindqvist PG, Epstein E, Nielsen K, et al. (2016). Avoidance of sun exposure as a risk factor for major causes of death: a competing risk analysis of the Melanoma in Southern Sweden cohort. Journal of Internal Medicine, 280(4), 375-387. <https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.12496>
- Note: The controversy: Sweden is at high latitude with little sun, so the conclusions may not transfer directly to China. And those who avoid the sun may already be frail or reluctant to go out. Also, don't sunbathe to the point of sunburn; at midday with strong light, still cover up, because sun exposure raises skin cancer risk.

### 33. Keep BMI in the 20–25 range; lose weight if overweight
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. Diet and exercise take time every day. This is the most willpower-intensive entry in the section: long-term control of food intake and getting moving; maintaining after loss is harder than losing.
- Plain-language summary: The probability of dying is lowest in the BMI 20–25 range. At BMI 27.5–30 it is about 20% higher, 30–35 about 45% higher, 35–40 nearly double. East Asians are more sensitive to overweight — for each 5-point increase in BMI, risk rises about 40%.
- Benefit: An individual-participant-data pooled analysis of 239 prospective studies (recording only, no grouping). This analysis kept only three categories: never-smokers, those without chronic disease at enrollment, and those who survived 5 years past enrollment. Result: mortality risk was lowest at BMI 20–25. BMI 25–27.5 about 7% higher (HR 1.07). 27.5–30 about 20% higher (HR 1.20). 30–35 about 45% higher (HR 1.45). 35–40 about 94% higher (HR 1.94). 40–60 is 2.76 times (HR 2.76). For those with BMI 25 or above, each 5 kg/m² increase raised East Asian population risk by about 39% (HR 1.39).
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Global BMI Mortality Collaboration (2016). Body-mass index and all-cause mortality: individual-participant-data meta-analysis of 239 prospective studies in four continents. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)30175-1>; Flegal KM et al. (2013). Association of all-cause mortality with overweight and obesity using standard body mass index categories: a systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2012.113905> (the controversy side)
- Note: Controversy. One side argues "being a bit plump actually makes you live longer." They pooled studies and got: overweight people have about 6% lower mortality risk, mild obesity about 5% lower. The disagreement is mainly over whether to exclude three categories of data: smokers, those already ill, and the first years after enrollment. The reason to exclude is that seriously ill people lose weight first. The pooled analysis of 239 studies excluded these three categories, and the overweight risk reappeared. Also note that no randomized trial proves that weight loss itself lowers total mortality. The benefit above is from horizontal comparison of people with different body sizes, not evidence that losing weight reduces risk by that much. Those trying to lose weight need not tinker with meal timing — breakfast and 16:8 intermittent fasting offer no extra benefit, see Section 6, Entry 26 (don't count on breakfast or 16:8 to control your weight).
### 34. Eat chili peppers four or more times per week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free, and takes no time.
- Plain-language summary: People who eat chili peppers four or more times a week have about a 23% lower probability of dying during the same period than those who rarely eat them, and cardiovascular mortality is about a third lower.
- Benefit: An Italian study of 22,800 people, with half followed for at least 8.2 years; 1,236 died in total. Those who ate chili peppers 4+ times per week had about 23% lower mortality risk than those who rarely ate them (hazard ratio 0.77, 95% CI 0.66–0.90, the confidence interval). Cardiovascular mortality about 34% lower (0.66, 0.50–0.86).
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Bonaccio M, Di Castelnuovo A, Costanzo S, et al. (2019). Chili Pepper Consumption and Mortality in Italian Adults. Journal of the American College of Cardiology, 74(25), 3139-3149. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2019.09.068>
- Note: The controversy: this is tracking data in the context of the Mediterranean diet, and those who eat spicy food may have an overall diet closer to local tradition. Also, those with acid reflux, hemorrhoids, or irritable bowel syndrome find that spicy food worsens symptoms — don't force it.

### 35. Drink one or two servings of milk or yogurt a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A few yuan per day, takes no time.
- Plain-language summary: People who drink two or more servings of milk or yogurt a day have about a 17% lower probability of dying during the same period than those who don't.
- Benefit: The PURE cohort covered 21 countries, 136,000 people, average follow-up 9.1 years, 6,796 deaths in total. Those who ate two or more dairy servings per day had about 17% lower mortality risk than those who ate none (hazard ratio 0.83, 95% CI 0.72–0.96, the confidence interval), trend P=0.0052.
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Dehghan M, Mente A, Rangarajan S, et al. (2018). Association of dairy intake with cardiovascular disease and mortality in 21 countries from five continents (PURE): a prospective cohort study. Lancet, 392(10161), 2288-2297. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)31812-9>
- Note: The controversy: PURE includes many low- and middle-income countries, where being able to drink milk every day itself indicates decent household finances — this confounder is hard to fully remove. Also, those with lactose intolerance should switch to yogurt or low-lactose milk; no need to force cow's milk.

### 36. Eggs need not be given up, but don't eat three or four a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free, and takes no time.
- Plain-language summary: Each additional half-egg a day raises the probability of dying during the same period by about 7%. Swapping that half for egg whites, fish, poultry, or nuts lowers each mortality outcome instead. No need to give up eggs — just don't eat three or four a day.
- Benefit: A U.S. cohort study of 521,000 people, 129,000 deaths in total. Each additional half-egg per day raised mortality risk by about 7% (hazard ratio 1.07, 95% CI 1.06–1.08, the confidence interval). Swapping that half-egg for an equal amount of egg whites, poultry, fish, dairy, or nuts/legumes lowered mortality risk further; cardiovascular, cancer, and respiratory mortality were all lower as well.
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Zhuang P, Wu F, Mao L, et al. (2021). Egg and cholesterol consumption and mortality from cardiovascular and different causes in the United States: A population-based cohort study. PLoS Medicine, 18(2), e1003508. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1003508>
- Note: Significant controversy: there are also long-term cohort studies with large samples, and pooled analyses of multiple studies, that found no link between eggs and mortality. The U.S. Dietary Guidelines have removed the daily cholesterol cap. What to avoid is only eating three or four a day; one a day need not be a worry.

### 37. If conditions allow, take baths — don't just shower
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A bit more in water and electricity. Half an hour each time.
- Plain-language summary: People who take a bath almost every day have about a 28% lower risk of cardiovascular events than those who bathe fewer than twice a week, and intracerebral hemorrhage about 46% lower.
- Benefit: A Japanese study of 30,000 people, followed for 19 years, with 2,097 cardiovascular events in total. Those who bathed almost every day had about 28% lower cardiovascular event risk than those who bathed no more than twice a week (hazard ratio 0.72, 95% CI 0.62–0.84, the confidence interval). Coronary heart disease about 35% lower (0.65, 0.45–0.94). Stroke about 26% lower (0.74, 0.62–0.87). Intracerebral hemorrhage about 46% lower (0.54, 0.40–0.73).
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Ukai T, Iso H, Yamagishi K, et al. (2020). Habitual tub bathing and risks of incident coronary heart disease and stroke. Heart, 106(10), 732-737. <https://doi.org/10.1136/heartjnl-2019-315752>
- Note: The controversy: this was done in a Japanese population and bathtub culture; most Chinese households don't have the conditions for daily baths. Also, the water shouldn't be too hot, and don't soak too long. For older adults and those with cardiovascular disease, water that's too hot and soaking too long are actually dangerous — every year many older adults in Japan die suddenly in the bathtub.

### 38. Keep naps under half an hour, don't exceed one hour; if you can't get by without sleeping an hour or two, get checked for the cause
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. Set an alarm before sleeping; almost no willpower needed.
- Plain-language summary: People who nap no more than one hour have no increase in mortality and chronic disease risk, and are slightly more clear-headed. Those who nap more than one hour have about 30% higher coronary heart disease risk and about 20% higher diabetes and obesity. So set an alarm and wake yourself at half an hour. If you have to sleep an hour or two every day to get by, that is a signal to get your body checked.
- Benefit: An umbrella review pooled 16 meta-analyses and 244 health outcomes. An umbrella review is a synthesis of existing pooled studies. The conclusion: those who nap less than 60 minutes have no increase in total mortality and chronic disease risk, and the cognitive improvement is most pronounced (SMD 0.69, 95% CI 0.37–1.00, the confidence interval). A short nap of 20 to 30 minutes gives the greatest improvement in motor performance (SMD 0.99, 0.67–1.31). Those who nap more than 60 minutes have about 30% higher coronary heart disease risk, and about 20% higher diabetes and obesity risk. Another study had 1,338 older adults over 56 wear wristwatches to measure naps objectively, not by self-report. Follow-up up to 19 years, with 926 deaths. Each additional hour of napping raised mortality risk by about 13% (HR 1.13, 95% CI 1.04–1.23). Each additional nap per day raised it about 7% (HR 1.07, 1.02–1.13). Those who napped in the morning had about 30% higher risk than those who napped in early afternoon (HR 1.30, 1.03–1.64).
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Du P, Li J, Hua Z, et al. (2026). Multiple Health Outcomes of Daytime Napping: A Comprehensive Umbrella Review. Public Health Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.3389/phrs.2026.1609013>; Gao C, Cai R, Zheng X, et al. (2026). Objectively Measured Daytime Napping Patterns and All-Cause Mortality in Older Adults. JAMA Network Open. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.7938>; Dashti HS et al. (2021). Genetic determinants of daytime napping and effects on cardiometabolic health. Nature Communications. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-20585-3>
- Note: Controversy. The two above are recording-only, no-grouping studies. Those who nap longer are more likely to have sleep apnea, anemia, hypothyroidism, depression, or some other chronic condition. So how much of "longer nappers die more" is caused by napping itself is unclear. Causal inference using genes (Mendelian randomization, 453,000 plus 541,000 people) supports only one thing: the more frequent the nap, the slightly higher the blood pressure and waist circumference. It did not find a causal effect of napping on coronary heart disease or diabetes. So don't treat "napping less" as a mortality-reduction tool. The "get checked" half is the author's recommendation and is C-grade: if you're so sleepy in the daytime that you must sleep long, first check your nighttime sleep — snoring and breathing pauses during sleep warrant a sleep apnea check, then a complete blood count and thyroid. For how a short nap refreshes, see Section 3, Entry 11 (sleep 10 minutes when sleepy in the afternoon). For how long to sleep at night, see Entry 13 of this section (about 7 hours a night).

### 39. After staying up late, catch up on sleep the next night — don't save it for the weekend
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. The extra hour or two of sleep is something you wanted anyway.
- Plain-language summary: People who don't catch up the night after short sleep have about 15% higher mortality risk than those with regular schedules, and severe short-sleep without catch-up about 42% higher. Those who caught up showed no risk increase. So after staying up late, go to bed early the next night.
- Benefit: UK Biobank, 85,618 people, mean age 61.8, wristwatch-recorded daily sleep, median follow-up 8 years. The study first calculated "short-sleep nights" based on each person's own sleep need — defined as sleeping 2.5+ hours less than one's need. The first night after a short-sleep night was defined as the "catch-up night" — sleeping more than one's need on that night counted as catching up. Five patterns were derived. Compared with regular-schedule people, those who slept short and didn't catch up had about 15% higher all-cause mortality (HR 1.15, 95% CI 1.01–1.31, the confidence interval). Those with cumulative short-sleep exceeding 3.5 hours without catch-up about 42% higher (HR 1.42, 1.24–1.63). The two catch-up groups showed no significant association, with short-sleep-then-catch-up at HR 1.12 (0.98–1.28). Among those who were already short sleepers, short-sleep-without-catch-up was about 19% higher (1.19, 1.01–1.40), and severe-short-sleep-without-catch-up about 38% higher (1.38, 1.17–1.63). This result was also replicated in another U.S. health survey of 4,586 people.
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Li X, Zhang M, Li Z, et al. (2026). Acute sleep rebound following sleep restriction is associated with reduced mortality risk. Nature Communications. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-72461-1>
- Note: This is a recording-only, no-grouping study. Those who can't catch up may be busier or in worse health to begin with, and this confounder can't be fully removed. "The catch-up groups showed no risk increase" only means no difference was detected — their point estimates are still greater than 1, so catching up does not mean everything is fine. So make up lost sleep as soon as possible, and don't take this as license to stay up freely. Catch-up in this study means sleeping more on the first night after short sleep — it does not include saving it for the weekend. Making "weekday sleep loss, weekend catch-up" a fixed weekly rhythm is called social jet lag, itself associated with cardiovascular disease — see Entry 13 of this section (about 7 hours a night, fixed schedule). For keeping wake time fixed on weekends see Section 3, Entry 2 (fix wake time, even on weekends); it does not conflict with this entry: this entry tells you to go to bed early that night, not to sleep until noon the next day.

### 40. The longer you work night shifts, the higher your cardiovascular risk — if you can transfer, do it early
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=中 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Transferring or changing jobs may bring in less money, and the night-shift differential goes away too. Job hunting takes time, and the decision is hard. If you take this accounting into account when choosing a job, the cost is zero.
- Plain-language summary: Night-shift workers have about 13% more cardiovascular disease than non-night-shift workers, and about 27% more cardiovascular deaths. And the longer you do it, the more: each additional 5 years of night shifts brings about 7% more cardiovascular disease. So this accounting is done by years — if you can transfer, do it early. As for night shifts causing cancer, the evidence is much softer than the popular claims.
- Benefit: A pooled analysis of 23 cohort studies, recording only, no grouping. Night-shift workers have about 13% higher cardiovascular event risk (RR 1.13, 95% CI 1.10–1.16, the confidence interval). Cardiovascular death about 27% higher (RR 1.27, 1.18–1.36). By years: each additional 5 years of night shifts raises cardiovascular disease incidence by about 7% (RR 1.07, 1.04–1.09), and cardiovascular death by about 5% (RR 1.05, 1.03–1.06). By disease: coronary heart disease incidence about 22% higher (1.22, 1.16–1.28), coronary heart disease death about 22% higher (1.22, 1.10–1.36), ischemic heart disease death about 39% higher (1.39, 1.06–1.84), stroke death about 49% higher (1.49, 1.04–2.12). Stroke incidence was not significant (1.06, 0.95–1.18).
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Xi J, Ma W, Tao Y, et al. (2025). Association between night shift work and cardiovascular disease: a systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis. Frontiers in Public Health. <https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2025.1668848>; Esposito G, Bravi F, Santucci C, et al. (2025). Night shift work and breast cancer risk in healthcare workers: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Occupational Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1093/occmed/kqaf040>; Shen QM, Li ZY, Tan YT, et al. (2026). Night shift work and risk of total and site-specific cancer: results from a prospective cohort study among Chinese men. Scandinavian Journal of Work, Environment & Health. <https://doi.org/10.5271/sjweh.4290>; Czeisler CA, Johnson MP, Duffy JF, et al. (1990). Exposure to bright light and darkness to treat physiologic maladaptation to night work. New England Journal of Medicine, 322(18), 1253-1259. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199005033221801>
- Note: These are all recording-only, no-grouping studies. Night-shift workers already differ from others in smoking, weight, and income, and adjustment is incomplete. As for night shifts causing cancer, the evidence is much softer than popular claims. In a pooled analysis of 12 studies and 12,132 breast cancer cases, those who had worked night shifts had only about 5% higher risk, with the confidence interval crossing 1 (RR 1.05, 0.96–1.14). The 20+ year tier was about 25% higher (1.25, 1.01–1.55), but in influence analysis this item was no longer significant, and after correcting for possible publication bias it approached 1; the original wording is that this association is "far from established." Another study following Chinese men for 16.1 years with 8,202 cancer cases found no association between night shifts and total cancer or most common cancers; only pancreatic cancer in the cumulative 11-to-20-year tier was about 59% higher (HR 1.59, 1.09–2.31). So this entry is counted by cardiovascular accounting, not cancer. Not everyone should leave night-shift work — this accounting by years is one you have to do yourself. Those already working nights should first fix what can be fixed: quitting smoking see Entry 1 of this section (quit smoking, the earlier the better); blood pressure and lipids see Entry 12 of this section (if you have high blood pressure or high cholesterol, take medication regularly as prescribed); for how to catch up on sleep after a night shift see Entry 39 of this section (catch up the next night after staying up late). Two other strategies to try: concentrate eating in the daytime, and use bright light plus strict daytime darkness to shift your biological clock. The latter is the only method proven to actually shift the biological clock. Under ordinary indoor lighting, after six consecutive night shifts the biological clock does not move at all; with 7,000 to 12,000 lux of bright light plus near-total daytime darkness, it shifts fully in four days. **Bright light at night, strict darkness during daytime sleep, and sunglasses on the way home to block morning light — all three are indispensable**; skipping the last one undoes everything. The endpoints for both strategies go only to metabolic markers and sleep; no trial has tested whether following them lowers cardiovascular risk. Returning to a daytime schedule on days off pushes the clock back. Doses, raw numbers, and other sources, along with how the body tells time, are in [docs/生物钟和夜班.md](../docs/生物钟和夜班.md).

### 41. Buy prepackaged cooking oil with an SC number, not loose self-pressed peanut oil from small workshops
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Buy from a different place, the cost is about the same. Just check whether the package has a food production license number starting with SC. The hard part is persuading older relatives used to buying self-pressed oil.
- Plain-language summary: Peanuts that get damp and moldy grow aflatoxin, a confirmed carcinogen that mainly injures the liver. After Guangzhou regulated small workshops, residents who often ate self-pressed oil had about a 35% drop in abnormal liver function. In Guangxi, pregnant women who ate self-pressed peanut oil had about 90% higher risk of low birth weight, and about 80% higher prematurity.
- Benefit: Aflatoxin is a Group 1 carcinogen (definitely carcinogenic to humans) as classified by the International Agency for Research on Cancer, mainly causing liver cancer. A Guangzhou study measured aflatoxin B1 in small-workshop self-pressed peanut oil from 2010 to 2022. The median was 1.29 μg/kg, with the middle half of samples between 0.12 and 6.58. After local small-workshop management regulations were introduced, the toxin in oil dropped immediately by 2.865 μg/kg, and then by another 2.593 per year. The proportion of residents with abnormal liver function in areas where self-pressed oil was commonly eaten also dropped (PR 0.650, 95% CI 0.469–0.902, the confidence interval), about 35% lower. In a Guangxi cohort of 1,611 pregnant women, 81.7% ate self-pressed peanut oil. Eaters had about 90% higher risk of low birth weight (aOR 1.9, 1.1–3.2), and about 80% higher prematurity (aOR 1.8, 1.1–3.0).
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Lei J, Li Y, Wang Y, et al. (2024). The impact of small food workshops management regulations on aflatoxin B1 in home-made peanut oil and the liver function of high-consumption area residents: an interrupted time series study in Guangzhou, China. Frontiers in Public Health, 12. <https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2024.1484414>; Zhong Y, Lu H, Jiang Y, et al. (2024). Effect of homemade peanut oil consumption during pregnancy on low birth weight and preterm birth outcomes: a cohort study in Southwestern China. Global Health Action, 17. <https://doi.org/10.1080/16549716.2024.2336312>; IARC (2012). Chemical Agents and Related Occupations. IARC Monographs on the Evaluation of Carcinogenic Risks to Humans, Vol 100F. <https://publications.iarc.fr/123>; National Health and Family Planning Commission, China Food and Drug Administration (2017). National Food Safety Standard Maximum Levels of Mycotoxins in Foods (GB 2761-2017)
- Note: Both studies only track and record, with no grouping control. The endpoint in the Guangzhou study was liver function tests, not liver cancer or death. The median toxin level in self-pressed oil is actually below the national limit of 20 μg/kg for peanut oil; the risk comes from long-term consumption and individual high batches. The national limit applies to all peanut oil, but prepackaged oil must pass production licensing and factory inspection, while small workshops are managed under provincial-level regulations with far less sampling. The beneficiary of this entry is mainly you and the family members who eat with you.

### 42. Use vegetable oil instead of lard and butter for cooking; you don't need to switch oils for health, and don't count on flaxseed oil to protect your heart
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No extra money. Vegetable oil is already the mainstay in most households. The hard part is changing the taste habit of cooking or mixing rice with lard.
- Plain-language summary: Eating less saturated fat like lard and butter, and replacing with vegetable oil, reduces heart disease and stroke by about 17%, but total mortality shows no change. There is no evidence of any difference among soybean oil, corn oil, and rapeseed oil. Eating too much flaxseed oil also shows no reduction in heart disease risk.
- Benefit: A Cochrane pooled analysis of 15 randomized trials and 56,675 people, each running at least two years. Eating less saturated fat reduced cardiovascular events by about 17% (RR 0.83, 0.70–0.98). Total mortality was essentially unchanged (RR 0.96, 0.90–1.03), and cardiovascular mortality also unchanged (RR 0.95, 0.80–1.12). Replacing with polyunsaturated fat (the main component of soybean and corn oil) and replacing with carbohydrates showed no difference. Replacing with monounsaturated fat (the main component of rapeseed and olive oil) had too little data to draw conclusions. Another Cochrane pooled analysis of 19 randomized trials and 6,461 people, increasing omega-6 (linoleic acid) gave RR 1.00 (0.88–1.12) for total mortality, and RR 0.97 (0.81–1.15) for cardiovascular events — no benefit and no harm. A third pooled analysis of 86 randomized trials and 162,796 people, increasing plant-derived omega-3 (the main component of flaxseed oil and perilla oil) gave RR 1.01 (0.84–1.20) for total mortality and RR 1.00 (0.82–1.22) for coronary heart disease events — essentially no effect.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Hooper L, Martin N, Jimoh OF, et al. (2020). Reduction in saturated fat for cardiovascular disease. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, (5), CD011737. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD011737.pub3>; Hooper L, Al-Khudairy L, Abdelhamid AS, et al. (2018). Omega-6 fats for the primary and secondary prevention of cardiovascular disease. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, (11), CD011094. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD011094.pub4>; Abdelhamid AS, Brown TJ, Brainard JS, et al. (2020). Omega-3 fatty acids for the primary and secondary prevention of cardiovascular disease. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, (3), CD003177. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD003177.pub5>; Chinese Nutrition Society (2022). Chinese Dietary Guidelines (2022). People's Medical Publishing House
- Note: Controversy: some argue that the omega-6 in soybean oil and corn oil is pro-inflammatory when consumed in excess, so we should eat less and switch oils. The omega-6 randomized trials above showed no harm, and the authors self-rate the evidence quality as low. The benefit magnitude is graded as medium based on cardiovascular events being 17% lower; total mortality is unchanged. Whatever oil you use, control the amount — the dietary guidelines suggest 25 to 30 g of cooking oil per person per day.
