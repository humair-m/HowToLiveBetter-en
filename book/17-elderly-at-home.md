# 17. Elderly Family at Home

Metric: money and personal freedom. With an elderly person at home, most of the trouble isn't a shortage of money — it's that the words weren't spoken clearly in advance and the signatures weren't signed in advance. The last entry is about pressure ulcer in long-term bedridden patients, calculated by mortality, and is not compared with the other entries in this section.

### 1. While the elderly person is still lucid, designate a future guardian in writing
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: A few hundred yuan at a notary office. One conversation with the elderly person can settle it. The hard part is bringing it up — having to say to their face that the day will come when they can't manage their own affairs.
- Plain-language summary: While the elderly person is still mentally clear, spend a few hundred yuan at a notary office to put in writing who will be their guardian in the future. Without this document, when the elderly person can no longer make their own decisions, who signs for them and who manages their money is decided by a court. If family members disagree, it drags on even longer.
- Benefit: The Civil Code provides that an adult who is mentally clear and can make their own decisions (legally "an adult with full civil capacity") may agree in advance with someone on who will serve as their guardian in the future. The other party can be a close relative, or another individual or organization willing to serve as guardian. Once agreed, the person must "determine their guardian in writing." When the person can no longer make decisions, or can only make some decisions (legally "has lost or partially lost civil capacity"), the person named in writing takes over. Without this document, who will sign for them and manage their money in the future is decided by a court
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code (Article 33). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>
- Note: This document is called voluntary guardianship (意定监护), meaning a person designated in advance by the individual. This person ranks ahead of the legal default order of guardianship. Elderly people who live alone, whose children are in another city, or whose family relationships are complex, have even more reason to arrange this in advance. Notarization at a notary office is recommended.

### 2. Make the will; remember a later will overrides an earlier one, and a notarized will is no longer privileged
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Writing your own will (遗嘱) by hand costs nothing. Notarizing it at a notary office costs several hundred to over a thousand yuan. The hard part is bringing it up — talking with family about what happens after you're gone.
- Plain-language summary: The law recognizes six forms of will, but if the form is wrong, the entire will is void — this is the most common way wills fail. The handwritten kind must be written by the testator from start to finish, signed, and dated with year, month, and day. If several wills are made and their contents conflict, the last one made prevails. Since 2021, a notarized will no longer automatically takes precedence.
- Benefit: The Civil Code recognizes six forms of will: handwritten by the testator, written on behalf of the testator by another, printed, audio/video recorded, oral, and notarized. Each has its own mandatory procedural requirements (legally "formal requirements"). If several wills are made and their contents conflict, the last one made prevails. Since 2021, a notarized will no longer automatically takes precedence
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code (Articles 1134–1142). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>
- Note: A handwritten will must be written by the testator from start to finish, signed, and dated with year, month, and day. A printed will requires two or more witnesses. Both the testator and the witnesses must sign each page and date it with year, month, and day. Heirs, anyone written into the will to receive property, and their close relatives, may not serve as witnesses. A defect in form voids the entire will — this is the most common way wills fail

### 3. Keep the elderly person's money in a separate account; set a rule requiring two-person confirmation for large expenditures
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. One conversation with the family to set an amount. The hard part is how to bring it up — don't make the elderly person feel you're guarding against them.
- Plain-language summary: The scams that trick elderly people into buying supplements, collectibles, or wealth-management products all use the same playbook: spend a few months building familiarity, then harvest them all at once. Agree on a rule in advance — any expenditure above a certain amount must be mentioned to a specific person first. This delays the moment of payment past the most impulsive stretch.
- Benefit: Scams targeting the elderly for supplements, collectibles, and wealth-management products all use the same playbook: build trust over a few months, then harvest all at once. Agree in advance that "any expenditure above a certain amount must be mentioned to a specific person first." This pushes the payment decision later, past the most impulsive stretch
- Evidence grade: C
- Source: Author's experience, no direct literature; general anti-scam rules in Section 8 and Section 13
- Note: Don't frame this as "we're afraid you'll be scammed." Frame it as "the household's money is kept in a single ledger" — elderly people accept this more easily. And don't hand all of the elderly person's money to children to manage — that creates another kind of dispute.

### 4. Give the elderly person a scripted excuse they can say at any time
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. One conversation with the elderly person, a few minutes will do.
- Plain-language summary: Sales pitches and scams rely on on-the-spot pressure, making it awkward to refuse in the moment. Agree on a phrase in advance with the elderly person: "I need to go back and discuss it with my family." They can say it at any time, giving themselves a graceful exit on the spot. This works better than reasoning with them afterward.
- Benefit: Sales pitches and scams rely on on-the-spot pressure. Agree in advance on the phrase "I need to go back and discuss it with my family" — give the elderly person a graceful exit. This is more effective than reasoning afterward
- Evidence grade: C
- Source: Author's experience, no direct literature
- Note: Use this together with Entry 3 (keep the elderly person's money in a separate account). Make the fall-prevention modifications around the house at the same time — see Section 1. This phrase gives the elderly person a graceful exit so they don't have to refuse head-on in the moment; it is not for guarding against them. When surrounded by a sales push, the hardest part is opening your mouth to refuse in front of a room full of people — harder than judging whether it's real or fake.

### 5. Don't touch any "investment-style eldercare" that asks the elderly to pay first: membership cards, bed reservations, eldercare apartments, residential eldercare tours, buying elderly products — it's the same illegal fundraising
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. Read the categories below to the elderly person once — a few minutes. From then on, refuse on sight when encountered: don't go to lectures, don't go on tours, don't leave your phone number. The hard part is if the elderly person has already been a few times and grown familiar with the people — they're hard to call back.
- Plain-language summary: Any eldercare scheme that asks the elderly to pay first and promises a return is the same scam: membership cards, bed reservations, eldercare apartments, residential eldercare tours, or sales meetings selling elderly products. Per regulation, money lost this way has to be borne by yourself. One group collected 1.36 billion yuan by pre-selling eldercare apartment beds and ensnared over 2,100 people. The only step you can intercept is the moment of payment.
- Benefit: The Ministry of Civil Affairs lists five typical forms — recognizing the names is more reliable than recognizing the people. The first, under the guise of an eldercare service institution: no actual entity, just a temporarily rented venue, claiming a cooperation agreement with an eldercare institution to ride on its reputation. The second, under the guise of providing "eldercare services": selling "VIP cards," "membership cards," "prepaid cards," having people prepay "eldercare service fees," with promised services clearly exceeding the institution's bed supply capacity. The third, under the guise of investing in "eldercare projects." Common forms include taking shares in a wellness/eldercare base, and selling fictitious eldercare apartments. Also long-term leasing of eldercare beds and "time bank" mutual-aid eldercare. The fronts are return-of-principal sales, post-sale leaseback, and agreed repurchase. The fourth, under the guise of selling "elderly products": product repurchase, consignment sale, consumption rebates, free checkups, gift giveaways, conference marketing, wellness lectures, expert free clinics. The fifth, under the guise of "residential eldercare tours": low-cost or free travel, stored-value rebates, points-based eldercare. The money cannot be recovered — the regulation states this clearly. Article 25 of the Regulations on the Prevention and Handling of Illegal Fundraising provides that "losses incurred from participation in illegal fundraising shall be borne by the participants themselves"; the money lost has to be borne by yourself. In cases officially reported, Dai Mouping and others illegally raised 1.36 billion yuan under the name of pre-selling eldercare apartment beds, with more than 2,100 victims; Dai Mouping was sentenced to 15 years for fundraising fraud and fined 400,000 yuan. Cao Mouming committed fundraising fraud of 13.207 billion yuan under the name of investing in the eldercare industry, involving over 110,000 people; he was sentenced to life imprisonment, deprived of political rights for life, and all personal property confiscated. Health-product conference sales are prosecuted as fraud. Ni Mourong used free banquets, gift giveaways, and chauffeured pickup to bring elderly people to lectures. The lecturer used "false persona, false cases, falsely inflated then-discounted prices" to fabricate cancer-prevention and anti-cancer efficacy. Elderly people were induced to buy "dihydroquercetin" at tens of times the cost price. Sales exceeded 620,000 yuan. Ni Mourong was sentenced to 10 years and 3 months and fined 120,000 yuan (national; regulation effective May 1, 2021)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Ministry of Civil Affairs (2023). Risk Alert on Illegal Fundraising in the Eldercare Services Sector. <https://www.gov.cn/lianbo/bumen/202306/content_6886556.htm> (published on the Chinese Government Network); Ministry of Civil Affairs, China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission (2021). Risk Alert on Illegal Fundraising in the Eldercare Sector. <https://www.mca.gov.cn/n152/n164/c36171/content.html>; State Council (2021). Regulations on the Prevention and Handling of Illegal Fundraising (State Council Order No. 737, Articles 2, 25, 30). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-02/10/content_5586632.htm>; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2022). Typical Cases of Procuratorial Organs in Cracking Down on Eldercare Fraud Crimes. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/202206/t20220617_560010.shtml>; Supreme People's Court (2025). Typical Cases of Punishing Fraud Crimes in Livelihood-Related Areas in Accordance with the Law (Case 5: Ni Mourong Fraud Case). <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/482861.html>
- Note: Don't judge by "this place looks legitimate." Judge by "whether they ask you to pay first, and whether they promise a return." Eldercare institutions are supposed to be selling a service — the moment the talk includes investment, rebates, or principal return, don't trust it. Elderly people being taken to lectures, tours, or free clinics is not in itself illegal, and is hard to stop. What you can intercept is the moment of payment — use this together with the two-person confirmation rule in Entry 3. If money has already been paid, immediately call 110 or 96110 per Section 8, Entry 2 to request payment suspension, and at the same time report to the local lead department responsible for handling illegal fundraising. Reverse mortgage (以房养老) is a separate path — see Entry 6 (housing reverse mortgage pension insurance).

### 6. Aside from insurance company housing reverse mortgage pension insurance, don't touch any other "housing-for-eldercare" scheme; never mortgage the home to buy wealth-management products
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. Don't hand the property certificate to anyone who comes to your door under the name of "housing-for-eldercare." For mortgage, guarantee, or loan agreements, show them to your children before signing. The hard part is that the people who come to the door are often warm and have made many trips — the elderly person is embarrassed to refuse.
- Plain-language summary: The only legitimate form of housing-for-eldercare is insurance company housing reverse mortgage pension insurance, which is still in pilot. The door-to-door sales version has nothing to do with it — it tricks the elderly into mortgaging their home, then uses the borrowed money to buy the wealth-management products the salespeople recommend. Some people don't even know their home has been mortgaged, and end up losing the house while still carrying the loan.
- Benefit: The CBIRC Consumer Protection Bureau spelled out the difference between the genuine and the fake. The only legitimate form of housing-for-eldercare is "Elderly Housing Reverse Mortgage Pension Insurance." An elderly person with full lawful property rights to their home mortgages the property to an insurance company. The home remains theirs to live in, use, and rent out. To sell or dispose of it requires the consent of the mortgagee (the insurance company). The elderly person receives pension payments per agreed terms, until death. This business is still in the pilot stage, with few participants. The "housing-for-eldercare" pitched by fraudsters is "completely unrelated" to the national pilot program of housing reverse mortgage pension insurance — it is "a means of borrowing national policy to drum up publicity for illegal fundraising." Its operation is to trick the elderly into mortgaging their property, then use the borrowed money to buy the wealth-management products it recommends — "often a Ponzi scheme of 'robbing Peter to pay Paul'." The consequences, in the original wording, are that "some participants did not even know their property had been mortgaged, and ultimately lost their homes while still carrying loans." The alert also gives a line for reading returns. The original wording: "If a wealth-management product promises a return exceeding 6%, question it; exceeding 8%, it is very dangerous; above 10%, prepare to lose the entire principal — 'capital-guaranteed high returns' is financial fraud." The same alert also requires that you "treat the contract signing stage with care, and do not sign blank contracts" (national, June 2021)
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission Consumer Protection Bureau (2021). Risk Alert on "Investment-Style Eldercare" and "Housing-for-Eldercare" Financial Fraud (2021, No. 3). <https://dfjrjgj.hunan.gov.cn/dfjrjgj/yhlj/202106/t20210608_19450856.html> (reposted by the Hunan Provincial Local Financial Supervision Bureau); Ministry of Civil Affairs, China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission (2021). Risk Alert on Illegal Fundraising in the Eldercare Sector (Fourth typical form: illegal fundraising under the guise of "housing-for-eldercare"). <https://www.mca.gov.cn/n152/n164/c36171/content.html>
- Note: Rated B because these statements come from supervisory authorities' risk alerts and experienced judgment, not research data. The 6%, 8%, and 10% thresholds are reference lines given to consumers by the regulator, not legal standards. The CBIRC has been merged into the National Financial Regulatory Administration; the original publication page is no longer accessible, so the repost page from the provincial financial supervision bureau is cited here. Losing money on wealth-management products is the lesser problem — once the home is mortgaged, the elderly person still owes a loan, and the loss far exceeds the principal. General rules on signing and blank contracts are in Section 8, Entry 17; on how to suspend payment after being scammed, see Section 8, Entry 2.

### 7. If an elderly family member is bedridden long-term or has severe disability, apply for long-term care insurance at the local medical insurance department; it's not only for the elderly
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Submit an application once to the medical insurance department where you're enrolled. Then undergo a disability-grade assessment. If the assessment is passed on the first try, the assessment fee is paid by the fund — you don't pay out of pocket.
- Plain-language summary: Long-term care insurance (长期护理保险) helps pay for hiring someone to provide care. If someone in the family has severe disability lasting more than half a year, apply at the medical insurance department where you're enrolled. You don't have to pay care costs out of pocket first; employee enrollment reimburses around 70%, urban-rural resident enrollment around 50%; bed fees and meal fees are not covered. There is no age threshold — young people with severe disability can also apply.
- Benefit: Eligible long-term care service costs have no deductible threshold. A deductible threshold means you first pay out of pocket up to a certain amount before reimbursement begins; here you don't have to. For those enrolled under the employee policy, the fund reimburses about 70%. For those enrolled under the non-employed urban-rural resident policy, about 50%. The maximum annual reimbursement (annual maximum payment limit) does not exceed 50% of the per-capita disposable income of urban-rural residents in the local pooling region in the previous year
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: National Healthcare Security Administration and seven other departments (2026). Notice on Issuing the "Implementation Plan for Accelerating the Establishment of a Long-Term Care Insurance System." <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202603/content_7063915.htm>: The coverage population is "persons with disability who are enrolled and pay premiums per regulations, whose disability state has persisted long-term (generally 6 months or more), and who are recognized through application and assessment"; "in the initial stage of the system, persons with severe disability are covered"; "no deductible threshold applies to benefits. For eligible long-term care service costs, those enrolled under the non-employed urban-rural resident policy have a fund payment ratio of about 50%; those enrolled under the employee policy have a fund payment ratio of about 70%, with retirees receiving employee-policy benefits"; "the annual maximum fund payment limit for enrollees does not exceed 50% of the per-capita disposable income of urban-rural residents in the pooling region in the previous year"; "the waiting period for benefit eligibility is set at 6 months in principle"; see also the General Office of the CPC Central Committee and the General Office of the State Council, "Opinions on Accelerating the Establishment of a Long-Term Care Insurance System." <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2026/issue_12666/202604/content_7065119.html>
- Note: Disability must be "long-term persistent (generally 6 months or more)"; a few weeks of hospitalization does not meet the application criteria. There is also a 6-month waiting period before benefits begin. Bed fees and meal fees are not considered care costs and are not paid by long-term care insurance. Medical expenses that should be paid by medical insurance are also not paid by long-term care insurance. Those already receiving workplace injury (工伤) living care allowances do not receive these in parallel. This money and the care subsidy in the two disability subsidies are two separate procedures and two separate sums — they don't conflict; see Section 7, Entry 8 (two subsidies). Receiving care at home or in the community has favorable reimbursement rates — institutional admission is not strictly required. This system is being rolled out in batches across regions, and disability assessment criteria also differ. First call 12393 or your local medical insurance agency to ask whether it has started locally and how to schedule an assessment. Don't give up if the assessment is not passed — reapply if the disability state worsens.

### 8. If someone at home is bedridden long-term, treat pressure ulcer as the number one enemy: use an electric air mattress, reposition on schedule, and check bony prominences once a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A household electric alternating pressure air mattress starts at several hundred yuan. Repositioning takes only a few minutes. The hard part is having to reposition every two to three hours, including through the night — one person alone can't carry it.
- Plain-language summary: Elderly people who have a pressure ulcer before hip fracture surgery have about a 20% higher chance of dying within 30 days. Switching to an electric air mattress reduces new pressure ulcers by about 60%. Repositioning on schedule, truly done, reduces them by about 70%. Don't leave the person prone for long periods. Check the sacrum/coccyx, heels, hips, and shoulder blades once a day — if you find redness that doesn't fade when pressed, find a nurse or doctor the same day.
- Benefit: 19,520 hip fracture surgery patients (US NSQIP 2016 to 2019). Those who had a pressure ulcer (压疮) before surgery had a higher 30-day mortality. After accounting for other factors, this association still held (OR 1.2, an odds increase of 21%, P=0.004). These patients were also more likely to have other problems: deep vein thrombosis (OR 1.59), pneumonia (OR 1.39), unplanned readmission (OR 1.43), with average length of stay also 0.4 days longer. 324 hospitalized elderly (mean age 86) followed for a median of 18 months. Pressure ulcer independently predicted long-term mortality (HR 1.500, 95% CI 1.059 to 2.126, P=0.020). "Independently predicted" means after removing the effect of other factors, it predicts on its own. 65 randomized trials pooled (network meta-analysis). The comparison is between electric active airflow support surfaces and standard hospital bed mattresses (standard hospital bed support surfaces). Electric active airflow support surfaces are the electric air mattress type. Those who used them had a lower pressure ulcer incidence (RR 0.42, 95% CI 0.29 to 0.63, moderate certainty). Electric hybrid airflow support surfaces RR 0.22 (0.07 to 0.66). Cochrane review of 11 trials, 4,462 participants (updated 2026). Comparing repositioning every 2 hours vs. every 4 hours, the difference is uncertain (RR 1.05, 95% CI 0.79 to 1.39, very low certainty). Comparisons between other repositioning intervals are similarly very uncertain. In a trial of 1,226 ICU patients, a wearable sensor that gave real-time reminders and visual prompts to actually carry out 2-hourly repositioning produced a lower pressure ulcer incidence than usual care (RR 0.28, 95% CI 0.10 to 0.75, moderate certainty). In a trial of 116 participants, the prone (face-down) group had a higher pressure ulcer incidence than the supine (face-up) group (RR 4.55, 95% CI 2.31 to 8.98)
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Porter SB, Pla R, Chow JH, et al. (2022). Preoperative Pressure Ulcers, Mortality, and Complications in Older Hip Fracture Surgery Patients. JAAOS Global Research & Reviews, 6(11). <https://doi.org/10.5435/JAAOSGlobal-D-22-00117>; Ottaviani S, Rondanina E, Longo E, et al. (2026). Impact of pressure ulcers and frailty on long-term mortality: a prospective cohort study of hospitalized older adults. European Geriatric Medicine, 17(2), 665-672. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s41999-025-01331-8>; Shi C, Dumville JC, Cullum N (2018). Support surfaces for pressure ulcer prevention: A network meta-analysis. PLoS ONE, 13(2), e0192707. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0192707>; Latimer SL, Chaboyer WP, Probst S, et al. (2026). Repositioning for pressure injury prevention in adults. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 6, CD009958. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD009958.pub4>
- Note: Rated B for three reasons. First, the mortality studies only show that "people with pressure ulcers fare worse afterward" — this does not equal "preventing pressure ulcers lowers mortality." Second, the conclusions of the randomized trials on repositioning intervals are very shaky. Third, the air-mattress evidence is more solid, but the studies compared mattresses on hospital beds — applying this to household mattresses is an extrapolation. Checking the sacrum/coccyx, heels, hips, and shoulder blades once a day for "redness that doesn't fade when pressed" is standard nursing practice, with no separate literature. For the care services reimbursable by long-term care insurance, see Entry 7 of this section. If one leg suddenly swells, treat as deep vein thrombosis — see Section 13, Entry 11. For the period bedridden after a fall or severe injury, see Section 1, Entry 34.
