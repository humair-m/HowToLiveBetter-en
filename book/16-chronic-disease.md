# 16. Living After a Chronic Disease Diagnosis

Metric: overall mortality and money. After a chronic disease diagnosis, you often have decades still to live; this section calculates how to manage those decades.

### 1. Take medication to completion per doctor's orders — don't stop just because you feel better
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free of charge — it actually saves the money you'd later spend treating complications. Taking medication daily takes only a few minutes; the hard part is not skipping a single day.
- Plain-language summary: People who take their medication to completion per doctor's orders have roughly half the risk of dying during the same period as people who take it sporadically. Take this number with a discount. Even people who finished a placebo (fake pill) had lower mortality — meaning part of the benefit comes from "people who stick to a medication regimen already take better care of their health." But the harm from stopping is certain, especially for blood pressure medication, anticoagulants, and anti-rejection drugs.
- Benefit: 21 studies, 46,847 people pooled (meta-analysis): compared with people who took medication sporadically, those who took it to completion per doctor's orders had about a 44% lower risk of dying during the same period. The raw value is odds ratio 0.56, 95% CI 0.50–0.63 (confidence interval). Looking only at treatments with proven efficacy, about 45% lower (0.55, 0.49–0.62)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Simpson SH, Eurich DT, Majumdar SR, et al. (2006). A meta-analysis of the association between adherence to drug therapy and mortality. BMJ, 333(7557), 15. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.38875.675486.55>
- Note: Controversy. This is also the number in this book that most needs to be discounted. In the same pooled analysis, people who even finished a placebo (fake pill) had lower mortality (0.56, 0.43–0.74). This means a substantial part of the benefit comes from "people who stick to a medication regimen already take better care of their health." The authors call this confounding factor the healthy adherer effect. But the harm of stopping medication itself is clear, especially for blood pressure medication, anticoagulants, and anti-rejection drugs

### 2. First obtain outpatient chronic/special disease certification, then complete out-of-area treatment registration — hypertension, diabetes, radiotherapy/chemo, dialysis, and anti-rejection can be directly settled out-of-area
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. Before you leave, complete out-of-area treatment registration on the medical insurance app — can be done on your phone.
- Plain-language summary: Hypertension, diabetes, outpatient radiotherapy/chemo for malignant tumors, uremia dialysis, and post-organ-transplant anti-rejection — these five can be settled directly via medical insurance for outpatient visits even when you're out of area. You don't have to pay out of pocket first and then be reimbursed at your enrollment location. But you must first obtain outpatient chronic/special disease (门诊慢特病) certification at your enrollment location, then complete out-of-area treatment registration. Both must be done for it to work.
- Benefit: The National Healthcare Security Administration's document says this step has been achieved. The original wording is "the cross-provincial direct settlement of outpatient chronic/special disease expenses for 5 conditions — hypertension, diabetes, outpatient radiotherapy/chemo for malignant tumors, uremia dialysis, and post-organ-transplant anti-rejection therapy — is basically accessible at the county level." "County-level accessible" means it can be done at the county level. The document also adds 5 more conditions on top of these 5
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: National Healthcare Security Administration General Office, Ministry of Finance General Office (2024). Notice on Steadily and Orderly Expanding the Scope of Outpatient Chronic/Special Disease Categories Covered by Cross-Provincial Direct Settlement (Medical Insurance Office Document No. 19 [2024]). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202409/content_6974467.htm>
- Note: The premise is that both must be done: first obtain outpatient chronic/special disease certification at your enrollment location, then complete out-of-area treatment registration. How far the 5 newly added conditions have been rolled out varies by region; rely on what your local medical insurance department publishes

### 3. Do follow-up visits at the intervals the doctor gives, and record each visit's indicators in the same notebook
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Each test costs tens to several hundred yuan. Going to the hospital plus waiting for results takes some time. The hard part is having to go back every so often — easy to keep putting off.
- Plain-language summary: Chronic disease is about changes over a period of time, not any single value. Record the date, indicators, current medication, and dosage in the same notebook. When you switch hospitals, take it out — no need to redo the tests and pay for them again.
- Benefit: Chronic disease is about how values change over time; a single reading is not enough. Recording in one place has another benefit: when you switch hospitals, you don't need to repeat tests or pay for them again
- Evidence grade: C
- Source: Author's experience, no direct literature
- Note: Each time, record at least four things: date, indicator value, current medication, and dosage. Photographing lab reports with your phone also works, but keep them all in one place — don't scatter them

### 4. Don't stop regular treatment to try folk remedies or health supplements
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free of charge — also saves the money you'd spend on supplements. Takes no time. The hard part is holding firm when others keep urging you to try.
- Plain-language summary: The harm of stopping medication is certain; the benefit of folk remedies and supplements is uncertain. This means trading a certain loss for an uncertain benefit. If you want to try a supplement, tell your treating doctor first. Some supplements affect the blood concentration of anticoagulants and anti-rejection drugs.
- Benefit: The harm of stopping medication is certain; the benefit of folk remedies is uncertain. This book lists a batch of common ineffective categories in Section 6
- Evidence grade: C
- Source: Author's experience, no direct literature; related evidence in Section 6
- Note: If you want to try a supplement, tell your treating doctor first. Some supplements interact with prescription drugs — for example, affecting the blood concentration of anticoagulants and anti-rejection drugs

### 5. For stable chronic disease, community hospitals can prescribe up to 12 weeks of medication at once
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. Ask at the community health service center whether they can issue a long-term prescription — one sentence.
- Plain-language summary: For people whose condition is stable and whose medication hasn't changed, the community health service center can prescribe up to 12 weeks of medication at once. This saves the half-day trip to a major hospital each month for queueing and registration. Within the same enrollment region, the reimbursement rate at primary hospitals is typically about 10 percentage points higher than at the next level up.
- Benefit: No need to travel to a major hospital every month to queue for prescriptions. Within the same enrollment region (also called the pooling region), the reimbursement rate at primary hospitals is in principle about 10 percentage points higher than at the next level up
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: State Council General Office (2026). Several Measures on Accelerating the Construction of a Hierarchical Diagnosis and Treatment System. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm>: "For chronic disease patients who meet the criteria, primary healthcare institutions may issue a long-term prescription of no more than 12 weeks of medication per visit"; same document: "In principle, within the pooling region, the inpatient reimbursement rate gap between healthcare institutions at successive levels is set at about 10 percentage points," and requires upper-level hospitals to set up outpatient clinics at the primary level for common chronic diseases such as hypertension, diabetes, and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease
- Note: "Meeting the criteria" is judged by the doctor — generally those whose diagnosis is already clear and whose medication regimen is stable. The medication is the same; what you save is the half-day trip each month and the registration fee. If the primary-level facility doesn't carry the drug you need, ask whether they have a drug-shortage registration and delivery service.

### 6. Before signing with a community family doctor, ask which services are covered by medical insurance and which are self-pay
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. Ask one more question before signing — one minute.
- Plain-language summary: The basic service package is covered by medical insurance per regulation. The personalized service package must be paid entirely out of pocket. When someone pitches an "upgrade package," first ask whether it's a basic or personalized package, whether it has been filed, and how much it costs per year.
- Benefit: The basic service package is paid by medical insurance per regulation. The personalized service package must be paid entirely out of pocket, and such packages must also be filed with the county-level health commission
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: State Council General Office (2026). Several Measures on Accelerating the Construction of a Hierarchical Diagnosis and Treatment System. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm>: "Strengthen the linkage between primary-level outpatient payment and contracted service policy; the basic service package is included in medical insurance payment per regulation; the personalized service package is filed by the contracting primary healthcare institution with the county-level health department per procedure, with fees paid by the individual."
- Note: The value of signing lies in having someone follow your condition over the long term, not in how many items are bundled in. When someone pitches an "upgrade package," ask three questions first: is it a basic or personalized package, has it been filed, and how much does it cost per year.

### 7. Get a dilated eye exam at diabetes diagnosis, then at intervals the doctor gives; check feet once a year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A dilated eye exam costs tens to several hundred yuan. Foot exams are usually included in the outpatient visit and don't cost extra. The hard part is remembering to go even when your eyes and feet don't feel anything wrong.
- Plain-language summary: Diabetes damages the retina and the feet — and before it gets bad enough to blind you or ulcerate, there's almost no sensation. Caught early it's still treatable. For type 2 diabetes, get a dilated eye exam at the time of diagnosis; for type 1, start 5 years after onset. If one or two exams are normal and blood sugar is at target, the interval can be relaxed to once every 1 to 2 years. Check feet once a year.
- Benefit: Diabetes damaging the retina is called retinopathy; damaging the feet is called diabetic foot. Both produce almost no sensation before they cause trouble. Caught early, they are still treatable. By the time you can't see or your feet ulcerate, it's too late
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: American Diabetes Association (2026). Standards of Care in Diabetes 2026, Chapter 12: Retinopathy, Neuropathy, and Foot Care. Diabetes Care: "People with type 2 diabetes should have an initial dilated and comprehensive eye examination by an ophthalmologist or optometrist at the time of the diabetes diagnosis." "Adults with type 1 diabetes should have an initial dilated and comprehensive eye examination by an ophthalmologist or optometrist 5 years after the onset of diabetes." "If there is no evidence of retinopathy from one or more annual eye exams and glycemic indicators are within the goal range, then screening every 1–2 years may be considered." "Perform a comprehensive foot evaluation at least annually to identify risk factors for ulcers and amputations." <https://doi.org/10.2337/dc26-S012>
- Note: For type 2 diabetes, check at diagnosis; for type 1, start 5 years after onset. The difference is that type 2 has often been silently elevated for years. If one or two exams are normal and blood sugar is at target, the interval can be relaxed to every 1 to 2 years. People whose foot sensation has dulled should check the soles of their feet daily — using a mirror works too.

### 8. If you've had kidney stones, drink 2.5–3 L of water per day and keep salt under 6 g
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. Make drinking water at any time a habit. On heavy-sweat days, drink even more; the hard part is remembering every day.
- Plain-language summary: Among people who form a calcium stone for the first time, about a quarter will form another within five years if they do nothing. For those who drink enough water daily, the five-year recurrence is just over 10% — more than half lower. The UK guideline amount is 2.5 to 3 liters of water per day, with salt no more than 6 grams.
- Benefit: A 5-year randomized controlled trial in two arms, enrolling 199 patients with a first calcium stone of no identifiable cause (also called idiopathic calcium stone). One arm drank at least 2 liters of water per day; the other had no intervention. Recurrence over 5 years: 12/99 (12.1%) in the high-water group vs. 27/100 (27.0%) in the no-intervention group, P=0.008. The smaller the P value, the less likely the difference between the two groups is by chance. Mean interval to recurrence: 38.7±13.2 months vs. 25.1±16.4 months, P=0.016. Baseline 24-hour urine output was significantly lower in stone patients than in those without stones: 1,057±238 mL vs. 1,401±562 mL in men (P<0.0001), and 990±230 mL vs. 1,239±440 mL in women (P<0.001). NICE guideline NG118, section 1.8.1, recommends 2.5 to 3 liters of water per day for adults, and 1 to 2 liters per day for children and adolescents depending on age. Add fresh lemon juice to water, avoid carbonated drinks, and keep salt under 6 grams per day for adults
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Borghi L, Meschi T, Amato F, Briganti A, Novarini A, Giannini A (1996). Urinary volume, water and recurrences in idiopathic calcium nephrolithiasis: a 5-year randomized prospective study. The Journal of Urology, 155(3), 839-843. <https://doi.org/10.1016/s0022-5347(01)66321-3>; National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (2019). Renal and ureteric stones: assessment and management. NICE guideline NG118, section 1.8.1. <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng118/chapter/Recommendations>
- Note: Recurrence fell by about 55%, and it costs nothing — by this book's ranking rule, this is the highest cost-effectiveness entry in this section. What to track is 24-hour urine output, with a target of more than 2 to 2.5 liters — not how much you drink. People who sweat a lot or work in hot environments need to drink more. That trial was on patients with a first episode of idiopathic calcium stone. Stones come in more than one composition — uric acid stones, infection stones, and cystine stones each have their own countermeasures. Anyone who has had a recurrence should consider a stone composition analysis and a 24-hour urine test, then decide whether to add medication. Do not cut calcium out to prevent stones — see Section 6, Entry 21. When an attack hits with severe flank/abdominal colic, go to the hospital — it is not something to be flushed out with more water. Painless hematuria has other things to investigate — see Section 1, Entry 27

### 9. At gout diagnosis, start long-term urate-lowering medication; push serum uric acid below 360 µmol/L and keep it there
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Allopurinol costs a few to tens of yuan per month. At first, check serum uric acid every few weeks to adjust the dose; once stable, check every few months. For the first 3 to 6 months, also take a flare-prevention medication. The hard part is keeping at it even when not in pain.
- Plain-language summary: Gout requires long-term urate-lowering medication to push uric acid below 360 µmol/L — not just painkillers when it hurts. Among patients whose medication was adjusted by a nurse based on lab results, 95% reached target at 2 years; among those who saw their doctor as usual, only 30%. Reaching target does not mean stopping the drug. Diet alone cannot get uric acid down to this line, and quitting alcohol only lowers it by 1.6 mg/dL.
- Benefit: A randomized controlled trial in two arms, 517 adults who had had a flare within the past 12 months. One arm was nurse-led: nurses explained to patients what the disease was, then adjusted uric acid to target. The other arm saw general practitioners as usual. The proportion with serum uric acid reduced to <360 µmol/L (6 mg/dL) at 2 years was 95% vs. 30%. The likelihood of reaching target was 3.18 times that of the usual-care group (RR 3.18, 95% CI 2.42–4.18, this interval is the confidence range of the number), P<0.0001. Secondary outcomes at year 2 — flare frequency, tophi, quality of life — were also better than in the usual-care group. Cost per quality-adjusted life year gained was £5,066. The same trial also did a follow-up study, with questionnaires sent to 438 people and an 82% response rate. Both groups self-reported their number of flares in the past 12 months. The median for the at-target group was 0 flares (the middle half fell within 0–0). The usual-care group was 1 flare (0–3), P<0.001. The range in brackets is called the interquartile range. The proportion still taking urate-lowering medication was 1.19 times higher in the at-target group than in the usual-care group (adjusted RR 1.19, 1.09–1.30). The American College of Rheumatology 2020 gout guideline strongly recommends starting urate-lowering therapy in three groups: those with subcutaneous tophi, those with bony erosions from gout seen on imaging, and those with ≥2 flares per year. The approach is to continuously monitor serum uric acid and adjust the dose based on results until the target is reached, with a goal of <6 mg/dL. Allopurinol is the first choice, including for those with stage 3 or higher chronic kidney disease, at a starting dose of ≤100 mg/day. At initiation, an anti-inflammatory prophylactic medication is given at the same time, for at least 3 to 6 months. The same guideline also cites a case series. These patients, after long-term good control, stopped the medication, and serum uric acid remained <7 mg/dL. Among them, only 13% (27/211) had zero flares during a 5-year follow-up. On diet, those who limited or quit alcohol had serum uric acid 1.6 mg/dL lower than those who did not limit; one serving of beer raised serum uric acid by 0.16 mg/dL. Healthy diet, Mediterranean diet, and DASH diet had even smaller effects. There is disagreement on which urate-lowering drug to prefer. The American College of Rheumatology guideline strongly recommends allopurinol as the first choice, on the grounds that at adequate dose it is effective, well-tolerated, safe, and cheap. It also conditionally recommends that patients taking febuxostat who have a cardiovascular history, or who have a new cardiovascular event, switch to another drug if one is available. This recommendation is consistent with the FDA black box warning on febuxostat. One of the bases is the CARES trial, in which 6,190 gout patients with cardiovascular disease were randomized to febuxostat or allopurinol. The all-cause mortality risk in the febuxostat group was 1.22 times that of the allopurinol group (HR 1.22, 95% CI 1.01–1.47, about 22% higher). Cardiovascular mortality was 1.34 times (1.03–1.73, about 34% higher). The European FAST trial reached a different conclusion. It enrolled 6,128 patients aged over 60 with cardiovascular risk factors; febuxostat did not produce more major cardiovascular events than allopurinol (HR 0.85, 0.70–1.03). Deaths were 7.2% in the febuxostat group and 8.6% in the allopurinol group. For patients taking drugs that promote renal excretion of uric acid, the American College of Rheumatology conditionally recommends against alkalinizing the urine, because there is no evidence of effectiveness and the certainty of evidence is very low. It also requires telling these patients to drink more water; patients with known kidney stones or stage 3 or higher chronic kidney disease should not use such drugs
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Doherty M, Jenkins W, Richardson H, et al. (2018). Efficacy and cost-effectiveness of nurse-led care involving education and engagement of patients and a treat-to-target urate-lowering strategy versus usual care for gout: a randomised controlled trial. Lancet, 392(10156), 1403-1412. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)32158-5>; Abhishek A, Jenkins W, La-Crette J, Fernandes G, Doherty M (2020). Nurse-led care is preferred over GP-led care of gout and improves gout outcomes: results of Nottingham Gout Treatment Trial follow-up study. Rheumatology, 59(3), 575-579. <https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/kez333>; FitzGerald JD, Dalbeth N, Mikuls T, et al. (2020). 2020 American College of Rheumatology Guideline for the Management of Gout. Arthritis Care & Research, 72(6), 744-760. <https://doi.org/10.1002/acr.24180>; White WB, Saag KG, Becker MA, et al.; CARES Investigators (2018). Cardiovascular Safety of Febuxostat or Allopurinol in Patients with Gout. New England Journal of Medicine, 378(13), 1200-1210. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1710895>; Mackenzie IS, Ford I, Nuki G, et al.; FAST Study Group (2020). Long-term cardiovascular safety of febuxostat compared with allopurinol in patients with gout (FAST): a multicentre, prospective, randomised, open-label, non-inferiority trial. Lancet, 396(10264), 1745-1757. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(20)32234-0>; Chinese Society of Endocrinology (2020). Chinese Guideline for the Diagnosis and Management of Hyperuricemia and Gout (2019). Chinese Journal of Endocrinology and Metabolism, 36(1), 1-13. <https://doi.org/10.3760/cma.j.issn.1000-6699.2020.01.001>
- Note: Controversy: which drug to use to lower uric acid differs across national guidelines. The American guideline prefers allopurinol, and patients with cardiovascular disease should switch off febuxostat if they can, but the European FAST trial did not find febuxostat to be more dangerous. Benzbromarone, a drug that promotes uric acid excretion, is still available in China. The Chinese Society of Endocrinology guideline (2019 version and 2024 update) gives separate recommendations on drug selection and urine alkalinization. Whether to add sodium bicarbonate to alkalinize urine when taking benzbromarone — the American guideline finds no evidence; follow your doctor's opinion, but do not skip the extra water. **People of Han Chinese descent should test for HLA-B\*5801 before taking allopurinol.** It is an innate gene variant that regulates immune response; one test gives a lifetime result. Among Han Chinese, Koreans, and Thais, this gene is present in 7.4%; among whites and Hispanics it is only 0.7%. Asians and blacks have three times the risk of allopurinol hypersensitivity syndrome compared with whites after taking allopurinol — in severe cases the skin peels off over large areas, and it can be fatal. The American College of Rheumatology 2020 gout guideline suggests considering testing these two groups before starting the drug. **Starting urate-lowering therapy paradoxically makes flares more likely at first**, so the guideline explicitly requires taking an anti-inflammatory prophylactic medication for the first 3 to 6 months. "I started the urate-lowering drug and it hurt more, so I stopped" is the most common way this fails. When a flare hits, what to do is add an anti-inflammatory per doctor's orders, not stop the urate-lowering drug. The target values differ in their notation: <6 mg/dL is approximately 360 µmol/L; Chinese lab reports mostly use µmol/L. Finding high uric acid on a checkup without ever having a flare is a separate matter — see Section 6, Entry 19. Sugary drinks and alcohol — see Section 2, Entries 7 and 20 (don't drink sugary drinks, drink little or no alcohol)
