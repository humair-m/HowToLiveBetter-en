# 18. Is Raising a Child Worth It

This section accounts for both money and time. As in Section 10's accounting for marriage, here I only break the ledger open for you to see — I do not draw the conclusion for you. If you are already pregnant and need to follow the procedures step by step, see Section 27.

### 1. First fold in what you can claim: the state childcare subsidy is 3,600 yuan per child per year, paid until age 3
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Apply at the location of the child's hukou (household registration); one trip handles it
- Plain-language summary: Children born on or after January 1, 2025 who are not yet 3 years old can receive this money. One parent applies at the location of the child's hukou. Each child receives 3,600 yuan per year, continuing until age 3. This money is not subject to individual income tax. It is also not counted as household income when applying for assistance such as dibao (minimum living guarantee)
- Benefit: The plan issued by the General Office of the CPC Central Committee and the General Office of the State Council stipulates: infants and toddlers born on or after January 1, 2025 and under 3 years old are paid "3,600 yuan per child per year." One of the child's parents or another guardian applies for it. This subsidy is "exempt from individual income tax." When determining eligibility for assistance such as dibao, it "is not counted as household or personal income"
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: General Office of the CPC Central Committee and General Office of the State Council (2025). Implementation Plan for the Childcare Subsidy System. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2025/issue_12206/202508/content_7035435.html>
- Note: This is the nationwide uniform minimum line. Some provinces and cities have their own local subsidies that can be claimed on top of this one. The three-year total of 10,800 yuan is only a small fraction of the cost of raising a child; do not treat it as the basis for whether to have one

### 2. Maternity leave is 98 days; the maternity allowance is paid by the maternity insurance fund at the unit's average monthly wage from the prior year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. The employer files for it; you do not need to run any errand yourself
- Plain-language summary: Giving birth entitles you to at least 98 days of maternity leave, of which up to 15 days can be taken before delivery. Difficult labor adds another 15 days. Each additional child born adds another 15 days. The money paid during this period is called the maternity allowance, paid out by the maternity insurance fund. If the employer has not enrolled you in maternity insurance, the employer pays it itself
- Benefit: The number of maternity leave days is set by the State Council. "Female employees enjoy 98 days of maternity leave for childbirth, of which 15 days may be taken before delivery; in cases of difficult labor, 15 days are added to maternity leave; in cases of multiple births, 15 days are added for each additional infant born." The money received during maternity leave is called the maternity allowance. For those already enrolled in maternity insurance, "payment is made by the maternity insurance fund at the standard of the employer's average monthly wage of employees in the prior year." For those not enrolled, the employer pays you at your pre-leave wage standard
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: State Council (2012). Special Provisions on Labor Protection of Female Employees (State Council Order No. 619, Articles 7 and 8). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2012-05/07/content_6584.htm>
- Note: 98 days is the minimum standard set by the state. The population and family planning regulations of each province generally add 60 or more days of "maternity reward leave," bringing the total to typically 158 days or more. Which party pays the wages during the reward leave period varies by province

### 3. Know this rule: wages may not be cut nor employees dismissed for pregnancy, childbirth, or breastfeeding
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money
- Plain-language summary: During pregnancy, childbirth, and breastfeeding, it is illegal for the employer to cut your wages or dismiss you; the penalty is 1,000 to 5,000 yuan per person. For a child under one year of age, you have one hour per day for nursing, and the employer may not assign overtime or night shifts. How to pursue a claim, see Section 19
- Benefit: The State Council stipulates: "Employers may not, on grounds of a female employee's pregnancy, childbirth, or breastfeeding, reduce her wages, dismiss her, or dissolve her labor or employment contract." During these three periods the employer may not cut your pay, dismiss you, or terminate your contract. For nursing an infant under one year of age, one hour of nursing time is allowed per day. The employer may also not arrange for you to extend working hours, nor assign night shifts. Employers who violate these are fined 1,000 yuan or more and 5,000 yuan or less per person
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: State Council (2012). Special Provisions on Labor Protection of Female Employees (State Council Order No. 619, Articles 5, 9, and 13). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2012-05/07/content_6584.htm>
- Note: If you are actually dismissed in violation of the law, the path to a claim is in Section 19. The key evidence is written notices of position reassignment or pay reduction and chat records — keep both safe

### 4. The time ledger is counted by "whose time is taken up," not by "how hard it feels"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money; what is spent is time. Before age 3, the child essentially occupies you around the clock. The hard part is that this is daily, not something you grit your teeth through for a few days
- Plain-language summary: Convert the daily hours of childcare and the number of nighttime wake-ups into time. Multiply by the hourly wage of the person doing the work. The resulting number is generally far larger than the cost of formula and diapers. So the division of labor is best negotiated before the child is born
- Benefit: Convert the daily hours of childcare and the number of nighttime wake-ups into hours. Multiply by the hourly wage of the person doing the work. The resulting number is usually far larger than the cost of formula and diapers. Tasks such as watching the child, cooking, and getting up at night are unpaid but should still be recorded into the cost this way. The method is the same one used in Section 10 for household chores. Negotiate the division of labor first, then decide whether to have a child
- Evidence grade: C
- Source: Author's experience, no direct literature; the metric is in Section 10
- Note: The number does not need to be precise. What is useful is for two people to sit down with the same table before having the child and negotiate the division of labor, so as not to fight afterward

### 5. The money ledger is split into three stages: 0 to 3, compulsory education, and after compulsory education
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: There is no fixed number; it varies widely by city and by how you raise the child. The hard part is sitting down to separate the three ledgers, rather than throwing out a number by feel
- Plain-language summary: The money for ages 0 to 3 is mainly spent on how the child is cared for: by yourself, by grandparents, or by a hired carer. The elementary to middle school stage is mainly spent on housing and extracurriculars. After that, the main spending is on the path to higher education. The spending in these three stages is completely different; mixing them together only produces a frightening total
- Benefit: The bulk for ages 0 to 3 is the care arrangement: self, grandparents, or hired. The bulk during compulsory education (elementary through middle school) is housing and extracurricular spending. The bulk after that is the path to higher education. What these three stages spend on is completely different; mixing them only yields a frightening total
- Evidence grade: C
- Source: Author's experience, no direct literature
- Note: First fold in the money you can definitely get — childcare subsidy, maternity allowance, medical insurance reimbursement — and then see how much is still short. Do not make the decision based on the numbers circulating online about "how many hundreds of thousands it costs to raise a child"; the methodology behind those figures is unclear — you don't know what they counted and what they left out

### 6. For "having a child for the elders," "for the marriage," and "for old-age support," keep a separate ledger for each
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money; what is spent is one serious conversation. The hard part is putting it into words rather than dancing around it
- Plain-language summary: Others wanting you to have a child can count as one factor in your consideration. But the time and money are recorded on your own ledger. "Raising a child to provide for old age" must be accounted for separately, because it is a bet on another person's economic capacity and willingness twenty years from now. Write down the consequences of not having a child too, and compare both sides
- Benefit: The approach is the same as in Section 10. Others' expectations can count as one factor in your consideration. But the cost goes on your own ledger, not on theirs. "Raising a child to provide for old age" in particular must be accounted for separately, because it assumes the economic capacity and willingness of another person twenty years from now
- Evidence grade: C
- Source: Author's experience, no direct literature; for a parallel analysis see Section 10
- Note: Also write down "the consequences of not having a child," and set them side by side with "the consequences of having one" for comparison
