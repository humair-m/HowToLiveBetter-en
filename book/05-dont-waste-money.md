# 5. Don't Waste Money

This section counts only money: how much can be saved, how the annual return differs, and how much a sum returns on long-term average. These are not converted to lifespan or time. Entries involving investment discuss only how to do the accounting, use only verified historical data, and do not constitute investment advice.

### 1. Turn off all auto-renewals; renew manually when due
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Go through the "auto-deduction" lists in Alipay, WeChat, Apple, and Android once, 10 to 20 minutes. After that, each renewal requires one extra tap.
- Plain-language summary: What you save is the subscriptions you no longer use that are still billing every month. Going through the Alipay, WeChat, Apple, and Android auto-deduction lists takes less than 20 minutes. By regulation, merchants must give you a clear reminder before billing, and must give you an always-available cancellation entry that's easy to find and tap.
- Benefit: What's saved equals the subscriptions you no longer use but are still paying for, summed up. Differs per person; no unified figure. Regulators require merchants to give you a clear reminder before renewal, and a "simple, anytime cancellation" option, with cancellation not requiring extra payment (China, effective 2021).
- Evidence grade: C
- Source: State Administration for Market Regulation (2021, 2025 amendment). Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Online Transactions (General Administration Decree No. 37, Article 18). <https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/fgs/art/2025/art_4b47c79b8d994a42bba4835997688faa.html>
- Note: For services you genuinely use often, annual billing is usually cheaper than monthly — keeping auto-renewal may be the better deal. What to turn off is what you've long forgotten but is still billing.

### 2. Do an annual individual income tax reconciliation between March and June; fill in the special additional deductions you qualify for
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Done on the "Individual Income Tax" app in a few minutes.
- Plain-language summary: These people usually get a refund: those who worked only part of the year, changed jobs mid-year, or didn't fill in deductions for children's education, mortgage, rent, or supporting elders. Done on the phone in a few minutes. If your annual comprehensive income is under 120,000 yuan, or the tax to be paid is under 400 yuan, you may skip it.
- Benefit: Wages and salaries, labor-service remuneration, author's remuneration, and royalties are pooled for one total reconciliation. Overpaid amounts are refunded; underpaid are topped up. Two situations allow skipping: your annual comprehensive income is no more than 120,000 yuan, or the tax to be paid is no more than 400 yuan. These two rules are from the State Taxation Administration's 2023 announcement, valid through December 31, 2027. Conversely, these people usually get a refund: those with income only in some months of the year, those who changed jobs mid-year, and those who didn't fill in deductions for children's education, mortgage, rent, or supporting elders.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: State Taxation Administration (2025). Administrative Measures for the Comprehensive Income Reconciliation and Settlement of Individual Income Tax (Article 6) and its interpretation. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202502/content_7006465.htm>; Ministry of Finance, State Taxation Administration (2023). Announcement on Continuing the Implementation of Policies Related to Individual Income Tax Comprehensive Income Reconciliation and Settlement. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202308/content_6900594.htm>
- Note: Those who need to pay additional tax must also do the reconciliation — not doing so is illegal. The special additional deductions total seven: children's education, continuing education, major illness medical, housing loan interest, housing rent, supporting elders, and care for children under 3. Of these, mortgage and rent are mutually exclusive — you can fill only one.

### 3. The housing fund (公积金) is not just for buying a house: rent, renovation, and property fees can all be withdrawn; new regulations effective September 20, 2026
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. One withdrawal procedure.
- Plain-language summary: The housing fund can be tapped for more than buying a house. The new regulations list nine situations that allow withdrawal, including paying rent, renovating your own residence, and paying property management fees. After you submit an application, the housing fund center must respond within 3 days on whether you may withdraw.
- Benefit: The amended regulations list nine situations that allow withdrawal. These include: paying rent, renovating your own residence, and paying property management fees for your own residence. The housing fund management center must make a decision on whether to approve withdrawal within 3 days of accepting the application.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: State Council (2026). Decision of the State Council on Amending the "Regulations on the Administration of the Housing Provident Fund" (State Council Decree No. 844, adopted at the 93rd executive meeting of the State Council on July 31, 2026, "effective September 20, 2026"): Amended Article 24 "Employees with any of the following circumstances may withdraw the storage balance in the employee's housing provident fund account: (1) paying rent; (2) purchasing, constructing, renovating, or major-repairing an owner-occupied residence; (3) repaying the principal and interest of a house purchase loan; (4) renovating an owner-occupied residence; (5) paying property management fees for an owner-occupied residence; (6) retirement or early retirement; (7) total loss of working capacity with termination of the labor relationship with the employer; (8) emigrating for settlement; (9) other housing consumption approved by the State Council." Article 25: "The housing provident fund management center shall make a decision on whether to approve the withdrawal within 3 days of accepting the application." Article 18 on the contribution ratio: "shall not be less than 5% of the employee's average monthly wage in the previous year."
- Note: The money in your account is your own wages plus the portion your employer matches. Left untouched, it earns only a very low interest rate. The regulations also state: when an employee dies or is declared dead, heirs and legatees may withdraw the account balance. What documents are required and how to process specifically differ by local housing fund management center. Before going, check the local center's service guide.

### 4. Recalculate your mobile and broadband plan once a year; downgrade what you don't use; if the carrier won't switch you, complain
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Checking the bill once takes a few minutes. The hard part is holding firm against customer service who won't downgrade you.
- Plain-language summary: Your old plan is often tens of yuan more expensive than the new ones being sold now. Tens a month is hundreds a year thrown away. By regulation, carriers cannot refuse to switch your number to another carrier without a legitimate reason, cannot drag their feet on processing it, and cannot set up special pricing just for those who switch. If they block you, complain.
- Benefit: The Ministry of Industry and Information Technology's regulations state that carriers may not "refuse, obstruct, or delay number-portability service to users without legitimate reason." They may also not "use interception, restriction, or other technical means to affect the communication service quality of ported users." They may also not "set up special pricing plans or marketing plans for ported users." Old plans are often tens of yuan more expensive than current plans. Not switching means hundreds of yuan wasted each year.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (2019). Provisions on the Administration of Number Portability Services (MIIT Letter [2019] No. 242). <http://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2019-11/13/content_5451483.htm>
- Note: First, check in the carrier's app how much data and call time you actually used in the last six months, then choose a plan by that usage. If customer service says "downgrade requires visiting a branch" or "can't change during contract," demand written basis. If they can't provide it, call 12300 to complain.

### 5. Don't buy lottery tickets
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Not buying is saving.
- Plain-language summary: By regulation, the minimum share of lottery sales earmarked as prize money for lotto/digital lottery is 50%, and for Double Color Ball 51%. So for every 1 yuan you put in, on long-term average you get back only 0.51 yuan — a stable 49% loss. In 2024, nationwide lottery sales were 623.486 billion yuan, of which 161.031 billion went directly to the public welfare fund — not part of prizes.
- Benefit: The Ministry of Finance set minimum prize-to-sales ratios: 50% for lotto and digital lottery, 65% for instant lottery, 73% for sports betting. Double Color Ball allocates 51% of sales as prizes — for every 100 yuan sold, only 51 yuan goes to prizes. For you, every 1 yuan invested returns on long-term average only 0.51 yuan — a 49% loss. In 2024, nationwide lottery sales were 623.486 billion yuan, of which 161.031 billion went to the public welfare fund and did not return to lottery buyers (China, 2024).
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Ministry of Finance (2015). Notice on Standardizing and Strengthening the Management of Lottery Fund Composition Ratios (Finance Comprehensive [2015] No. 94). <http://m.mof.gov.cn/czxw/201511/t20151113_1560814.htm>; Ministry of Finance (2025). Ministry of Finance Announcement No. 8 of 2025 (2024 lottery public welfare fund collection and distribution). <http://zhs.mof.gov.cn/zhengcefabu/202508/t20250828_3970977.htm>; China Welfare Lottery Double Color Ball Game Rules (Article 14). <https://mzj.nc.gov.cn/ncsmzj/flcp/202111/5dccc1a5da89434e94a62f8ea0e5887e.shtml>
- Note: On long-term average you are guaranteed to lose — this is built into the system and has nothing to do with luck. Buying is fine, but treat it only as 2-yuan-a-pop entertainment, not as investing.

### 6. When you see "high return," "principal-protected," or "guaranteed profit," walk away
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Just pause when you hear these words.
- Plain-language summary: The regulators' own words: returns above 6% warrant a question mark, above 8% are very dangerous, and above 10% mean prepare to lose all your principal. Legitimate wealth management no longer promises principal protection. Losses from participating in illegal fundraising are borne by you, by regulation — when something goes wrong, no one bails you out.
- Benefit: The chairman of the former China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission said at the Lujiazui Forum in 2018. His original words: "Returns above 6% should raise a question mark, above 8% are very dangerous, and above 10% mean prepare to lose all your principal." The new asset management rules require financial institutions "not to promise principal protection and guaranteed returns," and "when payment difficulties arise, financial institutions may not advance payment in any form." So legitimate wealth management no longer has principal protection. Losses from participating in illegal fundraising "shall be borne by the fundraising participants themselves," and no one compensates you when things go wrong (China, 2018, 2021).
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Guo Shuqing's speech at the 10th Lujiazui Forum (June 2018), reposted by Tianjin Municipal Local Financial Regulatory Bureau. <https://jrgz.tj.gov.cn/ztlm/ztzl/jrjfwphjr/202006/t20200625_2744709.html>; People's Bank of China et al. Regulations on the Prevention and Handling of Illegal Fundraising (State Council Decree No. 737, Article 25). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2021/content_5588815.htm>

### 7. Don't use credit-card minimum payment; don't take out installments or consumer loans for spending
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Need to have enough cash to pay in full before the billing date. The hard part is resisting the urge to spread out large expenses with installments.
- Plain-language summary: Minimum payment is not a perk — it's a loan accruing interest daily, equivalent to about 18% annually. Using it voids the interest-free period on the spot, and the unpaid principal accrues interest from day one. Look only at the annualized rate. The central bank already requires card issuers to display the annualized rate prominently, and prohibits writing only "daily rate."
- Benefit: Credit card overdraft rates had upper and lower limits before 2020: the upper limit was "0.05% daily," the lower was 0.7 times the upper. 0.05% converted to one year is 18.25%, simple interest — at 18.25% per year, interest does not roll into principal. From 2021, pricing was changed to be negotiated between the card issuer and the cardholder. After using minimum payment, the unpaid principal accrues interest daily on the full amount, and the interest-free period is simultaneously voided. The central bank already requires card issuers to display the annualized rate (interest rate converted to a full year) in a prominent manner, "and may not display only the daily rate or daily repayment amount." Look at this annualized figure before deciding (China, 2020 notice).
- Evidence grade: C
- Source: People's Bank of China (2020). Notice on Advancing the Market-Oriented Reform of Credit Card Overdraft Rates (PBOC Document [2020] No. 327). <https://xining.pbc.gov.cn/zhengwugongkai/4081330/4406346/4693549/4159909/index.html>
- Note: 18.25% is computed by multiplying "0.05% × 365" directly, without compounding. With monthly compounding it would be higher. Some banks offer discounts; the annualized rate stated on your own bill prevails. When you truly can't make ends meet, first seek a lower-rate legitimate channel to borrow — don't let minimum payment roll interest.

### 8. Don't tip livestreamers, don't recharge games, don't impulse-buy
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. A few minutes for three things: turn off password-free payment, set per-transaction and daily limits, remove the payment methods bound in the platform. The hard part is resisting that one tap in the moment.
- Plain-language summary: Chinese gamers spend an average of about 480 yuan per person per year on games — money that's basically unrecoverable. The law only allows refunding money spent by minors without parental consent; adults don't get this. The lowest-effort fix is to set up defenses beforehand: turn off password-free payment, set per-transaction and daily limits, and unbind payment methods.
- Benefit: This is a large expense item. In 2024, China's game market actual sales revenue was 325.783 billion yuan, with 674 million users — about 480 yuan per person (computed by dividing the two). Money spent is basically unrecoverable. Only one exception: for spenders with limited civil capacity (here meaning minors) who tipped or recharged without guardian consent, when the guardian requests refund, "the people's court shall support." Adults' own tips and recharges have no such rule. Also, minors playing online games are restricted to Friday, Saturday, Sunday, and statutory holidays, 8 p.m. to 9 p.m. each day, one hour (China, 2020, 2021).
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: National Press and Publication Administration (2024). China's game market revenue exceeded 325.7 billion yuan, a record high (China Audio-Video and Digital Publishing Association Game Work Committee, "2024 China Game Industry Report"). <https://www.nppa.gov.cn/xxfb/ywdt/202412/t20241216_877445.html>; Supreme People's Court (2020). Guiding Opinions on Properly Trying Civil Cases Related to the COVID-19 Pandemic (II) (Fa Fa [2020] No. 17, Article 9). <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/230181.html>; National Press and Publication Administration (2021). Notice on Further Strict Management to Effectively Prevent Minors' Addiction to Online Games (Guo Xin Chu Fa [2021] No. 14). <https://www.nppa.gov.cn/xxfb/tzgs/202108/t20210830_666285.html>
- Note: Graded B because no one has directly measured "how much is saved by not tipping" — only aggregate industry figures. The two tactics of turning off password-free payment and setting limits are experiential methods, C-grade on their own. The original text of Civil Code Articles 19 and 145 is quoted verbatim in Entry 9 (children recharging/tipping on phones). Does not constitute investment advice.

### 9. If a child recharged or tipped on a phone, large amounts spent by a child 8+ can be refunded without parental ratification
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Takes time to gather evidence and negotiate with the platform; platforms usually refuse first, then refund. If negotiation fails, the only option is litigation — that takes months at minimum, and you pay your own legal fees. So get the materials together first; going through the platform and 12315 is more cost-effective. The hard part is that the child won't speak up — you need patience to ask clearly.
- Plain-language summary: For children aged 8 and above, as long as the amount is clearly disproportionate to their age and intellect, and the parent neither agreed nor ratified, the transaction is void and refund can be demanded. Children under 8 cannot make their own spending decisions in the first place. If the parent remains silent, the law treats it as refusal to ratify.
- Benefit: Minors aged 8 and above are persons with limited civil capacity — meaning they can independently do things matching their age and intellect. Civil legal acts disproportionate to their age and intellect require the consent or ratification of their legal representative to be valid: the parent must agree beforehand or ratify afterward. Children under 8 are persons without civil capacity, cannot act on their own; their matters are handled by their legal representative. If the legal representative makes no statement, it's treated as refusal to ratify: a parent's silence counts as not ratifying.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code. Full text reposted by the Supreme People's Procuratorate. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml> Article 19: "Minors aged 8 and above are persons with limited capacity for civil conduct. They perform civil legal acts through their legal representative or with the consent or ratification of their legal representative; however, they may independently perform civil legal acts that purely benefit them or civil legal acts appropriate to their age and intellect." Article 20: "Minors under 8 are persons without capacity for civil conduct and have civil legal acts performed on their behalf by their legal representative." Article 145: "Civil legal acts performed by a person with limited capacity for civil conduct that purely benefit them, or that are appropriate to their age, intellect, or mental health status, are valid; other civil legal acts performed by them are valid only with the consent or ratification of their legal representative." "If the legal representative makes no statement, it is treated as refusal to ratify."
- Note: The key is evidence: you must show it was the child operating the phone, not you. Useful materials include: recharge times clustering during school hours or the parent's work hours, device usage records, the child's chat and game records, and the child's own statement. "Appropriate to age and intellect" has no unified monetary threshold — a few dozen yuan for a game skin and tens of thousands of yuan for tipping are not the same. Prevention is cheaper: set password-free limits on payment methods, enable youth mode, and don't tell the child the payment password. For your own impulse tipping and recharging, see the entry in this section "Don't tip livestreamers, don't recharge games, don't impulse-buy."

### 10. Teach your child one sentence: anyone who asks you to take a parent's phone to operate it or to read out a verification code is a scammer
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Walk through the six types below with your child, and agree on one line: "If you get scammed, tell me first — I won't scold." Also set password-free limits on payment, enable youth mode, and don't tell the child the payment password. The hard part is, when actually scammed, holding back your anger.
- Plain-language summary: These six types of scams have different tactics, but the final step is always the same action: having the child take the parent's phone to transfer money, or read out a verification code. So teaching one sentence is enough: whoever asks you to operate a parent's phone or read out a verification code is a scammer. Add: "If you get scammed, tell me first — I won't scold." If the child dares to tell you the same day, the money may be recoverable.
- Benefit: The Quanzhou Anti-Fraud Center, together with the Quanzhou Education Bureau, broke down six types of scams specifically targeting youth. Their tactics differ, but the final step is the same action: having the child take the parent's phone to transfer money. Type 1: low-price private trades of game accounts and equipment. Xiao Wang privately transferred 260 yuan; the other party claimed "minors transferring is a violation, the account will be frozen," and he clicked a "verification link" on his father's phone — over 30,000 yuan was transferred from the account. Type 2: "lifting anti-addiction." The other party threatened "if you don't transfer money to cancel, your parents will go to jail," and the child transferred 7,000 yuan from the mother's phone. The original text notes that anti-addiction is officially set and "there is no 'private unlocking' channel at all." Type 3: tipping refunds. Scammers impersonated "official customer service," claimed minors' tips could be refunded, and required "using the parent's Alipay to send gifts to a designated livestream room to get the refund" — 73,000 yuan was tipped from the mother's account and the contact was blocked. The original text says "if someone adds you and asks you to 'send gifts for a refund,' it's 100% a scam." Type 4: impersonating classmates or teachers. Scammers hijacked accounts and sent voice messages naming the person to borrow money. During back-to-school season they also infiltrated class group chats to impersonate teachers, sending "pay 500 yuan for school fees," with avatar and nickname matching the real teacher, and arranged "shills" in the group saying "already paid." Type 5: idol worship. Scammers pulled the child into a "star fan group, claim 888 yuan red packet." First had the child transfer 48.88 yuan "for verification." Then scared them: "minors participating is a violation, the platform will freeze 80,000, your parents will go to jail." Total scammed: 16,000 yuan. The original text says "real police and lawyers will never contact minors online for investigation, let alone ask for parents' phones to operate." Type 6: on second-hand platforms, drawing people off-platform with low-priced goods for private transfers. The same material also listed three red lines for kids themselves. First, bank cards and phone cards: don't lend, sell, or rent — if used to launder stolen money, it may constitute the crime of aiding information network criminal activities, leaving a record that affects graduate school and civil service exams. Second, "phone-porting" part-time work is illegal and criminal. Third, overseas "high-salary recruitment" — people may be controlled on arrival overseas (nationwide, October 2025).
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Quanzhou Anti-Fraud Center (2025). First Lesson of the School Year: Anti-Fraud Is a Required Course for Youth — Avoid These 6 Major Traps! <http://gat.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/fjjffpzxrx/spjq/202510/t20251028_7026836.htm> (reposted by the Fujian Provincial Public Security Department)
- Note: Graded B because the official source provides breakdowns of scammer scripts and individual cases, with no incidence rates and no statistics on prevention effectiveness. Distinguish from Entry 9 (children recharging/tipping on phones): money a child spends on their own tipping/recharging can be refunded per the Civil Code. Money scammed away does not fall under that provision — only Entry 2 of Section 8 (calling police for payment hold) applies. Whether it can be recovered depends on whether the money is still in the account. The core script scammers use to scare children is "you violated the rules, your parents will go to jail." Telling kids clearly beforehand that this phrase itself is a marker of a scam is more effective than asking afterward. "Tell me first, I won't scold" must actually be honored. Children fearing scolding and hiding it is the common cause of escalating losses in these cases. Adult versions of impersonation scams, plus AI face-swapping, are in Section 8, Entries 3 and 4 (anti-fraud hard rules, hang up if transfer is involved).

### 11. Refuse extended warranties on electronics
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. The trade-off is that, after the warranty expires, if repair is needed, you pay yourself — but the probability is small.
- Plain-language summary: Extended warranties typically sell for 10% to 50% of the original product price. The markup on extended warranties is more than ten times that of regular goods. U.S. Best Buy in 2003 derived more than half its profits from extended warranties, while warranties were only 3% to 4% of revenue. This markup difference is the extra money you pay.
- Benefit: Extended warranties are usually priced at 10% to 50% of the product's original price. Industry estimates cited in research say the average gross margin on extended warranties is 50% to 60%: for every 100 yuan sold, 50 to 60 yuan is profit. That's about 18 times the gross margin of regular merchandise. In 2003, extended warranties were only 3% to 4% of Best Buy's revenue but contributed more than 50% of profit (U.S., 2000s retail data).
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Chen T, Kalra A, Sun B (2009). Why Do Consumers Buy Extended Service Contracts? Journal of Consumer Research 36(4):611-623. <https://doi.org/10.1086/605298>
- Note: The gross margin figure is from an industry estimate cited by the paper (Business Week 2004), not measured by the paper itself. The above calculation applies to ordinary people who can afford a one-time repair fee. People whose livelihood depends on a single device and have no backup should compute separately. Phone screen-break insurance and the like also need separate calculation: first estimate how often you drop your phone, then check whether the premium is worth it.

### 12. Prefer generic drugs that have passed consistency evaluation and centralized-procurement selected drugs
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. When the doctor prescribes or you go to buy, ask one more question: "Is there a centralized-procurement or generic-name version?"
- Plain-language summary: The second batch of centralized procurement drugs dropped in price by an average of 53%, with the largest drop at 93%. By April 2026, 11 batches had been done, covering 490 drugs. Asking one more question — "Is there a centralized-procurement or generic-name version?" — gets you the same active ingredient for much less money.
- Benefit: National-organized drug centralized procurement: the second batch averaged a 53% price drop, with the largest at 93%. As of April 2026, 11 batches had been conducted, covering 490 drugs. Drugs that have passed or are deemed to have passed consistency evaluation reached 1,695. This is about two-thirds of clinically commonly used chemical drugs (China, 2020 to 2026 official data).
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: National Healthcare Security Administration (2020). Q&A on the Second Batch of National-Organized Drug Centralized Procurement and Use. <https://www.nhsa.gov.cn/art/2020/1/17/art_38_2264.html>; National Healthcare Security Administration (2026). State Council Regular Policy Briefing: Establishing a Sound Full-Cycle, Full-Channel Drug Price Formation Mechanism. <http://www.nhsa.gov.cn/art/2026/4/15/art_14_20221.html>; General Office of the State Council (2016). Opinions on Conducting Consistency Evaluation of Generic Drug Quality and Efficacy (Guo Ban Fa [2016] No. 8). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2016-03/05/content_5049364.htm>; China Food and Drug Administration (2016). Announcement on Issuing Three Technical Guidelines Including the Selection and Determination of Reference Preparations for Ordinary Oral Solid Preparations (2016 No. 61), Appendix 1 and Appendix 3, reposted by Hainan Provincial Drug Administration. <https://amr.hainan.gov.cn/himpa/ztzl/fzy/201607/t20160720_1591587.html>; National Healthcare Security Administration (2024). Strict Quality Supervision of Centralized-Procurement Drugs, "Full Coverage" of Enterprise Inspections and Variety Sampling. <https://www.nhsa.gov.cn/art/2024/12/30/art_52_15287.html>; National Healthcare Security Administration (2025). Healthcare Security and Drug Regulatory Officials Accept Interview with People's Daily Health Client on Issues Related to Centralized-Procurement Drugs. <https://www.nhsa.gov.cn/art/2025/2/9/art_14_15647.html>
- Note: Controversy: it's often said that "consistency evaluation only compares ingredients, not efficacy." This is half right. The evaluation method is to have healthy people take the drug, draw blood, look at the concentration curve as the drug enters the bloodstream, then compare this curve with the originator drug's — falling within 80% to 125% passes. Drugs where slight dose differences cause issues have stricter requirements. Excipients — the fillers and coatings other than the active ingredient — may differ from the originator. Regulations only require that the active ingredient, dosage form, usage, and therapeutic effect be the same. So it is indeed not "give patients the drug for a while and see if they get better." Patient-level efficacy is answered by a separate body of research. The National Healthcare Security Administration organized real-world studies in hospitals. By December 2024, these covered 63 centralized-procurement drugs, nearly 100 tertiary hospitals, and over 300,000 patients. The results all showed no difference from originator drugs. For metformin, the glycated hemoglobin target-achievement rate was 83.4% in the generic group and 78.9% in the originator group; fasting glucose target-achievement was the reverse, 83.8% generic vs 87.6% originator. The two groups traded wins. In January 2025, a doctor publicly stated that centralized-procurement drugs "don't lower blood pressure, anesthetics don't put to sleep, laxatives don't work." ### 19. Don't bet your money on a single stock, a single platform, or a single property
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is accepting: you won't happen to bet on the single best-performing one.
- Plain-language summary: As long as several things don't move perfectly together — same up, same down — splitting your money across them keeps the average return the same but reduces account fluctuation. Conversely, betting everything on a single stock, platform, or property means if it goes wrong, you go wrong entirely.
- Benefit: As long as several assets don't move perfectly together, splitting money across them keeps expected return (average earned) unchanged while reducing volatility (account fluctuation). This is the basic conclusion of portfolio selection proposed by Markowitz in 1952, and has been used for decades since. Conversely, betting all on one stock or one platform means its variance is your entire variance — how much it rises or falls is how much you rise or fall. When something goes wrong, there is no hedge, and no other asset can help cover the loss.
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Markowitz, H. (1952). Portfolio Selection. The Journal of Finance, 7(1), 77–91. <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1952.tb01525.x>
- Note: Graded B because that classic paper is pure mathematical derivation, with no actual figure for "how much less loss diversification yields." Diversification only smooths account fluctuation — it does not guarantee you won't lose. Buying a broad-based index fund (see Entry 17 of this section) is itself the lowest-effort form of diversification. Does not constitute investment advice.

### 24. Don't stock up for "strikethrough prices" or for major sales
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. The trade-off is missing some genuine discounts. You need to remember the everyday price of the few things you buy regularly. The hard part is resisting the urge to stock up during the sale.
- Plain-language summary: People judge whether something is expensive by the first number they see, and any later adjustment is insufficient. So "original 999, now 499" systematically makes you overestimate how cheap it is. That strikethrough price may not have actually been sold.
- Benefit: People's judgment gets anchored by the first number seen, and later adjustments are usually insufficient. So "original 999, now 499" systematically inflates your estimate of "how much cheaper." Regulations provide that the benchmark for discounts should be the lowest transaction price at the same store in the 7 days before the promotion; discounts are computed against the lowest actual sale price in those 7 days. The strikethrough price may not have actually been sold (China, effective 2020).
- Evidence grade: C
- Source: Tversky A, Kahneman D (1974). Judgment under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases. Science 185(4157):1124-1131. <https://doi.org/10.1126/science.185.4157.1124>; State Administration for Market Regulation (2020). - Note: The anchoring effect was derived from lab experiments; no one has calculated how much stockpiling actually loses. Stockpiling also has several costs you won't record: things expiring, taking up space, and "since we have it, use more." ### 37. Sell losing stocks too, by pre-set rules — don't try to flip them by averaging down
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is admitting this trade has lost, and then selling it.
- Plain-language summary: Human instinct is to sell winners and hold losers. In 10,000 U.S. accounts over 7 years, stocks that rose were sold at more than 50% higher rates than stocks that fell. The same people were also more eager to add to losing positions. But the losing stocks they kept underperformed the sold winners by an average of 3.4 percentage points over the following year.
- Benefit: This study looked at the complete trading records of 10,000 accounts at a U.S. discount brokerage from 1987 to 1993. The proportion of gaining stocks sold (PGR) was 0.148. The proportion of losing stocks sold (PLR) was 0.098. The difference was 0.050, with a t-value over 35. Per day, gaining stocks were more than 50% more likely to be sold than losing stocks. The same accounts were also more active in adding to losing positions. Losing stocks were re-bought at a rate of 0.135, gaining stocks at 0.094, t-value 19. The study also tested the idea that "held losers will come back." The result was the opposite: sold winners' excess return over the following year averaged 3.4 percentage points higher than the held losers. "Excess return" means the portion above the market index. The author also computed an example by averages: selling 1,000 USD of a losing stock vs a gaining stock, the losing sale returned about 4.4% more over the following year. This 4.4% includes the early tax benefit of selling losers. Tax benefit is computed per the U.S. tax system (U.S., 1987 to 1993).
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Odean T (1998). Are Investors Reluctant to Realize Their Losses? The Journal of Finance 53(5):1775-1798. <https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00072>
- Note: This entry governs "sell or not"; Entry 15 (don't trade stocks frequently) governs "don't act constantly" — the two don't conflict. Setting rules in advance and following them actually reduces trading frequency. Averaging down is arithmetically correct — buying more does lower average cost. What's wrong is treating it as a recovery method; its actual effect is to increase the money you've bet on that one stock, see Entry 19 (don't bet your money on a single stock). The tax benefit section is per the U.S. tax system; China doesn't follow it, but the rest still holds. This is U.S. account data from the 1980s and 1990s; A-share trading costs and tax systems differ. Does not constitute investment advice.

### 38. When markets boom and crash, act less — don't increase trading at the hottest times
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is, when everyone else is making money, not going along with them.
- Plain-language summary: The Shanghai Stock Exchange's 40 million accounts were tallied for the 2014–2015 boom and crash. The bottom 85% of household accounts lost 250 billion yuan to frequent trading vs buy-and-hold — about 30% of their initial stock market value. In calmer two-and-a-half-year periods, the gap was only 1% to 3%.
- Benefit: The study used the Shanghai Stock Exchange's daily account-level data, covering all approximately 40 million accounts. The sample period was July 2014 to December 2015, 18 months. In those 18 months, the SSE Composite Index first rose more than 150%. It peaked at 5,166.35 points on June 12, 2015, and then fell 40% by year-end. Household accounts were grouped into four tiers by initial market value. The smallest tier held 85% of household accounts; the largest held 0.5%. The two tiers' initial total stock market values were similar — 880 billion yuan and 808 billion yuan. The result was that the smallest 85% lost 250 billion yuan through active trading, while the largest 0.5% gained 254 billion yuan. The "loss" here is compared to doing nothing and holding throughout the same period. The two figures each represent about 30% of the respective group's initial stock market value — 28% for the former, 31% for the latter. For comparison, the two and a half years before June 2014 were calmer. In any 18 months from that period, the same gap was only 1% to 3% of initial market value. ### 45. Designate a specific person as the beneficiary on the policy, and remember to claim when something happens — life insurance 5 years, other insurance 2 years to assert
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Fill it in once when buying; changing the beneficiary later requires written notice to the insurance company.
- Plain-language summary: If no beneficiary is named, or the beneficiary dies first, the insurance payout counts as estate and must first pay off the deceased's debts. If the beneficiary is written as "wife, so-and-so" and you later divorce, it counts as not designated when something happens. Family members need to know this policy exists: life insurance must be claimed within 5 years of learning of the event, other insurance 2 years.
- Benefit: The Insurance Law provides that the beneficiary is designated by the insured or the policyholder. If designated by the policyholder, the insured's consent is required. In three situations, the insurance payout counts as the insured's estate and is inherited accordingly. First, no beneficiary was designated, or the designation was unclear. Second, the beneficiary died before the insured, and there is no other beneficiary. Third, the beneficiary legally lost or gave up the beneficiary right, and there is no other beneficiary. If the beneficiary and the insured die in the same event and the order cannot be determined, the beneficiary is presumed to have died first. Money counted as estate must first settle the deceased's taxes and debts, with the remainder distributed. The Supreme People's Court's interpretation states: if the beneficiary is written as "name plus relationship," and the relationship changes by the time of the event, it counts as no beneficiary designated. Changing the beneficiary without notifying the insurance company does not take effect for the company. The statute of limitations for claiming: 5 years for life insurance, 2 years for other insurance, both running from the date the claimant knew or should have known of the event. Life insurance coverage amounts are typically in the hundreds of thousands of yuan — benefit graded as large (nationwide, 2015 amendment, 2021 effective).
