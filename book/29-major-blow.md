# 29. After a Major Blow

Death of a loved one, being diagnosed with a serious illness, job loss, divorce. These events share something in research: what is affected is not only mood, but the body. The highest-risk period is concentrated in the first few weeks to the first year. This section does not talk about "moving on" — it only discusses what must be watched during this period, what decisions must be postponed, and when to make a doctor's appointment. Most entries calculate the probability of death. The four entries that address money calculate money: do not rush to pay for grief counseling (Entry 9), call 12356 and visit a psychology clinic (Entry 11), postpone major irreversible decisions (Entry 12), and do not treat death as a way to pay off debt (Entry 13). The two kinds of figures are not converted into each other. For those with no family and no friends to turn to, how to make "find someone to watch you" actionable is in Entry 6.

For procedures at the time of a loved one's death, the body, and the money to be claimed, see Section 25. For guardianship and wills of an elderly person before death, see Section 17. For what can be claimed after job loss, see Section 7. For exercise and light exposure during low mood, see Sections 3 and 22. When suicidal thoughts appear, first call 12356 — see Section 1, Entry 25; the timescale of suicidal ideation is in Section 1, Entry 32; sequelae after resuscitation are in Section 1, Entry 33. These are not repeated here.

### 1. Do not be alone in the first days after a loved one dies; those with heart disease, high blood pressure, or diabetes should take their medications as usual; if chest pressure pain occurs, call 120
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. Do not be alone in these days; take medications as usual.
- Plain-language summary: In the first day after a loved one's death, the probability of a heart attack is 21.1 times the baseline; it declines day by day afterward. People aged 60 to 89 have roughly twice the rate of heart attack or stroke within 30 days of a partner's death, falling back after one month. Those who already have cardiovascular disease should not stop medication in these days. If chest pressure pain or shortness of breath occurs, or one side of the body becomes weak, call 120 immediately.
- Benefit: 1,985 acute myocardial infarction patients, with each person's different time periods before and after the event compared against themselves. Among them, 270 (13.6%) had lost a significant person within 6 months before the event, and 19 had lost someone within 1 day before the MI. Within 24 hours after the death of a significant person, the acute MI rate rose 21.1-fold (95% CI 13.1–34.1, the true multiplier roughly between 13 and 34). It declined day by day thereafter. In actual headcount terms: among those with a 10-year MI risk of 5%, one additional case occurred per 1,394 people who had just lost a loved one. Among those with a 20% risk, one additional case per 320. Another UK study compared 30,447 bereaved partners aged 60 to 89 with 83,588 matched controls. Within 30 days of bereavement, 50 (0.16%) in the bereaved group had a MI or stroke. In the control group, 67 (0.08%). IRR 2.20 (1.52–3.15), that is, the probability of an event was about 2.2 times that of controls. Of these, MI 2.14 (1.20–3.81), about 2.1 times. Stroke 2.40 (1.22–4.71), about 2.4 times. The effect weakened after 30 days. Within 90 days, two additional categories were examined. One was non-MI acute coronary syndrome (acute blood-supply problem in the heart that does not yet meet the threshold for MI), IRR 2.20 (1.12–4.29). The other was pulmonary embolism (blood clot blocking a vessel in the lung), IRR 2.37 (1.18–4.75).
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Mostofsky E, et al. (2012). Risk of acute myocardial infarction after the death of a significant person in one's life: the Determinants of Myocardial Infarction Onset Study. Circulation. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.111.061770>; Carey IM, et al. (2014). Increased risk of acute cardiovascular events after partner bereavement: a matched cohort study. JAMA Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2013.14558>
- Note: Hand the medicine box to someone else — do not rely on your own memory. Do not drive long distances in these days. Do not rely on alcohol to push through — for alcohol's ledger, see Section 2. For how to recognize MI and stroke and call transport, see Section 13. Both are studies that went back through population data, and cannot distinguish how many were already about to have an event. But the direction and time period are consistent across the two. For those with no one to hand the medicine box to and who will inevitably be alone in these days, see Entry 6 of this section.

### 2. The week you are diagnosed with a serious illness, do not go alone to pick up the report; postpone decisions other than treatment
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. Have someone accompany you to pick up the report. Postpone major non-treatment decisions by a few days.
- Plain-language summary: In the first week after a cancer diagnosis, the probability of suicide is 12.6 times that of those without a diagnosis, and the probability of dying from cardiovascular disease is 5.6 times. By the first year, suicide risk drops to 3.1 times, more pronounced for cancers that are harder to treat. So bring someone on the day you receive the report, and do not be alone at home afterward. Decisions like quitting a job or selling a house should wait until the next clinic visit.
- Benefit: Swedish national registry data tracked over 6.07 million people (6,073,240 people) from 1991 to 2006. Compared with those without a cancer diagnosis, the relative risk of suicide in the first week after diagnosis was 12.6 (95% CI 8.6–17.8), about 12.6 times. The true multiplier is roughly between 8.6 and 17.8. The incidence in this week was 2.50/1,000 person-years, meaning 2.5 cases per 1,000 people followed for one year. By the first year, it dropped to 3.1 (2.7–3.5, 0.60/1,000 person-years), about 3.1 times. Cardiovascular death in the first week was 5.6 (5.2–5.9, 116.80/1,000 person-years), about 5.6 times. The first 4 weeks, 3.3 (3.1–3.4, 65.81/1,000 person-years). The risk elevation declined rapidly within the first year after diagnosis. The harder the cancer was to treat and the lower the survival probability, the more pronounced the elevation. Comparing the same patients across their own different time periods yielded the same conclusion.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Fang F, et al. (2012). Suicide and cardiovascular death after a cancer diagnosis. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1110307>
- Note: These are data from cancer patients. For other major diagnoses, there is no source of equivalent weight. The people who experience events are actually very few: the first-week suicide rate was 2.50/1,000 person-years, meaning 2.5 cases per 1,000 people followed for one year. Stating this is not to frighten, but to show that "the first few days after learning" require someone by your side. For long-term management, reimbursement, and follow-up after a diagnosis, see Sections 16 and 24. For those who cannot find someone to accompany them, see Entry 6 of this section (replacing "that person" with neighbors and a community list).

### 3. After job loss, first stabilize your routine, medical insurance, and job-search schedule; do not sit at home all day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. First file for unemployment registration and claim unemployment insurance, then arrange job hunting as a fixed schedule. The hard part is getting up on time when no one is pushing you.
- Plain-language summary: Unemployed people have a roughly 60% higher subsequent mortality probability than employed people, more pronounced in men and younger individuals, highest in the first ten years. Removing the influence of smoking and drinking reduces the elevation by about a quarter — this portion is within your control. First register unemployment and claim unemployment insurance; during the claiming period, medical insurance is paid by unemployment insurance on your behalf.
- Benefit: 42 studies, 235 mortality risk estimates, over 20 million people pooled. After smoothing out the influence of age and other conditions, the average HR for all-cause mortality among the unemployed was 1.63, about 60% higher than employed people. Higher in men than women. The effect was more pronounced for those in early and mid career, and smaller for those in late career. The risk was highest in the first 10 years of follow-up and declined thereafter. In the subset of studies that also accounted for health behaviors such as smoking and drinking, the average HR was 24% lower — that is, the calculated elevation was reduced by about a quarter.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Roelfs DJ, et al. (2011). Losing life and livelihood: a systematic review and meta-analysis of unemployment and all-cause mortality. Social Science & Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2011.01.005>
- Note: This is a study that went back through population data and cannot establish causal direction. People in poorer health are also more likely to lose employment. But "after removing the lifestyle portion, the effect is 24% lower" gives an actionable angle. For what can be claimed after job loss, where to claim it, and how to continue social insurance, see Section 7. For how much severance you should receive if dismissed, and not signing a "voluntary resignation," see Section 19.

### 4. If a loved one died by suicide, accident, or homicide, do not count on toughing it out; actively seek professional help
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Calling 12356 is free. To see a doctor, a psychology clinic visit costs the same as an ordinary visit. The hard part is speaking up to find someone.
- Plain-language summary: People whose spouse died by suicide have an approximately 80% higher (men) / 70% higher (women) probability of being diagnosed with a mental disorder within five years, compared with the general population. Compared with those whose spouse died of other causes, the elevation is 70% to 100%. Such bereaved individuals are more likely to feel alienated and ashamed. This is precisely the reason to actively seek out help — do not wait to feel better first.
- Benefit: Danish national registry data tracked 6.7 million adults from 1980 to 2014, totaling over 136 million person-years (all person-years of follow-up summed). Among those bereaved by a spouse's suicide, the risk of a newly diagnosed mental disorder within 5 years, compared with the general population, IRR for men 1.8 (95% CI 1.6–2.0), that is, about 80% higher. For women 1.7 (1.6–1.8), about 70% higher. Compared with those bereaved by other causes of death, IRR for men 1.7 (1.5–1.9) and women 2.0 (1.9–2.2), that is, 70% to 100% higher. Within this group, suicidal behavior, mortality, and the proportion requiring municipal assistance were also higher. Liver cirrhosis, sleep disorders, and other physical conditions were also elevated. A separate review of 57 studies was summarized qualitatively without pooling the numbers. Bereavement by suicide was associated with four outcomes. First, elevated risk of suicide in the partner. Second, elevated risk of psychiatric hospitalization in parents after a child's suicide. Third, elevated risk of maternal suicide after an adult child's suicide. Fourth, elevated risk of depression in children after parental suicide. Compared with those bereaved by other violent deaths, those bereaved by suicide more often reported feeling alienated and ashamed.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Erlangsen A, et al. (2017). Association Between Spousal Suicide and Mental, Physical, and Social Health Outcomes: A Longitudinal and Nationwide Register-Based Study. JAMA Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2017.0226>; Pitman A, et al. (2014). Effects of suicide bereavement on mental health and suicide risk. The Lancet Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(14)70224-X>; Kristensen P et al. (2012). Bereavement and mental health after sudden and violent losses: a review. Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1521/psyc.2012.75.1.76> (the review cited in the note)
- Note: Sudden, violent death refers to accidents, homicides, and disasters. After such deaths, the proportions of post-traumatic stress disorder, depression, and complicated grief are all higher than after death by illness. Recovery is also slower. For what to do when you yourself have suicidal thoughts, see Section 1, Entry 25.

### 5. In the first six months after widowhood, hand over meals, sleep, chronic disease medications, and follow-up visits to a fixed person to watch
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. Find a specific person — a child, sibling, or friend — to visit regularly or call. The hard part is asking and entrusting this task.
- Plain-language summary: Within six months of widowhood, the probability of dying in the same period is about 40% higher than for non-widowed. After six months, it drops to about 15% higher. Men about 23% higher; the figure for women is small enough that it may be chance. This gap cannot be endured by "looking on the bright side." What should be done is to assign medications, meals, and follow-up to a specific person — do not count on your own memory.
- Benefit: 15 prospectively registered cohort studies, 2,263,888 people pooled. With widowhood of less than 6 months, mortality RR 1.41 (95% CI 1.26–1.57), that is, the probability of dying in the same period is about 40% higher than for non-widowed. After 6 months, 1.14 (1.10–1.18), dropping to about 15% higher. For men RR 1.23 (1.18–1.28), about 23% higher. For women 1.04 (1.00–1.08, not statistically significant — that is, this difference may be chance). No clear difference between those under 65 and those 65 and older. Another analysis pooled 123 papers, 1,377 risk estimates, over 500 million people. After smoothing out condition differences, HR 1.23 (1.19–1.28), that is, about 23% higher overall. Men still higher than women. Men 1.27 (1.19–1.35), 27% higher. Women 1.15 (1.08–1.22), 15% higher.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Moon JR, et al. (2011). Widowhood and mortality: a meta-analysis. PLoS ONE. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0023465>; Shor E, et al. (2012). Widowhood and mortality: a meta-analysis and meta-regression. Demography. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s13524-012-0096-x>
- Note: These are studies that went back through population data, and other confounding factors cannot be removed. Couples' lifestyles and illnesses are already similar to begin with. But "the first six months being much higher than later" holds across two independent meta-analyses, making it worth treating as a care window. Men have higher risk and are also less likely to ask for help. For households with a widowed elderly person, follow Section 17 to sort out accounts, guardianship, and wills together. For those who cannot find such a person (living alone, lost an only child, with no surviving children), entrust this to the community and the phone — see Entry 6 of this section.

### 6. For those with no family and no friends, replace "the person watching you" with three things: a neighbor who can enter your home, the community visit list, and an emergency contact on your phone
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. The hard part is speaking up — telling a neighbor and the residents' committee, "I live alone, and something has just happened at home."
- Plain-language summary: Without family and without friends, two things matter: someone discovering you if something happens, and taking medications and eating on time. Medications and meals are up to you — write them as a list, set alarms, do not rely on memory. For being discovered, three things: give a neighbor a spare key, ask the residents' committee whether you can be put on the visit-and-care list, and set up emergency contacts on your phone — do not turn it off.
- Benefit: 44,573 outpatients, all with risk or history of atherothrombosis (the kind of condition where plaque builds up in vessels and clots easily block them). They were enrolled by multinational physicians into the international REACH project and followed, enrolled December 2003 to December 2004, followed for 4 years. Among them, 8,594 (19%) lived alone. All-cause mortality over 4 years among those living alone was 14.1%, versus 11.1% for those cohabiting. Cardiovascular death 8.6% vs 6.8%. Both log-rank P<0.01, differences unlikely to be chance. The gap differed by age group (interaction P=0.03). Ages 45 to 65: living alone vs cohabiting mortality 7.7% vs 5.7%. After smoothing other conditions, HR 1.24 (95% CI 1.01–1.51), that is, about 24% higher. Ages 66 to 80: 13.2% vs 12.3%, HR 1.12 (1.01–1.26), about 10% higher. Over 80: 24.6% vs 28.4%, HR 0.92 (0.79–1.06), with no elevation observed. The Ministry of Civil Affairs and nine other departments' 2022 opinion requires that visit-and-care services be carried out. The targets are elderly people who live alone, are empty-nest, left-behind, disabled, severely disabled, or are special family-planning households. Service methods are "regular in-home visits, phone or video calls, remote monitoring." Township (street-level) governments conduct surveys; village (resident) committees assist, capturing basic information and "willingness to receive visit-and-care services" and incorporating it into grassroots grid management — that is, assigning dedicated personnel by zone. By the end of 2025, "ensure that the monthly visit rate for special-difficulty elderly reaches 100%," and "for special-difficulty elderly with disabilities, conduct visit-and-care no less than once per month." The document also advocates equipping households with smart call systems, smart electricity and water meters, health monitoring devices, and eldercare monitoring devices, "so that when monitoring detects an anomaly, timely warning can be issued and prompt information sent simultaneously to emergency contacts." Service personnel who detect urgent problems "shall promptly assist in calling emergency numbers."
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Udell JA, et al. (2012). Living alone and cardiovascular risk in outpatients at risk of or with atherothrombosis. Archives of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/archinternmed.2012.2782>; Ministry of Civil Affairs and nine other departments (2022). Guiding Opinion on Conducting Visit-and-Care Services for Special-Difficulty Elderly (Min Fa [2022] No. 73). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-10/13/content_5718017.htm>
- Note: How to set up the three things. First, give a spare key or a temporary door lock code to a neighbor or building management, and tell them "I am alone these days." No friendship is required — only that someone knows you are inside. Second, tell the community residents' committee that you live alone, and ask whether you can be put on the visit-and-care list. Among the elderly, those who live alone, are empty-nest, or have lost an only child are explicitly named service targets; while you are at it, ask whether the locality has free smart call devices, smart water meters, or similar. Third, set up emergency contacts and a medical emergency card on your phone; do not silence or power off the phone in these days. The reasons for Grade B are two. First, the association between living alone and mortality comes from studies that went back through population data, and cannot remove the reverse explanation that "people who are already in poorer health and poorer condition are more likely to live alone" — the original paper itself states this finding awaits confirmation. Second, the visit-and-care document covers only the elderly. Those under 60 have no corresponding dedicated system. What can be relied upon is one line in the document requiring grid workers and social workers to "promptly detect psychological crisis risks such as family change, unemployment, and dropout" (see Entry 11 of this section; to talk to someone, call 12356). Why "having someone in the room" matters — see Section 13, Entry 1 (chest compressions). Of out-of-hospital cardiac arrests, 79.2% occur at home. When someone present performs compressions, 16.1% survive to discharge; without someone, only 3.9%. No one in the room means falling into the lower tier. Two other things that come along. Arranging a funeral itself requires visits to the funeral home, community health service institution, police, housing fund, and several other windows (see Section 25). Arranging it as a fixed daily outing schedule means these days' outings are already built in. The hotline can be called repeatedly — not just once (see Entry 11 of this section; to talk to someone, call 12356). "Living alone" is itself also a long-term risk factor. Living alone carries a mortality odds ratio of 1.32, about 30% higher — see Section 22, Entry 10 (treating regular face-to-face contact as a health expenditure). But here we address only these next few days; the long-term ledger is separate.

### 7. After a parent's death, tell the child the truth, let them participate in the farewell, and do not send them elsewhere to "change environment"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. The hard part is that you are also suffering, and must still explain clearly to the child.
- Plain-language summary: Children who lose a father or mother before age 18 have an approximately 50% higher probability of dying by early adulthood than those who did not experience this. When parents died by accident or suicide, the elevation is about 80%; when parents died of disease, about 30%. What the household can do is tell them truthfully, let them participate in the farewell, do not interrupt school, and do not send them to a relative's home to "change environment."
- Benefit: All those born in Denmark 1968 to 2008 and in Sweden 1973 to 2006 were included. Plus 89.3% of those born in Finland 1987 to 2007. Total over 7.3 million people. Among them, 189,094 lost a parent before age 18. All-cause mortality in these children was 50% higher than in non-bereaved people (mortality ratio 1.50, 95% CI 1.43–1.58, that is, about 1.5 times). When parents died of non-natural causes such as accidents or suicide, 1.84 (1.71–2.00), about 80% higher. When parents died of natural causes such as disease, 1.33 (1.24–1.41), about 30% higher. The elevation was highest when the child's cause of death and the parent's cause of death fell in the same category.
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Li J, et al. (2014). Mortality after parental death in childhood: a nationwide cohort study from three Nordic countries. PLoS Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001679>
- Note: When speaking with the child, do not make up "gone on a long trip." Guardianship and custody arrangements must be in writing. The Grade B is not because the numbers are weak. This is a whole-population cohort study across three countries, and the figures themselves meet the standard for Grade A. Grade B is given because from this number to the specific practices of "how to talk to the child, whether to attend the farewell," there is no direct trial evidence. The authors also note that data on parent-child relationships and child lifestyles after bereavement are lacking. The elevation contains both an innate biological component and the long-term effect left by bereavement. For designating a guardian and arranging property, see Section 17. For child support and schooling, see Section 18.

### 8. If grief is still stuck after six months and daily life has broken down, see a psychiatrist or clinical psychology department
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Consultation fee plus treatment fee. This kind of treatment is delivered in courses; in the trial, an average of 16 sessions over 19 weeks. The hard part is taking yourself to make this appointment.
- Plain-language summary: About one in ten bereaved adults gets stuck: still persistently missing the deceased after six months or more, unable to eat, work, or care for children; numb; feeling life has no meaning. This is not a lack of toughness — it has diagnostic criteria and dedicated treatment. The targeted therapy response rate is 51%, versus 28% for ordinary psychotherapy, with faster onset of effect.
- Benefit: 14 studies pooled, of adults whose loved ones did not die by violence and who were not themselves in psychiatric care. Prolonged grief disorder prevalence 9.8% (95% CI 6.8–14.0), that is, about 10 per 100 bereaved adults. The older the age, the higher the proportion. Another study tested whether the diagnostic criteria were accurate; it recruited 291 bereaved individuals and conducted three interviews at 0–6, 6–12, and 12–24 months after bereavement. The core feature of the diagnosis is intense yearning for the deceased. Plus at least five of these nine items. The first five are emotional numbness, shock, feeling life has no meaning, distrust of others, and pain over this loss. The latter four are difficulty accepting, difficulty recognizing who one should be, avoidance of reality, and inability to move forward with life. These manifestations must occur every day, or be severe enough to interfere with functioning. And they must persist for at least 6 months after the loved one's death, still affecting daily life. Another trial randomized 95 people into two groups, delivering 16 sessions over an average of 19 weeks. Response rate for complicated grief therapy was 51%. Ordinary interpersonal psychotherapy 28% (P=0.02, the difference unlikely to be chance). The former also had faster onset. Number needed to treat 4.3, meaning that for every 4.3 people treated, one additional person improved.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Lundorff M, et al. (2017). Prevalence of prolonged grief disorder in adult bereavement: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Affective Disorders. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2017.01.030>; Prigerson HG, et al. (2009). Prolonged grief disorder: Psychometric validation of criteria proposed for DSM-V and ICD-11. PLoS Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1000121>; Shear MK, et al. (2005). Treatment of complicated grief: a randomized controlled trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.293.21.2601>
- Note: Do not count months yourself. The real test is "affecting eating, sleeping, work, childcare" — if it matches, go to the appointment. The specialty is called psychiatry, psychology, or clinical psychology; public psychiatric specialty hospitals all have it. This diagnosis is called complicated grief in some sources and prolonged grief disorder in others. Different criteria specify different durations, some 6 months, some 12 months. The 9.8% proportion comes from 14 studies whose methods differed considerably; the authors caution that it may not generalize to other populations.

### 9. Do not rush to pay for grief counseling; first check whether your grief is genuinely stuck (the symptoms in Entry 8)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free of charge, conversely saving a counseling fee.
- Plain-language summary: Providing counseling to all bereaved people yields little benefit, and after a while no benefit is detectable, because most people recover on their own. But for those already clearly unable to adapt, treatment is as effective as psychotherapy for other psychological problems. So first compare against Entry 8 (grief stuck — see a psychiatrist); only spend this money if it matches.
- Benefit: 61 controlled studies pooled. Universal psychological intervention for the bereaved yields small benefit at the end of treatment, and after a period no benefit is detectable. Interventions that enroll only bereaved individuals who are "already clearly unable to adapt" are as effective as psychotherapy for other psychological problems. Studies that did not screen in advance showed worse results, because the no-intervention group also recovered on their own over time. Another 14 randomized trials pooled: treatment for those already affected was significantly better than controls at end of treatment and at follow-up, and the effect grew over time. Preventive interventions applied universally to those not yet affected were ineffective. Another study followed 205 people from before widowhood through 6 and 18 months after. This study identified five trajectories: common grief, prolonged grief, prolonged depression, improvement during bereavement, and resilience (coping well). The resilience type was most common; the common grief type was relatively less common.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Currier JM, et al. (2008). The effectiveness of psychotherapeutic interventions for bereaved persons: a comprehensive quantitative review. Psychological Bulletin. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.134.5.648>; Wittouck C, et al. (2011). The prevention and treatment of complicated grief: a meta-analysis. Clinical Psychology Review. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2010.09.005>; Bonanno GA, et al. (2002). Resilience to loss and chronic grief: a prospective study from preloss to 18-months postloss. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.83.5.1150>; Stroebe M et al. (2007). Health outcomes of bereavement. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(07)61816-9> (the review cited in the note)
- Note: The point is "not everyone needs it," not "no one should do it." The high-risk bereavement of Entry 4 (loved one died by suicide, accident, or homicide) and those already stuck in Entry 8 (grief stuck — see a psychiatrist) are precisely the people for whom there is evidence to support intervention. A Lancet review reaches the same conclusion: grief is not an illness, and most people do not need professional intervention. Effort should be directed at high-risk individuals. And at those who already have complicated grief, post-bereavement depression, and stress disorders.

### 10. After separation or divorce, do not drop medical insurance, and do not prop yourself up with alcohol
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free of charge. Keep medical insurance, stop alcohol. The hard part is that during this period alcohol is easily used as a prop.
- Plain-language summary: People who are separated or divorced have a markedly higher probability of premature death than those still married, more pronounced in men and younger individuals. This is not necessarily caused by divorce — divorced people may already differ in health and income. Two things are within your control: do not let medical insurance and check-ups lapse, and do not lean on tobacco or alcohol.
- Benefit: 32 prospectively registered cohort studies pooled, covering over 6.5 million people, 160,000 deaths, over 755,000 divorces, across 11 countries. Compared with those still married, separated or divorced adults had markedly elevated premature death risk. The elevation was more pronounced in men and younger individuals than in women and older individuals. The authors discussed four possible pathways. First, social selection — people who are already different in health or condition are more likely to divorce. Second, resource disruption — income and security cut off. Third, health behavior change — tobacco, alcohol, daily routine worsen. Fourth, chronic psychological stress (chronic strain).
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Sbarra DA, et al. (2011). Divorce and Death: A Meta-Analysis and Research Agenda for Clinical, Social, and Health Psychology. Perspectives on Psychological Science. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691611414724>

### 12. In the first three months after a major change, all irreversible major decisions should be postponed

- Plain-language summary: During this period your judgment of "opportunities" is at its worst. People claiming to contact the deceased, claiming to recover your lost money, urging you to quit your job and start over elsewhere, recruiting you into a partnership — all will come. Anything involving amounts exceeding one month's income, or with high cost to reverse, should be postponed by three months. First explain it to someone with no stake in the matter; do not decide on the same day.
- Benefit: Avoid making irrevocable property dispositions during the period right after a blow, when judgment is at its worst. These decisions typically involve amounts ranging from tens of thousands of yuan to the value of a house, so the benefit is rated Medium. Official documents likewise list "family change, unemployment, dropout" as psychological crisis risks that the community should actively screen for — the state during this period is treated as one requiring outside help.

### 13. Do not treat death as a way to repay debt: life insurance does not pay within two years, work injury is not recognized, and debts are still deducted from the estate first
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free of charge; merely settling this ledger.
- Plain-language summary: A life insurance contract does not pay for suicide within two years, only returning the small refundable amount in the policy. Work injury is not recognized either, and the three work-death payments are entirely forfeited. Debts are first deducted from the estate you leave behind; the family only takes what remains. They also bear the health cost described in Entry 4 of this section (bereavement by suicide or violent death).
- Benefit: Article 44 of the Insurance Law governs contracts that pay insurance benefits upon the death of the insured. Counted from the date the contract is formed or its effectiveness restored, if the insured commits suicide within two years, the insurer does not bear the obligation to pay the insurance benefit. If the insured, at the time of suicide, is a person without civil capacity — that is, a person who cannot legally be responsible for their own actions — this does not apply. In that case, the insurance company shall return the cash value of the policy according to the contract, that is, returning only the small refundable amount in the policy, not the sum insured. Life insurance sums are typically in the hundreds of thousands of yuan, so the benefit is rated Large. Article 16, Item 3 of the Work Injury Insurance Regulations: self-injury or suicide shall not be recognized as a work injury or deemed a work injury. The three work-death benefits in Article 39 are forfeited entirely. First, the funeral subsidy, paid as 6 months of the local average monthly wage of employees in the previous year. Second, the survivor pension for dependents. Third, the one-time work-death compensation, equal to 20 times the previous year's nationwide per-capita disposable income of urban residents. Article 1159 of the Civil Code: when dividing the estate, taxes and debts that the deceased was legally obligated to pay shall be paid off first; the remainder is then divided. Article 1161: heirs settle liabilities only up to the actual value of the estate they receive — that is, only the inherited portion is used to repay; nothing more need be paid out of pocket, and those who disclaim inheritance may be exempt from the obligation to repay.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2015 revision). Work Injury Insurance Regulations (State Council Order No. 586), Articles 16, 39. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1778064.htm>; National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code of the People's Republic of China, Articles 1159, 1161. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff808081729d1efe01729d50b5c500bf>

Article 44 of the Insurance Law governs only the first two years; it is stated here for completeness, not as a timetable. For the health cost to family, see Entry 4 of this section (a spouse's death by suicide raises new mental disorder risk by 70–80% within 5 years).
