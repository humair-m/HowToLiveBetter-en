# 26. Building a Website or Platform: Qualifications, Filings, and Servers

You want to build a website or app. This section answers four questions: what kind of business it legally constitutes, which license you need to obtain, where the server is located, and who is responsible for content posted by users and money collected on the platform. The metric is personal freedom and money. The entries are ordered as follows: first what can send you to prison, then what requires a license in advance, then what the platform must do every day, and finally an entry on choosing a server, which can only give judgment, not numbers. If you are an employed technical worker, the red lines are in Section 11. How to register a company and pay taxes is in Section 12 and is not repeated here. For the comparison table of various licenses and the decision table for choosing a server, see [docs/做平台要办哪些证.md](../docs/做平台要办哪些证.md).

### 1. The platform should not collect money and then forward it to sellers; let money be settled directly through a licensed payment institution
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. It takes some time to integrate a licensed payment institution's product. A licensed payment institution is a company that has obtained a payment license from the People's Bank of China. Using its split-settlement or escrow transaction function, buyer money is settled directly to the seller, without entering the platform's own account
- Plain-language summary: Buyer money enters your account first, then you forward it to the seller — that is engaging in the payment business. This business requires approval from the People's Bank of China, and you must truly put up 100 million yuan in capital. Without approval, you will be shut down, your earnings confiscated, and fined, with the boss and the handler additionally fined. Serious cases also carry prison sentences. The only safe approach is one: money does not enter your account
- Benefit: The regulation defines the payment business as "transferring monetary funds according to the electronic payment instruction submitted by the payee or payer." In plain language, transferring money on someone's instruction. To engage in this business, "approval must be obtained from the People's Bank of China and a payment business license obtained." Registered capital is at least 100 million yuan, and it is paid-in monetary capital — this 100 million yuan must be actually deposited into the company account. "Without lawful approval,擅自 establishing a non-bank payment institution, engaging in or disguisedly engaging in the payment business, shall be banned by the People's Bank of China in accordance with the law, and illegal gains confiscated." Without approval, the operation is shut down and earnings confiscated. Where illegal gains are 500,000 yuan or more, an additional fine of 1 to 5 times the illegal gains. Where there are no illegal gains, or illegal gains are less than 500,000 yuan, a fine of 500,000 to 2,000,000 yuan. The legal representative and the person directly responsible are each additionally fined 100,000 to 500,000 yuan. The Criminal Law also treats "illegally engaging in fund payment and settlement business" as illegal business operations. Serious cases carry sentences of up to 5 years; particularly serious cases over 5 years. What counts as "serious" is delineated in the Supreme People's Court and Supreme People's Procuratorate's interpretation. The line is illegal business volume of 5,000,000 yuan or more, or illegal gains of 100,000 yuan or more (nationwide, from May 2024)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: State Council (2023). Regulation on the Supervision and Administration of Non-Bank Payment Institutions (State Council Order No. 768, Articles 2, 6, 8, 27, and 47). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2024/issue_11086/202401/content_6924970.html> ; People's Bank of China (2024). Implementing Rules for the Regulation on the Supervision and Administration of Non-Bank Payment Institutions (People's Bank of China Order [2024] No. 4, Article 68). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2024/issue_11546/202408/content_6970979.html> ; National People's Congress (1997). Criminal Law (Article 225, item 3). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml> ; Supreme People's Court and Supreme People's Procuratorate (2019). Interpretation on Several Questions Concerning the Application of Law in Criminal Cases of Illegally Engaging in Fund Payment and Settlement Business and Illegally Buying and Selling Foreign Exchange (Articles 1, 3, and 4). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/201901/t20190131_407161.shtml>
- Note: Designing money so it does not enter your account is the only safe approach. The market calls this kind of collection-and-forwarding "second clearing" (erqing), but the official documents do not use this term. The implementing rules use the term "disguisedly engaging in the payment business," meaning transferring money on someone's instruction without approval. The Supreme People's Court and Supreme People's Procuratorate's interpretation lists only three situations: fake transactions for cash-out, cash-out through corporate accounts, and cash-out through checks. It does not name platform collection-and-forwarding, so you cannot say that platform collection-and-forwarding "necessarily constitutes a crime"

### 2. A fee-charging website requires a license; matchmaking transactions is a separate class of license; free sites only need a filing
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=多 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Filing is free. The value-added telecommunications business operating license costs money; it governs "whether a website that charges users may operate." To obtain this license, you must first have a company. Operating only within the province, registered capital of no less than 1,000,000 yuan. Operating across provinces, no less than 10,000,000 yuan. Review period 60 days; license valid for 5 years
- Plain-language summary: A website that charges users must first obtain a value-added telecommunications business operating license. This license is issued only to companies; individuals cannot obtain it. A non-charging website only requires filing, which is free. Without a license and charging users to operate, your income is confiscated and you are fined, with serious cases shutting the site. Whether your platform counts as the matchmaking-transactions category is not concluded for you in this entry; the reason is in the note
- Benefit: The regulation's original wording: "The state implements a licensing system for commercial internet information services; it implements a filing system for non-commercial internet information services. Without obtaining a license or completing filing procedures, no one may engage in internet information services." Charging means you must first obtain a license; not charging means you must first file — without either, you cannot operate. What counts as commercial? The regulation says "providing information or webpage production and other service activities to internet users for a fee through the internet." In short, charging users. The matchmaking-transactions category is called online data processing and transaction processing business, abbreviated EDI in the industry. The regulation defines it as "providing online data processing and transaction/affair processing services to users through public communication networks or the internet, using various data and transaction/affair processing application platforms connected to public communication networks or the internet." It also "includes transaction processing business, electronic data interchange business, and network/electronic device data processing business." What this describes is the website processing data online for users, or matchmaking and processing transactions between buyers and sellers. Operating without a license, or operating beyond scope, results in confiscation of illegal gains, plus a fine of 3 to 5 times the illegal gains. Operating beyond scope means holding a license but doing work beyond the scope written on the license. Where there are no illegal gains, or illegal gains are less than 50,000 yuan, a fine of 100,000 to 1,000,000 yuan. Serious cases result in orders to close the website (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: State Council (2000). Administrative Measures for Internet Information Services (State Council Order No. 292, second revision December 6, 2024, Articles 3, 4, 7, and 19). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1860864.htm> ; State Council (2024). Decision on Amending and Repealing Some Administrative Regulations (State Council Order No. 797, item 18). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2025/issue_11786/202501/content_6997034.html> ; State Council (2000). Telecommunications Regulation (2016 second revision, Articles 7, 9, 13, and 69). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2016/content_5139478.htm> ; Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (2017). Administrative Measures for Telecommunications Business Operating Licenses (MIIT Order No. 42, Articles 6, 9, 11, and 14). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2017/content_5240090.htm>
- Note: Whether what you want to do falls under the EDI category, take the definition in the Telecommunications Business Classification Catalog and match it yourself. No official statement explicitly says "e-commerce platforms with matchmaking and fund handling must obtain an EDI license." The MIIT service guide only says "apply for the corresponding telecommunications business license according to the business definition." So whether you need to obtain one can only be judged from the definition yourself. One more point: this license cannot be obtained by individuals; the applicant must be a lawfully established company

### 3. Live streaming requires a Network Culture Operating License; the license for audiovisual programs is essentially unattainable for private companies
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=多 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: The Network Culture Operating License, commonly called the "wangwen" license, governs "whether you can make money from network performances such as live streaming and game commentary." It is approved by the provincial-level cultural administrative department, with results within 20 days of acceptance, valid for 3 years, renewed 30 days before expiry. The audiovisual program license governs "whether you can release programs online," and requires the applicant to be a wholly state-owned or state-controlled entity
- Plain-language summary: Live streaming and game commentary require a Network Culture Operating License issued by the provincial-level cultural department. The business scope on the license must expressly include network performance. Results within 20 days of acceptance; the license is valid for 3 years. Videos you have streamed must be retained for at least 60 days. Operating without a license will result in being called in, warned, and fined up to 30,000 yuan; continuing leads to being blacklisted
- Benefit: Entities engaging in network performance must first obtain a license. The regulation's original wording: "shall, in accordance with the Interim Provisions on the Administration of Internet Culture, apply to the provincial-level cultural administrative department to obtain the Network Culture Operating License, the business scope of which shall expressly include network performance." Playing games for others to watch, with commentary, is also governed under this set, with the regulation's original wording "managed by reference to these measures." All video materials of network performances "shall be retained for no less than 60 days" — videos you have streamed must be retained for at least 60 days. Engaging in commercial internet cultural activities without approval results in being ordered to stop and given a warning, with an additional fine of up to 30,000 yuan. Continuing after being called in leads to being listed on the cultural market blacklist. Releasing programs online is another license: "shall obtain the Information Network Transmission of Audiovisual Programs License." The applicant must "have legal person status and be a wholly state-owned or state-controlled entity" — it must be a wholly state-owned or state-controlled entity; private companies are essentially unable to obtain it. Engaging without this license results in a warning, an order to correct, and an additional fine of up to 30,000 yuan. Serious cases are penalized under the Radio and Television Administration Regulation (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Ministry of Culture (2016). Administrative Measures for Network Performance Business Activities (Wen Shi Fa [2016] No. 33, Articles 2, 4, 6, 13, and 20). <https://zwgk.mct.gov.cn/zfxxgkml/scgl/202012/t20201206_918194.html> ; Ministry of Culture (2011). Interim Provisions on the Administration of Internet Culture (Ministry of Culture Order No. 51, revised in 2017 by Ministry of Culture Order No. 57, Articles 7, 8, 9, 16, 21, and 28). <https://zwgk.mct.gov.cn/zfxxgkml/zcfg/bmgz/202012/t20201204_905340.html> ; State Administration of Radio and Television and Ministry of Information Industry (2007). Administrative Provisions on Internet Audiovisual Program Services (Order No. 56, 2015 revision, Articles 2, 7, 8, and 24). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/2022-08/23/content_5722664.htm> ; Cyberspace Administration of China and six other departments (2021). Guiding Opinions on Strengthening the Standardized Management of Network Live Streaming (Guo Xin Ban Fa Wen [2021] No. 3, item 9). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-02/10/content_5586472.htm>
- Note: The seven-department document is the clearest on which business requires which license. Platforms making money from live streaming require a wangwen license plus an ICP filing (ICP 备案), which is the procedure for registering the website with the MIIT. Releasing programs online requires an audiovisual license, or completing registration. Publishing news requires an Internet News Information Service License. Additionally, the Interim Provisions on the Administration of Internet Culture reproduced by the China Government Network in 2025 is the unrevised 2011 original text; for citation, the current text of the Ministry of Culture and Tourism prevails

### 4. Servers located in mainland China must be filed; the access provider must itself hold a value-added telecommunications license
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Filing is free. It is submitted through the service provider together with the server, usually waiting several days to several weeks. Checking whether the service provider has qualifications takes ten minutes
- Plain-language summary: Servers located in mainland China must be filed before launching the site. Filing is free, submitted through the service provider together with the server, generally taking several days to several weeks. Without filing, you are ordered to correct within a time limit and fined 10,000 yuan; failure to comply results in site closure. Not posting the filing number on the homepage results in an additional fine of 5,000 to 50,000 yuan
- Benefit: The regulation's original wording: "Without filing, no one may engage in non-commercial internet information services within the People's Republic of China." Websites that do not charge users, as long as they are located in mainland China, must also first be filed. Without filing, ordered to correct within a time limit, and fined 10,000 yuan. Refusing to correct results in closure of the website. The service provider that connects your server to the internet is also regulated: it "may not provide internet access services for organizations or individuals not filed who are engaging in non-commercial internet information services." The service provider that charges you is the same. The regulation says such service providers "may not provide access or fee-collection and other services to units or individuals that have not lawfully obtained an operating license or completed non-commercial internet information service filing procedures." Meaning: sites without a license and without filing — regular service providers may not connect them. Furthermore, server room colocation and cloud hosting are called internet data center business in the regulation; broadband access is called internet access service business — both of these themselves require a license, and the service provider must first hold one. Where the website's homepage does not display the filing number, ordered to correct, with an additional fine of 5,000 to 50,000 yuan (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Ministry of Information Industry (2005). Administrative Measures for the Filing of Non-Commercial Internet Information Services (MII Order No. 33, revised in 2024 by MIIT Order No. 68, Articles 5, 13, 18, and 22). <https://www.miit.gov.cn/gyhxxhb/jgsj/cyzcyfgs/bmgz/xxtxl/art/2024/art_84a0cfa0ebd049bbbe751dca9a008e56.html> ; Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (2017). Administrative Measures for Telecommunications Business Operating Licenses (MIIT Order No. 42, Articles 20 and 24). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2017/content_5240090.htm> ; Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (2015). Telecommunications Business Classification Catalog (2015 edition, B11, B14, B21, B25). <https://www.miit.gov.cn/zwgk/zcwj/wjfb/tg/art/2020/art_e98406cd89844f7e92ea1bcf3b5301e0.html>
- Note: Before negotiating price, first check whether the service provider holds a license. To check licenses, use the MIIT Telecommunications Business Market Comprehensive Management Information System at tsm.miit.gov.cn, searching by full company name or license number. To check website filing, use beian.miit.gov.cn. A "server room" without the corresponding licenses — when something goes wrong, you will not even know who to appeal to. Placing machines overseas does circumvent filing. But where users are in China and money is in China, the obligations in Entries 5 through 10 of this section do not diminish; instead, an additional layer of cross-border data transfer cost is added

### 5. Letting users come sell things means the platform must verify, register, report information, and retain records for three years
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. You must verify seller qualifications in the back-end, and also build a registration file. Report tax-related information once per quarter, and retain transaction data for three years. The hard part is that it is year-round work, not a one-time task
- Plain-language summary: Letting others sell things on your platform means you must first verify sellers' identity, address, contact information, and licenses, then register and file. After filing, re-verify at least once every six months. Who the sellers are is reported to market regulation; tax-related matters are reported quarterly to the tax authorities. Goods and transaction records are retained for three years. Missing any item results in being ordered to correct; failure to correct results in a fine of 20,000 to 100,000 yuan
- Benefit: The law imposes a set of requirements on the platform. The original wording: "shall require operators applying to enter the platform to sell goods or provide services to submit their identity, address, contact information, administrative licenses, and other true information, conduct verification and registration, establish registration files, and periodically verify and update." Before a seller enters, they must provide identity, address, contact information, and the licenses they should hold. You must verify, register and file, and afterward periodically re-verify. How long is "periodically"? The departmental rules are more specific: "verify and update at least once every six months." You must also report the identity information of operators on the platform to the market regulation department. The tax authorities must also be reported to, with the original wording: "in accordance with the laws and administrative regulations on tax collection and administration, report to the tax department the identity information of operators on the platform and information related to taxation." So who the sellers are goes to market regulation; the seller's identity and tax situation go to the tax authorities. Goods and service information and transaction information "shall be retained for no less than three years from the date of transaction completion." From the date a transaction is completed, data is retained for at least three years. Failure to do any of the above results in being ordered to correct within a time limit. Failure to correct past the deadline results in a fine of 20,000 to 100,000 yuan. Serious cases result in being ordered to suspend business for rectification, plus an additional fine of 100,000 to 500,000 yuan. Tax-related information must "be reported within the month following the end of the quarter" — within the month after each quarter ends. Failure to report on time carries the same penalties (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2018). E-Commerce Law (Articles 10, 27, 28, 31, and 80). <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf8af050b81> ; State Administration for Market Regulation (2021). Administrative Measures for the Supervision of Online Transactions (SAMR Order No. 37, Articles 8, 24, 25, and 31). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2021/content_5602020.htm> ; State Council (2025). Provisions on the Submission of Tax-Related Information by Internet Platform Enterprises (State Council Order No. 810, Articles 2, 3, 4, and 10). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2025/issue_12146/202507/content_7030983.html> ; State Taxation Administration (2025). Announcement on Matters Related to the Submission of Tax-Related Information by Internet Platform Enterprises (STA Announcement 2025 No. 15). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202506/content_7029727.htm>
- Note: Sellers themselves must also undergo market entity registration. The law lists several exceptions that do not require registration: "individuals selling self-produced agricultural and sideline products, household handicraft products, individuals using their own skills to engage in convenience labor activities that do not require a license under the law, and small sporadic transactions." "Small sporadic" has a specific number; Article 8 of the Online Transaction Supervision Administrative Measures sets annual transaction volume at 100,000 yuan. The platform also has two further things to do: distinguish between registered and unregistered sellers, and not delete consumer reviews

### 6. You must manage content posted by users: review mechanisms, reporting channels, and immediately stopping transmission and reporting upon discovery of illegal information
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: You must spend money hiring people for review, which is the platform's largest long-term expense. Reporting channels and the records of each handling must be kept. The hard part is that someone must watch it every day; one day's lapse can lead to an incident
- Plain-language summary: For things users post, the platform is responsible when something goes wrong. You must build this set of systems: registration, post-review, comment-review, daily patrol, incident handling. Place a reporting entry in a prominent place on the page. Upon seeing illegal information, immediately stop transmission, preserve records, and report to the competent department. Failure to do so results in a fine of 50,000 to 500,000 yuan, with serious cases higher
- Benefit: The regulation states the platform "shall assume the primary responsibility for information content management"; content posted by users is managed by the platform itself. Specifically, it must "establish systems for user registration, account management, information publication review, follow-up comment review, layout and page ecology management, real-time patrol, emergency handling, and the handling of internet rumors and black-industry-chain information." From registration, posting, and comments, to daily patrol and incident handling, each link must have written rules. It must also "place a convenient complaint and reporting entry in a prominent position" — the reporting button must be placed where users can see and click it at a glance. Upon discovery of illegal information, "shall immediately take disposal measures in accordance with the law, preserve relevant records, and report to the relevant competent department." The Cybersecurity Law is more specific. The original wording: "Upon discovery of information that laws and administrative regulations prohibit from being published or transmitted, shall immediately stop transmitting that information, take elimination and other disposal measures to prevent information dissemination, preserve relevant records, and report to the relevant competent department." Failure to comply results in an order to correct, a warning, a notification, and an additional fine of 50,000 to 500,000 yuan. Refusal to correct, or serious cases, results in a fine of 500,000 to 2,000,000 yuan, and may also result in being ordered to suspend business for rectification, close the website or application, or have the license or business license revoked. The person directly responsible is each additionally fined 50,000 to 200,000 yuan (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Cyberspace Administration of China (2019). Provisions on the Ecological Governance of Network Information Content (CAC Order No. 5, Articles 8, 9, 10, 16, and 37). <http://www.cac.gov.cn/2019-12/20/c_1578375159509309.htm> ; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2025). Cybersecurity Law (2025 amendment, effective January 1, 2026, Articles 49 and 69). <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm> ; State Council (2000). Administrative Measures for Internet Information Services (Articles 15, 16, and 23). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1860864.htm>
- Note: You do not have to devise review rules yourself; copying is fine. The nine categories of content that may not be posted are listed clearly in Article 15 of the Administrative Measures for Internet Information Services. Live streaming has three additional requirements: a live content review platform must be built; the ability to cut off a live stream in progress at any time (the law calls this "real-time blocking"); and live content and logs must be retained for 60 days. Network performance video materials likewise for no less than 60 days

### 7. Providing information publishing or instant messaging services requires that users be required to provide true identity information
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: A real-name verification channel must be integrated, and such channels are charged per use. The product is designed on "back-end real-name, front-end voluntary": the back end stores the real name; the front end displays a nickname
- Plain-language summary: If your website allows posts or chat, you must require users to provide true identity information when they open the service. Without it, they may not use it. The method is: store the real name in the back end, and let users themselves decide what nickname the front end displays; verification by phone number is enough. Without real-name, first ordered to correct. Failure to correct results in a fine of 50,000 to 500,000 yuan, and possible site closure
- Benefit: The law's original wording: "Where a network operator provides users with network access, domain name registration services, processes fixed-line telephone, mobile phone, and other network access procedures, or provides information publishing, instant messaging, and other services to users, when entering into an agreement with the user or confirming the provision of services, it shall require the user to provide true identity information. Where the user does not provide true identity information, the network operator may not provide the relevant services to them." Failure to require real-name, or where the user does not provide true identity and you still let them use, results in being ordered to correct. Refusal to correct, or serious cases, results in a fine of 50,000 to 500,000 yuan, and may also result in being ordered to suspend the relevant business, suspend business for rectification, close the website or application, or have the license or business license revoked. The person directly responsible is each additionally fined 10,000 to 100,000 yuan. Live streaming services, on the principle of "back-end real-name, front-end voluntary," conduct "real-name identity authentication based on methods such as mobile phone numbers." Meaning: the platform's back end stores the user's real name; the front end displays whatever nickname the user chooses; verification is by phone number and similar. Live streaming with sales has stricter requirements: "real-name identity authentication based on ID card information, unified social credit code, and the like." The unified social credit code is the string on the business license (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2025). Cybersecurity Law (2025 amendment, effective January 1, 2026, Articles 26 and 64; the 2016 text had Articles 24 and 61). <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm> ; Cyberspace Administration of China (2016). Administrative Provisions on Internet Live Streaming Services (Articles 12 and 16). <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2016-11/04/c_1119847629.htm> ; Cyberspace Administration of China and six other departments (2021). Administrative Measures for Network Live Streaming Marketing (Trial) (Guo Xin Ban Fa Wen [2021] No. 5, Article 8). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-04/23/content_5601682.htm>
- Note: The article numbers have moved. The Cybersecurity Law was amended in 2025, effective January 1, 2026. The real-name article moved from Article 24 to Article 26, and the penalty article from Article 61 to Article 64. The text is unchanged. ### 8. Do not open live streaming accounts for those under 16; tips are tiered by age
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. Add a flow at the registration and payment steps each: first determine age, then confirm guardian consent
- Plain-language summary: Those under 16 may not register for live streaming broadcasting accounts on your platform. Those 16 or above must undergo identity verification, and also obtain the consent of a parent or other guardian. Tipping is divided into three tiers: under 8 may not tip; 8 to 16 requires guardian consent; 16 and above requires guardian consent or verification of income proof. Money tipped by a child without guardian consent, when the guardian seeks its return, the court will order it refunded
- Benefit: The law's original wording: "Network live streaming service providers may not provide network live streaming broadcaster account registration services to minors under the age of 16; when providing network live streaming broadcaster account registration services to minors aged 16 or above, they shall authenticate their identity information and obtain the consent of their parents or other guardians." The regulation also requires "establishing a dynamic verification mechanism for the true identity information of network live streaming broadcasters" — the identity of those broadcasting must be re-verifiable at any time. Additionally, it must "reasonably limit the single-transaction amount and single-day cumulative amount for minors of different age stages using its services," setting per-transaction and per-day spending caps for minors by age. Tipping is now divided into three tiers: "may not provide tipping services to minors under the age of 8. To provide tipping services to minors aged 8 to 16, the consent of their guardian must be obtained. To provide tipping services to minors aged 16 or above, the consent of their guardian or verification of their income proof materials must be obtained." One further rule: where a person with limited civil capacity tips without guardian consent, when the guardian seeks the money back, the court shall support it, and the money can be recovered. A person with limited civil capacity refers to minors who, under the law, cannot yet fully act on their own (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2020). Law on the Protection of Minors (Articles 74 and 76). <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2020-10/22/c_1604928959588622.htm> ; State Council (2023). Regulation on the Network Protection of Minors (State Council Order No. 766, Articles 31 and 44). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2023/issue_10806/202311/content_6913813.html> ; Cyberspace Administration of China (2026). Notice on Strengthening the Standardized Management of Network Live Streaming Tipping (item 7). <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2026-04/13/c_1777815804150225.htm> ; Supreme People's Court (2020). Guiding Opinions on the Proper Adjudication of Civil Cases Involving the COVID-19 Pandemic (II) (Fa Fa [2020] No. 17, Article 9). <https://www.court.gov.cn/fabu/xiangqing/230181.html>
- Note: Where a refund dispute actually arises, handle it in the direction most favorable to the minor. The wording on this matter has changed. The 2022 opinion of four departments and the 2020 notice of the State Administration of Radio and Television both stated that minors may not tip at all. The April 2026 new notice changed to tiering by 8 and 16, but did not state that the previous two were void, nor did the official explain how the two connect. So "minors may not tip at all" is now inaccurate

### 9. Upon receiving an infringement notice, handle it promptly; if no complaint or lawsuit follows within 15 days of forwarding the counter-statement, restore the content
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. You must build a fixed procedure: receive the notice, forward it to the seller, receive the seller's counter-statement, and publicize the result. Each step is documented
- Plain-language summary: Where someone sends an infringement notice with preliminary evidence, you must promptly delete, block, or disconnect the link, and simultaneously forward the notice to the seller. Delaying means you and the seller jointly bear the additional damages. If the seller counter-claims, you forward the counter-statement to the other party and wait 15 days. If the other party neither complains nor sues, restore the content
- Benefit: The right holder "has the right to notify the e-commerce platform operator to take necessary measures such as deletion, blocking, disconnection, termination of transactions and services. The notice shall include preliminary evidence of infringement." The right holder is the owner of rights such as trademark, copyright, and patent. A single sentence is not enough; preliminary evidence must be attached. After the platform receives the notice, it "shall promptly take necessary measures and forward the notice to the operator within the platform; failure to take necessary measures in a timely manner results in joint and several liability with the operator within the platform for the expanded portion of the damages." If you delay, you and the seller jointly bear the additional damages. After the seller counter-claims, the platform "shall, within 15 days after the counter-statement is forwarded to the intellectual property right holder, if no notice of complaint or lawsuit has been received from the right holder, promptly terminate the measures taken." Meaning: forward the seller's counter-statement to the right holder, and wait 15 days. If the other party neither complains nor sues, restore what was deleted or taken down. Where the platform knew or should have known of the infringement and failed to act, it bears joint and several liability with the infringer. The intellectual property department may also order you to correct within a time limit; failure to correct past the deadline results in a fine of 50,000 to 500,000 yuan, with serious cases 500,000 to 2,000,000 yuan. Conversely, "where a malicious false notice causes losses to operators within the platform, the compensation liability is doubled" (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2018). E-Commerce Law (Articles 42, 43, 44, 45, and 84). <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf8af050b81> ; National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code (Articles 1195, 1196, and 1197). <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/233181.html>
- Note: If you are not an e-commerce platform, but a general website, then the notice-and-takedown rule of the Civil Code applies. The difference is how long you wait after the other party counter-claims: the E-Commerce Law specifies 15 days; the Civil Code only says "a reasonable period." Additionally, publicizing the notices received, the seller's counter-statements, and your handling results is expressly required by Article 44 of the E-Commerce Law

### 10. Do not casually move user information overseas; cross-border transfer has statutory conditions and volume thresholds
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. The simplest approach is to keep user data in mainland China. Where transfer overseas is genuinely necessary, use the standard contract or obtain certification
- Plain-language summary: Transferring the information of mainland users to overseas machines is called cross-border transfer. Locating the server overseas does not mean the data has not gone cross-border. To transfer cross-border, you must take one of four routes: a security assessment by the cyberspace administration; certification by a specialized body; signing a standard contract; or other legal provisions. You must also inform the user of who overseas is receiving the information and what it will be used for, and specifically ask them once on this matter
- Benefit: Transferring the information of mainland users to overseas companies or storing it on overseas machines is legally called providing personal information overseas, and "shall meet" one of four conditions. First, pass a security assessment organized by the cyberspace administration. Second, obtain personal information protection certification through a specialized body. Third, sign a contract with the overseas recipient using the standard contract formulated by the cyberspace administration. Fourth, other conditions specified by laws and administrative regulations. At the same time, you must inform the user of: the name and contact information of the overseas recipient, the purpose of processing, the method of processing, the type of personal information, and how they may exercise their rights. And you must "obtain separate consent from the individual," specifically asking the user once on this matter. Specific thresholds are tiered by cumulative number of people. Ordinary enterprises are called, in the regulation, data processors other than critical information infrastructure operators. Such enterprises have an exemption line, with the original wording "from January 1 of the current year, cumulative provision of less than 100,000 individuals' personal information overseas (excluding sensitive personal information)." Below this line, the three — security assessment, standard contract, and certification — are all exempted. For 100,000 or more but less than 1,000,000, a standard contract must be concluded or certification obtained. For 1,000,000 or more, or sensitive personal information reaching 10,000 or more, a security assessment must be declared (nationwide, from March 2024)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2021). Personal Information Protection Law (Articles 38, 39, and 40). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202108/t20210820_527244.shtml> ; Cyberspace Administration of China (2024). Provisions on Promoting and Regulating Cross-Border Data Flows (CAC Order No. 16, Articles 3, 4, 5, 7, and 8). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2024/issue_11366/202405/content_6954192.html> ; Cyberspace Administration of China (2022). Measures for Security Assessment of Outbound Data Transfer (CAC Order No. 11, Article 4). <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2022-07/07/c_1658811536396503.htm>
- Note: A server located overseas does not equal "the data has not gone cross-border." Where a user registers in mainland China and you transfer their information to an overseas machine, that is cross-border transfer. One category does not need to go through the three routes of security assessment, certification, or standard contract: cross-border shopping, cross-border delivery, and booking flights and hotels — these are "necessary for concluding or performing a contract in which the individual is a party." Also note how the number is calculated: by "cumulative from January 1 of the current year," not by counting back twelve months from today

### 11. When choosing a server, first look at whether downtime is tolerable, then at whether there is someone to operate and maintain it, and only then compare prices
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Mainstream cloud-vendor cloud hosting, hundreds to thousands of yuan per month. Small service-provider VPS, starting at tens of yuan per month. A VPS is a small virtual server. Dedicated server colocation starts at thousands of yuan per year, and the hardware must be purchased by you
- Plain-language summary: The difference among the three tiers is not price — it is what you have in hand on the day something goes wrong. Big vendors have filing channels, public qualifications, announcements and compensation clauses when things break, and you can find someone. Small service providers cost half as much, but when they over-sell, run off, or get cut off by upstream, you do not even know who to appeal to. For a static site without user data, a tens-of-yuan VPS is enough
- Benefit: The difference among the three tiers is not price — it is what you have in hand on the day something goes wrong. Mainstream cloud vendors have filing channels, public qualifications, fault announcements, and compensation clauses in the contract, and you can find someone when something goes wrong. Small service providers can be half the price, but when they over-sell, run off, or get cut off by upstream, you have no one to appeal to, so before negotiating price, first check the MIIT system for whether they hold a value-added telecommunications business license. Dedicated server colocation is only worth it when both conditions hold: bandwidth or hardware cost is clearly cheaper, and you yourself have someone to do operations and maintenance. How to choose: for a static site without user data, the cheapest VPS is enough. For a site with user registration and transactions, choose cloud hosting that can scale elastically and take snapshot backups, with backups stored in at least two places
- Evidence grade: C
- Source: Author's experience, no direct literature; for statutory requirements on service provider qualifications and filings, see Entry 4 of this section; for the 6-month log retention and classified protection (等级保护) obligations, see Section 11, Entry 16
- Note: The above is experiential judgment, with no documentary evidence. What can truly be verified is one thing only: the service provider's license. To save money, save on configuration and bandwidth — do not save on qualifications
