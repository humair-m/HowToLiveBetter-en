# 13. Emergencies: What to Do First

This section tells what to do first, what to do next, and what not to do. The first-aid and wilderness entries tally specific causes of death and survival rates. The threat and scam entries tally money and personal freedom. The two kinds of numbers are not interconvertible. Entries are ordered from highest to lowest by "how common this is × how many lives separate doing it right from doing it wrong". These entries are primarily for you, your spouse, and your direct relatives. Cardiac arrest occurs at home in 79.2% of cases, so the person you are most likely to save is a family member. Friends and colleagues come next. Strangers are not without benefit, but this tier is lowest, and you may be extorted or pulled into a case. So these entries put self-protection actions and liability exemption clauses first.

This section collects only actions that take minutes. Recognizing a scam or being blackmailed is handled by the day, not in this section. The hard rules of anti-fraud and seven high-frequency scams are in Section 8, Entry 3 (hard rules of anti-fraud). Being blackmailed with private photos or sex-chat recordings is in Section 8, Entry 32 (the other party comes demanding money with leverage). If you have already transferred money, immediately call 110 or 96110 to request a stop payment; see Section 8, Entry 2 (stop payment).

### 1. If someone collapses and is not breathing, immediately press hard on the chest, have a bystander call 120 and find an AED
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=medium willpower=none benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. A Red Cross or hospital CPR class, half a day to a day, learned once lasts for years. Even without training you can do it: stack your hands on the midpoint of the line between the two nipples, press down hard about 2 times per second, do not stop.
- Plain-language summary: The person you are most likely to press on is a family member: cardiac arrest happens at home in 79.2% of cases. When someone does compressions, the survival-to-discharge rate rises from 3.9% to 16.1%. If they don't respond and aren't breathing, press directly. The position is the midpoint of the line between the two nipples; stack your hands and press hard, 2 times per second, without stopping. Have a second person call 120 and get an AED; your hands don't stop.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis of 79 studies and 142,740 people: of those whose hearts stopped outside a hospital (out-of-hospital cardiac arrest), only 7.6% survived to discharge. When a bystander did CPR, the survival-to-discharge rate rose from 3.9% to 16.1%. A bystander witnessed the onset in 53%, but only 32% actually received bystander compressions. The Chinese BASIC-OHCA registry enrolled 38,227 emergency cases. Of these, 30,282 (79.2%) had cardiac arrest at home. In this group, the bystander compression rate was 20.3%, and survival to discharge was 1.2%. The sequence is to press first, then have a second person call and get the AED. After the machine turns on, follow its voice prompts
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Sasson C et al. (2010). Predictors of survival from out-of-hospital cardiac arrest: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Circulation: Cardiovascular Quality and Outcomes. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCOUTCOMES.109.889576> ; Zheng J et al. (2023). Incidence, process of care, and outcomes of out-of-hospital cardiac arrest in China: a prospective study of the BASIC-OHCA registry. The Lancet Public Health. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(23)00173-1> ; National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code (Article 184). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>
- Note: Do not pinch the philtrum first, do not look for family first, do not shoot video first. Do not be afraid to press hard for fear of breaking ribs. Civil Code Article 184 states: voluntary emergency rescue that injures the person being rescued does not incur civil liability for the rescuer.

### 2. If an elderly person falls or someone collapses, first squat down, call to them, and call 120; don't rush to lift them up; for strangers, walking away is also legal, and if you stop, don't move them by hand
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=some benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Squat down, ask a question, make a phone call — a few minutes. The hard part is resisting the urge to reach out and help them up. For strangers, there's an extra account to tally: calling 120 leaves your number, you may be called back, and you may be asked to make a statement. If the family targets you, a lawsuit is months at minimum. And even if you ultimately win, you pay your own legal fees.
- Plain-language summary: The main line of this entry is your own elderly family members and acquaintances. First check whether they are breathing; if not, press on the chest per Entry 1 (press hard on the chest). If they are breathing, do not lift them up either: the person may have fallen because they had a stroke or syncope first; forcibly lifting may worsen it. Squat down, call to them, ask whether they remember how they fell. Back pain, anything that looks like a fracture — don't move them, call 120 from where they are.
- Benefit: The Ministry of Health's "Technical Guide for the Prevention and Intervention of Falls in Older Adults" states two sentences. One is "falls are the leading cause of injury death among people 65 and older in China". The other is "if you find an older adult has fallen, do not rush to lift them up; handle it case by case". If unconscious, immediately call the emergency number. If vomiting, turn the head to one side and clear the mouth and nose. If breathing and heartbeat have stopped, immediately do chest compressions. "If movement is necessary, ensure stability, lying flat as much as possible". If conscious, first ask whether they have memory of the fall, "if not, it may be syncope or cerebrovascular accident". With severe headache, or facial droop, slurred speech, or limb weakness, "immediately lifting the older adult may worsen cerebral hemorrhage or cerebral ischemia, aggravating the condition". With signs of fracture, or back pain, or incontinence, "without relevant professional knowledge, do not move them casually". All of the above require immediately calling emergency services. If the older person wants to stand up on their own, you may assist them to rise slowly, "sit or lie down to rest and observe, and only leave after confirming no harm". On "fear of being extorted", the law reads as follows. Article 90 of the Supreme People's Court's Interpretation on the Application of the Civil Procedure Law of the People's Republic of China has two sentences. The first is "parties shall provide evidence to prove the facts underlying their litigation claims". The second is "before a judgment is made, if a party fails to provide evidence or the evidence is insufficient to prove its factual claim, the party bearing the burden of proof bears the adverse consequences". Article 1198 of the Civil Code reads: "Where the operator or manager of a business premise such as a hotel, shopping mall, bank, station, airport, sports venue, or entertainment venue, or the organizer of a mass event, fails to fulfill the duty of safety protection and causes harm to another, tort liability shall be borne". Article 184 of the Civil Code: "Where voluntary emergency rescue causes harm to the rescued, the rescuer does not bear civil liability". The Supreme People's Court has published a typical case. A pharmacy operator gave CPR to a person in cardiac arrest and fractured their ribs. The rescued person was hospitalized for 18 days and claimed over 9000 yuan. The court ruled that the rescuer did not bear civil liability
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Ministry of Health (2011). Technical Guide for the Prevention and Intervention of Falls in Older Adults ("Management after an older adult falls"). Full PDF available at Guangdong Provincial Center for Disease Control and Prevention <http://cdcp.gd.gov.cn/gcdcdfiles/editor/uploadfile/20111027094832117.pdf>; on-site management portion full text reposted by Guinan County People's Government <https://www.guinan.gov.cn/lnb/zc1__zwgk/zc/aztfl/qt/content_27609778>; National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code (Articles 183, 184). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>; Supreme People's Court (2023). Typical cases of people's courts grasping fairness and efficiency and practicing socialist core values (Case 13, Qi v. Sun health-rights dispute, Kangping County People's Court, Liaoning Province). <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/408162.html>; Supreme People's Court (2022 second revision, in force April 10, 2022). Interpretation on the Application of the Civil Procedure Law of the People's Republic of China (Article 90). <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/353651.html>; Civil Code Article 1198 same as above Supreme People's Procuratorate reposted full text; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2023 revision, in force January 1, 2024). Civil Procedure Law (Articles 152, 164). Full text reposted by Shanghai Municipal Development and Reform Commission <https://fgw.sh.gov.cn/ys-syjf-zc-2.4.1-h5/20240408/073febdb2b04464390546232d4089f51.html>; State Council (2006). Litigation Fee Payment Measures (State Council Order No. 481, Articles 6, 29). <https://www.gov.cn/ziliao/flfg/2006-12/29/content_483682.htm>; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2025). Public Security Administration Punishment Law (2025 revision, in force January 1, 2026, Article 26 item 1, Article 50 item 5). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml>
- Note: Graded B because the guide only states how to handle the situation, without numbers on "how many fewer deaths if followed". Article 184 exempts damages caused during rescue, on the premise that you acted voluntarily in an emergency. "Taking it upon yourself to stuff someone into your private car to take them to the hospital" is not covered; waiting for 120 is safer. After calling 120, don't hang up; do as the dispatcher instructs. This judgment set is primarily for your own elderly family and acquaintances. Switch to a stranger on the street, and walking away is not illegal and is the most effortless option: the law does not require you to rescue. The bill for stopping must be tallied layer by layer: time, leaving your number, being approached by family, being sued, the dispute reaching your workplace, being filmed and posted online. For details, see [docs/stranger-in-trouble.md](../docs/stranger-in-trouble.md). If you do decide to stop, the only thing you absolutely must do is **not move them by hand**, for medical reasons: forcibly lifting may worsen cerebral hemorrhage or spinal injury. There is one exception: if you operate, manage, or organize the activity at this place, walking away may incur liability. Article 1198 of the Civil Code imposes a duty of safety protection that requires you to bear a share of safety responsibility for the people on your premises. For fall prevention itself, see Section 1, Entry 13 (train balance and leg strength). For delayed symptoms after an older adult bumps their head, see this section, Entry 10. For money matters after being injured while rescuing, see this section, Entry 39. For preserving evidence and reporting after cyberbullying, see Section 8, Entry 16.
### 3. Sudden facial droop, one-sided arm weakness, slurred speech: immediately call 120; don't wait, don't drive yourself
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=none benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Three actions, learn by watching once: ask them to smile, raise both arms, say a complete sentence.
- Plain-language summary: Facial droop, one arm cannot be raised, slurred speech — immediately call 120. Receiving thrombolytics within 3 hours of onset makes the odds of recovering to basic self-care about 1.75 times that of no thrombolytics. Delaying to 3 to 4.5 hours leaves 1.26 times. Beyond 4.5 hours, no difference is seen. Write down the exact time of symptom onset. Don't give water or medication, don't drive yourself.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis pooling individual data from 16 intravenous thrombolysis randomized trials and 6756 patients. Intravenous thrombolysis is using drugs to open the blocked vessel. Using alteplase for thrombolysis within 3 hours of onset produces an odds ratio of 1.75 (95% CI 1.35–2.27) for recovering to basic self-care (modified Rankin Score 0–1). The probability of this outcome is about 1.75 times that of no thrombolysis. Delayed to 3 to 4.5 hours, 1.26 (1.05–1.51). Beyond 4.5 hours, 1.15 (0.95–1.40), statistically no longer different
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Emberson J, Lees KR, Lyden P, et al. (2014). Effect of treatment delay, age, and stroke severity on the effects of intravenous thrombolysis with alteplase for acute ischaemic stroke: a meta-analysis of individual patient data from randomised trials. Lancet, 384(9958), 1929-1935. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(14)60584-5>
- Note: Thrombolysis is more effective the earlier it is given; beyond 4.5 hours it is essentially useless. Call 120 instead of driving yourself because the ambulance goes directly to a hospital with a stroke center, and notifies them in advance to prepare. Write down the exact time of symptom onset — the doctor needs it to decide whether thrombolysis is possible. Don't give water or medication, don't "wait and rest a bit to see".

### 4. Sudden severe dizziness, inability to stand, double vision, or one eye going black, or inability to point a finger at your own nose — also call 120 as a stroke
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=none benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Add two actions to the "face droop, raise arm, speak" three (Entry 3, sudden facial droop and slurred speech). One is to have them stand straight with eyes closed, see whether they sway, and ask whether they have double vision or missing visual fields. The other is to point a finger at your own nose when in doubt, then close your eyes and point again.
- Plain-language summary: Looking only at "face droop, raise arm, speak" misses more than ten percent of strokes, because some strokes at onset neither droop the face nor paralyze the arm. Adding unsteady gait, double vision, or missing visual fields reduces the missed to 4.4%. Sudden severe vertigo with inability to stand, double vision, or inability to point at your nose — also call 120 and also note the time.
- Benefit: The University of Kentucky Stroke Center reviewed in 2014 the records of 736 acute ischemic strokes (strokes caused by a blocked vessel). Of these, 14.1% of patients had no facial droop, arm weakness, or slurred speech at onset (FAST). Adding "unsteady gait, leg weakness, visual symptoms" to the screen reduced the missed proportion from 14.1% to 4.4%
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Aroor S, Singh R, Goldstein LB (2017). BE-FAST (Balance, Eyes, Face, Arm, Speech, Time): Reducing the Proportion of Strokes Missed Using the FAST Mnemonic. Stroke, 48(2), 479-481. <https://doi.org/10.1161/STROKEAHA.116.015169>
- Note: When vessels in the cerebellum and brainstem area are blocked (posterior circulation infarction), there is often no facial droop or arm paralysis. It presents only as severe vertigo, gait deviation, diplopia (double vision), choking on food and water, and is most easily mistaken for cervical spondylosis, hypoglycemia, or BPPV. Inability to point at the nose accurately, inability to stand with eyes closed, are signs of cerebellar involvement. But no action can conversely rule out stroke: sudden onset, no relief — call 120, and record the exact time of onset. That study reviewed charts from only one hospital; the authors themselves note it needs further validation.

### 5. If one eye suddenly goes black like a curtain being pulled down, even if it resolves on its own in minutes, go to the emergency department for a stroke evaluation the same day
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=medium willpower=none benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Emergency evaluation and vascular examination take half a day to a day.
- Plain-language summary: One eye suddenly going black like a curtain being pulled down means the retinal vessel is blocked; the longer the blockage, the less retina can be saved. It is also a form of cerebral ischemia; in the following days, stroke risk is high. For those evaluated and treated the same day, the rate of recurrent stroke within 90 days dropped from 10.3% to 2.1%. Go to the emergency department with a stroke center that same day; do not wait for the next day's ophthalmology clinic.
- Benefit: The American Heart Association's 2021 scientific statement on central retinal artery occlusion (blockage of the main vessel supplying the retina) states: "acute central retinal artery occlusion is an emergency". The treatment system should identify it as an emergency and prioritize it. The longer the blood flow is cut off, the less retina can be saved. The Oxford EXPRESS study looked at two kinds of patients: minor stroke, and cerebral ischemia with symptoms that resolved on their own (medically called transient ischemic attack). After these patients were changed to same-day evaluation and same-day treatment initiation, the rate of recurrent stroke within 90 days dropped from 10.3% (32/310) to 2.1% (6/281)
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Mac Grory B, Schrag M, Biousse V, et al. (2021). Management of Central Retinal Artery Occlusion: A Scientific Statement From the American Heart Association. Stroke, 52(6), e282-e294. <https://doi.org/10.1161/STR.0000000000000366> ; Rothwell PM, Giles MF, Chandratheva A, et al. (2007). Effect of urgent treatment of transient ischaemic attack and minor stroke on early recurrent stroke (EXPRESS study): a prospective population-based sequential comparison. Lancet, 370(9596), 1432-1442. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(07)61448-2>
- Note: Graded B because EXPRESS was not a randomized trial. It compares the periods before and after a practice change in the same population, and other conditions may also have changed between the two periods; that part cannot be separated. Brief vision loss in one eye that recovers on its own in minutes is most easily dismissed as "tiredness" or "low blood sugar". It is a form of transient ischemic attack, and the next few days are exactly the high-risk window for stroke. Painless, non-red, non-tearing monocular vision loss should be taken especially seriously. Do not wait for the next day's ophthalmology outpatient clinic; go directly to the emergency department of a hospital with a stroke center.

### 6. One eye swollen, aching, red, with rainbow rings around lights, plus headache, nausea, and the urge to vomit — go to the ophthalmology emergency department that day
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=medium willpower=none benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. The ophthalmology emergency department measures intraocular pressure and examines the anterior chamber; takes half a day.
- Plain-language summary: This is an acute glaucoma attack. The fluid inside the eye cannot drain, and intraocular pressure spikes. The longer the delay, the more optic nerve damage, and damage is irreversible. During the Wuhan lockdown, patients delayed an average of 241 hours before treatment, and 21.87% went blind. In the same period the following year without lockdown, the average delay was 121 hours, and 7.84% went blind.
- Benefit: A Wuhan ophthalmology hospital compared two periods of acute primary angle-closure glaucoma patients. During the 76-day lockdown in 2020, 54 patients with 64 eyes; in the same period in 2021, 46 patients with 51 eyes; the two groups had similar demographics. From symptom onset to treatment, the lockdown group averaged 241.84±211.95 hours, the control group 121.53±96.12 hours (P=0.001). Intraocular pressure at presentation was 52.63±12.45 mmHg vs 45.16±9.79 mmHg (P=0.001). The blindness rate was 21.87% vs 7.84%. Glaucomatous optic neuropathy was found in 20/64 eyes (31.25%) vs 7/51 eyes (13.73%) (P=0.03). A Korean hospital reviewed 50 eyes that had had one attack and later had the lens removed. After one year, 25 (50%) had varying degrees of visual field defect. The longer the time from symptom onset to intraocular pressure reduction, the more likely a defect remained (P=0.005). A separate prospective study had 43 people with shallow anterior chambers lie prone in a dark room for 1 hour. Both eyes' intraocular pressure rose significantly (P<0.01). The median rise in the normally dominant eye was 3.60 mmHg, and 2.70 mmHg in the other (P<0.05)
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Zhou L, Wu S, Wang Y, Bao X, Peng T, Luo W, Ortega-Usobiaga J (2022). Clinical presentation of acute primary angle closure during the COVID-19 epidemic lockdown. Frontiers in Medicine, 9, 1078237. <https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2022.1078237> ; Sung MS, Kim HJ, Park SW (2023). Predictors of long-term visual field outcome after an episode of acute primary angle closure. Clinical & Experimental Ophthalmology, 51(4), 291-299. <https://doi.org/10.1111/ceo.14206> ; Wang J, Wang J, Ng TK, Huang C (2025). Asymmetric intraocular pressure changes in dominant and contralateral eyes: the dark room prone provocative test. Seminars in Ophthalmology, 40(4), 325-331. <https://doi.org/10.1080/08820538.2024.2443972>
- Note: Graded B for two reasons. First, the Wuhan two groups were compared by looking back at old medical records; the lockdown itself brought other differences that cannot be separated from "delay". Second, the Korean group had only 50 eyes and was also retrospective. The triggers are not screen use; they are two things stacked: in the dark, the pupil dilates; prolonged head-down or prone posture also pushes the lens forward. People with already-shallow anterior chambers are easily triggered this way, so the truly high-risk group is those over 50, farsighted, with a family history of angle-closure glaucoma. Young people playing games all night rarely have this kind of attack. The most common misjudgment at onset is treating it as gastroenteritis, migraine, or high blood pressure, because headache and vomiting are more prominent than the eye symptoms. Painless, non-red monocular vision loss is a different matter; see Entry 5 (one eye suddenly goes black). Whether blue-light-blocking glasses help is in Section 6, Entry 16 (blue-light-blocking glasses).

### 7. Chest pressure pain, tightness, or squeezing that does not resolve for more than 15 minutes: call 120; don't tough it out and don't drive yourself
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=none benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free.
- Plain-language summary: Chest pressure pain, tightness, squeezing lasting more than 15 minutes without relief — call 120. In hospital, if the blocked vessel is opened within 55 minutes, 30-day mortality is 2.8%. Beyond 55 minutes, 9.7%. Every delay costs another piece of dead heart muscle. Don't drive yourself; losing consciousness on the road is a second accident.
- Benefit: Israel conducted a national acute coronary syndrome survey. The analysis was on ST-elevation myocardial infarction patients, a type of heart attack. It computed the interval from hospital arrival to balloon dilation (opening the blocked vessel with a balloon). Interval ≤55 minutes: 30-day mortality 5/178 (2.8%). Interval >55 minutes: 30-day mortality 15/155 (9.7%)
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Karkabi B, Meir G, Zafrir B, et al. (2021). Door-to-balloon time and mortality in patients with ST-elevation myocardial infarction. European Heart Journal - Quality of Care and Clinical Outcomes. <https://doi.org/10.1093/ehjqcco/qcaa037>
- Note: That study compares the delay after entering the hospital, not from onset to hospital arrival. But the principle is the same: every delay kills another piece of heart muscle. The pain may be in the upper abdomen, back, or jaw, or the left arm may be numb. Diabetics and the elderly may have no pain, only shortness of breath. Don't drive yourself; losing consciousness on the road is a second accident.

### 8. Sudden tearing pain that moves from the chest to the back and down to the waist: call 120 and say clearly "the pain is moving"
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=none benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free.
- Plain-language summary: Tearing pain that moves from the chest to the back and down to the waist — when calling 120, say directly "it feels like tearing, the pain is moving downward". This disease has a 58% mortality rate with medication alone and no surgery. Its management is exactly the opposite of myocardial infarction: the doctor must think of it to order contrast-enhanced CT, rather than giving you anticoagulants and thrombolytics. If the blood pressure differs greatly between the two arms or a pulse cannot be felt on one side, say that too.
- Benefit: The International Registry of Acute Aortic Dissection (IRAD) enrolled 464 cases across 12 centers. Aortic dissection is the disease with tearing chest-back pain. Among the symptoms patients first reported, the most common was sudden-onset severe sharp pain. In-hospital mortality overall was 27.4%. For Type A dissection managed with medication alone and no surgery, mortality was 58%
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Hagan PG, Nienaber CA, Isselbacher EM, et al. (2000). The International Registry of Acute Aortic Dissection (IRAD): new insights into an old disease. JAMA, 283(7), 897-903. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.283.7.897>
- Note: The management of dissection and myocardial infarction is exactly opposite. Heart attacks use anticoagulants and thrombolytics to keep blood from clotting; using them on a dissection is equivalent to letting the tear bleed freely. So at the hospital, proactively say "tearing pain, moving downward", to make the doctor think of ordering contrast-enhanced CT. A large blood pressure difference between the two arms or absent pulse on one side is a clue. Those with poorly controlled blood pressure, Marfan syndrome, or a family history of aortic aneurysm or dissection are at higher risk. Again, do not drive yourself; do not wait until the next day.

### 9. Sudden "worst headache of your life" reaching peak within an hour: immediately go to the emergency department for a head CT
<!-- cost-tag: money=little time=medium willpower=none benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: The emergency visit itself does not cost much. A head CT in the emergency department runs a few hundred yuan.
- Plain-language summary: What matters is how fast the pain reaches its peak. Severe headache reaching peak within an hour — about 6% are ruptured vessels on the brain surface. Age 40 or older, neck pain or stiffness, witnessed loss of consciousness, onset during activity, instantaneous explosion, inability to flex the neck forward — meeting any one warrants going to the emergency department for a head CT. Most who get checked are fine, but missing one rebleed is death.
- Benefit: A prospective cohort study at 10 Canadian emergency departments enrolled 2131 cases of "severe headache reaching peak within an hour", with criteria set in advance, then tracked and recorded. Of these, 132 (6.2%) were subarachnoid hemorrhage (ruptured vessel on the brain surface). The Ottawa SAH Rule has six items: age 40 or older, neck pain or stiffness, witnessed loss of consciousness. The other three are onset during activity, instantaneous explosive onset, and limited neck flexion on examination. Meeting any one requires investigation. This rule caught all the bleeders, sensitivity 100%, 95% CI 97.2%–100.0% (confidence interval). The cost is that only 15.3% of those without bleeding were excluded by it (specificity 15.3%)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Perry JJ, Stiell IG, Sivilotti MLA, et al. (2013). Clinical decision rules to rule out subarachnoid hemorrhage for acute headache. JAMA, 310(12), 1248-1255. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2013.278018>
- Note: What matters is not "how much it hurts" but "how fast it reaches peak"; headache that explodes within seconds to a minute is the danger sign, not migraine that builds gradually. Accompanying vomiting, neck stiffness, photophobia, or brief loss of consciousness warrants even more attention. The specificity is only 15.3%, meaning the vast majority of those checked under this rule are fine. But missing one rebleed is death — worth checking. Do not pop a painkiller, suppress it, and go back to sleep.

### 10. Two to three weeks to a few months after an older adult bumps their head, if they become unsteady, slow, drowsy, or one-sided weak, get a head CT
<!-- cost-tag: money=little time=medium willpower=none benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: A head CT costs two to three hundred yuan.
- Plain-language summary: After an older adult bumps their head, blood typically does not accumulate until the third week. At the moment of impact, everything may be completely fine, and the person may not even remember the bump. This disease has a post-surgery recurrence rate up to 33% and a fatality rate up to 32%. If you notice an older adult suddenly confused, gait unsteady, always sleepy, one hand weak — first ask whether they fell and bumped their head in the past two or three months, then get the CT.
- Benefit: The Neurosurgery Branch of the Chinese Medical Association and others published the "Chinese Expert Consensus on Drug Treatment of Chronic Subdural Hematoma". Chronic subdural hematoma is a slowly accumulating pool of blood between the meninges. The technical description is a chronic space-occupying lesion formed by blood accumulation between the arachnoid and dura mater. It "usually forms in the third week after a craniocerebral injury", and is more common in older adults. After surgery, the recurrence rate can reach 33%, and the fatality rate can reach 32%. For patients 90 and older, only 24% recover well overall. Systematic review: global incidence is 3.39 to 39.1 new cases per 100,000 people per year, rising with age, with those 80+ reaching three times. The most common causes are trauma and falls, followed by long-term heavy alcohol use, use of anticoagulant drugs, and violent injury. Geriatric medicine review: clinical presentation "typically includes confusion, cognitive decline, gait disturbance, or somnolence, and may resemble other geriatric syndromes"
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Zhang J et al. (2021). Expert consensus on drug treatment of chronic subdural hematoma. Chinese Neurosurgical Journal, 7(1), 47. <https://doi.org/10.1186/s41016-021-00263-z> ; Dziho A et al. (2025). Global prevalence and incidence of chronic subdural hematoma: A systematic review. Brain and Spine, 5, 105893. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bas.2025.105893> ; Mathon B, Shotar E (2026). Diagnostic and therapeutic management of chronic subdural hematoma in elderly patients. Gériatrie et Psychologie Neuropsychiatrie du Vieillissement, 24(1), 30-41. <https://doi.org/10.1684/pnv.2026.1274>
- Note: At the moment of impact, everything can be completely fine, and the older person may not even remember the bump. So if at home you notice "recently suddenly confused, gait unsteady, always sleepy, one hand weak", first ask whether they fell or bumped their head in the past two or three months, then get the CT. Don't directly attribute it to "senile dementia" or "the aftermath of the last stroke"; misattribution causes delay. Older adults taking aspirin, clopidogrel, warfarin, or novel oral anticoagulants are at higher risk; so is long-term heavy alcohol use. For fall prevention, see Section 1, Entry 13 (train balance and leg strength).

### 11. One leg suddenly swollen, tight, tender to pressure: seek medical care promptly; if also sudden shortness of breath or chest pain, immediately call 120
<!-- cost-tag: money=little time=medium willpower=none benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: A leg vascular ultrasound (lower-extremity venous ultrasound) costs one to two hundred yuan.
- Plain-language summary: One leg suddenly swollen, a circle larger than the other, tender to pressure — promptly get a leg venous ultrasound. Participating in a group affray: up to 3 years; with many people, on the street, or with weapons: 3 to 10 years. Watching also risks injury by stray hits. The right thing to do is back away and leave. If you're willing to report, retreat to a safe distance and call 110, stating the location, how many people, whether weapons such as knives or sticks are present, and whether anyone is lying motionless.
- Benefit: Criminal Law Article 292: "for group affray, the ringleaders and other active participants are sentenced to up to three years of fixed-term imprisonment, criminal detention, or public surveillance". For repeated group affrays, large-scale affrays with serious social impact, those causing serious social-order disruption in public places or traffic arteries, or those carrying weapons: "three to ten years of fixed-term imprisonment". "Group affray causing serious injury or death" is convicted and punished as intentional injury or intentional homicide. The Public Security Administration Punishment Law also has provisions. Assault of others: detention 5 to 10 days with a fine of 500 to 1000 yuan. Group affray is handled as picking quarrels and provoking trouble, with detention up to 15 days and a fine up to 2000 yuan. Article 5 of the Guiding Opinions on the Lawful Application of the Self-Defense System: an unlawful infringement "includes both infringements against oneself and infringements endangering the state, public interest, or against others". Article 9 of the same Opinions: where one party strikes first and the means are clearly excessive, or one party strikes first and continues the infringement despite the other party's efforts to avoid conflict, the responding party should generally be recognized as acting in defense
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: National People's Congress (1997). Criminal Law (Articles 232, 234, 292, 305, 310). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml>; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2025). Public Security Administration Punishment Law (2025 revision, Articles 30, 51). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml>; Supreme People's Court, Supreme People's Procuratorate, Ministry of Public Security (2020). Guiding Opinions on the Lawful Application of the Self-Defense System (Fa Fa [2020] No. 31, Articles 5, 9). <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/251611.html>; National People's Congress (2018). Criminal Procedure Law (2018 revision, Articles 62, 110, 111, 193). Full text reposted by Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region People's Procuratorate <https://www.nx.jcy.gov.cn/zwsy/qwfb/202011/t20201119_608817.html>
- Note: Graded B because the consequence half has hard statutory text, but the "back away and report, don't jump in to break it up" step has no official source — it is inferred from the statutes and "how group affray is characterized". "I came to break it up" is not a talisman; what matters is whether you used your hands and who you hit. If the other party has already fallen or fled, and you chase and hit, it no longer counts as defense. Seeing someone being beaten one-sidedly by a group, the law does allow you to intervene. Self-defense protects not only yourself but also others. But going up bare-handed mostly just adds another casualty. Calling out, recording video from a distance, and reporting to police are more effective. "Don't get involved, don't watch" benefits only you. The benefit of calling 110 mostly accrues to the person on the ground, the lowest tier in this book's beneficiary framework; weigh whether you spend that time yourself. Before weighing, know a few things. The duty to testify already exists: Article 62 of the Criminal Procedure Law states that anyone who knows about a case has a duty to testify; not making that call does not make the duty disappear. In serious injury or death cases, the police will pull surveillance and still find those present; standing and watching is most easily logged. If the reporter does not want their name disclosed, the investigating organ "shall keep it secret" (Article 111). Stating the location, number of people, and whether weapons are involved is enough, then hang up; no need to stay on scene. What can escalate to a reprimand or up to 10 days' detention is being summoned by the court to testify and being unable to provide a justifiable reason yet refusing (Article 193) — passersby rarely reach this step. Not reporting is not itself illegal: Article 110 of the Criminal Procedure Law's "have the right and the duty" carries no penalty; the Criminal Law also has no offense for ordinary people who know but don't report; walking away is a legal option. After walking away, there are only two red lines. One is afterward helping to hide the person or providing false testimony to harbor them (Criminal Law Article 310: up to 3 years; serious circumstances, 3 to 10 years). The other is, when called to testify, deliberately giving false testimony (Article 305: up to 3 years; serious circumstances, 3 to 7 years). Silence and truthful speaking are both fine; fabricating stories is not. Being drawn into a conflict yourself is in Section 8, Entry 10; being robbed by a stranger is in this section, Entry 36; money matters after being injured while rescuing is in this section, Entry 39.

### 38. Possibly exposed to HIV: get post-exposure prophylaxis within 72 hours, the earlier the better
<!-- cost-tag: money=high time=little willpower=some benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: A 28-day course of medication costs over a thousand yuan, self-paid in most regions. You have to go to the hospital that night. The hard part is admitting on the spot that you may have been infected, and immediately heading out.
- Plain-language summary: If you suspect you have been infected with HIV, 72 hours is the hard line; best to take the medication within 2 hours. The medication must be taken for 28 consecutive days. Beyond 72 hours, this option is gone, so don't wait until the next day to decide.
- Benefit: The Beijing Municipal People's Government HIV prevention science column: post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) should "be administered as prophylactic medication within 2 hours whenever possible, ideally within 24 hours, and no later than 72 hours". It must also "be taken continuously for 28 days". Beyond 72 hours, initiation is no longer recommended — this is a hard deadline
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: Beijing Municipal People's Government. Can't tell pre-exposure from post-exposure? Six Q&As for comprehensive understanding of HIV pre-exposure/post-exposure prophylaxis. <https://www.beijing.gov.cn/fuwu/bmfw/jhsyfwzdzx/sjazbr/fakp/202311/t20231110_3299397.html>; National Center for AIDS/STD Control and Prevention, Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention (2020). HIV Post-Exposure Prophylaxis Technical Guide (Trial). <https://www.chinacdc.cn/jkyj/crb2/yl/azb/jswj_azb/202410/t20241027_302131.html>
- Note: Where to get it: the local CDC-designated HIV-designated medical institution, usually under the infectious disease department (STD/HIV outpatient). Many cities have set up 24-hour clinics; Chongqing's public data shows 29 clinics across 26 districts and counties, 14 of them 24-hour. Before going, call the local CDC to ask which one has the medication; don't waste time in the emergency department. What counts as "exposure" includes sex without protection, condom breakage, needlestick injury, blood on mucous membranes; whether to use the medication is decided by a doctor. ### 39. If rescuing someone got you injured or cost you money, first go after the perpetrator and use medical insurance, then apply for a Good Samaritan recognition
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=medium willpower=none benefit=large metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. The application requires submitting materials and waiting for recognition; a few trips, weeks to months.
- Plain-language summary: If rescuing someone got you hurt, you don't have to swallow the loss. A Good Samaritan injured is treated first, billed later. Medical expenses are paid by the perpetrator; if the perpetrator flees or cannot pay, basic medical insurance covers it. Where there is no perpetrator, the person you rescued is legally obligated to provide appropriate compensation. At the same time, apply for Good Samaritan recognition; the injured person's benefits are handled as work-injury insurance.
- Benefit: Civil Code Article 183: "Where a person suffers harm in protecting another's civil rights and interests, the tortfeasor bears civil liability, and the beneficiary may provide appropriate compensation. Where there is no tortfeasor, the tortfeasor has fled, or the tortfeasor cannot bear civil liability, and the victim requests compensation, the beneficiary shall provide appropriate compensation". Guo Ban Fa [2012] No. 39 covers what to do for injured Good Samaritans. Medical institutions are to establish green channels, "adhering to the principle of 'treat first, bill later'". For medical expenses, the original text reads: "medical expenses arising from emergency treatment are borne by the perpetrator or responsible person where there is a perpetrator or responsible person; where there is no perpetrator or responsible person, or where the perpetrator or responsible person has fled or cannot bear the liability, they are resolved through the basic medical insurance system per regulations". For deaths, "those meeting the conditions for martyr evaluation shall be evaluated as martyrs according to law"; "those that qualify as deemed work injuries receive a one-time work-death subsidy and a special survivor subsidy equivalent to 40 months of the person's wages". For disability, "those meeting the conditions for work-injury insurance benefits receive the corresponding benefits under the Work Injury Insurance Regulations; those who do not are evaluated for disability grade by the civil affairs department and receive the corresponding benefits". On employment, "priority inclusion in employment assistance, with key support"; public welfare positions "priority placement for qualifying Good Samaritans". On housing, "priority inclusion in the housing support system, priority allocation of public housing or rental subsidies"
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code (Article 183). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>; General Office of the State Council, transmitting Ministry of Civil Affairs and others (2012). Opinions on Strengthening the Protection of the Rights and Interests of Good Samaritans (Guo Ban Fa [2012] No. 39). <https://www.gov.cn/zwgk/2012-07/26/content_2191928.htm>
- Note: Graded B because what benefits you can get are fixed by national documents, but how much the reward is and how the recognition process works are governed by each province's Good Samaritan regulations; numbers vary by region and were not verified province by province this round. Typically you apply to the public security organ or the Good Samaritan work organization (foundation) where the incident occurred. Whether recognition is granted depends on evidence: the police report receipt, on-site surveillance, witnesses' names and phone numbers, hospital medical records — preserve them on the spot. If you need to sue, Good Samaritan civil rights are already within the scope of free legal aid. The national standard for the one-time work-death subsidy and how disability grades convert to money are in Section 19, Entries 15 and 16. How to apply for legal aid is in Section 7, Entry 3.

### 40. A knife, rebar, or glass is stuck in the body: press around the foreign object to stop bleeding, do not pull it out
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=some benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is resisting the "pull it out first" instinct, staying with the person and waiting for the ambulance.
- Plain-language summary: Pulling it out may cause massive bleeding, and also makes it harder for the doctor to assess depth. The procedure: call 120, use your hand or a dressing to press around the foreign object to stop bleeding, and pad the object to keep it from wobbling. For an abdominal wound with internal organs protruding, do not push them back in; cover with a clean moist dressing. For a chest wound that is bubbling, do not seal it with plastic wrap — sealing may collapse the lung.
- Benefit: International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies' 2025 International First Aid Guidelines: "first aid providers should press around the foreign object (such as a knife) penetrating the body, stabilize the object, and not pull it out". "Do not remove any embedded object from an abdominal or chest wound; removal may cause massive bleeding, and also makes it harder for the doctor to assess the severity". "An object embedded in the body should be stabilized; if it pulsates with the pulse, let it pulsate, just loosely stabilize it". "First aid providers should not push protruding internal organs back into the body". An open chest wound without actively spurting blood "can be left open without a dressing, because sealing the wound may allow air to accumulate in the chest cavity, causing partial or complete lung collapse (pneumothorax)". All penetrating chest and abdominal wounds require immediately calling emergency services
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies (2025). International First Aid, Resuscitation and Education Guidelines 2025, pages 256–257, 267–268. <https://www.ifrc.org/sites/default/files/2026-03/IFRC%20International%20First%20Aid,%20Resuscitation%20and%20Education%20Guidelines%202025.pdf>
- Note: Graded B because this is consensus practice in the guidelines; there are no randomized trials — you cannot exactly divide casualties into "pull" and "don't pull" groups to test. The only exception is a foreign object blocking the airway with the person unable to breathe. At the hospital, go directly to the emergency department's severity-triage desk and report "penetrating injury". This is the level that requires immediate entry to the resuscitation room; do not queue at the registration window (see Section 24, Entry 8). If genuinely impaled, call 120; the ambulance will notify the hospital in advance to prepare. For how to press for massive bleeding and how to apply a tourniquet, see Entry 12.

### 41. If you suspect a fracture, do not move that body part; support it without moving, cold compress for under 20 minutes, do not try to realign it yourself
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=none benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Find a hard board, a rolled-up magazine, or clothing for support. Apply cold through a layer of cloth.
- Plain-language summary: For any injured limb, treat it first as a fracture; being able to stand or move does not rule it out; only X-ray can confirm. Keep the injured part still, support it with your hand or with a triangular bandage or hard board in its current position. Apply cold as early as possible, no more than 20 minutes per session. Do not try to realign it yourself. For bones breaking through the skin, first stop the bleeding, cover with a clean dressing, and do not push the bone back.
- Benefit: The International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies' 2025 International First Aid Guidelines state that "any injury to a hand or foot should first be treated as a possible fracture". It also states that "the only accurate way to confirm a fracture is X-ray. If in doubt, treat as a fracture". The purpose of immobilization is "to minimize movement and pain". Cold compresses "for up to 20 minutes at a time can reduce pain; cold for longer than this may damage the skin". "When a long bone such as the femur is injured, bleeding from the bone alone can be fatal". When pain or swelling is pronounced, when there are signs of shock, when the femur (thigh bone) is fractured, or the limb is clearly malpositioned, emergency services should be called. For a fracture bent at an angle, two conditions must both be met before considering on-site reduction (realigning). One is "remote location (or limited resources), and the limb beyond the angle is cold or pale, or the first aider cannot immobilize for transport". The other is that the rescuer is trained. For open fractures (bone breaking the skin), the priorities are bleeding control and infection prevention
- Evidence grade: B
- Source: International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies (2025). International First Aid, Resuscitation and Education Guidelines 2025, pages 301–302. <https://www.ifrc.org/sites/default/files/2026-03/IFRC%20International%20First%20Aid,%20Resuscitation%20and%20Education%20Guidelines%202025.pdf>
- Note: Graded B because these are consensus practices. Pre-hospital traction immobilization for femur (thigh bone) fractures is supported by only one retrospective study with very low evidence credibility. The result was less blood transfused within 24 hours and shorter hospital stays; mortality and complication differences were too small to be clear. As for whether to reduce first (realign the bone) or immobilize as-is, no evidence was found at all. Three common misconceptions to correct: being able to stand or walk does not rule out fracture; use cold, not heat, and do not alternate hot and cold; sprains don't "work themselves out", rest first. For moving contraindications in older adults with suspected fractures after a fall, see Entry 2. After casting or bed rest, one leg swelling should raise concern for thrombosis, see Entry 11. After treatment, whether to do a disability assessment and apply for a disability certificate is in Section 24, Entries 10 and 11.
