# 9. Legal Red Lines Ordinary People Easily Cross

This section tallies two accounts: your personal freedom, and your money. The things below are what ordinary people often think are "no big deal" but actually lead to detention, fines, and in serious cases prison sentences. Each entry gives the specific numbers from the statute, plus cases published through official channels. The Public Security Administration Punishment Law cited in this section is the 2025 revised version, in force from January 1, 2026; the article numbers differ from the old law. The Criminal Law cited is the consolidated text "revised according to Criminal Law Amendment (XI)" reposted by the Beijing Municipal Public Security Traffic Management Bureau; the corresponding amendment is noted in the body. Entries are ordered from highest to lowest by combining "easiest to accidentally trip over" with "heaviest consequence".

### 1. Do not forward disaster, epidemic, or police messages of unknown truth in group chats; do not Photoshop images or use AI to fabricate scene photos
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=some benefit=large metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Before forwarding, take a look to see whether it is an official notice. If unsure, don't forward it; don't alter the image, don't add "reportedly". The hard part is resisting the urge to be the first to break the news.
- Plain-language summary: A disaster, epidemic, or police message that you know is fake and still forward is punished as heavily as if you had fabricated it yourself. Even if you didn't make it up: detention 5 to 10 days, plus a 1000 yuan fine. If it seriously disrupts social order, up to 3 years; if it causes serious consequences, 3 to 7 years. Photoshopped images and AI-generated fake scene photos both count as fabricating it yourself.
- Benefit: Anyone who intentionally spreads rumors, falsely reports danger, epidemics, disasters, or police incidents, and disrupts public order, is detained 5 to 10 days. An additional fine of up to 1000 yuan may be imposed. The lighter tier: detention up to 5 days, or a fine up to 1000 yuan. Fabricating false danger, epidemic, disaster, or police information and spreading it online is punished with up to 3 years. Knowingly spreading what you know is fake falls in the same tier. The precondition is seriously disrupting social order; the statute's phrase is "seriously disrupting social order". If serious consequences result, 3 to 7 years (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2025). Public Security Administration Punishment Law (2025 revision, Article 29). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml>; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2015). Criminal Law Amendment (IX) (Article 32, i.e. Article 291-1, paragraph 2 of the Criminal Law). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180205_364562.shtml>; National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law (revised according to Criminal Law Amendment (XI), Article 291-1). <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html> (reposted by Beijing Municipal Public Security Traffic Management Bureau)
- Note: Knowingly forwarding false information and fabricating it yourself fall under the same clause used by the police. "I just forwarded it" does not exempt you. Photoshopped images and AI-generated fake scene photos both count as fabricating it yourself. The Ministry of Public Security published several batches of typical cases involving online rumors about disasters and dangers in 2025. At the time of writing, the Ministry's website was inaccessible, so no specific cases are cited.

### 2. Do not post or forward images, stickers, or comments insulting martyrs; do not joke about the national flag or anthem
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=some benefit=large metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. For content involving martyrs, the national flag, national emblem, or national anthem, do not comment, alter images, or forward. The hard part is holding back from chiming in when someone cracks a joke in a group chat.
- Plain-language summary: Joking about martyrs, the national flag, or the national anthem — lighter cases are detained 5 to 10 days, or fined 1000 to 3000 yuan. More serious cases: detention 10 to 15 days. If it constitutes a crime, up to 3 years. Someone sent 2 messages defaming Yuan Longping in a 499-person village WeChat group, and was sentenced to 6 months of public surveillance — not locked up, but with restricted movement.
- Benefit: Anyone who infringes the name, portrait, reputation, or honor of heroic martyrs through insult or fabrication of facts, thereby harming the public interest, is punished under the following tier: detention 5 to 10 days, or a fine of 1000 to 3000 yuan. Desecrating or denying the deeds and spirit of martyrs, or producing and spreading speech, images, or audio/video that glorifies wars of aggression, and disrupting public order, falls in the same tier. The more serious tier: detention 10 to 15 days, with an additional fine of up to 5000 yuan. Reaching the "serious circumstances" tier constitutes a crime, punished with up to 3 years. Insulting the national flag or emblem in public, or altering the words or music of the national anthem, or singing the anthem in a distorted or derogatory manner, also carries up to 3 years in serious cases. Official cases: Xiao sent 2 messages defaming and insulting Yuan Longping in a 499-person village WeChat group, and was sentenced to 6 months of public surveillance (public surveillance is a punishment that does not involve prison but restricts movement). Qiu used a Weibo account with over 2.5 million followers to post 2 messages distorting the deeds of border defense heroes, was sentenced to 8 months, and was also required to publicly apologize in national media (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2025). Heroes and Martyrs Protection Law (Articles 22, 26). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/gyssshmhsh/201912/t20191202_440079.shtml>; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2020). Ten typical cases on the protection of heroes and martyrs' rights and interests (Case 2, Case 3). <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/382301.html>

Before that, such matters could only go through the Criminal Law or through civil public interest litigation filed by prosecutors. A WeChat group of several hundred people counts as a public occasion in the eyes of the court.

### 3. Read things on overseas websites and then close the page; do not screenshot, forward, copy into group chats, or chime in in comment sections
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=some benefit=large metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Once the page is closed, the matter ends. Do not archive, forward, or move content onto domestic platforms. Do not use an account tied to your real identity to post political content long-term on overseas platforms. Do not accept manuscript fees or funding from overseas institutions, organizations, or individuals. The hard part is resisting the urge to screenshot and share when you see something striking.
- Plain-language summary: These statutes punish outward dissemination, not viewing. Forwarding, copying into a group, or chiming in in a comment section all count as dissemination during case handling. If it reaches the tier of inciting subversion or inciting secession, up to 5 years; ringleaders get 5 years or more. Books and audio/video of terrorism and extremism are an exception: knowingly holding them, in serious circumstances, carries up to 3 years.
- Benefit: Anyone who incites others to subvert state power or overthrow the socialist system through rumor-mongering, defamation, or other means, is punished with up to 5 years. Ringleaders (the statute calls them "principal offenders") or those whose crimes are major, 5 years or more; the maximum fixed-term imprisonment is 15 years. Inciting splitting the country or undermining national unity carries the same sentence. Colluding with overseas institutions, organizations, or individuals to commit these two acts brings heavier punishment. Domestic or overseas institutions, organizations, or individuals that fund others to commit these acts: the person directly responsible gets up to 5 years; in serious circumstances, 5 years or more. Anyone who steals, spies on, buys, or illegally supplies state secrets or intelligence for overseas institutions, organizations, or personnel: 5 to 10 years. In this item, those with especially serious circumstances get 10 years or more or life imprisonment; lighter circumstances get up to 5 years. Crimes endangering national security all carry an additional 1-to-5-year deprivation of political rights, during which the right to vote and to hold public office is revoked. Confiscation of property may also be imposed, with personal property taken into state ownership. Administrative penalties also apply. No individual or organization may use the internet to engage in activities endangering national security, honor, or interests. Nor may anyone incite subversion of state power or overthrow of the socialist system. Nor may anyone incite splitting the country or undermining national unity. Anyone who publishes or transmits such information is punished according to the relevant laws and administrative regulations (nationwide). There is another category that does not require dissemination. Anyone who knowingly holds materials that propagate terrorism or extremism, in serious circumstances, is punishable. Materials here include books, audio-video materials, or other items. The sentence is up to 3 years of fixed-term imprisonment, criminal detention (short-term custody), or public surveillance (no custody but restricted movement). A fine is imposed concurrently or alone — money is fined alongside the sentence, or only money is fined. The basis is Article 120-6 of the Criminal Law, added by Criminal Law Amendment (IX).
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: National People's Congress (1997). Criminal Law (Articles 45, 55, 56, 103, 105, 106, 107, 111, 113 — these articles have not been modified by amendments). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml>; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2025). Cybersecurity Law (2025 revision, in force January 1, 2026, Article 13 paragraph 2, Article 71 item 1). <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm>; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2015). Criminal Law Amendment (IX) (added Article 120-6). Full text reposted by State Bureau for Letters and Calls <https://www.gjxfj.gov.cn/gjxfj/fgwj/flfg/webinfo/2017/03/1601761496614423.htm>
- Note: The statutes cited in this entry punish inciting others, outward dissemination, and supplying others — mere viewing is not included. Materials of terrorism and extremism are the explicit statutory exception: merely holding them is enough for conviction (Article 120-6), so this category must never be archived. If you see something, stop there: do not screenshot or archive, do not forward, do not add comments, do not pull others to forward alongside you. Forwarding and chiming in in comments both count as dissemination during case handling; "I just forwarded it" does not exempt you. Content originating from overseas platforms may bring heavier punishment. If you cannot tell true from false, even less should you forward it; see Section 9, Entry 1 (do not forward messages of unknown truth). How wall-climbing tools themselves are punished is covered in Section 11, Entry 11. Disseminating this kind of content brings no tangible benefit, but the price is a prison term, deprivation of political rights, and confiscation of property. Publicly verifiable cases of this kind appear only in overseas media and documents released by family members — for example, a blogger who had long posted political commentary on overseas platforms was sentenced to 7 years in 2023. This book did not obtain a verifiable judgment through official channels, so only the statute is cited, without attached cases.

### 4. Porn videos are for personal viewing; don't send them to group chats, don't sell "resources", don't form groups
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=none benefit=large metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Do not forward, do not save into group files, do not become the owner or administrator of such groups.
- Plain-language summary: Personal viewing is not in the punishment clause. Sending it to a group once counts as disseminating it to the whole group: detention 10 to 15 days, with an additional fine up to 5000 yuan; in serious circumstances, up to 2 years. Selling "resources" for money: up to 3 years, up to life in the most serious cases. Forming a dissemination group of 30 or more members — the founder, the administrators, and the main posters are all treated as committing a crime.
- Benefit: Anyone who disseminates obscene information online is detained 10 to 15 days, with an additional fine up to 5000 yuan. The lighter tier: detention up to 5 days, or a fine of 1000 to 3000 yuan. If the content involves minors, punishment is heavier. Disseminating obscene materials reaching the "serious circumstances" tier is punished with up to 2 years. Selling or disseminating for profit is punished with up to 3 years and a fine. In this item, serious circumstances carry 3 to 10 years, and especially serious circumstances carry 10 years or more or life imprisonment. Forming a group primarily used to disseminate obscene electronic information, with 30 or more members, is treated as a crime. The same applies if serious consequences result. The founder of the group, the administrators, and the main posters are all convicted of the crime of disseminating obscene materials (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2025). Public Security Administration Punishment Law (2025 revision, Article 80). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml>; National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law (revised according to Criminal Law Amendment (XI), Articles 363, 364). <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>; Supreme People's Court, Supreme People's Procuratorate (2010). Interpretation (II) on Several Issues Concerning the Specific Application of Law in Handling Criminal Cases of Making, Copying, Publishing, Selling, or Disseminating Obscene Electronic Information via the Internet, Mobile Communication Terminals, and Audio Service Platforms (Article 3). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/zdgz/201002/t20100204_25001.shtml>
- Note: Both statutes punish "dissemination"; private personal viewing is not in the clause. But sending it to a group once counts as disseminating to the whole group. How much is "serious circumstances" is set by judicial interpretation; this section did not verify the specific figures.

### 5. "Part-time jobs" that ask you to use your own card to receive, withdraw, or transfer money — do not do them no matter how much the cut
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=none benefit=large metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Walk away from "daily-settled hundreds, bring your card to the bank" jobs.
- Plain-language summary: These part-time jobs earn a few hundred; the price is a criminal record. Someone brought their ID and 1 bank card to a designated location, assisted with transfers and withdrawals, and earned over 500 yuan. The procuratorate did not prosecute, but the police still detained them for 10 days and fined 1000 yuan. Another person handed their own and others' bank cards and phone cards to a gang for receiving payments and withdrawals, and was sentenced to 2 years and 3 months with a 22,000 yuan fine.
- Benefit: Anyone who, knowing that money or property is the proceeds of crime, helps transfer, purchase, or sell it, or helps conceal it by other means, is punished with up to 3 years and a concurrent or sole fine (money fined alongside the sentence, or only money fined). Official case: Wang, on instructions, brought his ID and 1 bank card to a designated location to assist with transfers and withdrawals, and earned over 500 yuan. The procuratorate did not prosecute; the police, under the Anti-Telecom and Online Fraud Law, detained him for 10 days and fined 1000 yuan. Man handed his own and others' bank cards and phone cards to a gang for receiving payments, transfers, and withdrawals, and was sentenced to 2 years and 3 months with a 22,000 yuan fine (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law (revised according to Criminal Law Amendment (XI), Article 312). <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2023). Typical cases of procuratorial organs lawfully punishing telecom and online fraud and associated crimes (Case 9). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/202311/t20231130_635188.shtml>; Supreme People's Court, Supreme People's Procuratorate (2025). Typical cases of lawfully punishing the crime of covering up or concealing criminal proceeds and the proceeds of crime (Case 5). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/202508/t20250825_704514.shtml>
- Note: The consequences of lending your bank card to others or helping people "run points" are covered in Section 8, Entry 8 (lending your bank card). This entry covers personally handling cash withdrawals and transfers. "I didn't know where the money came from" is not necessarily accepted during case handling. An absurdly high payoff, requests for face-recognition cooperation, and a transaction that looks abnormal in every way — these alone can establish that you actually knew. The lightest outcome is still 10 days of detention plus a criminal record.

### 6. People pulling you to "package materials" for loans, splitting you a cut by loan amount — do none of it
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=none benefit=large metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Refuse outright. Do not lend your ID or your credit record to others, do not sign loan contracts you haven't read, do not show up in person at a bank to sign on someone else's behalf.
- Plain-language summary: Helping people "package materials" for loans and taking a cut is treated as fraud. A gang of 126 packaged unemployed people as "quality customers" and defrauded over 30 million yuan from more than 80 banks. Those who showed up to sign got a 30% cut of the loan amount; of the 80 sentenced so far, 76 received 1 year and 4 months to 6 years and 6 months. The loan in whose name it was issued is whose debt it is.
- Benefit: Using fraudulent means to obtain loans, bill acceptance, letters of credit, guarantees, etc. from banks or other financial institutions — if it causes major losses to a bank or other financial institution, up to 3 years. A concurrent or sole fine is also imposed. Causing especially heavy losses, or having other especially serious circumstances, 3 to 7 years and a fine. The threshold for police to file and pursue is direct economic loss of 500,000 yuan or more. Those who never intended to repay, and used fabricated reasons such as bringing in capital for projects, fake economic contracts, fake certification documents, or fake property certificates to defraud loans, are guilty of loan fraud. This item is filed once 50,000 yuan is defrauded; the sentence is up to 5 years with a fine of 20,000 to 200,000 yuan. For huge amounts or other serious circumstances, 5 to 10 years; for especially huge amounts, 10 years or more up to life imprisonment. Forging, altering, buying, or selling official documents, certificates, or seals of state organs: up to 3 years; in serious circumstances, 3 to 10 years. Official case: A gang of 126 packaged unemployed people as "quality customers" across 16 provinces and cities, and defrauded over 30 million yuan from more than 80 bank outlets. The "blank accounts" who showed up to take out loans got a 30% cut of the loan amount. The procuratorate prosecuted 105 "blank accounts". Of the 80 sentenced in the first-instance ruling in 2026, 76 were sentenced for loan fraud to 1 year and 4 months to 6 years and 6 months with fines. Another 4 were sentenced for both loan fraud and money laundering, with combined punishment for several crimes (sentences from several crimes added together). These 4 received 2 years and 4 months to 7 years and 6 months (nationwide). The crime of fraudulently obtaining loans in this entry follows the text of Criminal Law Amendment (XI) from March 2021 onward.
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law Amendment (XI) (Article 11, modifying Article 175-1 of the Criminal Law). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202012/t20201227_503700.shtml>; National People's Congress (1997). Behind the loan-fraud assembly line hides a money-laundering black hand (Procuratorial Daily). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/zdgz/202604/t20260428_726494.shtml>; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2012). - Note: "I intended to repay" was not accepted in that case. The procuratorate established that these people had no job and no fixed assets, were fundamentally unable to repay, and had spent all the money they got, so they were deemed never to have intended to repay. Those who withdrew cash for the gang may also pick up an additional money-laundering charge. The loan in whose name it was issued and whose signature is on the contract makes that person the borrower. Even if it does not constitute a crime, the debt and the credit record are yours. Running overseas after taking the money does not end it either: once a case is filed, no matter how many years you hide, you can still be pursued. Anyone who has been sentenced but not yet served the sentence, anyone being treated as a criminal suspect or defendant, is barred from leaving the country. In unfinished civil cases, the court can bar you from leaving the country; if an enforced party fails to comply, the court can also restrict exit. ### 7. Don't withdraw money from a bank card you picked up; if you pick up a phone or wallet, contact the owner or hand it to the police, don't pocket it
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=none benefit=large metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free, takes a few minutes. Make a phone call to find the owner, or take the item to a police station.
- Plain-language summary: Keeping found items for yourself, once the amount reaches "relatively large" and you refuse to return them, is up to 2 years; for huge amounts, 2 to 5 years. Withdrawing cash from a found bank card at an ATM is heavier — counts as misusing another person's credit card; "relatively large" already brings up to 5 years and a fine of 20,000 to 200,000 yuan. For the cash withdrawal, the police file directly; the owner does not need to file a complaint in person.
- Benefit: Anyone who finds another person's lost property must return it, promptly notify the owner to claim it, or hand it over to public security or other departments. Keeping lost property, when the amount reaches "relatively large" and the person refuses to hand it over, constitutes the crime of embezzlement, with up to 2 years. For huge amounts, 2 to 5 years. Using another person's credit card found at an ATM counts as "misuse of another person's credit card". If it reaches the criminal threshold, it is handled as credit card fraud: for "relatively large" amounts, up to 5 years and a fine of 20,000 to 200,000 yuan. For huge amounts or other serious circumstances, 5 to 10 years and a fine of 50,000 to 500,000 yuan (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Source: National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code (Article 314). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>; National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law (revised according to Criminal Law Amendment (XI), Articles 196, 270). <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2008). Reply on how to characterize the act of using a credit card picked up from another person at an ATM. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/flfg/sfjs/201208/t20120830_47871.shtml>
- Note: The crime of embezzlement is "prosecuted only upon complaint"; the owner must sue in court themselves; the police do not take initiative. But withdrawing cash from a found card is different — the owner does not need to file; the police file directly. How much counts as "relatively large" is set by judicial interpretation; this section did not verify. After property is handed to a department, the owner must pay a custodial fee when claiming it (Civil Code Article 317).

### 8. Claim discounts with your real identity; don't buy virtual numbers to mass-register alt accounts; don't exploit loopholes to claim repeatedly
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=little willpower=some benefit=large metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Pass on tasks in "wool-pulling task groups". If you find a platform loophole, don't touch it. The hard part is resisting the urge to treat discounts as a replicable income stream.
- Plain-language summary: Using your real identity, one account, one coupon claim, is not in the punishment scope. Mass-registering fake users, placing orders yourself to receive orders yourself and claim more, is treated as fraud; defrauding 3000 yuan already brings up to 3 years, 30,000 yuan and up brings 3 to 10 years. One delivery rider used virtual numbers at 0.1 yuan each to farm coupons, cashing out over 90,000 yuan; 12 accomplices were all sentenced.
- Benefit: Defrauding public or private property, "relatively large" amounts: up to 3 years, with a concurrent or sole fine (money fined alongside the sentence, or only money fined). For huge amounts or other serious circumstances, 3 to 10 years and a fine. For especially huge amounts or other especially serious circumstances, 10 years or more or life imprisonment, with a fine or confiscation of property. The three tiers — relatively large, huge, especially huge — are set by each province within the following ranges: large is 3000 to 10,000 yuan or more; huge is 30,000 to 100,000 yuan or more; especially huge is 500,000 yuan or more. Teaching the method to others, selling "tutorials": punished as the crime of teaching criminal methods, up to 5 years; serious circumstances 5 to 10 years; especially serious 10 years or more or life imprisonment. The upstream and downstream of buying and selling phone numbers and verification codes: punished as the crime of infringing citizens' personal information, up to 3 years; especially serious, 3 to 7 years. Using mass accounts, automatic network address switching systems, and mass SMS verification code-receiving platforms is itself explicitly prohibited by Article 14 of the Anti-Telecom and Online Fraud Law. Illegally buying, selling, renting, or lending phone cards and internet accounts, and helping others pass real-name verification, falls under Article 31. Violating Article 14: all money earned is confiscated, with an additional fine of 1 to 10 times the earnings. If no money was earned or earnings were under 50,000 yuan, a fine of up to 500,000 yuan; in serious circumstances, detention up to 15 days is also imposed. Violating Article 31: the same 1-to-10-times fine applies; if no money was earned or earnings were under 20,000 yuan, a fine of up to 200,000 yuan; in serious circumstances, detention up to 15 days is also imposed. Participation in telecom and online fraud that does not reach the criminal threshold: detention 10 to 15 days. Money earned is confiscated, with an additional fine of 1 to 10 times. If no money was earned or earnings were under 10,000 yuan, a fine of up to 100,000 yuan. Official case 1: Delivery rider Liu bought virtual phone numbers at 0.1 yuan each and registered new users on a platform. He claimed the 29-minus-15 new-user coupon to place orders, then resold the goods to small shops for cash. He also used virtual numbers to invite each other and claim 2 to 20 yuan in rewards. He and rider Tan respectively defrauded over 90,000 yuan and over 70,000 yuan. The Shanghai Yangpu District Procuratorate charged 12 people with fraud; the court sentenced them to 3 years and 6 months down to criminal detention of 3 months. Official case 2: Wang exploited the time gap between refunds and the recycling of virtual raffle coins at an online store. He refunded 1156 orders, withdrew 224 figures, amounting to over 249,000 yuan. He also resold on second-hand platforms; the court sentenced him to 3 years with 4 years' probation for fraud, plus a 30,000 yuan fine. He returned the stolen goods and prepaid the fine during the trial, totaling 330,000 yuan. Official case 3: New-user coupons and driver subsidies of a ride-hailing platform were defrauded through "self-ordering, self-accepting" fake trips, involving 19 provinces and more than 70 cities, amounting to over 2 million yuan. The Liaoning Chaoyang Longcheng District Procuratorate charged 163 people; all were convicted. The principal offenders Huang and Cai were sentenced for combined crimes of fraud, infringement of citizens' personal information, and teaching criminal methods (sentences from several crimes added together), each receiving 3 years and 6 months. Another 34 accomplices with minor circumstances were not prosecuted by the procuratorate but were transferred to public security for administrative penalties (nationwide)

### 20. If a child is born and you can't raise it, civil-affairs registration is the only legal way out: taking money to give the child away may be treated as the crime of trafficking; abandoning is the crime of abandonment
<!-- cost-tag: money=0 time=medium willpower=none benefit=large metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Go to a civil-affairs department at the county level or above to handle adoption registration. You will need to submit materials, and there is a review and adoption assessment, which takes some time.
- Plain-language summary: If you can't raise it, there is a way out, but only one is legal: handle adoption registration at the civil-affairs department; the adoption relationship is established only on the day of registration. Privately giving the child to someone else, especially for money, may turn "placement" into the crime of trafficking in children, with a starting sentence of 5 years or more. Abandoning the child and refusing to raise them, in egregious circumstances, is the crime of abandonment, up to 5 years.
- Benefit: Criminal Law Article 240: "Anyone who trafficks women or children is sentenced to between five and ten years of fixed-term imprisonment and a fine". With aggravating circumstances, ten years or more or life imprisonment, plus a fine or confiscation of property. "In especially serious circumstances, the death penalty, plus confiscation of property". Article 17 of the Opinions on Lawfully Punishing Crimes of Trafficking in Women and Children concerns distinguishing selling one's own child from private placement. The standard is "the key lies in whether the actor had the purpose of illegal gain". That is, whether the actor intended to make money from this. Three circumstances can be deemed as selling one's own child, convicted as the crime of trafficking in women and children. The first is "using reproduction as a means of illegal gain, selling the child immediately after birth". The second is "knowing that the other party has no purpose of raising the child, or not considering at all whether the other party has the purpose of raising the child, 'placing' the child with another for money". The third is "'placing' the child with another for sums of money clearly not 'nutrition fees' or 'thank-you fees'". Conversely, where the actor is not motivated by profit, genuinely forced by life difficulties to privately place the child, and only accepts a small nutrition fee or thank-you fee, this is private placement and is not treated as trafficking. Next, Article 261 of the Criminal Law: "Anyone who has the obligation to support an elderly person, young child, sick person, or other person without the ability to live independently, and refuses to fulfill that obligation, in egregious circumstances, is sentenced to up to five years of fixed-term imprisonment, criminal detention, or public surveillance". Civil Code Article 1094 provides that only three categories can be placers: guardians of orphans, child welfare institutions, and biological parents with special difficulties unable to raise their children. Article 1105: "Adoption shall be registered with the civil-affairs department of the people's government at or above the county level. (2) The policyholder, insured, or beneficiary fabricates false causes for an insurance accident that occurred, or exaggerates the extent of the loss. (3) Fabricating an insurance accident that never occurred. (4) The policyholder or insured intentionally causing a property-loss insurance accident. (5) The policyholder or beneficiary intentionally causing the death, injury, or illness of the insured. For "relatively large" amounts: up to 5 years of fixed-term imprisonment or criminal detention, with a fine of 10,000 to 100,000 yuan. For huge amounts or other serious circumstances: 5 to 10 years, with a fine of 20,000 to 200,000 yuan. For especially huge amounts or other especially serious circumstances: 10 years or more, with a fine of 20,000 to 200,000 yuan or confiscation of property. The final paragraph of this article covers those who help: appraisers, witnesses, and property assessors of insurance accidents. Anyone who intentionally issues false certification documents or creates conditions for others to defraud insurance is treated as an accomplice in insurance fraud and charged alongside the defrauder. Insurance Law Article 27: if no insurance accident occurred but a claim is falsely made, the insurer has the right to terminate the contract and not refund the premium. If forged or altered documents are used, or the cause of the accident is falsely stated or the loss exaggerated, the insurer does not pay the excess portion and may recover or be compensated for amounts already paid out.
